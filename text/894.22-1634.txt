Subject: Representation of Peruvian Interests: Citizenship of Fernando Ychiro ABEY.

The Consul General has the honor to refer to the
Department's instruction of April 10, 1948, File No.
194.22/3-304, concerning a request from the Peruvian
Government for information concerning Fernando Ychiro
ABEY.

Investigation of the records of local Army authorities reveals that Fernando Ychiro Abey, born at Lima,
Peru, on October 25, 1920, the son of Torahichi and
Teru Abey, has resided in Japan since April 1941.
Since that time he has attended school and engaged in
an export-import business. It is reported that during
the war he was under strict surveillance of the Japanese
police. There is no evidence indicating that he has
ever served in the Japanese Armed Forces.

Local Water Front Police claim that since the end
of the war, the subject has been a constant purchaser
of fish coming into Japan through illegal channels;
however, the Japanese authorities have taken no action
against him because of his alleged Peruvian citizenship.

For the Department's information, it is believed
that Fernando Ychiro Abey and Fernando Abey, the sub-
ject of Yokohama's OMV No. 40, September 15, 1947 and
the Department's OMV No. 96, April 26, 1948, are one
and the same person.

With respect to references made in the last paragraph
of despatch No. 87 dated May 13, 1948 from the Yokohama
Branch, Office of the United States Political Adviser,
Yokohama, Japan, Miss Williams on June 2, 1948 obtained
from VD its file on the case showing the following in summary:

1. 9/15/47 OMV No. 40 from Yokohama enclosing copy
of document presented by Mr. Fernando Abey in
connection with his application for a United States
transit visa; applicant states document sent direct
from Peru through international mail; Yokohama
inquires concerning types of evidence of citizenship
and travel document which should be acceptable in
lieu of regular Peruvian passports.

2. 10/9/47 Memo from VD to NWC inquiring concerning
wishes of Peruvian authorities or whether inquiry
should be made through office at Lima.

3. 10/21/47 Memo from NWC to VD stating that Mr. Fry
from the Peruvian Embassy called and advised sending
inquiry to Lima; Mr. Fry thought that, pending
establishment of Peruvian representation in Japan,
procedure of accepting affidavits with affixed
certificates might be followed when they evidence
fact that Peruvian passport has been issued previously
indicating that person is entitled to receive passport.

4. 11/6/47 OMV No. 9 to Lima enclosing copy of OMV
from Yokohama together with its enclosure; inquiring
concerning wishes of Foreign Office re safe conduct
or other documentation acceptable for travel.

5. 4/6/48 Despatch No. 268 from Lima enclosing copy
of note of March 30 from Foreign Office stating
Fernando Abey "has lost Peruvian nationality through
residence in the country of his father, during his
minority, and that therefore it is not possible to
authorize the revalidation of his passport or to
issue a new travel document for his return to Peru."

# MEMORANDUM

The Department of State refers to its memorandum dated April 16, 1948 to the Embassy of Peru stating that, in accordance with the Embassy's wishes, the appropriate representatives in Japan were being requested to endeavor to obtain information with regard to the possibility that Mr. Fernando Ychiro Abey, a Peruvian citizen by birth, might have served in the armed forces of Japan during the war.

A communication has now been received from the Yokohama Branch, Office of the United States Political Adviser, Yokohama, Japan, stating that the records of the local Army authorities reveal that "Fernando Ychiro Abey, born at Lima, Peru, on October 25, 1920, the son of Torahichi and Teru Abey, has resided in Japan since April 1941. Since that time he has attended school and engaged in an expert-import business. It is reported that during the war he was under strict surveillance of the Japanese police. There is no evidence

=== Page 8 === (confidence 0.96)
-2-

evidence indicating that he has ever served in the Japanese Armed Forces."

Since the above-mentioned memorandum referred to Mr. Abey as a Peruvian citizen, the Embassy is advised that the Peruvian Foreign Office on March 30, 1948 informed the American Embassy at Lima that one Fernando Abey had lost his Peruvian nationality through residence in the country of his father during his minority. This information was given in response to an inquiry received from the Yokohama office where Mr. Abey, in making application for a United States transit visa, had presented a document received direct from Peru as supporting evidence of his alleged Peruvian nationality. It is believed that this person may be identical with the aforementioned Mr. Abey.

Department of State,
Washington, June 8, 1942.
