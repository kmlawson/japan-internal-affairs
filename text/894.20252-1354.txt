DEPARTMENT OF STATE,
ASSISTANT SECRETARY
A-L/H

March 11, 1942

TO: A-L, Mr. Long
FROM: VD, Mr. Warren

The Spanish Ambassador telephoned to protest informally against two press notices that appeared in today's newspapers, one the New York Times and the other the Washington Times Herald, reflecting adversely on the activities of the Spanish Government in the protection of the Japanese interests. He said that the reference to Madrid as a center for Japanese intrigue is especially offensive to the Spanish Government.

I told him that I had not yet seen the daily newspapers and as soon as I had a chance to look at them I would bring the press notices together with his observations to the attention of Mr. Long.

A. M. Warren

VD: AMW: BB: MWS

# ALASKA WOMEN'S LIFE ASSOCIATION ACTIVITIES

## O. O. F. Director Says Madrid Embassy Group Is Trying to Create Disunity Here

### AXIS PROPAGANDA CITED

Report Quotas From Berlin and Rome Broadcasts Repeating Remarks of Isolationists

By FRANK L. KLUCKHOHN
Special to THE NEW YORK TIMES.

WASHINGTON, March 10—Axis propagandists are seeking to create disunion in the United States through short-wave broadcasts and by making use of fifth columnists in this country, Archibald MacLeish, director of the Office of Facts and Figures, stated in a report today.

Axis propagandists are capitalizing upon expressions of former "but still vocal" isolationists in the United States in attempting to discourage friendly nations in South America from expecting aid from "a feeble ally," Mr. MacLeish charged.

He said that in a recent Rome broadcast to South America a fascist commentator quoted the chairman of the Senate Naval Affairs Committee [Senator Walsh of Massachusetts] as having said the following:

"The President of the American Senate Navy Committee stated in the Senate that the United States coasts are practically defenseless and that the day will soon come when it will be necessary to call the American Navy, which is far away, to protect the coasts."

"In numerous broadcasts," Mr. MacLeish said, "the Axis spokesmen have sought to bring about a cleavage between the British and American peoples by picturing each as anxious to exploit the other.

"President Roosevelt is being held up to the British as a would-be dictator who aspires to rule the whole English-speaking world, but to the American people as the stooge of Churchill, who has sold out the United States to the British."

MAR 11 1942

Washington Times-Herald

# MacLeish Bares Tokyo 'Plot' to Overthrow U.S.

OFF Head's 'Discovery' Eyed as Opening Gun Of Primary Election

On the eve of what are conceded to be the most important primary elections in the nation's history, the Office of Facts and Figures yesterday announced the discovery of a plot to overthrow the Roosevelt Administration.

In an official release O.F.F. Director Archibald MacLeish says intelligence officials have discovered that the Jap embassy in Madrid is the center of a plot aimed at overthrowing the American government. Then in the body of the release the plot is said to be aimed at "weakening or overthrow of the Roosevelt Administration."

## Seen As Political Keynote

Veteran political observers said the release sounded the keynote of the Administration's political campaign—that Democrats will endeavor to argue that a vote for the party is a vote for unity and a vote for a Republican is a vote for the enemy.

Discovery of the activities of the Japanese representatives in Spain was recited in a report, the text of which MacLeish described as "confidential, because of the necessity of protecting the source, but utterly reliable."

## Spain Used as Rumor Mill

It is the aim of the Japanese, according to the report, to use technically neutral Spain as a market-place of gossip for propaganda material to be passed on to secret agents and fifth columnists in the United States.

The one portion of the text of the report, which MacLeish made public states:

"They (Japanese attaches in Madrid) are particularly interested in obtaining items of information which fall under the following heads:

1. Political and economic problems that might make possible the weakening or overthrow of the Roosevelt government.
2. Racial questions that might lead to disunity.
3. Social problems, and problems related to inflation."

"The report of the Japanese activities provides additional evidence that they are following the Nazi precept of 'divide and conquer,' " said MacLeish.

SECRET

Madrid, September 7, 1944

"MATSUO, only Jap correspondent here, formerly in Paris, representative of YOMIURI, tells a German informer and friend, long anti-Nazi, that SUMA, Jap Minister here, is at last getting his own back at the German Embassy here for constant slights and rejections of his efforts to make friends. The anti-Nazi German, non-Jewish, has a wife in Tokio, which explains his continued connection with the Japs, for whom he seems to have little sympathy. MATSUO says that LEQUERICA has informed DIECKHOFF that he wants to see him in the FO as little as possible henceforth, that he wants the Germans to pass over as many matters of a routine nature as possible to SUMA to present on their behalf. MATSUO tells our mutual friend that SUMA will remain here, that others of the 22 Japs in town may be packing up to depart from Barcelona with the Jap Military Attaché and other Japs in Lisbon. He says SUMA is very downhearted convinced of the eventual Jap defeat. He says all Japs here are waiting to hear the news that HOSHIMA, their Ambassador in Berlin, has harikirried. They say he has always been a blind admirer of Hitler and the Nazis, has always reported to Tokio the most pro-Nazi view of things, that even now while Tokio is turning on the heat demanding an explanation he replies that all will be well, that the Germans will not be defeated. MATSUO says Hitler's defeat means HOSHIMA's suicide and vice versa while Tokio is now paying more attention to the reports of SUMA and their observers in Bern and Stockholm which have always been at odds with HOSHIMA. He says the Japs are leaving Lisbon because they do not wish to be associated with the eventual German capitulation negotiations which they believe will be initiated or are being initiated in Lisbon."

LES:

Las Palmas despatch no. 11, February 19, 1942.

According to a Canary Islands newspaper a Catholic Missionary priest has been given fifty films by the Japanese Foreign Ministry which he has been showing throughout Spain for the purpose of collecting funds before returning to his parish in Japan.

The Consul states that the priest's purpose is religious but "the occasion allows for other propaganda".
