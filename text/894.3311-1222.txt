From: The Chief of Naval Operations.
To: The Commander in Chief U. S. Fleet.
The Commandant Fourteenth Naval District.

Subject: Visits of Foreign Ships to Hawaii.

Reference: (a) CinCus Confidential letter A4-5(1)/01570)
dated Sept. 14, 1940, with endorsement by
Comfourteen.

1. The Chief of Naval Operations concurs in the recommendation made by the Commandant Fourteenth Naval District in paragraph 4 of the endorsement to reference (a), viz: that the restriction of privileges of personnel visiting Japanese merchant vessels is not required at the present time, in the absence of factual proof of propaganda or espionage activities.

2. Inasmuch as the State Department is not at present granting permission for Japanese public vessels to visit any port in Hawaii, the necessity for defining the phrase "organized sight-seeing parties" no longer applies. Requests to visit Honolulu received subsequent to the visit of the SHIRIYA, have not been approved.

3. Copies of reference (a) and of this letter are being forwarded to the Department of State for information and for such reference to other government departments as may appear advisable.

NAVY YARD, PEARL HARBOR, HAWAII, U.S.A.

CONFIDENTIAL October 14, 1940.

FIRST ENDORSEMENT

From: Commandant, Fourteenth Naval District.
To: The Chief of Naval Operations.

Subject: Visit of Foreign Merchant Ships to Hawaii.

Reference: (a) Presidential Proclamation Issued June 27, 1940.

1. The Commandant concurs in the recommendation of the Commander in Chief: "That all recreation ashore in Honolulu for crews of public Japanese vessels be limited to or anised sight-seeing parties" and "That the State Department endeavor to discourage the visits of Japanese public ships to Honolulu." In this connection attention is invited to the fact that the State Department when authorizing permission for the visit of the Japanese Naval Tanker SHI IYA to Honolulu during the period 22-26 August, 1940, included the request that some recreation be limited to "organized sightseeing parties." (It is assumed that this request was addressed to the Japanese Government. The Commandant was informed of the action taken by the State Department, and the District Intelligence Officer, in compliance with instructions from the Commandant, informed the Acting Governor of the Territory, Collector of Customs, and the Mayor of Honolulu. The Governor's Office informed the Commandant's Office that there was nothing the Territorial authorities could do in the matter unless orders were received from the Department of the Interior. Later the Governor's Office was informed by the Department of the Interior and, in turn, notified the Japanese Consulate that recreation ashore was to be limited to organized sightseeing parties. It was determined that the Japanese Consulate informed the Commanding Officer of the SHI IYA, via radio. The Assistant Collector of Customs informed the Commandant's Office that the Treasury Department had directed that the courtesies of the port be extended to the SHI IYA, as usual. During the visit of the SHI IYA personnel were observed wandering through the business section of Honolulu, in groups of three or more. The State Department's request therefore was not respected, at least not in the manner intended.

2. Reference (a) accords the Customs authorities absolute control over any vessel, foreign or domestic, and members of the crew and passengers while on board the vessel. The wide police powers accorded the customs authorities under this Proclamation

would permit the control of visits ashore by members of the crews and passengers of visiting foreign vessels. It would appear therefore that if any restrictions are placed upon visits ashore by members of the crew of visiting foreign public vessels, that it is a function of the Customs authorities to insure that such restrictions are observed.

3. With reference to paragraph (4) of basic letter. It appears that the only effective means of reducing the opportunity for propaganda or espionage efforts on the parts of the officers and crews of foreign merchant ships during their visits to ports in the Hawaiian Islands, would be to reduce, or eliminate, the opportunity for contact between ship personnel and local residents. It is believed that the wide authority accorded the Treasury Department by reference (a) would permit such action. The present orders of the Bureau of Customs are not to interfere with visits ashore by members of the crews or passengers of visiting foreign merchant vessels, unless there is evidence, or suspicion of violations of Customs law or regulations; etc.

4. Although personnel of visiting Japanese merchant vessels are afforded the opportunity to carry on propaganda and/or espionage work, it is not believed that, under existing conditions, and in the absence of factual proof of such propaganda or espionage, a recommendation to restrict the privileges of personnel of visiting Japanese merchant vessels is appropriate at this time.

5. In the cases of visiting Japanese public vessels, it has been established to the satisfaction of all concerned that personnel from these vessels have engaged in propaganda work, by lectures ashore, contacts with local residents, etc., and, therefore, the recommendation that recreation ashore be limited to organized sightseeing parties is considered justified. It is further recommended that the Navy Department initiate conversation between itself and the State and Treasury Department which will: (a) define the phrase "organized sightseeing parties" and (b) definitely place upon the Bureau of Customs the responsibility for enforcing the restrictions on liberty ashore.

(Signed) C. C. B och.

Sept. 14, 1940.

CONFIDENTIAL

From: Commander in Chief, United States Fleet.
To: The Chief of Naval Operations.
Via: Commandant, Fourteenth Naval District.

Subject: Visit of Foreign Merchants in the U.S.

Reference: (a) Enclo. ( ) to CIC Conf. 16, C-11 /FC July 16 Serial No. 113 (CIC No-5( )/27 of 16 July 1940.

1. In connection with the visits of foreign public and merchant ships to the U.S., I have the following summary of restrictions, as they are in Chief, exist:

(a) Public Ships.
Public ships may visit the port of Honolulu only.

(b) Merchant Ships.
No restrictions except limited.

2. The Commander in Chief recommends:

(a) That all recreation as one in Honolulu for crews of public Japanese ships be limited to or mixed of his-seeing parties.

(b) That the State Department endeavor to discourage the visit of Japanese public ships to Honolulu.

3. The Commander in Chief believes that positive steps should be taken along the lines of the suggestion made in the following extract from reference (a):

"The Department of State ventures to suggest, however, that the wide police powers accorded to the United States Customs authorities by the Presidential Proclamation issued June 27, 1940 under the authority of Section 191, Title 50, of the United States Code may afford the opportunity to institute routine safeguards which would seriously hinder any propaganda activities which might be undertaken by merchant vessels visiting outports in the Hawaiian Islands. The War and Navy Departments may care to consult in this connection with the appropriate officials in the Bureau of Customs of the Treasury Department."

=== Page 7 === (confidence 0.98)
4. In order that the Chief of Naval Operations may have available additional information upon which to conduct these discussions, the Commander-in-Chief suggests that the Commandant Fourteenth Naval District make recommendations for definite steps to be taken by the Bureau of Customs of the Treasury Department in reducing the opportunity for propaganda or espionage efforts by the officers or crews of Japanese merchant ships during their periods in ports in the Hawaiian Islands.

(Signed) J. C. Richardson.
