Subject: Patent Infringement of Hooked Rug Machine

My dear Mr. Geist:

Assistant Commercial Attache Donald W. Smith, at Tokyo, called our attention, on April 9, 1940, to an alleged patent infringement in Japan of a hooked rug machine belonging to Mr. Albert White, presumably of New York. Copies of the material received from Tokyo were forwarded to our New York District Office, which discussed the case with Mr. Samuel H. Feinson, attorney for Mr. White.

After being informed by our District Office of the advisability of placing the case in the hands of a correspondent attorney in Tokyo, Mr. Feinson stated that he wished to follow the course suggested. We supplied him with a list of attorneys practising in that Consular District, in order that he may select and correspond with an attorney in Tokyo to act in his behalf in this matter. Due to this information, we are closing our files on this case.

It will be appreciated if the above were transmitted to the attention of the American Consulate at Tokyo, in order that the Assistant Commercial Attache may be informed of the disposition made in this instance.

Forwarded:
James L. Brown, Acting Chief,
Division of Commercial Laws.

George Wythe,
Liaison Officer.

February 24, 1943

For: FE

An article appeared in the Japan Times and Advertiser under date of April 19, 1942, to the effect that "Of these 3,000 enemy owned patents, 1,500 patents belonged to the United States and the rest to Britain, the East Indies, Canada, Australia and Cuba."

From various FCC intercepts it is ascertained that as of December 16, 1942, enemy patent rights already cancelled by the Japanese Board of Technology numbered 1,087. The Japanese Government has been following a procedure of periodically announcing the cancellation of patent rights of enemy ownership. A rather large number of these patents have automatically expired and it is presumed that the remainder of those in force will be cancelled in due course.

In this connection an FCC intercept

=== Page 3 === (confidence 0.99)
- 2 -

intercept from Tokyo dated December 22, 1942, quotes Chief Nakamura of the Patent Office as announcing the Government's intention of using as many of these enemy owned patents as possible. Also, that the Government had issued an Imperial order concerning methods of using hostile patents most effectively and for the encouragement of their use.

In the light of the above information it is believed that it can be safely assumed that Japan is now effectively using a very large number of American patents, such as Dupont's patent covering the manufacture of synthetic rubber, General Electric's patent for electric light bulbs, the Westinghouse patent for electric welding, the Gillette safety razor patent, and numerous others, as circumstances permit.

Frank C. Williams,
Foreign Service Officer.

From: J. Edgar Hoover - Director, Federal Bureau of Investigation

Subject: Enemy Patents Registered in Japan

As of possible interest to you, a confidential source has advised that in a message transmitted on January 2, 1943, from Japan to Germany, reference was made to 3,000 enemy patents registered in Japan at the beginning of the war, of which more than 1,000 have now been revoked. It was also stated that several hundred have now expired and that cancellation of the rest of the enemy patents is expected in a short time.
