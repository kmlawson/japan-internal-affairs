FEW JAPANESE AWAHE OF NEW HIGHTS DR. OPPLER POINTS OUT
Japan's Diet passed 15 major laws during the past month,
giving the people some of the greatest liberties in their
history, but the rank and file of Japan do not yet realize
their new rights, Dr. Alfred C. Oppler, head of Legal
Section's new Legislation and Justice Division, pointed out
today.
Launching a campaign to inform the Japanese people of
the liberties and protection they now have, Dr. Oppler, of
Cambridge, Mass., summarized the laws in the division's
monthly report.
"Nothing so greatly affects the very lives of men and
women in any country as the methods by which justice is
administered," said Dr. Oppler, former public administration
adviser to the State Department and Harvard University.
The most important features of the new laws, which
merge principles set forth in both continental and common
law, were explained by Dr. Oppler as follows:
REVISION OF CODE OF CRIMINAL PROCEDURE--Th18 law
strengthens safeguards for the accused by limiting protracted
detentions before charges are preferred, by compelling public
procurators to inatitute public action within a limited time
when a suepect is under arrest, by requiring warrants issued
by a judge when arreats are made or homes searched, by re-
quiring courts to hear witnesses rather than draw conclusions
from police reports, and by subjecting witnesses to cross-
examination.
HABEAS CORPUS ACT--This law establishes the right of any
person whose physical freedom has been restrained by illegal
processes to apply to the courts for relief. It also
subjects persons who illegally arrest a party to punishment
by fine and imprisonment.
LAW FOR INQUEST OF PROSECUTION--This law establ1shes
an Inquest Committee selected from the public as a check on
the activities of proourators in cases where they fail to
prosecute. If a person has formally complained to a pro-
curator or if he has been injured by a criminal, and the
procurator did not take action, the aggrieved pereon may file
a complaint with the Inquest Committee. The committee may
also aot on its own initiative. Following examination of the

LAW AMENDING CODE OF CIVIL PROCEDURE--The main
revision of the code was made in regard to procedures for
appeal. Improvements in trial procedure also were instituted,
including expansion of the right of oral argument, limitation
of the use of depositions, allowance of appeals from adverse
decisions on the basis of jurisdiction, and public inspeo-

REFORMATORY LAW AND AMENDMENT TO JUVENILE LAW--THOsO

Official corruption, for instance, should be exposed
publicly, regardless of rank and position of the offender."
"Only if you, the men and women of Japan, become
conscious of your newly won rights and defend them with
courage and wisdom," he said, "will criminal justice in
Japan be administered as behooves a free society."
