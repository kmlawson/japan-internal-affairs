THE RUBBER INDUSTRY IN JAPAN

The Japanese rubber industry probably had its beginning in 1883 when a small rubber factory was established in Tokyo. It was sixty years after the first rubber factory in the world had been founded by Charles Mackintosh in England. Since its inception, the Japanese rubber industry has made gradual progress, though in its early days progress was rather slow. Ebonite was first made in 1892, rubber balls in 1897, bicycle tyres in 1902 and rubber shoes in 1910. The progress achieved in the last decade has been extraordinary, and, to-day, the industry satisfies the home demand practically in all sorts of rubber goods, and, moreover, exports a fairly large volume of rubber products to all regions of the world.

Status of the Industry

At the end of 1931, the last year for which official figures are available, the number of manufacturing companies was 238, with a total authorized and paid-up capital of 38 million yen and 26 million yen respectively. In all, there existed 593 rubber works on the same date, 469 works of which, or nearly 80% of the total, were small-scale enterprises employing less than 100 men.

Production

The annual production value of rubber goods in recent years was in the neighbourhood of 70 million yen, the most important products being tyres, footwear, hose, belting, and toys. In 1931, the total production figure was 59.7 million yen, of which tyres and tyre accessories accounted for 19.5 million yen or about one third of the total, followed by footwear with a value of 15.9 million yen or 27%. Though last year's production figures are not yet available, it is expected that production in that year was by far higher than that of the previous year, as both the import of raw rubber and the export of rubber goods in 1932 were much larger than in 1931.

Exports

To-day, Japan is not only able to satisfy the home demand, but also exports annually a fairly large volume of rubber products. In 1932, the export of rubber goods was valued at 16.3 million yen, the figures in 1931 and 1930 being 11.5 million yen and 13.2 million yen respectively. More recently, exports have been highly encouraging, due mainly to the depreciation of Japanese currency. The export figure in the first half of this year

The main items of rubber goods for export are tyres, boots and shoes, and toys. The most important buyers of Japanese rubber products are Great Britain, the United States, British India, China, Dutch East India and Kwantung Province.

## Imports

Contrary to the increase of exports, imports have decreased sharply in recent years. In 1930, the total value of imported rubber goods amounted to 4.8 million yen which decreased to 3.2 million yen in 1931 and further to 1.5 million yen in 1932. More recently, this tendency has become more marked. The total value of imported rubber goods in the first half of this year reached only 479,000 yen which is about half of the corresponding period of last year, or about one third of that of 1931. Among imported goods, automobile tyres have been most important, but the import of this commodity has much decreased in recent years, due to the development of the home industry, whilst the import of other rubber goods such as unvulcanized articles, soft rubber products etc., experienced little change. The most important suppliers of automobile tyres were the United States and Great Britain.

The volume of raw rubber imported may be considered as a barometer of the progress of the Japanese rubber industry. The total import reached 57,000 metric tons in 1932, compared with 44,000 metric tons in 1931 and 33,000 metric tons in 1930. The main suppliers were the Straits Settlements and Dutch East India. Out of the total imported in 1932, 36,000 metric tons, or 64% came from the Straits Settlements and 18,000 metric tons or 32% from Dutch East India.
