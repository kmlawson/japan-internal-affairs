Tientsin, China, October 9, 1933.

Subject: Reported Japanese lams forirdrome at Nankai.

The Honorable
Nelson Trusler Johnson,

Peiping.

Sir:

I have the honor to report that on October 1 the I SHIH PAO (Chinese) and others of the vernacular press carried a story to the effect that the Japanese military at Tientsin had acquired, in June or July of this year, a large tract of land, comprising 1,050 mou, at Liulit'ai, Nankai, about one mile south of the Japanese Barracks at Haikuangssu, on which it was proposed to build an aviation field having a capacity of 1,000 aeroplanes. According to the I SHIH PAO the property

=== Page 3 === (confidence 0.99)
-2-

property had been leased to the Japanese military by a certain Yang Chih-hsiang and a certain Hsu Shou-lin, apparently acting without the authority of the original owners, the Kwang Jen T'ang, a Chinese charitable institution organized by the Chekiang, Anhwei and Jiangsu gentry, by which institution the land had been purchased in the 6th or 7th year of Kuanghsu (1880 or 1881) and subsequently leased to local farmers. The Kwang Jen T'ang is represented as having warned Mr. Yang and at the same time made inquiry of the Bureau of Public Works as to whether a building permit had been issued covering the ground in question. The Bureau is said to have replied that a permit had been applied for but had been disapproved in accordance with the ruling which prohibits building beyond 3 li outside of the native city. The Bureau is also supposed to have instituted an investigation.

The Kwang Jen T'ang thereafter published notices in the local press asserting its legal ownership of the property in question, and stating that without its sanction no one was authorized to dispose of it.

On October 3 the I SHIH PAO reported in its columns that the work on the so-called aviation field was in full swing, five hundred additional laborers having been employed. It stated further that according to Japanese sources the land had been leased by Okura and Company, Limited, a Japanese firm,

No. 491.

AMERICAN CONSULATE GENERAL,
Tientsin, China, October 28, 1933.

Subject: Reported Japanese Plans for Airdrome at Hankai.

The Honorable
Nelson Trusler Johnson,
American Minister,
Peiping.

Sir:

I have the honor to refer to this Consulate General's despatch dated October 9, 1933, on the above subject, and to state that the I SHIN PAO (Chinese) of today's date reports that the 1055 mou of land on which the Japanese military authorities were supposed to be planning the construction of an airdrome had been rented by Yang Jen T'ang, the original owners, to a certain Yang Chih Bsiang, for a rental of approximately $5,000 a year, but that, in view of the size of the property, Yang had effected, with the Japanese firm of Okura and Company, the organization of an agricultural company for its development, Yang's share of $50,000 in the capitalization of the new concern apparently being the land, while Okura and Company contributed $150,000 in money. The work of clearing the field was stated to have actually begun on September 1, but was stopped by orders of the local authorities about October 1.

The
