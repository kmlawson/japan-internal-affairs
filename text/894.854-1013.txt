SIR:

I have the honor to refer to the Department's
memorandum instruction of July 12, 1937, File No.
193.4/248, with which was enclosed a copy of a letter
from the Department of Commerce dated June 24, 1937,
requesting information with regard to the current
ship inspection laws of Japan, special rules and
regulations relating to the inspection of American
vessels, the fees charged thereunder, and the policy
of the Japanese Government in this respect.

There are enclosed two copies each of the Law
for the Safety of Vessels (Law No. 11 of March 14,
1935) and the Regulations for the Enforcement of the
Law for the Safety of Vessels (Ordinance No. 4 of
Department of Communications, February 1, 1934),
translated into English by the Mercantile Marine
Bureau, Department of Communications of the Imperial
Japanese Government.

The Department of Communications states that
foreign vessels which have both a Safety Certificate

SIR

=== Page 13 === (confidence 1.00)
-2-

and a Load Line Certificate issued by the competent authorities of their country of registry in accordance with agreements among countries parties to the International Convention for the Safety of Life at Sea, London, 1929, and the Load Line Convention, London, 1930, are not required to undergo inspection in Japan. In the case of vessels not having such certificates, the consulate of the country of registry will be advised that upon its next call at a Japanese port the vessel will be required to present such certificates.

Failure to comply with this requirement will subject the vessel to the application of a part, or all, of the regulations for the inspection of Japanese vessels, in accordance with the provisions of Article No. 14 of the Law for the Safety of Vessels. The provisions of the regulations which will be applicable to the inspection of foreign vessels have not yet been decided upon but the question is now being studied.

It may be said that it is not the policy of the Japanese Government to require that foreign vessels undergo inspection when they call at Japanese ports. It is stated that up to the present time only one foreign vessel has been inspected at a Japanese port. This vessel was under Chinese registry, it did not have the required certificates, and its owner was considered

In cases where inspection of foreign vessels is carried out in Japan, the same fees as those applying to inspections of Japanese vessels will be charged. These fees are enumerated in Pages 75 to 79 of the English translation of the Regulations for the Enforcement of the Law for the Safety of Vessels.

My dear Mr. Secretary:

The Imperial Japanese Government, in accordance with the LAW FOR THE SAFETY OF VESSELS (Law No. 11 of 14th of March, 1933) has promulgated Ordinance No. 4 of Department of Communications, February 1, 1934, and in said Ordinance No. 4 there is contained on pages 75 to 79 tables for the imposition of fees for the inspection of vessels in Japanese territorial waters.

As American vessels calling at Japanese ports may be required to undergo inspections pursuant to the Japanese Inspection Laws and the International Convention for Safety of Life at Sea, 1929, the above fees will be assessed against such American vessels for the inspection services rendered by the Japanese authorities.

In view of the policy of the United States to extend its inspection services to foreign vessels without the imposition of fees, it is requested that the Department of State undertake diplomatic negotiations with the Imperial Japanese Government with the view of reciprocally exempting American vessels in Japanese waters from the imposition of any fees for any inspection services rendered American vessels in Japanese ports.

If, after diplomatic negotiations, the above agreement cannot be concluded, it is the intent of this Department to impose the same fees against Japanese vessels calling at American ports as are charged against American vessels calling at Japanese ports, pursuant to the authority vested in the Secretary of Commerce by the provisions of Section 4406 of the Revised Statutes.
