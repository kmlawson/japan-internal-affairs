San José, Costa Rica.
Sir:
Referring to our recent conversation, I have the
honor to report that I have been unable to discover
any Japanese resident in or about Port Limon nor are
any known to be resident in the province of Limon.
Also, my enquiries have failed to elicit any instan-
ces of Japanese travelling up and down the coast for
fishing or other purposes. Fishing is carried on ex-
clusively by negroes, mostly of Haitian origin, from
small canoes in the immediate vicinity of the port, the
size and nature of the craft used precluding any voyage
or more than a few hours duration. Coasting launches
and sohooners are also manned exolusively by negroes,
for the most part Colombians from the island of Old
Providence and West Indians born here. I enquired of
a captain of one of these launches, who has spent many
years on the coast, whether he knew of any Japanese
owning or travelling on these boats and he replied
that he had never heard of any instanoes whatsoever.
The only oriental that he knew of was a Chinaman who,
some years ago, owmed a fishing canoe at Cahuita.
At the Port Captain's office passenger and crew lists
of
