Requirements of the Occupied Areas of the Far East total 245,400 metric tons, as follows:

The above contracts will supply approximately 94,800 tons of K2O during the current fertilizer year. In order to obtain the balance of the minimum requirements, 56,700 tons, the Office of the Quartermaster General recently requested bids on a world-wide basis.

My dear Mr. Loomis:

Your letter of November 29, recapitulating the potassic fertilizer requirements of the occupied areas in the Far East and the procurement program of the Department of the Army, is acknowledged with thanks.

It is noted that the potash procurement program outlined in your letter has been discussed with the Economic Cooperation Administration and that your office has been assured that the ECA has no objection thereto.

The Department concurs in the procurement of all or a part of the indicated additional quantity of potassic fertilizer (56,700 metric tons of K₂O) from Germany, including the Bisons. In this connection, the Department understands that the Department of the Army has taken steps to obtain, for shipment to other occupied areas, first call on such supplies of potash from the Bisons as may be available for export to the so-called non-participating areas.

With respect to exportable supplies of potash from Spain, apprehensions recently have been expressed informally to the Department by a representative of one of the OEEC countries concerning their prospects of obtaining adequate supplies of potash in 1948-49. Although it was previously considered likely that all OEEC countries would have their 1948-49 potash requirements fully covered by contracts by this date, actually this does not appear to be the case. Existing arrangements of the Netherlands for its 1948-1949 potash supplies afford that country only 53 percent of its 1948-49 requirements as stated to the OEEC, and only 48 percent of its average (1937-39) prewar consumption.

We understand that the Dutch are actively endeavoring to obtain additional supplies and that they regard Spain as their likeliest source; their concern is that dollar-area buyers may foredoen their efforts.

In view of the assistance being given to the Netherlands by the United States Government through the IEFC on nitrogenous fertilizer supplies, which shipments are made possible only by the use of special authority granted by Congress, it would appear advisable not to impair this assistance by thwarting, even indirectly, the efforts of that country to meet its potash requirements to the greatest extent possible and thus to achieve a proper balance in its fertilizer supplies.

It is felt that existing information concerning the possible adverse effects upon the supply position of one or more of the CEEC countries of further procurement of Spanish potash by the United States Government may be inconclusive, but is of a character to call for further joint examination of the situation before action is taken. The Department is not prepared at this time, therefore, to concur in the procurement of Spanish potash additional to the 13,608 metric tons of K₂O stated in your letter as already under contract.

Sincerely yours,

C. W. Nichols
Assistant Chief, International Resources Division
