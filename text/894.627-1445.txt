Japan intends to engage in extensive fur seal hunting after October 22, 1941, when the four-power pact on seal protection expires, according to a recent article in the Hochi Shimbun, a vernacular newspaper. This source states that there are now about 6,000,000 fur seals in the Arctic Ocean region, which will prove immensely valuable to Japan. Not only are the furs highly desirable, but the meat can be eaten, oil can be produced from the fat, and the entrails can be used as a source of hormone medicines. As a source of food, it is reportedly already in use in the North-Eastern part of Japan Proper, where the meat sells for about Yen 0.40 per pound, wholesale.

Mr. S. Yokota, president of the Kurile Islands Development Company, is quoted as stating that fur seals can be hunted all year round, and that plans are being laid for the capture of about 600,000 a year.

With reference to your request to have the Japanese text checked with the translation in part 2 of the Yokohama Consulate's voluntary report (dated December 2, 1938) on whaling attached hereto, the following comment is made:

While FE cannot presume to be familiar with all the technical terms appearing in the Japanese text in question, the translation in the Consulate's report appears to be substantially correct except for subsection (c) under Section 41 beginning on page 5. The following is a translation made in FE of this subsection: An operator of a mother ship must take steps so that whales shall not be captured in excess of the number which can be processed within 36 hours by the personnel and processing equipment employed.
