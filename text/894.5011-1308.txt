Caution concerning 4th Census.

1. This investigation is a part of the census in Japan, and at the same time, is a registration of those residing abroad.
2. To register is the duty of Japanese citizens residing abroad, and is, furthermore, becomes the basis for various proof and evidence to foreign as well as Japanese government officials.
3. The contents of this questionnaire will positively not be published.
4. Cautions concerning filling of registration blank:
(a) Registration blanks must be filled out in duplicate and returned by the end of September.
(b) Those residing here alone, in case they have family elsewhere must fill out information concerning their family on pages 1 and 2.
(c) Second generation nationals must also register without fail.

DEPARTMENT OF STATE
DIVISION OF FAR EASTERN AFFAIRS
December 20, 1940.

With reference to the attached photostatic copy of a questionnaire circulated by the Japanese consular authorities in the United States and Hawaii in connection with the Fourth Japanese Census, it is noted that item 4-c of the document reads (FE translation) "Second generation persons also must register without exception" which would seem to indicate that persons of the Japanese race, even including those who do not possess Japanese nationality, are being solicited by Japanese consuls to register. Mr. Hoover's letter of December 2 states that the questionnaire form was distributed to "second generation Japanese" in the United States and Hawaii.

It is not improbable that FBI was volunteered this information by persons of Japanese descent not possessed of Japanese nationality who are apprehensive of Japanese efforts to control them. It has been conjecturally estimated by FBI that there are some 46,000 such citizens in the Territory of Hawaii. FBI may have records in regard to a number of these individuals which would establish whether or not Japanese consular officials are soliciting such persons to make census returns. It is accordingly suggested that inquiry be made of FBI along the lines of the attached draft letter with a view toward bringing the matter to the attention of the Japanese Embassy in the event that the evidence at hand has established that the Japanese consular officers are in fact soliciting persons not having Japanese nationality.

My dear Mr. Secretary:
You may recall our previous reports regarding the Japanese Census in 1935 and again last year between Dec. 1940 to Jan. 1941 of all Japanese in America and Hawaii.
Our agent from Japan informs me, based on his knowledge of the records of House Ministry file just completed, that there are 9810 illegal entrant Japanese in the Western Hemisphere including the Hawaiian Islands. The informant says that they are divided as follows:
1. 2,810 in United States of America including Hawaii; among them 381 are women.
2. 545 in Canada.
3. 817 in Mexico.
4. 5,638 in Central and South American Republics.
I am also informed that there are 159,565 American citizens of Japanese ancestry, listed as dual citizens, now in Hawaii and the Continental U.S.A. Last year just before the taking of census the Tokyo Gazette published this significant statement (Sept.1940):
"In the forthcoming census emphasis will be laid, in view of the current situation at home and abroad, to collecting data for completing and strengthening the national structure for a total war. And the scope and methods of investigation accordingly will differ from those of previous censuses."

Summary of Information:

Information has been received to the effect that a census of native and American-born Japanese and associated classification of persons is being currently conducted by official Japanese Diplomatic and consular agencies.

In this connection information has been received relative to Kaname Andrew Susuki, a Japanese alien, residing at Orange, Texas. Susuki is Treasurer of the Orange Petroleum Company, Orange, Texas, and has been acting in that capacity for the past 10 years. Approximately a year ago, Susuki informed a post office clerk that he was in the process of conducting a census of Japanese residing in or near Orange, Texas, and toward that end he had dispatched mail to the various Postmasters in and around Orange, Texas.

Informant advised that further information in this regard could be obtained from the office of the Immigration and Naturalization service at Port Arthur, Texas, in as much as that office was presently attempting to secure information about Susuki in order that this individual may be deported.
