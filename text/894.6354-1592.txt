DEPARTMENT OF STATE
Memorandum of Conversation
DATE: August 8, 1947

SUBJECT: Price for Tin found in Japan Previously Looted from Malaya and the NEI

PARTICIPANTS: Marion Worthing O D, Commerce
Virginia Karchere IR, State

COPIES TO:

In a conversation with Marion Worthing concerning the discussions with the British and the Dutch on the price for tin found in Japan which had been looted from Malaya and the NEI, she stated that there was an understanding made between the British and the US sometime in 1946 that none of this tin should be cheap tin that is none should be sold for less than prevailing prices on tin. Since we had informally agreed to this position, Miss Worthing felt that the US should not now insist on the US buying price in Malaya but rather should agree to the US selling price as all other countries receiving tin allocations were having to pay at least if not more than this price for their tin.

The author stated that she had not been aware of this previous understanding. When the author in uired as to why the US buying price had been agreed upon for 1946 if such a price were contrary to some prior understanding Miss Worthing stated she felt this informal agreement should have been considered then in setting the 1946 price and further felt that there were even more reasons for considering it now. While in December when the 1946 price basis was agreed upon it was not certain which countries would purchase this Japanese tin, now it was obvious that the US was probably going to purchase practically all of this tin. In view of this Miss Worthing felt the US should not insist on obtaining a price which was at least 13 a ton less than that paid by any other country for their allocation. She stated that she would therefore merely like to state now her reservations to the U.S. position in this regard.

VDKarchere
