Tokyo, February 6, 1936

No. 1670

SUBJECT: JAPANESE FISHING CONCESSION IN ARGENTINE WATERS.

CONFIDENTIAL

The Honorable
The Secretary of State,
Washington.

Sir:

I have the honor to report the announcement in the
Japanese press that the Nippon Mangyo Kaisha, an important
Japanese fishing company, has obtained a concession from
the Argentine Government permitting it to operate fishing
vessels along the Atlantic coast of Argentina. The
secretary to the Imperial Fisheries Association (Teikoku
Suisankai), Mr. Be, stated to a member of the Embassy
staff that Mr. Amari and his assistant Mr. Yasuhaga,

respectively

respectively managing director and assistant, of the South American Fishery Company, which is the operating subsidiary of the Nippon Sangyo Kaisha, are still in Argentina where they have definitely succeeded in getting the concession agreement signed.

According to the JOHN CHRONICLE of January 30, 1936, the South American Fishery Company is capitalized at one million yen, has secured fishing rights for the Atlantic coast of Argentina, and will send a 500-ton trawler to Argentina in April or May to begin operating. The TOKYO PEARL of January 28, 1936, also carried the same news, with the additional statement that the new company was especially interested in the abundant supply of anchovy and other small fish to be found there, and that it intended to market the catch in North and South America as well as in Japan.

The secretary of the Argentine Legation, Mr. Arturo A. Montenegro, informed a Secretary of this Embassy that no official news of any kind referring to the announced fishing concession given the Japanese by his Government had thus far been received at his Legation. He stated that he had read of it in the local press, and could assure his interpellant that there was no economic or political significance attached to the reported concession. He mentioned that Japanese fishing interests had for several years attempted to obtain fishing rights from his Government without success. The reason he advanced was that concessions of that nature could be granted only by the Ministry of Marine, which was very reluctant to grant them to
