SPECIAL DIVISION

1/28/42 copy in U.S.

Dear Morris:

Our draft of a reply to the Japanese is being put before Mr. Long in rough form this morning. We have suggested that ships be not marked as hospital ships in order to avoid misunderstanding and, borrowing from German ideas advanced on the subject have drafted a suggestion that ships be marked as follows:

Hulls painted white
Sides to be painted with:
national flag
name of vessel
name of country
the word "DIPLOMAT"
Decks to have national flag painted fore and aft

If you can obtain from the Navy Department any suggestions for improvement of these markings (size, arrangement, etc. of markings or whatever else they can suggest), it would be appreciated.

We are also suggesting that the vessels proceed fully lighted at night with identifying markings fully illuminated.

MEMORANDUM

TO: Director, Central Division, Navy Department.
FROM: Liaison Officer.
SUBJECT: Ships which the Japanese plan to designate
as hospital ships.

I refer to your letter of January 28 relative
to a message from the British Admiralty to the effect
that the Japanese have intimated that they will employ
the Hikawa Maru, Takasago Maru, and Asahi Maru as
hospital ships.

In this connection I enclose a copy of a memorandum of January 6, 1942 from the Spanish Embassy in
Washington making, on behalf of the Japanese Government, a proposal for the exchange of Japanese and
American personnel. I direct your attention particularly to paragraph 6 of this memorandum from the
Spanish Embassy. The information contained therein
is all that has been received in this Department
concerning

894.2d1
1 F54

concerning the ships mentioned.

This Department has as yet made no reply to this memorandum from the Spanish Embassy. However, there is under consideration a draft reply in which the suggestion is made that the three Japanese vessels in question not be marked as hospital ships in order that misunderstanding may be avoided. In this draft the suggestion is made that the ships have (a) hulls painted white; (b) sides painted with: national flag, name of vessel, name of country, the word "Diplomat"; (c) on decks national flag painted fore and aft; and (d) that the vessels proceed fully lighted at night with identifying markings fully illuminated.

Any suggestions that the Navy Department may have for the improvement of these markings (size, arrangement, etc.) would be appreciated.

Orme Wilson
Lidison Officer
