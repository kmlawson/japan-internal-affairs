THE AMERICAN INSTITUTE OF ARCHITECTS

May 14, 1936.

My Dear Mr. Boyce:

This will acknowledge your letter of April 23rd.

We respond carely and briefly on the two points you believe. We do so intelligently would require a great amount of research work and facilities which we do not possess.

In the thought that some governmental agency may be able to assist, we have referred your letter and a copy of this reply to the State Department, Washington, D.C., and feel sure that you will have a further response.

Sincerely yours,

(S) L. (illegible)
Executive Secretary.

C O P Y

AMERICAN CONSULAR SERVICE
American Consulate, Yokohama, Japan, April 23, 1936.

American Institute of Architects,
The Octagon,
New York Avenue N.W.,
Washington, D. C.

Gentlemen:

This Consulate is working upon an investigation of the development of the Japanese capacity for original investigation and invention in the fields of modern science and industry. I would appreciate very much any information you can give me upon the points outlined below. I would welcome as full a discussion as you feel inclined to make which tends to show to what extent Japanese discoveries or inventions have grown in volume or importance and their relative importance to American and other nations' discoveries or inventions. There is much haphazard thinking in regard to this subject, based mostly on the oft-repeated statement that the Japanese are imitators and not original thinkers or inventors, without stopping to inquire as to the causes and stimuli for such activities. It would appear that the Japanese are trying to make some progress, but I should like to discover, if possible, how much and how rapidly their efforts are felt abroad. I am sure you can assist me greatly.

1. List of outstanding discoveries or inventions in architecture by year, from all countries since 1900.
2. Effect upon architecture in the United States of any Japanese discoveries or inventions in that field.

Very truly yours,

(s) Richard F. Boyce,
American Consul.
