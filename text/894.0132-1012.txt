DIVISION OF FAR EASTERN AFFAIRS

April 8, 1938.

Reference Tokyo's despatch no. 2827 of March 19, 1938, "Proposed China Economic Affairs Bureau", and Dairen's despatch to Tokyo of February 5, 1938, "Japan's Plans for the Yangtze Region - Record of Conversation." (Copies attached)

The Japanese Government decided on March 15 to create a China Economic Affairs Bureau under the direction of the Cabinet, the principal duty of which will be to supervise the North China Development Company and the Central China Rehabilitation Company. This step represents a compromise between the Foreign Office's desire for a bureau subordinate to but not necessarily an integral part of the Foreign Office, and the Legislative Bureau's proposal to establish a Cabinet China Affairs Board under the Premier, with wide powers.

On page 4 of Tokyo's despatch it is pointed out that Mr. Hirota and Prince Konoye rather than Admiral Suetsugu are probably the leaders of the ultra-chauvinistic element in the Government which advocates a strong hand in China.

This opinion is supported by the enclosure to Dairen's despatch cited above, a record of a conversation presumably with Mr. Okazaki,

Japanese Consul General at large in China. Other interesting statements made by Mr. Okazaki to Consul Turner follow. The Japanese are worried about the disposition of conquered territory. A recent conference of military and civilian officials at Shanghai recommended that the military campaign not extend to Hankow and that it not include Canton but that it take in Ningpo and Suchow. There is no money available for financing rehabilitation in China, and it was said in Tokyo that there is no possibility of issuing a new currency (in Central China?) in view of the difficulty in keeping the yen at its present level. An interesting but not very clear comment on the Allison case appears on page 3 of the enclosure.
