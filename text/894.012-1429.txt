4

and the Ordinance (No. 26) of November 17, 1924. (Collection O
of Nationality Laws, pp. 382-387), but I find no provision
under which persons of Japanese descent may, while in the
2
United States acquire Japanese nationaltty, except the
provision of Article 11 of the law concerning "the naturaliza-
tion of an alien who has rendered specially meritorious
6
services to Japan". Apparently such persons may be granted
naturalization regardless of where they are domiciled. The
principal naturalization provision, which is found in
Article 7, is similar to our own law and requires that the
person to be naturalized must have "had a domicile in Japan
for five or more years consecutively". (See also the
special provisions found in Article 9 and 10 concerning
the expeditious naturalization of (1) children of
Japanese and (2) other persons having close connections
with Japan. They also require that the person to be
naturalized must be domiciled in Japan.)
I discussed this subject with Mr. Johansen of FE,
who mas formerly stationed in Japan, and he said that He
was quite sure that there is no general provision in the law
of Japan under which persons of Japanese blood born duitside
of Japan can be naturalized while they are still abroad. He
did not mention the provision of Article 1l, but the latter is
not applieable except in cases of individuals who have
rendered unusual services to Japan.
