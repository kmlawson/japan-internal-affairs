# PRICES OF COMMODITIES AND WAGES

## Prices of Commodities

After the end of 1924, prices of commodities in Japan showed a downward tendency till 1928, when there was a reaction and they rose, as explained in the previous number of this magazine. In 1929 prices again began to fall and they continued to fall throughout the year.

There are a number of causes for this abrupt fall in prices. In the first place, recently produce markets have expanded their sphere both horizontally and vertically. There have arisen many new undertakings. In the early months of the year, the banking association reduced its discount rate. It looked as if industrial circles would launch into great activity. Minute examination of the situation, however, gave grounds for pessimism. There were many concerns keeping up only on the profits which they had realized during the boom period caused by the World War. Many companies suffered great losses in several panics. Moreover the reckless expansion of producing companies flooded the market with surplus goods. In order to meet the situation, the producing companies resorted to limitation of output, but in vain. The demand for goods declined instead of increasing. The Hamaguchi Cabinet, which was organized in July, announced a retrenchment policy and, in November, a definite determination to lift the ban on the export of gold. With the fall in the prices of exportable commodities, caused by the anticipation of the lifting of the embargo on gold, the produce markets were thrown into a chaotic condition and an unprecedented business depression ensued.

It will be seen that the prices of commodities fell to an extent not witnessed for more than ten years through the defects of the produce markets themselves, the reduction or purchasing power caused by the retrenchment policy of the Government, and the depreciation of the market caused by the anticipation of the lifting of the embargo on gold.

This phenomenon, however, may be explained as meaning that the unusually high prices of commodities in Japan which have been maintained since the World War are being lowered to an international level as a result of the development of Japan's foreign trade and industry and of the lifting of the gold embargo. Last of all, we must not overlook the fact that the disastrous fall of stocks in the U.S. in November was a rather severe blow to Japanese produce markets.

The fall of prices in Japan is inevitable, and considering that prices in foreign countries which are already lower than those in Japan are still falling, it is evident that Japan may expect further depreciation. ## Wages

According to an investigation by the Bank of Japan, the general index numbers for employment and wages for 1929, taking the figures for 1926 as 100, were: employment, 91.1; wages, 98.6; earnings, 103.9. From the point of view of sex, the figures were for mailes, employment, 98.6; wages, 98.6; earnings, 102.6; and for females, employment, 83.8; wages, 97.4; earnings, 96.4. As compared with the previous year, employment decreased 0.7, wages 0.5, and earnings 1.4. This shows that just as the prices of commodities have shown a downward tendency since 1924, although 1926 was an exception, wages, with the climax in Dec., 1928, have in general followed commodities, though differing in degree.

The noteworthy thing in 1929 was the fact that earnings, which had maintained a high rate in the previous year (which was responsible for the high rate of average wages in 1928) suddenly fell for both sexes. This, together with the depreciation of prices of commodities, illustrates the extent of the business depression in the latter half of the year.
