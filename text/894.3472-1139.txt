PERSONAL AND STRICTLY CONFIDENTIAL
DEPARTMENT OF STATE FEB 27 1939

Dear Stanley:

I am sending to you enclosed, as of possible interest, copy of a confidential memorandum given me the other day by Alan Stoyne of the Embassy staff, regarding a conversation, as reported to him by Lord Pearsted, between the Japanese Ambassador and Lord Pearsted who is chairman of the Shell Transport and Trading Company. Lord Pearsted, incidentally, is a Jew. Whether this conversation is of any substantial importance or not I am hardly in a position to say, but I think it is doubtful that Pearsted deliberately invented it.

With best wishes,

Yours as ever,

Enclosure: as stated.

Dr. Stanley K. Hornbeck,
Adviser on Political Relations,
Department of State,
WASHINGTON.

STRICTLY CONFIDENTIAL

Memorandum

To - Mr. Herschel V. Johnson

From - Alan N. Steyne

Subject - Conversation of the Japanese Ambassador, London, with Lord Pearsted, Chairman of Shell Transport and Trading Company

Lord Pearsted telephoned me on Monday requesting that I lunch with him and his Managing Director, Mr. Godber, as he had some confidential information that he wished to give me. I therefore made an appointment for February 2nd and dined with the aforementioned gentlemen at the Shell offices on February 2nd.

Lord Pearsted informed me that a few days ago the Japanese Ambassador, whom he only knew very slightly, invited him to dinner. After the dinner the raison d'etre for the invitation became apparent. Paraphrasing Lord Pearsted's statement, the Ambassador called him aside and dilated upon the importance of oil for the Japanese Empire and the maintenance of continuous deliveries at

all

=== Page 4 === (confidence 1.00)
-2-

all times. The Emperor, he said, could not permit any delays or diminutions in the oil supplies of the Empire. He knew of course that the Shell concern realized the importance of this matter and would bear it in mind in the future. They had large properties in the East Indies. Should, for any reason, supplies of oil to Japan be diminished, Lord Bearsted would recall that Japan had the means of obtaining whatever oil she required from the East Indies in which event, of course, foreign oil properties in this area would undergo very substantial changes which he felt certain the Shell concern would wish to avoid. (end of paraphrase)

Lord Bearsted said that he had informed the Foreign Office and the Dutch Minister about this conversation and felt that the American Government should also be aware of it.

At my request, he had prepared the attached tables of last year's East Indian petroleum production and Japanese imports of oil during 1938. It will be noted that total East Indian production was 8.2 million metric tons and Japanese foreign oil imports were 5.8 million metric tons. Mr. Godber informs me, however, that

The following telegram was submitted by Mr. F. M. Davies, Chief of the Sguila Company here in Mexico. Mr. Davies believes his source to be entirely trustworthy.

994.3472/18

October 15

"Santiago de Chile

"Herzog
Petro Mex

"73 Buye remitale creo llegara jueves instrucciones y especificaciones completas combustible para Armada Japan" (your 73 send it air mail complete instructions and specifications fuel for Japanese Navy will arrive Thursday.)

Spindola

The foregoing message is apparently somewhat garbled, but it is the best translation I can make of it.

Herzog is Professor Silva Herzog, the managing director of Petroleum Mexicano. Spindola is the Mexican Ambassador to Chile and is well known to the Department for his activities in Nicaragua and Cuba.

Why Spindola in Chile should be interested in fuel oil for Japanese Navy is not known, and for that reason is forwarded to the Department for further investigation if one is desired.

W. M. Dillon
Commander, U. S. Navy
Naval Attaché
