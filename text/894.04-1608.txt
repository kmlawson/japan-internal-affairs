I have the honor to transmit a copy of a memorandum prepared by Mr. E. Herbert Norman, representative of the Canadian Ministry of External Affairs, containing hitherto suppressed information regarding the notorious trial for high treason during 1910 and 1911 of KOTOKU Shusui and others.

Although now primarily of historical interest, the case itself and the comments of Mr. Norman's informant, who has a high reputation in Japanese legal circles, are relevant to a study of the defects of the Japanese legal system as it has functioned in the past. The informant makes the observation that in Japan, contrary to the West, prosecutors are of more importance than judges. The fact is also mentioned that Baron HIRANUMA Kiichiro, now under detention as a war criminal suspect, was Assistant Prosecutor in this case.

Respectfully yours,

Enclosure No. 1 to despatch No. 127 dated December 27, 1945, from George Atcheson, Jr., United States Political Adviser to the Supreme Commander for the Allied Powers, Tokyo, Japan, on the subject "Japanese Lawyer's Reminiscences of KOTOKU Shusui Treason Trial of 1910-11 and Comments on Japanese Legal System".

# MEMORANDUM

SUBJECT: Some Reminiscences of the High Treason Trial of 1910 and 1911: Case of KOTOKU Shusui (Denjiro) and Others.

## Introductory Note:

Although this case is now primarily of historical interest, it has been completely shrouded in mystery by the Japanese police. It was a turning point in the history of police suppression of labor and left-wing movements, so the following reminiscences of it are of more than passing interest. Source of information was the lawyer FUSE Tatsuji of the Liberal Bar Association (Jiyu Hoso Dan), who, although not a defending counsel in the trial, attended the more important sessions. In later years, as a lawyer who defended political cases, he was intimately acquainted with political prosecutors in the Japanese thought-control legal system. Further information relating to present Japanese court procedure and methods in political cases will be available from the same source.

KOTOKU Shusui was one of the founders of the Japanese socialist movement, but following a trip to the United States where he was in touch with leaders of the I.W.W., he became an anarcho-syndicalist. On his return, the Japanese labor movement was split between the simon-pure socialists, led by KATAYAMA on the one hand, and the anarcho-syndicalists, represented chiefly by KOTOKU. KOTOKU believed that the only weapon of the labor movement was the strike and thought it was so much wasted effort to enter the political field, electing labor representatives to Parliament, etc. KATAYAMA and the socialists, on the other hand, while sympathetic to labor strikes, believed that it was necessary for labor to utilize the existing framework of the government, to elect labor representatives, and to press for labor legislation, etc. An important feature about the famous trial of 1910-11 is that not only KOTOKU's own following but members of other opposing groups were arrested by the police.

## The Government's Case:

The Japanese police had for many years been keeping a close watch on labor and social leaders. It was noticed by a plainclothes detective that NIIMURA Tadao of Nagano Prefecture, regarded by the police as an anarchist, made a mysterious trip into the mountain forest carrying a bundle under his arm. The policeman became curious as to what it might be, but did not succeed in discovering it. Afterwards, the distant sound of an explosion was heard in the district and the policeman at once assumed that the man had been carrying a bomb. Later investigation revealed that he had borrowed a yagen (a pharmacist's mixing bowl) from his brother NIIMURA Zembei, who was a pharmacist. It also was known that NIIMURA had become acquainted with a certain OISHI of Wakayama Prefecture, who was believed by the police to be skilled in the manufacture of explosives. OISHI through KANNO Shigako, the mistress of KOTOKU. In the trial it was admitted by the accused that NIIMURA had met OISHI through KANNO, but they emphatically denied that this introduction had anything to do with the manufacture of explosives. To anticipate the sequel, it was seen that the government's case rested entirely on circumstantial evidence of a particularly flimsy nature: no actual bomb was discovered by the police; even the admittedly anarchist NIIMURA, whose behavior may have been suspicious, showed no overt intention to assassinate the Emperor, as was the charge, and KOTOKU, the chief accused, had only the most indirect connections with NIIMURA. Nevertheless, on this evidence, twelve of the accused were executed and twelve given life imprisonment.

Among those sentenced was a certain MORICHIKA Umpei of Okayama. He had lived for a while in the United States and had been active in the Japanese labor movement and although not an anarchist himself had gone from his home to organize a welcome meeting in 1908 at Osaka for KOTOKU on the latter's return from the United States. This was enough for the police to arrest MORICHIKA and to implicate him with the others.

KOTOKU made a very striking statement after sentence had been passed which FUSE recalled apparently verbatim. Pointing to the mountainous pile of "confessions" which covered the prosecutors' tables, KOTOKU said, "All these mountains of confessions which have been concocted by you do not in the least contain our own statements or views. I have no responsibility for the crime of which we are accused, but I take full responsibility for my political ideas and full responsibility for my political leadership."

FUSE believes that the whole case was a gross miscarriage of justice and that the evidence on which the men were sentenced to death was most flimsy. The prosecutors were savage in their handling of the case and one of them, a certain TAKETOMI Sai, who was in charge of the preliminary examination of some of the prisoners, many years later admitted to FUSE that this case was particularly nasty, but he said that a procurator has to do unpleasant things in order to further his career. TAKETOMI further recalled his cross-examination of KANNO, KOTOKU's mistress. As he questioned her he was impressed by her mental maturity and skill in defending herself. The prosecutor said that never had he heard anyone make such a biting attack on the Emperor system as KANNO did. When asked her views on the Emperor, she replied, "No one has murdered more Japanese than the Emperor." When asked what she meant by this remark, she said, "All death sentences are given in his name and these sentences are nothing more than murders." In the trial she took full responsibility for having introduced NIIMURA to OISHI, but maintained that it was not for the purpose of acting as an intermediary in the manufacture of explosives. FUSE recalled her as a most unusual woman, especially for a Japanese. She was unusually tall, slender, and a woman of strong character; vivacious, witty and gifted with a swift repartee. She was only 24 or 25 when sentenced to death.

Chief Prosecutor was ITAKURA Matsutaro, who FUSE described as a singularly odious man, ruthlessly ambitious and unscrupulous. He had been at one time legal advisor to YUAN Shih-kai. In his prosecution, ITAKURA would begin many of his arguments by the following formula: "The defending counsel may say in defense..."

This technique so angered one of the counsels for the defense that he interjected and sustained his objection. He said, "There should be unity in the judicial spirit; there should be no difference in spirit between prosecution and defense. Both should be concerned to find the real facts in the case and to see justice rendered. Human justice should be indivisible and one should approach a trial in that sense." After this objection was sustained, ITAKURA had to abandon his pet formula. The lawyer who made this point was a certain ISOBE Shiro, who was a man of deep learning and culture who had accompanied Prince SAIONJI to France where he had studied together with the Prince and had imbibed deeply of French culture and political thought. All his life he was a great admirer of French literature and institutions. The Assistant Prosecutor was HIRANUMA Kiichiro, who later became the senior bureaucrat of Japan and is at present under detention.

In FUSE's opinion the lawyer who put up the best fight for the defense was HIRAIDE Shu. Both his command of legal knowledge and his forensic skill in debate were of the highest quality. He was a man of literary ability, being a close friend of the famous writer MORI Ogai. HIRAIDE was so disgusted with the verdict in the case that he later wrote a novel in which the chief actors in this trial appear in thinly disguised fiction. The novel itself is daring: "Chikusho Do". ("Chikusho" is one of the most abusive epithets in Japanese and literally can be translated as "boast", so the title of the book means "The Way of the Beast".) The book was banned on its publication and copies are extremely rare. Probably there would only be a few of them in private possession. (Interrogator's Note: FUSE suggested that one possible source for rare banned books in Japan would be the Suginaomi Police Station where a detective of the thought police made a large collection of banned books.)

Another able lawyer who was engaged in the defense was EGI Chu, who also wrote a book on the case which was banned. A whole issue of the well-known magazine "Taiyo" was devoted to the case but it was also banned. As a result of the strict secrecy in which the court proceedings were held and of the complete ban of any publication dealing with the case, the nature of the case has been completely obscured from the public for thirty-five years.

One lawyer who made a poor defense was KAWASHIMA Senshi, who defended the woman KANNO and pled insanity, saying that no one could have such views of the Emperor as she did unless they were insane. This defense disgusted FUSE and others.

Looking back on this and other political trials, FUSE said that Japan is a country where prosecutors are revered and judges are looked upon as of little account. In the west, on the contrary, it is customary for the community to respect its judges and look upon the prosecutor simply as a legal official carrying out necessary judicial functions. In Japan, however, prosecutors dominate the legal system and it has never happened once that a judge has become Minister of Justice. The Minister has almost always been appointed from the Procurator's Office.

During the second KONOYE cabinet, MIYAKE was Vice Minister of Justice and he was a judge. The Minister of Justice was YANAGAWA Heisuke. When YANAGAWA resigned, people wondered whether MIYAKE would become Minister of Justice and thus be the first judge to fill this position, but instead IWAMURA Michiyo, who had been Procurator General of the Supreme Court, became Minister of Justice. FUSE remarked ironically that this was fortunate for MIYAKE, as IWAMURA was named a war criminal and MIYAKE, having failed to become Minister, thus escaped.
