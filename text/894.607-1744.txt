Dear Mr. Butterworth:

In my earlier reply to your letter of 30 June 1949 concerning a Japanese Trade Fair I indicated that I would secure the views of the Occupation Authorities in the matter. The report received from SCAP is summarized as follows:

It is not considered a propitious time for a Japanese Trade Fair in the United States. The benefits from such an exposition are believed to be intangible and of doubtful value. It is further stated that the current situation demands most positive direct selling efforts, and it is considered that such funds as might be available for the purpose of a fair could be more advantageously utilized in sending Japanese commercial representatives abroad for concentrated, specialized, promotion and actual sales of Japanese exports. The report also states that it is essential for the Japanese to again become conversant with foreign markets for both exports and imports, which can be satisfactorily accomplished only by means of association with the various trades concerned in their areas of operation.

In view of the foregoing expression of views, I am inclined to believe that consideration of organizing a Japanese Trade Fair should be deferred for the time being. Until the full effects of the establishment of the unitary rate of exchange and the elimination of Japanese Government subsidies on export prices are reflected in a higher landed cost of Japanese imports to the United States, it appears inevitable that considerable pressures will continue to be felt from various segments of U. S. industry, due to the past use of multiple exchange rates in pricing goods for export.

Until low priced imports from past transactions have been absorbed a promotional program in the United States for the purpose of increasing imports from Japan would be likely to be severely criticized.

Kobe, Japan, September 15, 1949.

Subject: Plans to Hold Japan Trade and Industry Exposition in Kobe.

The Honorable
The Secretary of State,
Washington.

Sir:

I have the honor to report that I learned indirectly recently that plans were under way to hold a fair in Kobe to promote the export of Japanese goods. Unofficially, I let it be known to the Japanese Municipal authorities that I would be interested in learning of their plans for the fair. As a result I received a call from Mr. I. Iwama, Chief of the Foreign Affairs Section of the Hyogo Provincial Government, Mr. Jikusen Hashimoto of the Kobe Municipal Government, and Mr. Suzuki of the Kobe Chamber of Commerce and Industry. They provided me with the enclosed outline of the plans for Japan Trade and Industry Exposition.

As will be noted from the enclosure, the plans call for the exposition to run from March 15 to June 15, 1950, and its budget is fixed at Yen 471,830,000 (the equivalent of US$1,310,638). The costs of the exposition are expected to be met by grants from the local provincial and municipal governments, contributions from interested manufacturers, and admission fees, rentals of space, et cetera.

The main backing of the proposed exposition is coming from the Municipal Government. It is seeking for ways and means of reestablishing Kobe's position as a principal center of Japan's foreign trade and it has fastened on the idea of this exposition as one of the ways to achieve its aim. The scheme as yet is in an embargo stage. To be successful it will require the strong support of Japanese manufacturers and exporters, and, more important, it will have to attract overseas buyers, who are now congregated largely in Tokyo. A similar project recently undertaken in Yokohama, this office understands, was a dismal failure.

Subject: Yokohama Foreign Trade Fair.

The Acting Political Adviser has the honor to refer to this Mission's report No. 9 of December 20, 1948, "Japanese Foreign Trade Fair", and to forward fifteen copies of a pamphlet and poster advertising the fair.

Officials of Kanagawa Prefecture report that they are experiencing some difficulty in obtaining materials for the Foreign Countries Pavilion, and that they would greatly appreciate any publicity that may appropriately be given to the Fair in the United States. They state that they are particularly desirous of obtaining examples of contemporary American ceramics and glassware.

The Fair is still scheduled to open on March 15, 1949. Transportation to Yokohama will be performed at the expense of the foreign exhibitor, while transportation from the ship to the site of the Fair will be provided by the Fair.

# FAIR OF THE LARGEST SCALE HELD IN ASIA SINCE THE END OF THE WAR.

The Japan Foreign Trade Fair 1949 which is sponsored by the Kanagawa Prefectural Government and Yokohama City in cooperation with the approval of GHQ, SCAP, will be the largest display of Japanese Export Goods, ever held in Japan.

At two sites, Nogeyama Park and Tanmachi Area, in all some 1,996,000 sq. ft. scores of pavilions, amusement halls, amphitheatre, etc. are settled.

## 1. Foreign Trade Pavilion

-total floor space-28,800 sq. ft.

This will be used for exhibiting the most important items of domestic export merchandise. There will also be a display of all other types of merchandise which might have export possibilities and which would be of interest to foreign visitors and guests. In addition, foreign products of instructive value for future exports will be displayed for the benefit of Japanese traders. And also a wide range of imported merchandise and articles from every possible foreign source will be displayed. Detailed production activities of foreign industrial establishments will be shown with the aim of stimulating Japanese industrialists. The Pavilion is divided into following divisions:

a. Textile Articles Division
b. Machine and Metal Articles Division

Extensive statistical records and reference material on Japan's foreign trade before and after the World War II will be in this room. Posters and charts will be displayed. This will furnish comparative information to the Japanese.

## 2. Industrial Pavilion

-total floor space: 10,800 sq. ft.

This pavilion will give the summary of the manufacturing industry of new born Japan which is the most important in the foreign trade.

Manufactured articles on the following fields are sent from all Japan.

a. Electric Power Division
b. Mineral Division

d. Machine and Metal Division
e. Agriculture and Marine Product Division
f. Chemical Industry Division
g. Transportation Division

## 3. Scientific Invention Pavilion

-floor space: 7,200 sq. ft.

Scientific developments that have taken rapid growth in recent years such as astronomy, natural philosophy, medical science, applied chemistry, electricity machinery, civil engineering, and architecture, will be put on display with SCAP's permission and models of foreign scientific inventions will also be shown.

## 4. Foreign Countries Pavilion

-building floor space: 14,400 sq. ft.

This pavilion is to introduce the life and technique in the new age through the progress and tendencies of the post war life in foreign countries. The exhibition will be divided into the following six sections:

1. Home Life and Prefabricated House
2. Rebuilding Communities
3. Plastics Industry
4. Progress in the Industrial Technique
5. Atomic Power and Its Use
6. Industrial Design

## 5. Tourist Industry Pavilion

-building floor space: 10,800 sq. ft.

In cooperation with the Transportation Ministry and the Japan Tourist Bureau, this pavilion will exhibit photographic illustrations and panoramic views of famous places and beautiful scenery in Japan.

6. Pavilion of Prefectural Special Production
-building floor space: 18,000 sq. ft.
Each prefecture has its own originality and ingenuity for the production of goods and wares. This, united as one, represents Japan's industry and all prefectural offices have been invited to exhibit their products with the idea of teaching one another the value of international economic relationship, which is of vital importance for a successful program to restore Japan's economy. The articles on display may be purchased.

7. Children's Pavilion
-building floor space: 10,800 sq. ft.
Of vital importance to the rehabilitation of Japan in the mental, social, and physical development of the youth. The present welfare programs for orphans and handicapped children will be exhibited. There will be special exhibits on a child's ideal world, the encouragement for exchanging correspondence with children of other nations, and on children's handicraft.

8. Amusement Pavilion
-building floor space: 18,000 sq. ft.
Many varieties of Japanese and foreign side shows will be added to the main performance for entertaining visitors and guests.

9. Reception Hall
-building floor space: 5,400 sq. ft.
For entertaining Japanese and foreign visitors and guests.

10. Subsidiary Equipment
Lounges, clinics, post office, staff quarters, warehouses, symbol towers, etc.
