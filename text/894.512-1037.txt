The despatch reports new plans of
Finance Ministry, which are to be embodied
in the form of a bill for the next Diet, to
levy certain emergency taxes to help reduce
the anticipated deficit in the 1935 budget.
Although the present Finance Minister has
attempted to follow the bond-issuing nolicy
of the previous Minister he has now decided
that the enormous deficit in prospect for
the 1935 budget will necessitate the levying
of special taxes.
Although not admitted directly, the
despatch states that "the Cabinet now fears
that the saturation point of tle public's
ability to absorb further 'red ink' bonds will
shortly be reached." (Note: This aprears
to be the first indication renorted that Japan's
bond issuing capacity is about to be reached).
The immediate cause of the 1935-36 budget
deficit is seen in the expenditures for
relief the Gevernment is having to provide
for the Osaka industrial district, which was
recently damaged by a severe typhoon. The
total budget demands for the coming year
amount to Yen 2,850,000,000, an increase of

A summary of the emergency tax plan is
as follows:
A special tax will be levied upon
increased profits of corporations, and incomes

COMMENT:
A first impression from this desnatch
might be that Japan is facing a general
financial crisis. Some of the statements
appear to indicate concern in official
quarters in regard to the ability of the
Government to continue issuing bonds at
approximately the present rate. It appears
more likely that the tax plan ere renorted
has grown out of a desire of the Japanese
Government to relieve the national treasury
of extraordinary strain which it is suffering
as result of devoting large sums to the relief
of industrial areas damaged by the recent
typhoon. It seems, therefore, that the tax
plan is in fact an emergency sten taken to
supplement normal sources of Government
income for a special purpose.
