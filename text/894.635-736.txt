Dear Mr. Hornbeck:-

I refer to my remarks to you after the Council dinner last night regarding Japan's requirements of lead, copper and zinc, - the three most important non-ferrous metals used for munitions.

Japan has practically no lead production of her own and has in recent years been a steady purchaser of fairly large quantities of lead in the non-American markets. She can buy there cheaper than in this country where the market is protected by a duty. I find on investigation that statistics of purchases and shipments for the past few months are still so incomplete that they would not afford proof of my statement that Japan is believed to have been a particularly active purchaser during those last few months. That has, however, been the impression in the trade, which has noted a continuance of Japan's purchases as compared with the falling off of purchases by other countries on account of the industrial depression.

The foregoing remarks about lead apply likewise to zinc, except that Japan has a certain zinc production of her own which she supplements by purchases abroad in the non-American markets. In zinc too Japan's purchases have been rather conspicuous of late.

Japan is a relatively large producer of copper. Her potential production is considered to be substantially in excess of her industrial requirements.

I understand that you are interested in the foregoing matters only in a general way and have therefore not burdened you with figures. If at sometime in the future you should desire further information, statistical or otherwise, on this subject, I shall be glad if you will let me know.

The Takao Plant of the Japan Aluminum Company, producing since October 1937 at the rate of 6,000 metric tons of aluminum per year, announces that production will be stepped up to 8,000 tons per year within the near future, and a further increase of 1,000 tons is probable. In addition the company announces that it will invest in the East Taiwan Power Development Company, now being organized (as reported by this office in its voluntary report of September 16, 1938 on "Electric Power in Taiwan), and that by 1941 it will be able to produce 8,000 metric tons of aluminum per year in its projected East Taiwan factory in Karenko City. Ground was broken on October 3, 1938, for the erection of this aluminum plant at Beiron, Karenko City.

Details of the power plants in East Taiwan, to be constructed to supply power to the new aluminum plant, are as follows:
