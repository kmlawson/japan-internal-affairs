Gold Shipments Being Rushed Out of Japan in December,
1939
There was a noticeable tendency on the part of
Japan to hurry out shipments of gold to the United
States during December, 1939.
During the first half of this month there were
11 separate shipments, each between 4 and 5 metric tons,
1
totaling almost 42 metric tons and having a value of
2
close to 200 million yen.
Possible Reasons for Expediting Gold Shipments
It seems significant that golā is being speeded
to America during the few weeks remaining before the
termination of the treaty of commerce between the
United States and Japan in January, 1940. In view of
known public sentiment in the United States against
Japan's action in China there is in Japan some fear
that the United States may stop some exports which
Japan needs to carry on its campaign against the
Chinese. Japan may consider it necessary to use up a
lot of gold in order to buy immediately many materials
which Japan is not certain that it can continue to
obtain from the United States after the termination of
the treaty, even if a modus vivendi should be agreed
upon. Moreover, without shipments of gold the value
of the yen, now linked to the United States dollar
instead of the yen, might suffer in international
exchange and fall to a lower level.

Chosen Gold Mining Affected by Drought in 1939
The severe drought in Chosen in 1939 had an
adverse effect on gold mining operations dependent
on water and hydroelectric power according to at least
one official statement.
The dredgers in the mines in
the drought area could not be moved and the sifters
could not be operated.
On the other hand, some new mines were opened in
1939 and there is still hope of getting alluvial gold
2
from the seabed of the southern coast of Chosen.
Premium to Encourage Gold Production
From time to time the Japanese Government has been
urged to raise the domestic purchasing price of gold.
The price of ¥3.85 per gram which has been in effect
since May 2, 1938, is considered too low.
In November the Government yielded somewhat. It
agreed to pay operators who increased the amount of
newly mined gold sold to the Government over stipulated
quotas a premium of ¥2 per gram of increase above a
certain quantity to be fixed by the Finance Minister
3
and ¥1 per gram below that figure.

(Editorial from THE OSAKA MAINICHI, November 22, 1939)
It was significant that Finance Minister Kazuo
Aoki made the first announcement of the Government's
financial scheme by explaining next year's budgetary
plan, which has just been drafted, at the annual con-
vention of the bankers' associations in Kansai.
It is scarcely necessary to mention that the
Finance Minister should dwell on the clarification of
the financial scheme, making it the kernel of his
speech, even if the compilation of the budget has not
been in time for the gathering.
In recent years, however, the form of unofficial
showing of the national budget has undergone a marked
change. As the Government nowadays publishes only an
"outline" of the budget, being pressed by ear-end,
the people have no chance to hear anything about the
national budgetary plan until the resumption of the
Diet, following the year-end and New Year's holidays,
unless the plan's rudiments happen to be announced
on some appropriate occasion.
In that sense, the Finance Minister's speech at
the Kansai bankers' meeting is significant.Such a
statement of the financial policy should, by no means,
be confined to the bankers alone. A financial scheme
is not necessarily limited to the budgetary figures
and candid budgetary plans. Even a pronouncement of
the Government's conviction regarding the subject during
the next year, about which the people feel the deepest
concern and regarding which the Governnent is exert-
ing its utmost efforts, will do.
At the convention last year, although the compila-
tion of the budget was not in time, the same as this
year, Toyotaro Yuki, then the Finance Minister, made
a bold announcement and simultaneously sought the
active collaboration of the people for its realization.
What has Finance Minister Aoki stated vis-a-vis
next year's budgetary scheme? Throughout the whole
speech, we could find nothing but a reiteration of the
announcements in the past. Moreover, Mr. Aoki has
characterized the trend of our financial situation as
either calm or favorable. We wish it were.
Nowhere have we found anything which would give
the people a sense of financial tranquillity. Mr. Aoki
simply
