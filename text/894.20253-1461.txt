**April 1942**
**U.S. Japanese Propaganda - Circulars Released in Lisbon**

(GADSD "A")

Up to the time of the Japanese occupation of the Island of Timor, favorable Japanese propaganda appeared almost daily in the Portuguese press. In addition, the Japanese Legation in Lisbon gave out circulars, four copies of which (with translations) are attached hereto. These circulars, in the form of leaflets, are distributed by the Japanese Legation to people in government offices, hotels, important business establishments and to some private individuals known, or thought to be, Axis sympathizers.

The attached examples were obtained from the Portuguese War Veterans League and one of the larger hotels of Lisbon. As will be seen from Circular Number Six, the Japanese have attempted to explain their occupation of Portuguese, as well as Dutch, Timor. Since this incident, the circulars explaining the Japanese position or raising hearts about Japanese conquests have continued to be circulated, but favorable comment in the press has naturally ceased. Certain newspapers, in particular the Catholic "A VOZ", have attached the Japanese as a menace to Catholicism, particularly in the Philippines.

**Japanese Propaganda - Foreign Agents**

(GADSD "P")

A report from a reliable source indicates that the Japanese Military Attache, Lt. Col. Keizo Sakurai, will shortly leave for Tokyo (via Berlin) for conferences there. It is believed that his relief, also a Lt. Colonel, is now on his way to Lisbon. Like Lt. Col. Sakurai, the relief will be accredited to both Madrid and Lisbon.

RESTRICTED SPEECH Encl. (A) to P1. 69-42
delivered by
the Prime Minister, Gen. Hideki TOJO
before the Imperial Dist
Tokyo, Feb. 18, 1942
(full text)

As it has been already announced by the Imperial Headquarters, the Imperial forces occupied Singapore yesterday, Feb. 16. Army and Navy authorities will make their report concerning the circumstances of the actual fighting. However, I am glad to state my views and convictions at this opportunity.

At the outset of the war, following the issuance of a gracious Imperial rescript, our forces immediately crushed the main body of the American and British fleets; and in eighteen days, they occupied Manila and in seventy days they brought about the fall of Singapore. Thus important bases of the United States and Britain for their encroachment on East Asia for many years past have all fallen into the hands of the Imperial Forces.

Such strategic points as Borneo, Celebes and New Britain have also all been occupied by our forces and, moreover, the main body of the Dutch East Indies Fleet has been annihilated. The Imperial Army and Navy are now carrying out operations in a vast area, the largeness of whose scale is unparalleled in the history of mankind.

These brilliant victories are solely the result of the brave and energetic efforts of the officers and men of the Imperial forces who are fighting under the august virtue of His Majesty, the Emperor.

To the spirits of those who have offered their lives for the defence of the Empire with no concern for their homes and their own lives; to those officers and men who are sick or wounded to remote and strange lands; and to those who strenuously fighting on land and sea, and in the air, by defying extraordinary difficulties and dangers; and to those fellow countrymen who have sent their husbands, sons and brothers to the front, enabling the latter to exert themselves without worry, and who are looking after their homes or helping them, and are doing their best in serving on the home front by enduring all difficulties -- to all of them, I wish to render my profound appreciation.

As I have already stated on various occasions, the object of the war of Greater East Asia has its origin in the great ideal which inspired the founding of our Empire. It aims at securely establishing a new order of co-existence and co-prosperity on ethical principles, with our Empire as its nucleus, by making each country and people in Greater East Asia to have its proper place. It is completely different in its essential character from the attitude of the United States and Britain towards East Asia.

Singapore and other strategic points which have hitherto been bases of the United States and Britain for the invasion and the domination of East Asia are resurrecting with boundless hope and glory as bases for the construction of a new order and for the defence of the peoples of Greater East Asia. Hong Kong, the Philippines and the Malay Peninsula are already starting on the road for such a new construction with steady strides.

On this epochal occasion, I wish to reiterate once more the real intentions of Japan to the peoples and the countries concerned. The Imperial forces are steadily carrying out offensive operations in regions of Burma also, with its strategic points falling one after another into their hands. But the true intention of Japan in marching into Burma is to crush British Military bases there as well as to cut off the route for American and British aid to the Chiang Kai-shek regime. Japan does not consider the Burmese people as her enemy. Therefore, if the Burmese people take full cognizance of the actual situation of Britain, which has exposed her utter inability, and offer to cooperate with Japan by shaking off the shackles of British rule of many years past, Japan will gladly extend them her positive cooperation for the establishment of a Burma for the Burmese -- a long-cherished aspiration of the Burmese people.

It is a golden opportunity for India, having, as she does, several thousand years of history and a splendid cultural tradition to rid herself of the ruthless despotism of Britain and participate in the construction of a Greater East Asian Co-Prosperity Sphere.

Speech delivered by the Prime Minister, Gen. Hideki TOJO before the Imperial Diet, Tokyo, Feb. 16, 1942. (Pull Text)- Cont'd.

Japan expects India to restore her proper status as an India for the Indians and she will not stint herself in extending assistance to the patriotic efforts of the Indians. Should India fail to weaken to her mission, forgetting her history and tradition, and continue as before to be beguiled by British cajolery and manipulation and act at their beck and call, I cannot but fear that the opportunity for the renaissance of the Indian people will be forever lost.

As regards the Dutch forces which continue to resist us by cooperating with the United States and Britain, Japan will thoroughly crush them. However, if the Indonesian people understand our real intentions and cooperate with us for the construction of a greater East Asia, we will respect their desire and tradition and emancipate them from the despotic rule of the refugee Dutch Government which is merely a puppet of the United States and Britain and make their present region a land of contentment for the Indonesian people.

Australia and New Zealand also should avoid a useless war caused by their reliance upon the United States and Britain, which are not worthy of any dependence. Whether the peoples of these regions will or will not enjoy happiness and welfare depends entirely upon whether or not their governments understand the real intentions of Japan and take a fair and just attitude. How Britain has used and treated the officers and men of the Australian and New Zealand forces in Europe, Hong Kong, and again the Malay Peninsula, must be fully known to the peoples of Australia and New Zealand themselves.

Let us now turn to China. By the fall of Singapore, a corner of the anti-Japanese encircling front, boasted by the United States and Britain, has collapsed, and with the irresistible advance of the Imperial forces, the so-called Burma Route is doomed to be cut off shortly.

As a consequence, the Chiang Kai-shek regime is on the verge of becoming isolated and helpless and Japan intends resolutely to deal a finishing blow to that regime. But as I have repeatedly said, the attitude of Japan toward the people of China is that of regarding them as our brothers: Japan intends to carry on the construction of a greater East Asia with the people of China by helping each other. It is therefore to be sincerely regretted that on account of their being misled by some bigoted leaders, the Chinese people are still suffering a miserable plight at this glorious time of the rise of greater East Asia.

As regards South American and other neutral countries, it is my firm belief that they will understand our real intentions and will not commit the folly of pulling chestnuts out of the fire under the pressure of the United States and Britain.

I wish to take this opportunity to express, together with our people, profound appreciation to our allied countries for their cooperation and goodwill extended to us. It is a source of real gratification that Manchoukuo, the National Government of the Republic of China, Thailand and French Indo-China, sharing always their joys and trials with Japan, are going forward with Japan toward the construction of a Greater East Asian Co-Prosperity Sphere.

It is truly impressive that Germany and Italy, and other European allies, are achieving brilliant victories in close cooperation with Japan and are putting forth their efforts for the construction of a new world order. I wish to pay my profound respect for their brave and strenuous efforts and earnestly wish them increasing successes in battle.

Singapore has now fallen. But this means that it marks the completion of only the first stage in the prosecution of the war of Greater East Asia. There should be no relaxation of morale, nor elation on account of victory by our people. The war remains to be fought. With the recent victory as a turning point, Japan will make her cooperation with her allies still closer and carry on vigorous military operations, thereby dealing a crushing blow to the United States and Britain and their satellites.

Having received the report of the fall of Singapore, I, together with our entire nation, wholeheartedly congratulate the Imperial forces on their victories, and, at the same time, solemnly pledge, with the unity of all the people and with a renewed appreciation of the situation, as well as a renewed determination, to attain the object of the war by devoting the total strength of the nation thereby setting the mind of his Imperial Majesty at rest.

# EXPLANATION V

Absolute ignorance of the facts is the basis of North American Statements according to which, because of the superiority of the American income, not only is it possible for the United States to recover from the defeat inflicted by the Japanese forces since the beginning of the war, but also to come out victorious eventually, thanks to the fact that Japan is exhausted by her four years' incident with China and unable to carry on a long war.

The above-mentioned incident with China has not brought the economic condition of Japan to such a low state as the Americans believe in spite of strong pressure by the Americans which brought about the rupture between the two nations. It is true that in the pre-war period Japan found herself obliged to restrict her economy. The reasons for this came not only from the conflict with China but also because Japan, while wishing to avoid by all means at her disposal the use of force, nevertheless occupied herself with bringing to its maximum the production of war material in view of the probability of her relations with North America becoming worse because of the false understanding of Japan's position in the Far East by the American people. In consequence, the weakness of Japan's economic power was exaggerated but at the same time it should not be forgotten that there was in execution a gigantic expansion of Japanese war production.

This is so clearly true that when hostilities began, Japan proceeded to reveal her enormous war power which many countries had never supposed existed. In spite of being in war, Japan can continue without ceasing the plan for the general mobilization of her resources for a sufficiently long period while maintaining constantly at the same level the economic life of her people.

In virtue of this war in Greater Oriental Asia, the zone from which Japan is able to draw her economic resources is no longer limited to Manchuria, China and French Indo-China, but also takes in Thailand, Malaya, the Philippine Islands and the Dutch East Indies whence from now on she will be able to obtain supplies of materials which hitherto did not exist in her zone of influence and which will now meet all her needs. These materials are the following: petroleum, rubber, tin, iron, manganese, chrome, nickel, bauxite, tungsten, copper, phosphorous, quinine, rice, sugar, flax, wood, animal and vegetable fats. Thus, it appears from these facts that Japan is economically able to carry through a war of great duration and even to do this with advantage to herself if the war were to be prolonged.

On the other hand, according to an American War Department communique there are eighteen materials indispensable for the defense of America in time of war, which that country is not able to find within its own frontiers. Among these are seven which come within the Japanese zone of influence of Greater Asia. The percentages of these materials coming from Japan, China, French Indo-China, Thailand, the Philippines, Malaya, the Dutch East Indies in the totals imported by North America are as follows: rubber 86.88%; tin 85.39%; tungsten 85.43%; palmolive oil 82.73%; quinine 98.50%; flax 67.46%; silk 98.00%; thus it must be realized that if the war is prolonged, this represents the strangling of North America.

Thus, it is evident that being deprived by Japan of important war materials, the American people's belief that a prolongation of the war would be advantageous to them is an utopian dream.

# EXPLANATION VI

The Propaganda Division communicates the following declaration from the Japanese Government:

On the 17th December 1941 British, Indian, and Dutch troops disembarked on the Portuguese part of the Island of Timor, which they occupied in spite of the protests of the Portuguese Government. Since that time the British and Portuguese Governments have negotiated for the withdrawal of these troops. It appears that the Portuguese Government has made efforts to obtain a change in this situation. However, up to the present it has not been able to bring about improvement. In virtue of the progress of the military operations in the Dutch part of the Island of Timor and for the purpose of seeing to their own defense, Japanese forces find themselves faced with the necessity of driving British, Indian and Dutch troops out of this territory and occupying the Portuguese part of the Island of Timor. The Japanese Government understands fully the situation of Portugal exposed to serious difficulties because of the action of Great Britain and the Dutch East Indies, whose conduct has been in contradiction to good faith and international law. At the same time the Japanese Government declares itself ready to guarantee the territorial integrity of the Portuguese part of Timor. On the other hand the Japanese Government wishes it known that the Japanese forces will retire as soon as they have attained their objectives — objectives which were originally sought solely for the purpose of self defense — on the condition that the Portuguese Government maintain its attitude of neutrality. Japan holds no claims whatsoever so far as Portugal is concerned.

Japanese Legation, Lisbon
20 February, 1942.

JAPANESE ACTIVITIES IN PORTUGAL

Yesterday I went to see INOUYE, but he is sick in bed with stomach trouble. Instead I had a long talk with MORISHIMA himself.

He told me that he had made a three days' trip to Madrid in order to tell his colleague there about his conversations with Salazar.

The latest talk was on September 8th. Salazar told him that the Timor question would have to be settled as soon as possible because the Americans were exerting much pressure. He did not want to break off relations with Japan, but the development of the military situation in the Pacific might some day force him to do so. Salazar added that the report made by Captain Silva was very voluminous and contained many topics. Silva said in the report that the stories by refugees from Timor who landed in Australia were mostly untrue or exaggerated.

No killings or tortures had happened, though of course, the presence of the Japanese forces was very irksome. Salazar added that he did not intend to go through every point of the report with Morishima, but that there were two things outstanding and that had to be set right: the sovereignty of Portugal over Timor had to be restored, and the administration of the territory must be handed back to the Portuguese Government. Morishima answered that they would do all they can to ease the situation in Timor, but that, of course, military considerations came first. He pointed again to the fact that the Japanese did not take Macao and occupied Timor only after the English had taken it. The Japanese Government would withdraw part of the troops from Timor, but they hoped that Salazar would see the necessity of their keeping it occupied for the time being. Morishima said he told this to Huene but only the main point, not all the details as above, as Huene was leaving for Germany and in a great hurry. The main point he understands to be that Salazar does not like to break off relations and that he will tell Morishima beforehand if he should be forced to do so.

The transfer of some members of his staff has, he claims, nothing to do with the fear of a break, but is caused wholly by the stoppage of communications between Lisbon and Berlin. The Japanese Legation here had a big staff who was preparing material (out of magazines and newspapers and books) which then was sent to Toyko via Berlin. Now that no German trains can cross France any longer it is no longer possible to send much. The Legation made arrangements with the German Legation for some stuff to be
