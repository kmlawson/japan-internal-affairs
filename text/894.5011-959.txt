The population section of the perennial Food and Population Commission met on March 27th and approved draft recommendations toward the solution of the population problem. They consisted, in the main, of three items; namely, the establishment of a Social Department, the creation of a standing organ of inquiry into the population problem, and measures bearing on distribution and consumption. The purpose of the Social Department is to formulate an effective policy of the solution of the population and other social problems. The creation of a standing organ of inquiry is for constant investigation and study of the population problem, which reads not very differently from the purpose of the Social

In October the very first act of the head of every household in the Japanese Empire was to submit a census return, thereby concluding the second decennial census in Japan. By the end of the month the following interesting facts in this connection had been published: the population of the Hokkaido is 2,794,000 (505,000 households), an increase since 1925 of 297,000; the population of Karafuto (Japanese Saghalien) is 294,913, an increase of 91,159 since 1925; the number of Japanese in Chosen has increased in five years by 63,738, totaling at present 488,478, while the number of natives has increased by 241,111, totaling now 18,784,437; the city of Nagoya spent two days and Yen 50,000 in celebration of reaching the million mark in population, only to discover to its subsequent chagrin that there had been an over-estimate of 100,000, for which the Mayor publicly expressed regret.

Tokyo, Japan, December 4, 1930.

Japanese subjects resident abroad.

As of possible interest to the Department and other departments of the Government, I have the honor to submit the following in connection with the residence of Japanese subjects abroad.

The Japanese Foreign Office has just released some interesting statistics in connection with the residence of Japanese subjects abroad.

In reviewing the movement of Japanese abroad since 1920, it appears that emigration has shown continual increases except during the year 1923, during which period the total number abroad decreased by 8,374. Since 1925 annual increases have approximated as much as 20,000 a year. In 1929, however, an increase of 49,603 is scheduled. On October 1, 1929, it appears that 759,435 Japanese subjects were recorded as living abroad. This figure represents only 1.2 per cent. of the population of Japan on the same day which is given approximately as 65,000,000.

The Bureau of General Statistics of the Imperial Japanese Cabinet has just released certain fundamental preliminary figures in connection with the census of Japan taken on October 1, 1930. Officials in charge point out that these figures are based on an estimate made from aggregate returns received from census enumerators. It may therefore be necessary to affect slight changes when the individual returns have been more carefully tabulated.

Previous censuses were taken in 1920 and 1925. The total increase of population since 1920 is given as 15.2 per cent. Only a few fundamental figures have so far been released, which are tabulated below. There is little upon which to comment, since the figures speak for themselves.

Editorials and other press articles have drawn comparisons between the present population of Japan and that of other countries of the world. One paper states that Russia and the United States are the only countries with a population larger than that of Japan, while that of Germany just equals it.

It is interesting to note that the density of population at the rate given for Japan, 169 persons per square kilometer (439 persons per square mile), is less than that for Belgium, Holland, and England, which have respectively 245 persons per square kilometer (634 per square

The sex balance of the population is an interesting feature in which the ratio is given as 101 males to 100 females.

In connection with the stated population of the larger cities in Japan, the fact should be born in mind that the population as stated concerns limits imposed for administrative purposes only, that most of the cities have large adjoining suburbs. The line of demarcation between the cities and their adjoining suburban centers is frequently undefinable from the appertaining physical aspects concerned, so the population of the urban settlements is actually much larger than the figures indicate.

Japan's fourth national census was taken on October 1, 1935. The Bureau of General Statistics of the Imperial Japanese Cabinet has recently announced certain preliminary results of the census.

The ascertained population of the Japanese Empire on October 1, 1935, according to this preliminary release was 97,694,628, a total increase of 7,298,585, or 8.1 percent, over the actual population according to the 1930 National Census. This is an average annual increase of 1,459,717 for the five year period. Detailed figures showing the population by geographical areas, and of the principal cities, are given in the following tabulations. The immense increase in Tokyo's population is more apparent than real as it has been brought about by a consolidation into one unified metropolis of all the urban areas in Tokyo Prefecture surrounding the city of Tokyo.

The 127 cities in Japan Proper, according to the present census, have an aggregate population of 22,665,920, as compared with 46,585,345 for all other areas. This is a ratio of 32.7 to 67.3, as compared with 24 to 76 in 1930, indicating that there has been a tendency during the last five years for the population to concentrate in the urban districts.

The census figures announced show that the 1935 population of the Empire comprised 49,240,659 males, and 48,453,969 females, or a ratio of 101.6 to 100.

## Nagoya

With a total of 1,219,550 persons, Nagoya (the third largest city in the Empire) accounts for 40 percent of the population of the Prefecture. With 259,945 residences, there is an average of 4.6 residents per house. These statistics for the City represent an increase of 105,000 in the population and 28,745 in the number of residences over the estimated figures of the Municipal Bureau of Statistics of October 1, 1936.

## Fiftieth Anniversary of Nagoya

Preparations are being planned for the celebration in 1939 of the fiftieth anniversary of the establishment of the Municipality of Nagoya. At that time (October 1, 1889) with a population of 150,125, the City covered an area of 12.5 square miles. The present area of the City is 58.3 square miles, and its growth in commerce and industry, as well as in other respects, particularly in the last decade, has been little short of phenomenal.
