GENERAL HILLDRING
DEPARTMENT OF STATE

In reference to the following incoming cable;

"From: SCAP Tokyo
To: War Department for COS
Nr: Z 02642 13 June 1946

Reurad WCL 43181 and ourad Z-39518. Although very little traffic anticipated desire establish commercial telegraph service via United States between Japan and Military bases at Papeete Tahiti, New Caledonia, Suva Fiji and Samoa. Request authorization, rates and routing information.";

we have obtained rates from the U.S. carriers and cleared the wording of the following cable with Treasury (Mr. Parker). If it is agreeable to you, would you kindly forward the draft cable to CAD as a basis for reply to SCAP.

DRAFT CABLE

Reurad Z-02642 13 June 1946.

1. Interested government agencies approve the establishment of telecommunications via United States between Japan and military bases at Papeete Tahiti, New Caledonia, Suva Fiji, and Samoa.

2. Rates are determined as follows:

a. Present rate from Japan to U.S. is added to rate from U.S. to delivery point at bases.

b. Rates per word from U.S. to bases are 30 cents full; 20 cents code; 15 cents deferred; 10 cents night letter (minimum 25 words).

3. Route messages from Japan via San Francisco for onward transmission by U.S. carriers furnishing service to bases.

4. Present arrangements on Japan-U.S. service to apply, such as procedure for yen conversion by carriers, classes of messages, and restriction of service to U.S. Military and authorized personnel.

TO : A-H - JK Secretariat
FROM : JK - Mr. Whitman
SUBJECT: Commercial mail with Japan
DATE: October 16, 1946

On August 19, JK prepared a message on this subject. CAD raised some questions on the timing of the proposal in light of the fact that a limited mail service was just opening. Since that date SCAP has asked approval for commercial information telegraph communication service (C 64293) and the United States Government has recommended commercial information mail service (WAR 82429).

In the light of these steps it is felt that SCAP should now be approached on the question of using commercial mail as a method of stimulating exports. Allied businessmen would be permitted to correspond with firms in Japan, make preliminary arrangements for a deal, but turn over final contract to USCC. SCAP would be protected by censorship of mail and by final control of the action taken by Japanese firms.

It would be appreciated if the War Department be requested to transmit the following message to SCAP:

DRAFT CABLE

In consideration of proposals to opening telegraph service and expanding postal service to include first class letters (HYBRAD C 64293 Aug 19, and CURAD 82429 Oct 5,) believe scheme for stimulation of exports, through use of commercial information messages should also be examined. Agencies here believe exports can be expanded if following were adopted:

1. Blanket exemption to Trading with Enemy Act would be made for non-transactional correspondence
