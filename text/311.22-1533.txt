Your memorandum of 1946
Draft Cable to SCAP from War

Rourad C 64293, 19 August 1946. Interested government agencies considering your proposal for early reestablishment telecommunications between Japan and outside world. View here is that such service should not be opened for Japan until similar service has been extended to Korea. We favor opening service for telegrams or extending postal service to include first class letters as soon as SCAP feels that either type of traffic can be censored effectively. Both services would include personal and commercial-informational correspondence. We consider it preferable that both services be opened simultaneously if possible.

We offer following comments on your suggestions:

Reur 1(a). Suggest adding "not otherwise unacceptable to SCAP" after phrase "any person in Japan".

Reur 1(b). In second paragraph do you mean Washington agency should control accounts of other terminal countries or should arrange for establishment control accounts in other terminal countries? Neither arrangement feasible as no agency here set up to perform such functions. Since no Washington agency has facilities to keep records and accounts as stated will SCAP be able to establish such accounts and records?

Reur 1(c). Messages from Japan prepaid in regular yen currency

Reur (1) (c)

At present, all messages to or from non-accredited persons in Japan are paid for in U.S. dollars from sources other than WD XXXX appropriations or export fund. These dollars split between US carriers and export fund, which receives/dollar credit equivalent to value of services performed by Jap carrier. If this method of payment adhered to in any extension of services, volume of communications would be less than under your proposal, but every message to or from non-accredited person would increase funds for essential imports. Under your proposal, export proceeds from telecommunications would be less than under method just described. 1. Interested government agencies approve the establishment of telecommunications via United States between Japan and military bases at Papoete Tahiti, New Caledonia, Suva Fiji, and Samoa.

From: GHQ SCAP Tokyo Japan
To: War Department for WARCOS
Nr: C 64292
19 August 1946

1. In anticipation of the future need for enlarged international telecommunications service to and from Japan and the desirability of free communications under adequate censorship limiting messages to approved contents, the following policies for control of international telecommunications between Japan and other nations are submitted for consideration and approval.

(a) That subject to censorship, any person in Japan may communicate with any person overseas and vice versa by means of an international civil telecommunications circuit now, or hereafter, authorized by SCAP.

(b) That arrangements shall be made through the proper Washington Agency for the keeping of definitive records of telecommunications traffic in all countries in which terminal of a circuit from Japan is located, and that if possible, control accounts be established in such terminal countries in which the Japanese share of revenue derived from prepaid traffic from, or collect traffic to, such countries may be deposited in currency of the country for such future disposition as may later be determined.
