Enclosure to despatch no. 408 of April 10, 1941, from Frank P. Lockhart, American Consul General, Shanghai, China, on the subject: "Supplement to Report on "Developments in Agricultural Policy and Programs for the Japanese Empire and Occupied Areas in China and "Manchukuo" for the Year 1940".

# Control of Vegetable Oil.

On November 5, 1940, an ordinance was promulgated for the establishment of a Soybean and Soybean Oil Distribution Control Company. 1/ This company was designated by the Japanese Government as the sole purchaser and importer of soybeans and soybean oil both imported from Manchuria and produced in Japan. Parties other than this control company are not permitted to import soybeans and oil from foreign countries, excepting in specified cases. Following closely the establishment of this company on November 20, 1940, 2/ a Vegetable Oil and Seed Distribution Control Ordinance was promulgated providing for the establishment of a control company to purchase and distribute vegetable oils and materials except those needed for sowing purposes in Japan. According to this ordinance, manufacturers, importers and their associations must sell all their imported or manufactured products to the designated control company at prices fixed by the government. Manufacturers of vegetable oil thereafter would not be permitted to purchase their raw materials from sources other than the control company.

Specific items included in the control of this company are not stated but in view of the earlier establishment of the Soybean Control Company, it is presumed that the Vegetable Oil Control Company does not exercise control over soybeans and oil, at least as far as importation of such products, and marketing of domestic products are concerned. This newer established company seems to have a wider scope of power with respect to the products included within its provisions than the Soybean and Soybean Oil Distribution Control Company.

Dr. Nasu states (page 1) that the
population of Japan proper wells probably
increase by approximately ten million persons
during the next thirty years and then will
probably make no further increase.

At page 11 is included a table with regard
to the average size of farm land holdings in
Japan from which it appears that three-fourths
of the farm land owners of Japan possess holdings
of less than two and one-half acres.

The cultivated area of Japan is approximately one-twelfth of the cultivated area of the United States, but in Japan the number of persons gainfully engaged in agriculture is more than double that in the United States; and the area of cultivated land per person (presumably per person gainfully engaged in agriculture) is 25 times as large in the United States as in Japan (page 14).

Average income per day's labor of agricultural land owners in Japan is 1.48 yen (page 136). It is less for farmers who are not land owners (page 137).

Recent copies Japanese publications available here give following data on Japanese agricultural developments.

One. Japanese food situation (1941 - 1943) has steadily worsened due diminishing crops caused mainly by unseasonable weather conditions. Authorities have taken practically every possible step to increase domestic production foodstuffs and to rationalize distribution and consumption. Consequently results in 1944 and 1945 are expected not to be worse than those in 1943.

Two. Difficulties in rice culture have influenced authorities to expand culture of sweet potatoes, white potatoes, wheat, barley and rye with special emphasis on potatoes and barley since their nutritive value is high, cost of production is low and crops can be

Three. Crops in 1943 reached for soya beans 3,000,000 koku, Awa millet 500,000 koku, Hye millet 350,000 koku and buckwheat 650,000 koku. Production all these subsidiary cereals to be expended in 1944 by careful selection fields sown thereto. Average crop white potatoes equaled 500,000,000 kahu (3.75 kilograms) with 790,000,000 planned for 1943 and 908,000,000 for 1944; sweet potatoes average crop 1,000,000 kahu with 1,750,000 planned for 1944. Rice crop planned at 74,000,000 koku. 1943 in wheat, barley and naked rye equaled in quantity those of 1942. Plans for 1944 call for 10% increase in sown area with planned production 34,000,000 koku. To encourage crop expansion by peasants official price of barley raised for 1944 by 37%, naked rye by approximately 100% and wheat by 32%. Emphasis on barley and rye as compared to wheat due their greater yield. Increased production thereof in 1944 expected to reduce present dependence on imported rice.
