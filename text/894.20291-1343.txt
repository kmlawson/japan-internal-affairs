111, April 18, noon
Department's 91, April 15.

Arrests in question were carried out over last week by Iranian authorities but at British request. British Minister informs me he demanded this action because of his preoccupation at increasing pro-German sentiments and activities of many Iranians and because he hoped thereby to deter others of like persuasion. He states he had expected to leave matter of arrest and exile to Southern Iran entirely in hands of Iranian authorities. However, because police action was so half-hearted and dilatory and because he has had to exert such strong pressure to obtain detention he now intends to send accused to Ahwaz where they can be kept under British surveillance. Russian Ambassador was kept informed and offered no objection.

Following six have been arrested: (1) Hesamed Ing Haffary, employee of Japanese Legation and active Axis

Axis agent now in British custody in Tehran; (2) Alid Arroudi, rich Meshed merchant now arrested and en route to Tehran; (3) Mohamad Hosein Vasiri, official of Government travel agency now at Yadz; (4) Gholamre Zadarugar, Tehran soap manufacturer now at Yadz; (5) Asadollah Darush, official of Telegraph Department now at Yadz and (6) General Saremeddin Zolfaqari, high official of Ministry of Health now in Tehran. Case of a seventh, general Rez Aghul Iataoai, brother-in-law of Shah and head of Army Health Department is being handled secretly and in special manner. Shah has promised to prevail on him to leave Tehran for the south. While all of above are well known persons none is of really outstanding importance.

Persons arrested are accused of pro-Nazi and anti-constitutional activities. I have discussed cases with British Minister and examined some of files. Evidence of guilt is sketchy, vague and circumstantial but I have not slightest doubt that all those arrested are at least pro-German and perhaps are actively working in German interests.

Iranian reaction is unfavorable but relatively mild due partly to fact that Iranians are inured to arbitrary

arbitrary arrest and partly because they consider it only another British act which it is their fate to bear. Many Iranians feel that at least the accused should have been given a hearing.

While I believe the arrests will not have desired salutory effect but will only serve further to alienate Iranians, I should be reluctant to criticize British act. Iranians, while giving lip service to Allied cause, have frequently obstructed British and have offered little of the cooperation which their treaty obligations and official statements have given the Allies the right to expect.

MINOR
