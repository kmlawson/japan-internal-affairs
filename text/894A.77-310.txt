Subject: Railway Department considers methods of increasing revenue in Taiwan.

As a means of increasing the revenue of the Railway Department, which has decreased in recent years in an appreciable amount, that Department is contemplating inaugurating bus services between important centers as "feeders" to the railway line. There is also the probability that the Department will endeavor to buy up and eliminate bus services parallel to and competing with the railway lines, quite possibly instituting services supplementary to the railway services. There is, however, the difficulty of raising the capital to buy out the existing private bus companies in view of the cuts in the appropriations allowed under the present budget, it being estimated that several hundred thousand yen would be required for the purpose.

The success of the motor-operated light railway cars as supplementing the regular steam railway schedule, described in a report "The use of light railway cars in Taiwan" dated December 15, 1930, has caused a extension of this service in the south of Taiwan, in and around Taichu and Tainan. It is understood that the Railway Department is hesitating between a very wide development of this type of service and the inauguration of parallel bus lines. Indications point, however, to an increase in the number of light railway cars and the inauguration of bus services, when money becomes available.

In either case the action of the Railway Department will work to the benefit of importers of American automotives and machinery. The greater number of busses
