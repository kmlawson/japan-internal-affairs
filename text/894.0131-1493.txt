AMERICAN ASSOCIATION OF UNIVERSITY WOMEN
NATIONAL HEADQUARTERS, 1634 I STREET, N.W.
WASHINGTON 6, D.C.

October 13, 1945

President Harry C. Truman
The White House
Washington, D.C.

Dear Mr. President:

May I express to you, on behalf of the American Association of University Women, our deep satisfaction in the United States directive to Japan calling for the enfranchisement of women in that country?

No more fundamental step could be taken in the effort to turn Japan from a war-loving, oligarchic country to one that practices peace and democracy than to give the vote to the women of Japan as well as to the men. In a government by the people it is of course just, proper, and essential that women, as people, should vote and should share in that government. If Japan is to have a democratic government, it therefore should have a government by men and women. Moreover, as women in Japan learn to exercise the right of suffrage and to participate in government, their influence cannot fail to height the scales against that country's practice of aggressive war.

We rejoice that the directive issued by this country, through General MacArthur, has so promptly and vigorously insisted on this reform in Japan, and we congratulate you and all officials who have had a part in sharing the policy.

Sincerely yours,

Kathryn McHale
General Director
