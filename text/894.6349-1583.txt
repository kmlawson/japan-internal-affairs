MAY 26 1947

My dear Mr. Dulligan:

I have your letter of May 2, 1947 concerning the availability of
precious stones seized by the occupation authorities in Japan.

The Supreme Commander for the Allied Powers has not yet placed in
his export program any of the precious gems or similar items which
have been impounded by our forces in Japan. Inasmuch as many of these
items may be in the category of property looted from areas formerly
occupied by Japan, their disposition will be governed by policies yet
to be determined by the Far Eastern Commission. If such policies
subsequently provide for the sale of these items in the United States,
appropriate publicity will be given to the matter at the time they are
offered for sale.

Sincerely yours,

Edwin M. Martin
Chief
Division of Japanese and Korean
Economic Affairs

Mr. James F. Dulligan,
60 East 42nd Street,
New York 17, New York.

PROPOSED LETTER TO SCAP
8 July 1947

SUBJECT: Report on Precious Metals in Japan (Reports Cont'd Symbol WDC 451)

1. WCL 37391, 29 April 1946, established a monthly reporting procedure for stocks of gold and precious metals held by your command. Reports have been received through the month of April 1947.

2. It is requested that (a) the report be expanded to include a breakdown as to ownership, and to cover metals not in SCAP custody, in accordance with paragraph 3 below, and that (b) following the initial report on this basis, the reports be submitted quarterly, rather than monthly, as of 30 Sep, 31 Dec, 31 Mar and 30 Jun. If the 30 Jun report has been submitted, the first report using the breakdown below should be that for 31 Jul.

3. The following general breakdown is suggested. Within these categories, appropriate subsidiary breakdowns should be developed, with the objective of presenting significant classes of ownership.

A. Metals in SCAP custody

1. Known Japanese-owned

a. Government monetary stocks.

Includes items comprising government monetary stocks held by Japanese governmental fiscal agencies at time taken into SCAP custody.

b. Other stocks

Includes, but not necessarily limited to, government non-monetary stocks, holdings of banks, corporations and partnerships, and individual private holdings.

My dear Colonel Chard:

Reference is made to your letter of July 9, 1947, transmitting
for this Department's comments, a draft of a proposed letter to
SCAP on the subject of the report on precious metals in Japan.

In view of the consideration now being given to the desirability
of utilizing stocks of precious metals in Japan to help finance
Japanese foreign trade, it is suggested that in addition to the
information requested in the draft letter referred to above, SCAP
be requested to report the following information under paragraph 3 B
of the proposed letter, headed "Metals not in SCAP Custody":

1. Monetary metals; classified by:
a. Coins; indicating:
(1) Principal metallic content;
(2) Country of issue;
(3) Weight;
(4) Face value;
(5) Dollar value;
(6) Approximate time of issue (if available).
b. Other; indicating:
(1) Metal;
(2) Weight;
(3) Dollar value.

Colonel R. H. Chard,
Executive, Civil Affairs Division,
War Department, Pentagon,
Washington, D. C.
