June 19, 1946.

Ambassador of the Japanese Government to the United States,
Washington, D. C.

Dear Sir:

On April 10th after hearing over the radio that the
Emperor of Japan was seeking a tudor for his son, I sent a
letter of application to the radio station addressed to Miss
Kate Smith who was the person I heard make the announcement.

To date I have not received any answer to my letter
and since I am very much interested in the situation I am
sending to you a duplicate of my original letter.

The regular school work at Friends Academy where
I have been a teacher is over for the year and at present I
am at Teachers College where I am taking summer work.

Will you be kind enough to notify me whether or not
this position of tudor has been filled.

Very sincerely yours,

Helen Iwine Kennedy
(Mrs. W.C.)

=== Page 3 === (confidence 0.99)
COPY

His Royal Highness, the Emperor of Japan,
Royal Palace,
Tokyo, Japan

Dear Sir:

I have just returned to New York after a short vacation in Florida and have learned that Miss Kate Smith announced several days ago over the radio that you are seeking a tudor for your son.

This news is of great interest to me and I wish to place my name as an applicant for the position. There is a double reason for my interest: first, that I have done much of this kind of teaching and second, the fact that I enjoy working with boys whom I believe I understand. I am a widow and the mother of four sons whose joys, interests and activities I have shared as they grew.

At this time I am free to undertake the work and the opportunity of individual teaching appeals to me.

I have been a very active person who has enjoyed outdoor sports, have excellent health, have traveled on my own continent extensively and thoroughly enjoyed new experiences.

My personal qualifications age, education and experience I am listing on a separate sheet. I sincerely hope they will meet the requirements for which you are seeking.

Miss Helen Voorhees of the Appointment Bureau of Mount Holyke has references from past positions as well as my present one and you may consult her concerning my work and abilities.

In case your representative should desire to interview me I shall be glad to meet him by appointment.

Very respectfully yours,

Helen Irvine Kennedy

Office Memorandum • UNITED STATES GOVERNMENT

TO: The Secretary
Through: S/S
FROM: FE - Mr. Butterworth

DATE: February 18, 1949

SUBJECT: Education of Japanese Crown Prince in the United States and England

Discussion

The accompanying personal letter (Tab A) to me from Mr. Sebald (Acting Political Adviser to General MacArthur) recounts a suggestion informally made by the Japanese Empress regarding the possibility of the 15 year-old Crown Prince receiving preparatory school education in the United States, followed by higher education in England. Mr. Sebald regards the suggestion as a good one, both in the interests of United States-Japanese relations and in the interests of weaning the young Prince away from the enervating atmosphere of a royal court in which feudal customs may gradually reappear. General MacArthur agrees but feels that the boy should first learn to speak English and that the aim should be a four-year standard course in the United States without a period at a foreign preparatory school. Mr. Sebald requests my frank views on the matter.

My views, set forth in the attached letter (Tab B) are that it would be unwise at this time to send the Prince to a U.S. boarding school but that, if it is the desire of the Imperial Family that the Prince obtain his university training in England, we should not oppose such a move but encourage it.

Recommendation

Your approval of my letter is recommended.

Approved _______________
Disapproved _______________

Attachments:
(A) Letter from Mr. Sebald to Mr. Butterworth
(B) Proposed reply.
