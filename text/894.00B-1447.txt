September 5, 1944

COMMUNISM IN JAPAN

Information has been received from a reliable and confidential source
that Shushumu Okano, the leader of the Communist Party in Japan, has recently
escaped through the Japanese lines and has reached the headquarters of the
Chinese 8th Route Army (Communist). Okano has outlined a program to be followed
by Japanese Communists which Communist circles described as an instructive
example of the manner in which Communists in the Fascist countries are applying
Lenin's slogan of turning Imperialist war into revolution.

Okano stated that the military defeat of Japan by the United Nations
is a short cut to victory for the Japanese people. "What we want now is not
the military victory of the Japanese Government, but its defeat, because the
so-called East Asia war is a war of the militarists and their associates and
not our war, and its failure will be the failure of the militarists and their
associates and not our failure. Their collapse will furnish a good opportunity
for us to establish a people's government and build a new Japan." He then calls
upon Communists in Japan to organize a mass movement by working within existing
patriotic organizations and by re-establishing, secretly at first, the old
trade union and other organizations which have been suppressed.

According to Okano's statement, a political underground movement is
already in existence in Japan. He admits it is still weak and small, but claims
it has won the sympathy of the workingman and the support of the people for its
program and policies, "as was openly acknowledged by high officials at the 1943
spring session of the Diet."

Okano issued a proclamation which contained the following summing-up
of the present situation in Japan. This is reproduced, but it should be borne
in mind that Okano's appreciation of the position may be largely the result of
wishful thinking, or may be governed by the requirements of Communist propaganda:

(1) The exhaustion of economic power has reached its climax;

(2) The people's living has been worsened to an intolerable degree;

(3) Internal conflicts within the ruling class may be seen from the
chaos in the provisional Assembly of June, 1943, and the disunity
within the Yokusan Sejikai. The Tojo cabinet is tottering;

(4) The spirit of the people has changed. Weariness, dissatisfaction,
pessimism, wavering and hatred of the war prevail. The Japanese
people are awakening to their deception by the militarists and
mass struggles are beginning to appear;

(5) The spirit of the Army is also low - suicides, desertion and
surrender to the Chinese are increasing;

(6) The struggle of the Japanese underground is increasing and its
influence among the people grows, as was noted even in the
speeches of government spokesmen in the Diet;

(7) Conditions in the occupied territories are deteriorating.

August 7, 1941

PERSONAL AND CONFIDENTIAL
BY SPECIAL MESSENGER

Honorable Adolf A. Berle, Jr.
Assistant Secretary of State
Department of State
Washington, D. C.

My dear Mr. Berle:

As of possible interest to you, information has been received from a reliable confidential source that damage to machinery, believed to have been caused by saboteurs, forced the closing of the Kanebo Spinning Mills, Osaka, Japan, for a week following June 10, 1941. Out of 2,000 employees many were arrested and the talk which circulated following the occurrence blamed Communists for the trouble.

Sincerely yours,

J. E. Hoover

PERSONAL AND CONFIDENTIAL
BY SPECIAL MESSENGER

Honorable Adolf A. Berle, Jr.
Assistant Secretary of State
Department of State
Washington, D.C.

Dear Mr. Berle:

Information has been received from a generally reliable confidential source that Russia possesses the best available inside information on Japan, which it has received through the Japanese Communist Party. It was stated that the Japanese Communist Party is an efficient underground organization which has capable representatives in all of the principal cities and towns of Japan, as well as in the armed forces of that nation.

Sincerely yours,

J. Edgar Hoover

Shanghai's 3406, August 31, 1940,
"Newspaper article - German 'Fifth Column' activities in Japan".

An article on August 30, 1940, in the Shanghai Evening Post and Mercury, an American-owned newspaper, asserts that the Japanese arrests of a number of British in Japan in July 1940 were a consequence of German initiative under the direction of the German Ambassador to Japan.
