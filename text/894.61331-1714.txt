To the:

American Consul General,
Alexandria, Egypt.

The Secretary of State transmits for the information of the Officer-in-Charge correspondence which Ambassador Paulay has submitted with the request that a reply be made. For the background of the Officer-in-Charge the following facts are stated:

The Department of State is endeavoring to ascertain from Japan whether there are stocks of tobacco available for export. If available, tobacco would be treated as a commercial export, since it does not fall within the group of items subject to reparations claim. Any possible shipments of tobacco or similar products from Japan would have to be handled by some official government agency duly accredited to General MacArthur's Headquarters, since it is not yet possible to permit private trading relations between Japanese and the nationals of any other country.

It is suggested that any Egyptian firm communicate its interest in the purchase of Japanese tobacco to the appropriate members of the British Mission accredited to General MacArthur in Tokyo.

Enclosures:

Correspondence from
Mr. Raphael S. Soussi,
Alexandria, Egypt, to
Ambassador Edwin W. Paulay.

Dear Sir,

Subject: JAPANESE &amp; COREAN LEAF TOBACCO For EXPORT

The vernacular papers have reported the following cable from TOKYO, dated 8th November 1945:

THE AMERICAN COMMISSION FOR REPARATIONS ARRIVED IN TOKYO ON 7th NOVEMBER. IT is constituted by 15 members, most of whom are experts in economical questions in the Far East, having already resided long ago in JAPAN. The head of this Commission is Mr. PAULEY, actually in the PHILIPPINES, who will arrive on 18th November at TOKYO. The Commission intends to study ON THE SPOT the capacities of JAPAN proper for payment of reparations, and will go Throughout the territories of JAPAN, COREA and MANCHENIA.

The object of my present letter is to bring to your notice that JAPANESE AND KOREAN LEAF TOBACCO have been shipped in large quantities to EGYPT since 1918 up to November 1941. Japanese and Korean Tobacco imported for the manufacture of cigarettes (low qualities) in EGYPT reached up to 4,000,000 lbs. yearly and the highest item, say about 33% of total imports of leaf tobacco into EGYPT was from Japan. It is therefore presumed that from 1941 up to 1945, stocks of Japanese and Korean tobacco leaf accumulated in Japan, probably in the KOREA-TOKYO area, under the Type-names of: MATSUKAWA, BITCHU, BINGO, DANEMA, SATSUMA, NAMBU, KOREAN YELLOW, KOREAN HATANO, KOREAN SUIFU and FORMOSAN TOBACCO. All these qualities besides local consumption are suitable ONLY FOR EXPORT TO EGYPT, a little for Germany. The prewar value of Tobacco Imports in EGYPT from JAPAN varied from ONE to TWO MILLION DOLLARS, but present estimated value may amount to TEN MILLION DOLLARS or more.

From this you will gather that there is a substantial amount which can be recovered as REPARATIONS, should it be decided by your Department to ship to EGYPT whatever SURPLUS JAPANESE, KOREAN &amp; FORMOSAN TOBACCO LEAF would be available for EXPORT, and as to payment of its countervalue this can be easily arranged by: 1) Letter of Credit in Sterlings, Egyptian or American USA Dollars in your Favour, or, 2°) By Advance payment to the AMERICAN EMBASSY in EGYPT of 50% of the value of tobacco available for export, in Dollars or Egyptian Currency, as it may suit your purpose.

GENERAL HEADQUARTERS
SUPREME COMMANDER FOR THE ALLIED POWERS

AG 439(30 Jan 48)FSS/PC
(SCAPIN 1854)

APO 500
30 January 1948

MEMORANDUM FOR: JAPANESE GOVERNMENT

THROUGH: Central Liaison Office, Tokyo

SUBJECT: Distribution of American Tobacco as Incentive Goods

1. The Japanese Government is authorized to:
a. Distribute cigarettes from American tobacco products in the custody of the Japanese Government as incentive goods to encourage coal production and the delivery of rice to the official collection system.
b. Offer 250,000 packages (2 oz.) of pipe tobacco as incentive goods to coal miners.
c. Utilize not to exceed 1,000 pounds of pipe tobacco and cigars to conduct operating tests as to the feasibility of utilizing these products in the manufacture of cigarettes for distribution as incentive goods.
d. Release all chewing tobacco for use as raw material in the manufacture of insecticides for farmers.

2. Each package of cigarettes and pipe tobacco will be clearly and distinctively marked to indicate that these products are authorized for legal release and to distinguish them from American tobacco used by Occupation Forces.

3. The Japanese Government will take all necessary action to assure that this tobacco and any products manufactured therefrom, are distributed only through official channels and do not appear in public, unauthorized sales.

4. Detailed distribution plans and prices to be charged for these commodities will be submitted to General Headquarters, Supreme Commander for the Allied Powers for approval prior to actual distribution.

FOR THE SUPREME COMMANDER:

R. M. LEVY,
Colonel, AGD,
Adjutant General.
