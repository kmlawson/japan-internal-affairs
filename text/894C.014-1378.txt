My dear Mr. Herrick:

Reference is made to a letter dated January 5, 1943, received from Mr. Paul M. Hancock, Media Analysis and Language Research Department of the Office of War Information, New York City, requesting a clarification of this Government's pre-war policy toward the Mounting Leased Territory. A copy of Mr. Hancock's letter is enclosed for your information.

Mr. Hancock's understanding of the history of the lease of the Territory, as set forth in the second paragraph of his letter, is substantially correct. With regard to the attitude of this Government toward the extension in 1915 of the lease, it is thought that reference to the following correspondence and declarations of this Government might be found useful in a study of this subject:

Note of March 13, 1915 from the Secretary of State to the Japanese Ambassador, pages 105-111, Foreign Relations of the United States, 1915.

Identical note from the Secretary of State to the Japanese and Chinese Governments, Nov 11, 1915, page 148, Foreign Relations of the United States, 1915.

Statement of the Secretary of State, on behalf of the American Government, at the Fifth Circuit District of the Conference on the Limitation of Separated Washington, November 15, 1921-February 9, 1922, pages 334-438, Report of Conference.

Witness:

Mr. John Howrich,

Telegrams from the Secretary of State to the American Charge in Japan, March 27, 1823, and to the American Minister in China, April 5, 1923, pages 225 and 830, respectively, Volume I, Foreign Relations of the United States, 1923.

In effect, this Government after 1915 continued, as did all other governments that had treaty relations with Japan and China, to deal with Japan as the administrative authority in the Leased Territory. In so doing, however, both before and after 1915, this Government at no time lost sight of the basic conditions of the lease as provided for in the Russo-Chinese Convention for the Lease of the Liaotung Peninsula (Kwantung Territory) of March 27, 1898, especially the provision in Article I that the lease in no way violated the sovereign rights of China in the Territory.

In the third paragraph of Mr. Maneack's letter it is stated: "Any consideration of the status of Kwantung would naturally be made prior to 1937 when the Leased Territory was formally incorporated into the State of Manchukuo". As is well known, this Government has not recognized the regime which styles itself "Manchukuo" or the validity of acts by that regime. Moreover, this Department has no information that would indicate that the Kwantung Leased Territory has been incorporated into "Manchukuo". In 1937 the office of the Governor of the Kwantung Leased Territory was abolished and the Government of the Kwantung Leased Territory was reduced to a Bureau in the office of the Japanese Embassy at Hsinking. This change in the administrative structure of the Leased Territory, which was an internal measure of the Japanese Government, in no way modified Japanese jurisdiction over the Territory or with "Manchukuo" any authority within the Territory. On March 1, 1937 Japanese administrative rights were also taken in the same manner as the South Manchukuo Railway Corp. that day the right of way of that railway and power in the city of South Manchukuo to head the Leased Territory served by that railway, were transferred to "Manchukuo" in a "treaty" signed between Japan and "Manchukuo" on November 5, 1937.

Sincerely yours,

R. Robert E. Hall
Assistant Secretary

UNITED STATES OF AMERICA
OFFICE OF WAR INFORMATION
224 WEST 57TH STREET
NEW YORK CITY

January 5, 1942.

State Department
Division of Current Information
Washington, D.C.

Dear Sirs:

We would very much appreciate a clarification
of the Government's pre-war policy toward the Kwangtung
Leased Territory.

According to our understanding, the lease of
this area was transferred to Japan by Russia in compli-
ance with the Treaty of Portsmouth. Following the sub-
mission of the Twenty-One Demands on China and by the
treaties of 1915, the term of the lease was extended
from twenty-five to ninety-nine years.

Has the extension of this lease ever been re-
cognized by the United States Government? If not, does
the United States consider this territory as part of the
former province of Liaoning? Any consideration of the
status of Kwantung would naturally be made prior to 1937
when the Leased Territory was formally incorporated into
the State of Manchukuo.

Your enlightenment on this matter will indeed
be greatly appreciated.

Very sincerely yours,

Paul M. Hancock
Media Analysis and Language
Research Department
