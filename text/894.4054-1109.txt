Tokyo, Japan, February 13, 1937.

CASE OF WALTER SCHATZMAYR.

THE HONORABLE
THE SECRETARY OF STATE,
WASHINGTON.

SIR:

I have the honor to refer to the Department's memorandum instruction of December 4, 1936 (File No. 130 - Schatzmayr, Walter), requesting certain information in the case of one Walter Schatzmayr.

The information desired, as set forth in the enclosed letter of November 25, 1936 from the Department of Labor, is whether the authorities of the Japanese Government consider Walter Schatzmayr and a Japanese woman, Hisa Kamei, as legally married to one another, and whether the Italian Embassy at Tokyo regards these individuals as legally married, in view of the fact that Walter Schatzmayr's two daughters were included in his Italian passport.

In respect to the first question, on the basis of the statements contained in the Department of Labor's letter it must be said that there is no evidence to the effect that Walter Schatzmayr and Hisa Kamei have been married under Japanese law. Under Articles 775 and 776 of the Civil Code of Japan, legal validity of marriage is established upon notification thereof being

=== Page 7 === (confidence 0.99)
-2-

being made to and accepted by the Registrar (Mayor, head man, or other proper official) of the place where the husband is permanently registered or registered as a temporary sojourner. In effect, official notification is made, the notification is examined, and if not incompatible with various legal restrictions, is accepted, whereupon an entry is made in the official records and a receipt given the interested parties. The foregoing is the only procedure outlined and constitutes legal marriage insofar as Japanese subjects are concerned. In the absence of evidence to the effect that Walter Schatzmayr and Hisa Kamei have fulfilled the foregoing conditions, the conclusion is that they have not been married under Japanese law.

In this regard it would seem that Walter Schatzmayr has submitted no evidence to the Japanese authorities to establish a legal marital relationship with Hisa Kamei, particularly in view of the fact that the births of the two daughters were registered with the name of the father not given. Further, as no evidence is shown to the effect that Walter Schatzmayr at any subsequent time made an official acknowledgment of paternity as prescribed in Article 829 of the Civil Code, it would appear that he has no legal relationship to the two daughters or they to him under Japanese law.

It may be observed that it is possible for aliens in Japan to be married in accordance with the civil law of the husband's home country before a representative of his government and the marriage may be valid according

=== Page 8 === (confidence 0.99)
-3-

cording to Japanese law. As set forth in a legal commentary:

"There is no express provision to the latter effect, but this is what is implied in Article 777. As a matter of practice, it is customary to satisfy the requirements of all laws concerned."

It is understood, however, that the foregoing process is without any legal force in Japan when one of the parties is a Japanese subject: A Japanese subject marrying an alien in Japan must be married under the regular legal procedure, as previously outlined, regardless of the conjectural rights of two aliens to be married before a representative of an outside government. Walter Schatzmayr and Hisa Kamei, the latter being a Japanese subject, would have to be married in Japan under the prescribed procedure stipulated in the Civil Code for their union to be valid in the eyes of Japanese law.

The foregoing statements have been made after consulting English translations of and commentaries on the Civil Code of Japan and in the light of interviews with the Chief Registrar of the Kojimachi Ward Office. The reference books in question are "The Civil Code of Japan", translated by W. J. Sebald, LL.B., and "The Principles and Practice of the Civil Code of Japan", by J. A. DeBecker, LL.B., D.C.L. The Kojimachi Ward Office, situated in the center of Tokyo, has had considerable experience in dealing with the marriages of aliens resident in Japan; it handles the great majority of marriages in Tokyo involving Europeans and Americans.

Translation of a letter from the Italian Embassy, Tokyo, dated January 31, 1937.

No. 63

Tokyo, January 31, 1937.

Dear Mr. Crocker:

By your letter of the 14th of January addressed to my colleague Barbaccio you requested information regarding the marital status of one Walter Schatzmayr, the legal relationship and nationality of the two daughters born of his union with the Japanese subject, Hisa Kamei. Unfortunately, the archives of our Embassy were destroyed in the earthquake of 1923; I am therefore unable to give you a reply to the questions set forth above.

I can, if you so desire, request information through the municipal authorities in the town in Italy in which Schatzmayr was born.

The fact that the names of the two daughters were included in his passport is not sufficient in itself, to consider the relation of Schatzmayr with Hisa Kamei as constituting a legal union.

Very sincerely yours,

(signed) Scammacca.

My dear Mr. Schneider:

Receipt is acknowledged of your letter of July 10, 1939 stating that you desire to obtain, on behalf of your client, Marguerita Dunn, a properly exemplified copy of a divorce decree which was obtained by Harold S. Dunn in the No. 1 Civil Department of The Tokyo District Court, Tokyo, Japan.

On the assumption that your client is an American citizen, it is suggested that you send directly to the American Consul General at Tokyo, Japan, full information as to the character of the document desired, the name of the Court granting the divorce decree and the date thereof. Upon receiving such data, the Consul General will endeavor to obtain the document.

It is quite likely that the Japanese authorities may make a charge for searching the records and certifying the document.

Mr. Samuel R. Schneider,
Counselor at Law,
400 Madison Avenue,
New York, New York.
