Mr. McCain called upon Mr. Hamilton after a previous conversation with the Secretary concerning the matter under reference. Mr. McCain stated that the Aetna Insurance Company had been in negotiation with Johnson and Higgins of 63 Wall Street, New York, for the purchase of the common stock of the Standard Insurance Company of New York and the Standard Surety and Casualty Company of New York. The common stock of the two Standard companies, which are New York corporations, is owned by the Tokyo Marine and Fire Insurance Company, Ltd. Johnson and Higgins are apparently the insurance brokers or financial agents for these Japanese companies.

Mr. McCain stated that the purchase price of the common stock of the Standard companies would amount to between $7,000,000

$7,000,000 and $7,500,000. The Standard companies, he added, were in good condition and the Aetna would be able to make a very good deal at the proposed price. The favorable price is apparently due in part to fears that Japanese assets in the United States might be frozen shortly. The negotiations for the deal, Mr. McCain stated, were well advanced, the plan being to have a meeting of the board of directors on Monday or Tuesday for the ratification of the transaction and its announcement to the public. Payment would probably be made by Thursday.

Mr. McCain said that he had not been instructed to ask for this Department's approval of the transaction but that if the Aetna Company knew that the Department would object it would not go through with the transaction. He stated emphatically that Aetna was not committed to the transaction but that it would very much like to carry it through.

Mr. Hamilton said that the Department generally viewed with disfavor transactions which resulted in the acquisition of additional liquid dollar funds by Japan and that this was particularly the case in view of the Japanese policies which prevented the liquidation of American interests in Japan and the withdrawal of the proceeds. Mr. Hamilton stated that he would like to consider the matter further with other officers of the Department before Mr. McCain, as had

had been previously arranged, telephoned the Secretary at 12:30 for such comment as the Department might wish to make on the proposed transaction.

Mr. Hamilton and Mr. Luthringer subsequently discussed this matter with the Secretary and with Mr. Acheson. In the course of the discussion it was brought out that according to the Treasury Department as of May 7 Japanese deposits in banks in the United States amounted to a little over $90,000,000. Furthermore, there did not appear to be any immediate prospect for the freezing of Japanese funds. The Secretary stated that he had already told Mr. McCain that the Department did not view with favor transactions which released additional liquid dollar assets to Japan. The Secretary said that when Mr. McCain telephoned him later in the morning he would inform Mr. McCain that had the Aetna Insurance Company approached the Department about the matter several weeks ago it would have felt in a position to make more definite comment on the proposed transaction but that in view of the advanced stage of negotiations and of other considerations at this time the Department did not feel that it wished to make specific comment on the transaction.

Johnson &amp; Higgins, as General Managers, have filed notice of the discontinuance, on September 30, 1940, of the writing of fire insurance business by the United States Branches of the above-named companies, and of the reinsurance of the existing business of such companies in the Standard Insurance Company of New York, the American affiliate.

The Tokio will continue to transact the business of marine insurance under the Management of Appleton &amp; Cox, Inc. The Meiji has transacted only a fire business.

The financial condition of the Fire Branch of the Tokio, as of June 30, 1940, in condensed form, was as follows:

The Managers have also requested release of the non-trusteed
assets, or free funds, approximating $500,000.

No action has been taken, as yet, on the application for re-
lease of funds, nor will action be taken until contracts of
reinsurance have been submitted in acceptable form and approved,
as provided by statute. It is the intention of the Management
to secure the release of as much funds in the Fire Branch of
the Tokio and from the Meiji as possible for remittance to
the Home Office of the Tokio. In the absence of definite
figures as of September 30th, the date of reinsurance, it is
difficult to estimate just how much of the trusteed funds,
after payment to the Standard for such reinsurance, will be
released to the Managers. Needless to say, provision will be
made for the retention by the United States Trustee of
sufficient funds to provide for contingencies. The statutory
deposits will not be released.

Considerable speculation exists in insurance circles as to
the future of the American affiliates, the Standard Insurance
Company and the Standard Surety &amp; Casualty Company. Officials
of these companies profess to be ignorant of any plans. It
is our intention to discuss this feature with officials of
Johnson &amp; Higgins.
