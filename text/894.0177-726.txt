Considerable anxiety is being felt among Government employees over the Government's determination to reduce official salaries. In October, 1929, the Hamaguchi cabinet announced its intention to reduce salaries of Government employees including the military, but due to the storm of opposition that arose on all sides the matter was dropped, the Government barely escaping defeat due to loss of face. In the present instance, better management and the more determined attitude of the Wakatsuki cabinet enable it to weather the storm and announce that the reductions would actually go into effect on June first. The most serious opposition came from the Judicial officials who took the lead in opposing the previous attempt at reduction, and the railway officials, many of whom prepared to submit their resignations should the Government insist on the reduction of their salaries. The outline of the preparatory committee's plan for salary reduction is as follows:

1 - The Premier's salary to be reduced from Yen 12,000 to Yen 9,600 (20% reduction).

2 - The Ministerial salary to be reduced from the present Yen 8,000 to Yen 7,000 (12.5% reduction).

3 - All other officials of "shinnin" rank and all "chokunin" officials to suffer a cut of from 10.4 to 12 per cent.

4 - All "sonin" officials, except those drawing a monthly salary of less than Yen 100 to suffer a cut of from 5.8 to 11 per cent.

5 - "Hannin" officials drawing a monthly salary of over Yen 100 to suffer a cut, as a matter of principle. The present Yen 100 salary to be reduced to Yen 95 (5% reduction).

6 - A monthly salary of Yen 115 to be reduced to Yen 108; Yen 135 to Yen 126; Yen 160 to Yen 147; and Yen 200 to Yen 180.

7 - A Yen 98 or Yen 99 salary to be reduced to Yen 95.

8 - Assistant clerks, forestry rangers, assistant police sergeants, senior wardens, sergeants-at-arms, to be excluded from the application of the plan.

9 - The reduction of pay for primary school teachers and local public officials who do not fall under the purview of the Salaries Law to be considered later.

10 - Judicial officials and railway and other operatives to suffer a cut according to the standards already mentioned.

On May 27th Imperial Sanction was given the Government's program of salary reduction which therefore became assured of enforcement. The salary reduction will be enforced in substantially the same form as the original draft, an outline of which is given above, although certain changes became necessary as a result of the strong opposition aroused. Basically the plan reduces from 3 to 20 per cent all Government salaries over Yen 100 per month. But since a 3 per cent reduction on a Yen 100 salary would make it less than the salary of those receiving from Yen 95 to Yen 99, these latter salaries will be reduced to Yen 97. It was also decided to delay action on the salaries of court judges until the Diet has approved the action.

The rumor that the enforcement of the salary reduction would lead to strikes and other disturbances was unfounded as the opposition quickly subsided after the issuance of the Imperial Edict approving the reduction. It is reported that railway workers have agreed to slow up their work to make up for their loss in salary.
