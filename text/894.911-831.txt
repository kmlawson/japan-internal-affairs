Jiji-Shimpo

04.911-JIJI-SHIMPO/1

Mr. Williamson, of the Associated Press, tells me that the King of England and various other Chiefs of State are sending messages to the Jiji-Shimpo congratulating them on the completion of fifty years and that the President is willing to send a message if the State Department will prepare it. The Associated Press feels that the absence of Mr. Hoover's name will be very noticeable.

I am enclosing a suggestion for a message. I want, however, to point out that the President has, so far as I know, never done this to foreign newspapers and that, if he sends a message to the Jiji-Shimpo, it will inevitably create a precedent so that it may be very difficult for him in the future to avoid sending messages to papers of less importance. I sent forward to you a few days ago a report from the Military Attache at Tokyo in regard to the proposed amalgamation of the two principal Japanese news services.

There is attached a short but informative despatch from the Embassy on the same subject. It is to be noted that the proposed amalgamation has not as yet been consummated.
