February 6th, 1931.

The Honorable Henry L. Simpson,
Secretary of State,
Washington, D. C.

Dear Sir:

It is with a great deal of pleasure that I am preparing to announce within the next fortnight that I have become the American publisher for Mr. Yusuke Tsurumi, member of the Japanese Parliament, and the outstanding success among the Japanese writers.

I am announcing at the same time, my plans for publishing in the summer and in English translation, Mr. Tsurumi's novel, THE NIGHT, which, in the last year and a half has sold more than 200,000 copies in Japan.

I believe this is a significant undertaking. It is the first time that a modern Japanese creative writer has been translated and it will be the first opportunity of the English speaking public to read an example of the modern Japanese literature.

I have felt very keenly for several years the desirability of bringing about a project of this kind, because I feel that an understanding of Japanese character as portrayed for his own people by a Japanese writer will do much to bring an understanding of them to the American public. The publication of THE NIGHT is of unquestioned international literary significance, and as I have indicated, I believe it has even wider aspects.

I hope you may find it within your policy to make some comment on the announcement if it meets with your approval. I would appreciate the opportunity to include some expression from you in the announcement which will go to the newspapers and to the literary critics.

Very respectfully,

/s/ Hae H. Henkle
Hae H. Henkle

From "Reading the Almanac"
THE RETAIL BOOKSELLER for February 1st, 1931.
Published by The Baker &amp; Taylor Company.

"Meredith Reed is not so well known as yet but will become a family name to booksellers unless we are much mistaken. THE GLORY TRAIL is a novel of sentiment, perhaps nearest to the stories of Gene Stratton Porter in kind. It concerns a woman, a blind boy, and a broken doctor, of the struggle of the latter two against Fate and their own weakness. The book shows how the woman, by her own courage and faith, gives the other two strength with which to face life. It is not a story for your hard-boiled cynics, obviously, but then no one can complain of the market for such books as this.

"Meredith Reed, we believe, may appeal to the huge market of Temple Bailey, and if so --- well, we needn't say what that means."

(Now also, read the comment on the GLORY TRAIL in the American News Book Bulletin for February).

Sir:

The Department has received your letter of February the sixth requesting comment on the announcement of the publication of the forthcoming novel entitled "The Mother" by Mr. Yusuke Tsurumi.

In reply, you are informed that it is contrary to the practice of the Secretary of State to make any comments for the public on novels about to be published.

Very truly yours,

For the Secretary of State:

Richard Souther
Assistant Chief
Division of International
