Subject: Foreign Liaison Agencies and Activities.
Japanese Activities.

Under date of March 9, 1936, a reliable informant advises concerning Japanese espionage activities at the port of Buenaventura, Colombia, on the occasion of the courtesy visit of the USS "Gannet", USS Teal" and thirty planes, at that port, as follows:

"We have had no prominent Japanese at Buenaventura since the arrival of the immigrants with the exception of three visitors the other day when certain units of the American fleet were in port.

The scheduled arrival of the USS GANNET, the USS TEAL and about thirty planes of Patrol Squadrons VP-2F, VP-3F and VP-5F of the Fleet Base force on February 15-16 was published in the local press some time in advance. Three Japanese arrived on the night train February fourteenth and remained in Buenaventura the entire time of the visits of these squadrons. Although they took their meals at the Hotel Estacion there were no accommodations left at any of the local hotels, pensions or lodging houses where they would be required to register and therefore their names could not be learned.

One of the visitors, however, was stated to be Senor Yuzo Takeshima at present the representative of the Kaigai Kogyo Kaisa of Tokio and head of the new Colonia Agricola Japanese in the Cauca Valley and former representative of the Japanese Bureau of Emigration in Brazil. One of the others was reported to have been frequently seen on the streets of Bogota but the third was unknown.

The Japanese left by train for Cali on the morning of the seventeenth without having called on any of the business element or otherwise indicated the reason of their visit to Buenaventura. Some of the Colombian officials were of the opinion that the Japanese appeared to be much interested in the local sentiment and the general reactions of the inhabitants to the reception of the American naval units.

Source: As stated;

E. W. Campanole,
Colonel, Infantry,
Military Attache.

COLUMBIA
PROPOSAL FOR PURCHASES OF COLUMBIAN OIL BY JAPAN.

Some six months or more ago, Mr. Katsube, Secretary of the Japanese Legation in Bogota, requested the Tropical Oil Company to furnish information as to the specifications of the Colombian oils. As the specifications are determined by the Colombian Government, Mr. Katsube was advised to request the information direct. It is not known why Mr. Katsube requested this information and it is only related here to establish the time at which it was first reported that a representative of the Japanese Government expressed an interest in Colombian oil.

Within the past two weeks a Mr. Suganami called at the office of the Bogota Manager of the Tropical Oil Co., and the Indian National Corporation. Mr. Suganami is understood to be a Japanese Chamber of Commerce representative and possibly has some connection with the Japanese Government, although it has been impossible to substantiate this belief. On arrival at the aforementioned office, Mr. Suganami hastily scribbled a note to the Manager stating that he had just received an important cablegram from Japan and requested an audience. Mr. Suganami was received and he stated that a certain Japanese company desired to buy large quantities of oil from the Tropical Oil Company, payable in pesos in Colombia. In reply to a direct question, Mr. Suganami stated that it was a new company that desired to make the purchases and not the Mitsui. The Manager then informed Mr. Suganami that the Tropical Oil Company contracted the sale of their oil far ahead and advised that Japanese Agents should contact the head office of the company in Toronto, Canada.

The cablegram was not produced and my informant states that he doubts there was one. Informant further states that the present China-Japan situation was not mentioned and he is inclined to believe that the proposed oil purchase has a more direct bearing on the creation of exchange with which Japanese products might be purchased under the existing Colombia-Japan compensation arrangement. This arrangement has greatly reduced Japanese imports into Colombia during the past year and may have the bearing suggested. However, the undersigned considers it particularly noteworthy that an effort is being made to improve the exchange just at the time Japan may be in serious need of oil.

Although no definite statement has been heard, I am under the impression that the Tropical Oil Co., subsidiary through two other companies to the Standard Oil of New Jersey, does not care to sell oil to Japanese interests. However, being a commercial organization, sales might be arranged if the price were sufficiently attractive.

It is also reliably reported that Japanese representatives have had recent conferences with high government officials regarding the possibility of purchase of Colombian royalty crude. As to what progress has been made, the undersigned is not reliably informed. Examination, however, of the contracts between the Tropical Oil Co., the Indian National Corporation and the Colombian Government, show there are many practical considerations which would make it difficult for the government to dispose of her royalty crude to Japan were she so minded.

The practical considerations are briefly as follows: Royalty crude is payable in oil or money value at the point of production, which is El Centro. The Tropical Oil Company has always purchased the royalty oil from Colombia, but it is understood that payment in actual oil can legally be required at any time. Should payment be made in actual oil, it must be transported immediately as the

AMERICAN CONSULATE
Buenaventura, Colombia,
June 14, 1938.

CONFIDENTIAL

My dear Mr. Greene:

I have the honor to refer the Legation to my previous correspondence concerning the activities of the four Japanese engineers in this district.

The scene of the activities of the group sent to this district has been changed. Last December these engineers were sent to this district by the powerful Mitsui Company, which is controlled and directed by the present Minister of Finance and Governor of the Bank of Japan, Seihin Ikeda, to investigate the possibilities of developing any oil deposits in the Departments of Nariño and Santander, and platinum mines in the Choco district. They were out on the San Juan River the better part of two months making surveys, taking pictures, exploring the different lands and flats running along the wide and spacious river. However, outside of the maps and other information gathered, there has been no information available to determine just exactly what results were accomplished of the survey projected. These four engineers - Hiro Shisichi, Hirojo Takahashi, Alejandro Tujun and Voiechi Yotaha - have now transferred their field of activities to the Department of Nariño, after leisurely abiding their time at Cali since last January.

One of the group, it is reported, has recently been suffering considerably as a consequence of a social contagious disease probably contracted either while out on the San Juan River or in one of the periodic visits made to Cali. This last week they have been here in Buenaventura waiting for the plane going south.

Last Sunday, June 12, three of the engineers boarded the Peceta plane to Munaco. It has been reported that they contemplate scouting the placer country of the Mira, Telembi, and Patia Rivers to determine the possibilities of finding gold.
