My dear Mr. Berle:

Information was obtained from a confidential source to the effect that one John Joseph Callaghan and an individual by the name of Castanelli, first name unknown, have entered into a brokerage business in San Francisco, California, and are reported as making arrangements with one Tibbitts, of the Mitsubishi Company, for the purchase of ten purse seiners at the cost of approximately $40,000 to $50,000 each.

It is reported that the seiners average approximately 100 tons and allegedly have a top speed of 11 to 12 knots, and an average cruising speed of 8 knots. Among the vessels mentioned as possibilities in the transaction are the "Vivian A.", formerly the "J. A. Martinolich"; the oil screw "Oregon"; and the "Olympic II", formerly the "Star of France." It is believed these latter two boats are intended for reduction plants for the purse seiners.

The above information is being brought to your attention, inasmuch as it is confidentially reported there is a possibility that the Mitsubishi Company may be acting on behalf of the Japanese Government. In the event this Bureau receives any additional information concerning this matter, same will be furnished you immediately.

The above data are being transmitted also to the Secretary of the Treasury and to the Offices of Naval and Military Intelligence.

Sincerely yours,

JAPANESE BUYERS ACQUIRE A FORMER AMERICAN STEAMSHIP AND CHILEAN COPPER

On September 12th there arrived at the port of Valparaiso the steamship ATLANTICO under Panamanian register. The vessel had previously called at Buenos Aires with assorted cargo and had there changed part of the crew which is now chiefly composed of Argentinian citizens of Spanish and Italian origin. The vessel is leaving Valparaiso today for Antofagasta, there is load 7500 tons of electrolytic copper, and thereafter to proceed via San Pedro, California, to Japan where the ship and its cargo will be turned over to Japanese buyers.

The ATLANTICO, formerly belonging to the Pacific-Atlantic Steamship Company of Portland, Oregon, under the name of SAN BERNARDINO, is an oil-burning ship of 3555 net tons built at Seattle, Washington, in 1920. As the ATLANTICO the ship belongs to the Cía. de Navegación "La Interoceánica" of Panama. The change of name and flag took place on April 2, 1940.

Within the past few years, Japan has been buying from Chile a considerable tonnage of copper ores but purchases of electrolytic or standard copper have not exceeded 1600 tons in any of the last three years.

***

SOURCES OF INFORMATION: Captain Galvagno of the ATLANTICO.

My dear Mr. Long:

The receipt is acknowledged of your letter of October 19, 1940 (your file RA 825.6352/134), requesting any information which the Commission can furnish covering the change in registry of the Panamaian vessel ATLANTICO. It is believed that the vessel referred to is the ex-American SS. SAN BERNARDINO, formerly owned by the Pacific Atlantic Steamship Company of Portland, Oregon, for which the Commission, on March 26, 1940, granted the approval required by Section 9 of the Shipping Act 1910, as amended, of its sale to Compania de navegacion "La Interoceanica" S.A., Panama City, Panama, with transfer to Panamaian registry and flag. Our records indicate that the said sale and transfer were effected on April 3, 1940, at Portland, Oregon, while the vessel was at Philadelphia, Pa.

At the time the application for the sale and transfer was submitted, the Commission was informed that the vendee corporation was incorporated in Panama on February 17, 1940 and that the following were the Directors:

Mr. Alberto A. Bodero, an Argentine citizen
Prince Boni Boncompagni Ludovisi
Count Loris niccie
Mr. Rafael Creamuno

The Commission was also informed that the stockholders of the corporation included all of the Directors and Mr. Fritz Mandl, and that none of them was a citizen of the United States. Mr. A.A. Dodero, who was President of the corporation, advised the Commission that the ship was being acquired for the purpose of trading between South America and the United Kingdom and France and stated that there was no American interests, financial or otherwise, in his corporation. For your confidential information, the sales price of the vessel was $425,000 and, in view of the high cost of the vessel to the vendee, Mr. Dodero advised that the transfer of the vessel to the
