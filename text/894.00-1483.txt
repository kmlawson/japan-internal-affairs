Dear Mr. Hackett:

I have received your letter of December 13, 1944
to which was attached some printed matter that you
had received from the Japan Tourist Bureau. The
interest and generosity which prompted you to make
these papers available to the Department are greatly
appreciated. However, the Department finds it already
has access to this material.

I am returning these papers, since you asked
to have them back in the event the Department could
not make use of them.

Sincerely yours,

Erle R. Dickover

Division of Japanese Affairs

In reply refer to

My dear Joe:

Mr. Grew has asked me to answer your thoughtful letter of December 18, 1944.

It is gratifying to know that there are boys like you who are eager to learn about the Far East. I hope your interest in that part of the world will continue and that you will also encourage your friends to study about the Orient. In the future, America will need young men who through their own initiative and perseverance have acquired a sound knowledge of China, Japan and other areas of the Far East.

In answer to your inquiry with regard to a chronological history of Japan, you may care to look over the following volumes which may be obtained from the publishers: Sansom, Japan; A Short Cultural History, Appleton-Century Co., New York, New York; Gowen, Outline History of Japan, Appleton-Century Co., New York, New York; and Mourse, Kodo, War of the Emperor, Bobbs-Merrill Co., Indianapolis, Indiana.

The following books were published abroad and are out of print, but you may be able to secure them in one of the larger libraries: Brinkley, A History of the Japanese People from the Earliest Times to the End of the Keiji Era; Murdoch, A History of Japan; and Clements, A Short History of Japan.

Although I have suggested that you might look over the above mentioned volumes, you will understand, of course, that the Department of State can assume no responsibility for the accuracy of statements contained therein.

Mr. Grew appreciated receiving such a thoughtful and serious letter from so young a man.

Sincerely yours,

Erle R. Dickover
Chief
Division of Japanese Affairs

Mr. Joe Rice,
Mongolia, Arkansas.
