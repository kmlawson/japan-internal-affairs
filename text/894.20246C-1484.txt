THE HONORABLE

SIR:

I have the honor to report that the Consulate receives regularly from the Ceylon Information Officer, Mr. H. C. COCKS, a strictly confidential bulletin concerning enemy news broadcasts in English throughout this area. This bulletin, marked "Not for Reproduction", monitors the Japanese stations at Tokyo, Rangoon, Saigon, Singapore, Hong Kong, and a few smaller stations. It also covers Berlin broadcasts.

Japanese propaganda directed to Ceylon is generally transmitted only by Radio Singapore and a smaller station, presumably located in Malaya or Burma, identifying itself as "Free Lanka" ("Lanka" is an old name for Ceylon). The general tone and content of these broadcasts differ scarcely at all from Japanese propaganda, already well known to the Department, directed towards India and other dependent peoples in this area. As there are only some 12,000 receiving sets in Ceylon, of which about 9,600 are capable of receiving short wave, it is not believed that this propaganda is effective in reaching the mass of the population.

Nevertheless, the Japanese appear to be following political events in Ceylon with some care. On July 22 the Singapore radio referred briefly to the anti-British portions of a speech made on July 20, 1944, by the Leader of Ceylon State Council, D. S. Sonanayake. CONFIDENTIAL

REFERENCE TO CEYLON

RADIO SINGAPORE 2200 hrs 31 m:

TALK BY A. JALALUPEEN: The political storm that is now breaking over Ceylon is going to shatter the British administration to smithereens.

According to the Secy of State's final decision the British Govt. is not prepared to take any decisions on constitutional matters without the full knowledge and opinions of the various interests concerned. Also the British Govt. is not prepared to consider the Ministers' proposals as reflecting the unanimous view points of all the parties and interests concerned.

In the circumstances it is not surprising that the Ceylon Ministers have boldly withdrawn the proposals. That is exactly what any self-respecting body of Ministers would do. I congratulate the Ceylon Ministers on their courageous action, but at the same time I would earnestly request them not to forget the lessons that they have now learnt at such great cost to their own prestige and self-respect. Today they have been disillusioned. They imagined that they could get their freedom and independence as a gift from the British tyrants and they prepared a scheme, hoping that the Br. Govt, would immediately put their seal of approval to it. They have now found what hideous monsters and hypocrites the British are. The British have appeared in their true colours. They have openly discredited their pretence of sincerity and sympathy. The Br. are still holding fast to the "Divide and Rule" policy.

By publicly withdrawing the scheme the Ceylon Ministers have done a national service. They will no longer cooperate with the British or with their treacherous commission. It will be boycotted even as the notorious Simon commission was boycotted in India. Ceylon will no longer look to the British for freedom. They will themselves shape their destiny. They will no longer beg for British independence. They will fight for it and achieve it, fight for it in the same way as the Indians are now doing.

As Mr. D. S. Semanayake, Leader of the State Council, stated in the State Council the other day Ceylon too will march with the rest of Asia. (Quotes in full D. S. Semanayake's recent speech - "One would have thought that after Burma, Malaya ...").

Yes, Ceylon too will march with the rest of Asia. Ceylon is already on the march. The people of Ceylon are already up against the British. India and Ceylon are determined

SUBJECT: Utilization of Daily Mail Article as Enemy Propaganda
For Distribution

THE HONORABLE
THE SECRETARY OF STATE,
WASHINGTON.

In continuation of this Consulate's despatch no. 605 dated September 22, 1944, on the subject of "More Rumor Mongering by Reuter's", I have the honor to report that a confidential summary of enemy broadcasting, which is furnished daily to this Consulate, states that the Japanese radio has already made use of the Daily Mail article on the alleged commercial exploitation of Paris by American business men for propaganda purposes. A summary of the intercept states that the Japanese Broadcasting Station at Singapore announced in its broadcast at 9 p.m. on September 22 that the story published by the Daily Mail indicates "that the U. S. has shown the cloven foot". The announcer stated that the United States was trying to exploit not a backward country but a modern State by advertising that she has entered France to liberate her -- "what freedom can India expect from such greedy imperialists?"

Quite apart from the ethics of Reuter's News Agency in disseminating abroad a derogatory article from the Daily Mirror, it is now apparent that the Daily Mirror has directly, though doubtless inadvertently, played into the hands of the enemy by publishing such a story.

Respectfully yours,
Robert L. Buell
American Consul

SIR:

With reference to this Consulate's despatch no. 576, of September 6, 1944, I have the honor to enclose portions of recent monitoring bulletins of the Ceylon Department of Information which contained Japanese propaganda broadcasts meant for Ceylon.

It appears from these excerpts that the Japanese are making every effort to contrast their own "grant of Freedom" to Burma and the Netherlands East Indies with the apparent reluctance of the British to proceed speedily with measures granting more self-government to Ceylon. By reiterated references in these broadcasts to "Anglo-American" policies, the Japanese intend to create the impression that the United States Government shares responsibility with the British for the status of the subject peoples in India and Ceylon.

As stated in the previous despatch, it is not believed that these programs reach any appreciable number of listeners in Ceylon, but that they do indicate the type of Japanese propaganda directed towards dependent peoples in the Asiatic theatre of war.

JAPANESE BROADCASTS REFERRING TO CEYLON
FROM SEPTEMBER 3-22.

1. FREE INDIA -- SAIGON: "Ceylon cannot look indifferently to India's struggle for freedom. Lately, political crises have occurred in Ceylon. News received from there indicates that the proposals which the Ministers of the Ceylon Board sent to the Secretary of State for the Colonies, Oliver Stanley, have been rejected. The British have sent a Committee to Ceylon to tide over the political troubles there; but in protest, the Ministers of the Ceylon Board have withdrawn their proposals. Commenting on the situation in Ceylon the London 'Times' writes that the Ministers of the Ceylon Board committed a blunder in withdrawing their proposals. The people of Ceylon are eager to free themselves and the Royal Commission is trying to block all the efforts of the Sinhalese at emancipating their country from British rule.

"The Sinhalese Ministers now perfectly understand the sinister British designs and they have ceased cooperating with the British Government." - September 3.

2. RADIO SINGAPORE: "State Council Conference: There is further news to-day that the people of Ceylon are preparing to present a united front against the British in order to wrest their freedom. A Reuter despatch from Colombo says that the members of the State Council are holding a conference to-day with a view to presenting a united front against the British on the subject of the reforms.

"Their object is the organization of a country-wide boycott campaign of the British Commission which the Secretary of State is planning to send out to the Island." - September 7, 21.00 Hours 31 M.

3. FREE LANKA RADIO: "HELP JAPAN! The Br. have increased the plunder of India in the matter of foodstuffs to supply the Anglo-Am. armies. They exploit Lanka in the same way. Travelling is impossible for civilians because the Anglo-Am. armies have to be provided with means of travel. By this means, by the exploitation of our own country is this war to keep us in bondage being fought. Japan has granted freedom to Burma, Philippines and E. Indies and she has promised Boso full support of her powerful forces for the liberation of India. There is a National Army under Nethaji for the liberation of India. We must combine and drive out the British. Chiang-Kai-Shek is complaining of lack of equipment for his army. There is dissension in the Anglo-Am. camp. The Japs. will surely crush the Br. It is up to us to do our bit to deserve freedom and independence and help Japan so that thereby we may win our own freedom." - September 11, 22.10 HOURS.

4. RADIO FREE LANKA: Anglo-Ams. and Japan: Contrast:
"Brit. propagandists have been telling the world that the Axis Powers are the enemies of freedom and that they are the enemies of all Asia. They have also told you that the Anglo-Ams. are fighting for democracy and for small nations so that every nation will have freedom to live as it pleases. But the Brit. declared India a belligerent without even consulting the Indian people.

"Am. and Brit. may now express their championship of the cause of the small nations - but that is only for the purpose of exploiting our country's produce and manpower. Am. and Brit. are not motivated by altruism but by their own imperialistic aims and desire to dominate Asia.

"Japan has entered the war in the cause of freedom and liberty for the small nations of Asia. Indians living in East Asia as well as our other Asiatic compatriots will testify to this. Even in the midst of this war she has granted independence to Burma, and Premier Koiso has promised independence to Java." These are concrete examples of the sincerity of Japan and it is up to us to follow the right path if we would obtain freedom. Japan has sounded a clarion call for the freedom and independence of all Asiatic nations." - September 15, 22.13 Hrs.

5. FREE LANKA RADIO: Mr. Senanayake on Brit. Exploitation:
"Mr. D. S. Senanayake, the Leader of the Sinhalese, has bewailed the result of helping the Brit. when the Brit. Empire was tottering owing to the efforts of Germany and Japan. He has complained bitterly of the exploitation of the country by the Anglo-Ams. because it has brought no beneficial results to the country. Mr. Senanayake has awakened too late; and is it not surprising that he should be allowed to voice those sentiments? Do not be surprised. It has been tolerated because it is nothing but the truth - but yet not the whole truth for the picture of Brit. exploitation is far worse than that which Mr. Senanayake has drawn.

"We in this part of the world know the difference of being in Ceylon under the Brit. and in Burma under the Japs. We know what is freedom - we are treated as equals; there is no superiority complex such as characterises the dealings of Anglo-Ams. with us. There is no exploitation and there is no injustice done to us merely because we do not belong to the ruling race.

"Follow countrymen, every day we are growing more and more united in our efforts and desire to preserve our freedom and we long that this freedom be enjoyed by you also. The Indian National Army is daily growing in numbers and it is their determination to set you free." - September 16, 22.15 Hrs.

SUBJECT: Japanese Propaganda about Ceylon Directed Toward India

THE HONORABLE
THE SECRETARY OF STATE,
WASHINGTON.

SIR:

I have the honor to refer to this Consulate's despatches nos. 578 and 608 of September 6 and 25, 1944, and to enclose copies of two "confidential" monitoring reports on Japanese propaganda about Ceylon directed to India in Hindustani.

Japanese broadcasts directed toward Ceylon have continually encouraged the separate "independence" of Ceylon and have emphasized the present favorable opportunity to achieve freedom from British rule by "following the rest of Asia". These broadcasts to India in Hindustani show a desire to encourage Indian ambitions and self-importance by treating Ceylon as a part of India only. Both Indians and Ceylonese are urged to collaborate, under Indian leadership, in obliging "Lord Mountbatten...to go back to London".

Respectfully yours,

Max W. Bishop
American Consul

"NOT FOR REPRODUCTION"

EXTRACTS FROM SIMLA MONITORING BULLETINS

1. November 23-24, no station identification:

"CEYLON:- All Ceylon has raised a strong protest against the British Colonial Mission which is expected to visit the country next month. The British Government yesterday announced the so-called Ceylon Reform Commission will be headed by Lord Stolebury and will comprise a few high ranking imperialists including bankers and other capitalists. Reports from Ceylon reveal that the British Colonial Mission will be boycotted by the people of Ceylon. A serious crisis in the country is also regarded as likely in view of the strong protest raised by the Ceylon Board of Ministers against the sending of the mission."

2. November 29-30, from "Free India Station", Saigon, at 20:15 hours (Indian War Time) on 25.47 meters:

"Here is a talk entitled *Soulbury go back*

According to a London report a British Commission under Lord Soulbury will reach Ceylon by the end of next month. This Br. Commission will arrive in Ceylon, tour the island at its expense, enjoy delicious food and return to London. However the Singhalese are making feverish preparations to accord members of the Commission a suitable reception.

We have learnt that the Ceylon State Council passed a resolution by 26 votes to three expressing its intention of boycotting the British Soulbury Commission. As usual, the British Group of the Ceylon State Council opposed the resolution, which was supported by all other political parties. Lord Soulbury will be received with black flags, garlands of shoes and cries of 'Soulbury go back'!

Ceylon is a part of India. There was a time when Ceylon, the island of gold, produced precious stones and enjoyed prosperity. Unfortunately a falcon pounced upon this golden sparrow. Everything has a limit. Tired of British aggression and exploitation, Sinhalese nationalists launched their struggle for freedom. Soon after the outbreak of the present war, the Sinhalese made a demand for self-government. The Br. Govt. asked Sinhalese statesmen to submit their proposals on the form of Govt. they desired. Consequently the Ceylon Ministerial Board, the representative of the Singhalese, made certain suggestions and proposals after thorough consideration and submitted them to the Br. Govt. Though the Br. Govt. did not publish these proposals we know everything. A demand for the complete independence of Ceylon and the dissolution of the Br. Govt. was made in these proposals.
