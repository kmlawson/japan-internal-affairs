Subject: Report of Japanese Base Construction in Kurilo Islands.

The report below appears in the July 14th number of the London "Daily Herald". This report does not state the source of its information. Possibly Japan is merely spreading this report to counter the report from America that the United States is contemplating the organization of a strong naval base in the Aleutian Islands. Then, when naval negotiations are seriously begun, the two nations will be able, for a consideration, to discard these plans. However, the Aleutians appear to offer a more serious strategic threat to Japan than do the Kuriles to America. The report follows:

"The Japanese Government, despite financial difficulties, has decided to spend $2,000,000 on what is officially called a "fishing base" in one of the northern Kurilo islands in the North Pacific.

"Ostensibly the "base" will serve the Japanese fishermen who work in the area but since it will be equipped with radio station, hospital and other "facilities" it may well serve Japanese purposes in event of war in the Far East.

"The Japanese Government explains the decision to complete the "fishing base" inside five years at this great cost by saying that the activities of Russian fishermen make it imperative.

"It is claimed in Tokyo that there is a tendency on the part of the Soviet to transfer their fishing headquarters from Vladivostock up to Petropavlovsk in Kamchatka.

"And the Tokyo Government feels that its men must be "protected" by the establishment of a vast base as near Petropavlovsk as possible.

"The flotation of a new Japanese Government $11,000,000 loan to cover road construction, earthquake damage, expenses of the "Manchurian" incident" and the budget deficit was announced by Reuter on July 14th. At the same time a meeting of the Japanese Cabinet was held, at which it was decided that peace was to be the guiding principle in international relationship, and that Japan's aims should be realized through diplomatic channels.

"It was, however, also agreed that a programme of national defense should be formulated to preclude the possibilities of "foreign menace and insult" on one hand, and to harmonize with national resources on the other."

From: M.A. London. Report No. 36393. Date: July 16, 1934.

CONSULAR PAPER,
Lt. Col., F.A., M.A., London.

SUBJECT: Possible Purchase of Kumasaki Dochyards by Navy.

I have the honor to report that the Consulate has learned from reliable source in a position to know that the Navy is considering the purchase of the Kumasaki Dochyards at Kobe for a sum stated to be 750,000,000. It is yard like most others in Japan is authorized to do naval work (shitei kojo) at the present time, but with the prosperous conditions prevailing in the merchant shipping industry the Navy feels, it is understood, that its orders are not given the attention they deserve and that the workmanship is inferior; moreover, it wishes to ensure for itself an adequate supply of tonnage at naval standards at all times, particularly in view of the treaty-less era which has dawned. It appears that the present managing director, Mr. S. Itani, is violently opposed to the project, and is holding out for 770,000,000 in the hope of dissuading the Navy. It is felt, however, that this opposition will be overcome, and that the yard will become the Navy's property with Mr. Y. Kawasaki, heir to the Kumasaki interests, as the nominal managing director.

It is rumored, as a corollary to the above, that the Nippon Yusen Kaisha will absorb the Kawasaki Kisen Kaisha, the shipping subsidiary of the Dockyards, at the same time. The Kawasaki Kisen as the operator of over 60,000 tons of ships is one of Japan's eight largest shipping companies. This merger, if consummated, would give further impetus to the tendency towards monopolistic organization in the shipping industry. One of the most recent important steps in this direction was the merger of the Hokusai Kisen with the Osaka Shosen Kaisha. An important factor in this present movement is the personality of the present leader of Nippon Yusen Kisen, and Osaka Shosen Kaisha, Mr. M. Otani and Mr. D. Murata, who are on laying their wide popularity with all shipping operators to further the monopoly scheme.

It will be recalled that in Vice Consul Schuler's report "Shipping in the Japanese Empire of February, 1937," it was pointed out that the Ministry of Communications had been discussing an elaborate system of control over the shipbuilding industry, the impetus for which undoubtedly originated with the naval and military authorities. It is reasonable to assume that the policy of the Navy in endeavoring to obtain the Kawasaki Dockyards is but a part of the general movement toward active Government control of war industries, a movement which has been momentarily obscured by the policy of expediency adopted by the present Cabinet in obtaining the passage of its budget.
