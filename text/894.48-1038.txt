Japan. The tremors continued for 30 minutes; the initial
shock, 13 seconds.

No deaths are reported as having occurred outside of these two
Prefeotures. Houses reported destroyed numbered 78l in these

streams after periods of heavy rainfall. The natural
drainage system of Kobe could not have coped with the
unprecedented downpour; however, the seriousness of the
flood was multiplied by the reclamation projects undertaken
by city engineers in response to the pressure of an over-
crowded population.
These reclamation projects had taken the form of
draining some rivers and diverting courses through under-
ground channels. In other cases naturally wide river beds
had been greatly narrowed by building up artificial banks
and placing houses on the margins thus created. No doubt
that the gradual denuding of the forests on some slopes
back of the city also played a minor contributory role.
The torrents of July 5th led to the transformation of
the following rivers (enumerated from east to west) into
uncontrolled instruments of destruction: Ashiya, Muko,
Sumiyoshi, Oishi, Aotani, Nunobiki, Ikuta, Uji, and Minato.
The enormous amount of debris carried by these rivers
consisting principally of trees and large boulders was
augmented by wreckage of houses destroyed in the upper
reaches of the rivers. This debris dammed the natural
courses of the rivers particularly at bridges and under-
ground culverts precipitating the entire flow on to
surrounding

=== Page 68 === (confidence 0.99)
- 3 -
surrounding densely populated areas.
The steep gradient of the houses in the northern part
of Kobe, especially in the center and east, greatly
accelerated the flow of water.
Eyewitnesses stated that the avalanches of water des-
cending with cyclonic force were often so large as to
resemble tidal waves. In most areas, except a few in Nada-ku
where heavy land slides on the morning of the 5th had given
the residents ample warning, they appeared with great sudden-
ness giving the inhabitants of the doomed areas little or no
chance to escape. Persons able to leave their houses before
the latter collapsed were crushed by boulders and great
trees or were drowned because of the high speed and the
heavy silt content of the water. In many cases the silt
was packed so hard by the force of the water behind it that
it was as hard as cement despite its water content.
In the lower part of the city including the bus iness
sections the water lost much of its force. While colossal
amounts of debris and silt were carried into the downtown
streets near the sea no buildings were washed away. Loss of
life in the downtown area was megligible except for those
trapped in the Hanshin subway tube which will be dealt with
in a following section. From three to four feet of water
stood in much of the downtown area including Higashi and
Ito streets on which the Consulate is located. Damage to
basements and ground floors of office buildings and to
stored stocks of merchandise (chiefly intended for export)
was considerable although insignificant compared to damage
to property and to communication facilities farther north
of the city.
WELFARE

WELFARE OF AMERICAN COMMUNITY
The American community escaped from the flood with
very slight losses. No Americans were killed and none
received injuries of any consequence. So far as I have
been able to ascertain no important American property was
completely destroyed and no Americans, with the possible
exception of one small chemical factory controlled by
American interests, are known to have suffered losses which
could be considered a severe financial setback.
Minor discomforts are being suffered by Americans
living here along with other residents, while considerable
inconvenience and some financial loss was caused those few
Americans who were forced to move from their rented houses
as a result of flood damage.
CASUALTIES
The latest official report on flood casualties issued
by the prefectural officials shows a death list in Kobe and
adjoining suburban districts of 471 and a total of 156
missing persons (privately admitted to be dead), a total
of 626 persons. A total of 3,163 persons are reported as
injured in the official figures.
I regret to report that little if any credence can be
given the official death toll. I base this assertion on
the following facts:
1. I was personally informed by a high official of
the Hyogo Prefectural office on July 8th that 892 persons
were known to have been killed in this Prefecture by the
flood on July 5th.
2. The official announcement of the total injured
and killed has been slightly reduced from day to day for
the

3. The prefectural authorities have publicly and
categorically denied a loss of life in the Hanshin subway
tube near Sannomiya which, according to very strong evidence,
stands at a minimum of 135 persons. The attached enclosure
number 3 gives the denial of the authorities. However, I
have been informed by two reputable American citizens here
that each of them supplied a trusted Japanese employee for
clearance work who was used to remove bodies from the
Hanshin subway. These employees stated that in the course
of one evening (July 15th) they assisted in the removal of
more than 1o0 bodies from this subway. The work was done
in the greatest secrecy all workers being required to shift
constantly in order to prevent their counting the bodies
accurately. These employees stated to their employers
that other bodies were not yet removed from the tube; that
a total of nine cars in six units were trapped in the sub-
way. It appears that the casualties all occurred in two
trains which were trapped near the eastern terminus of the
subway between Sannomiya and Motomachi stations. The
powerful stench of decaying flesh which is very evident
around the outlets to the subway corroborates the evidence
that loss of life occurred there and that all bodies are
not yet removed. This deliberate suppression of actual
casualties by the Japanese authorities tends to discredit

HEALTH CONDITIONS
The reservoirs and water mains supplying the city with
water were badly damaged although no reservoirs burst as
was erroneously reported at first. The result has been
that most sections of the city were entirely without water
until July 2Oth. The city is now on a rationing system
most areas receiving water only two hours a day. The
authorities are aware of the danger of epidemics of enteric
diseases and have made compulsory the vaccination of all
residents against typhoid fever. Cases of dysentery amount
to about 175. The danger of epidemics will remain until
a clean and continuous water supply is restored. This may
be a matter of several weeks due to the many places in

EFFECTS ON THE GENERAL ECONOMY OF JAPAN
It cannot be said that the flood damage has directly
weakened the country in a military or an economic sense
to any appreciable extent. The loss of life among able-
bodied men was not heavy enough to be widely felt;
the
harbor and the docks were not damaged; the vital indus-
tries such as the Kobe Steel Works and the Kawasaki Dock
Yards were not badly damaged; destruction of imported
materials which would further impair Japan's foreign exchange
position was not great.
Nevertheless, the effects of the flood on Japan's
economy will be tangible although they may not be immediately
apparent. In the first place the energies of thousands
of manual laborers as well as of many engineers will be
consumed for many months in clearance and rehabilitation
work, whereas in normal times these efforts could be devoted
to directly constructive work. Furthermore, the landlord
and capitalist class of the city have received severe
financial setbacks which will be sure to result in decreased
tax collections in this city. Flooding of rice fields in
this district, chiefly in the western part of Hyogo Pre-
fecture, will reduce rice production for this year to a
moderate extent; employment of capital for reconstruction
work will diminish the amount available for investment in
Government bonds and extension of industrial facilities.
The shortage of water is seriously hampering the operation
of many factories. Many factories have been unable to
obtain water even at the exorbitant price of Yen 2.00 per
barrel which some of them have offered. The effect of
the

=== Page 76 === (confidence 0.99)

the flood on the morale of the local residents is a less
tengible factor which it is difficult to assess. The people
have met the emergency in a stolid manner. There is little
tendency to grumble and clearance efforts have been whole-
hearted. No signs of demoralization are noticeable.
NATURE OF RELIEF AND RECONSTRU CTION EFFORTS
First efforts were devoted to a reestablishment of trans-
portation facilities. Restricted service on the through line
of the Government Railways was restored on July l2th after
extensive repair operations between Kobe and Osaka to the

urban line was resumed on July 14th. Through service on the
Hanshin interurban line has not yet been restored. Con-
siderably more than half of the mileage of the city tram
system is in operation but the services in the eastern part
of the city are still paralyzed. There was little inter-
ruption in telephone and electric light service. Interruption
in gas service was more serious but it has now been restored
except in the devasted areas.
No effort will be made for some time to rebuild the
demolished areas. Clearance work is being given precedence.
It is anticipated that substantial progress in clearance
work will be made by the end of the summer.
Due to the strategic importance which the central
Government attaches to Kobe it is likely that reconstruction
and relief appropriations on liberal terms will be made.
The Home Minister, the Education Minister, and an Imperial
