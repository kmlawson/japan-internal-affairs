## TRAINED GERMAN POLITICAL WORKERS DID A THOROUGH JOB IN DAI NIPPON, SAYS FOREIGN WRITER

By "SEIYO-JIN"

EMPEROR HIROHITO rules in his moated feudal palace, surrounded by Tokyo's skyscrapers, and in the hearts of his seventy million hard-working, simple-living and up-and-coming subjects. Who actually governs the country is anybody's guess. It's partly closely-shaven colonels with the mud of China still on their boots, partly smooth aristocrats like Prince Kuroye and Marquis Kido who keeps a copy of Mein Kamui near the ice-water at his bedside, partly slick politicians who've figured that democracy is a longer rule, but mostly Nazi Fifth Column Commander General Eugen Ott (His Excellency the German Ambassador) is very old. He 10,000 active, unorthodox, German and native.

He has just heard the first Japanese Cabinet on totalitarian burs. Having hired the Army, which is anxious for the Axis tie-up and an adventure in the Dutch East Indies to distract the public attention from the way in which things are going, or not going, in China, he visited Prince Kuroye in his summer villa and said point-blank that the Prince had better take charge of things and set up a tote organization; otherwise there would be trouble with both Army and public. The suave Prince told the General at the first visit that Japanese Governments were generally made in Japan. The General returned, however, this time with a couple of Japanese generals—Terauchi, an aristocrat, and Sugiyama, a tough, but both ex-War Ministers and able to speak for some part of the Army. And together the three Generals told the Prince all about it and so now Japan has a tote Government and any adventure is possible.
