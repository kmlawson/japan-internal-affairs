AVIATION IN 1930
KWANTUNG LEASED TERRITORY

From: American Consul, Cabot Coville.
Date of Completion: March 24, 1931.
Dairen, Manchuria.
Date of Mailing: MAR 25 1931

The Kwantung Leased Territory is an area of the size of the state of Rhode Island, located on the peninsula south of Manchuria, and leased from China by Japan. It contains the city of Dairen. Its interest in commercial aviation at present is limited to a regular Japanese flying service connecting with Tokyo by way of Korea. Military control of the Territory is Japanese, but

but information concerning military aviation is not divulged. This report therefore is confined to commercial aviation.

Although the subject of this report is commercial aviation, it should be strictly understood that flying of any type in the Kwantung Leased Territory is subject to rigid restrictions. The Territory contains fortified areas, over which it is a prosecutable offence to fly without permission. Permission is seldom granted, and this Consulate knows of no case in which such permission has been granted to foreigners.

**Aircraft Production**

There is no production of aircraft in the Kwantung Leased Territory.

**Airport, Meteorology, and Topography**

This district possesses one commercial airport which is the flying field of the Japan Air Transport Company. It is situated some seven miles from the city of Dairen, and is the terminus of the air service connecting Tokyo and Dairen. The field is large and well chosen, and is equipped with one large hangar. Lights are available, but not ordinarily used. One of the main meteorological stations of the Japanese Empire is located at Dairen, and its services are at the disposal of aircraft using the field.

In the present state of aviation development, this single airport is sufficient for the need.

The peninsula on which the Kwantung Leased Territory is situated is so narrow and the climate so dry that there are

are no lakes and only small streams. The country is therefore not suitable for planes which take off from water. The topography in general is mountainous, but cultivated valleys permit adequate preparations for forced landings along the scheduled route. Local climatic conditions generally favor flying. The dry climate causes high visibility. Precipitation is unusually low. Weather records over a number of years show an average of 62% hours of sunshine with only 80 cloudy days per year. The mean temperature of Dairen is 50.5 degrees Fahrenheit. The unfavorable factor is wind. Mean velocity of the wind is 4.6 (Tokyo being 2.6).

**Aviation Training**

There are no institutions in the Kwantung Leased Territory for training commercial aviators.

**Passenger and Air Mail Lines**

The Japan Air Transport Company with head office at 7 Sakurada, Hongo-cho, Shiba-ku, Tokyo, operates the only air line from this district. This company is subsidized by the Japanese government and its control is semi-governmental. The Bureau of Communications of the local government has certain authority over it as far as its operations in the Kwantung Leased Territory are concerned.

The flying route from Dairen is first directly to a point in south Manchuria slightly west of Antung. This means that planes fly over the ocean though quite close to the coast line. From this point slightly west of Antung the course to Fukuoka, a city lying between Nagasaki and the

the straits of Shimonoseki on the island of Kyushu, is almost exactly straight. The first stop is at Heijo, Korea. The second stop is at Keijo, the capital. The third stop is at Urusan, a town on the coast of Korea facing Japan, slightly to the north east of Fusan. The fourth stop is Fukuoka and the fifth is Osaka, whence the planes fly direct to Tokyo.

The flying schedule is one plane each way daily except Sunday. However, in the event of adverse weather conditions, the planes do not put off. At seven o'clock in the morning, according to the schedule, a plane leaves the Dairen air field and arrives at Heijo at 10:20 a.m. Ten minutes later it takes off and arrives at Keijo at 11:40. At ten minutes after noon the plane leaves the Keijo field and reaches Urusan at 2 p.m. After a wait of twenty minutes the passengers leave Urusan in a hydroplane and arrive at Fukuoka at four o'clock. From Fukuoka the flight is continued the following day, leaving at 8:50 a.m. and arriving at Osaka at noon. At half past twelve the plane departs and reaches Tokyo at ten minutes past three. Leaving Dairen in the morning at seven o'clock on a given day, a passenger therefore passes that night at Fukuoka and arrives in Tokyo the next afternoon. This compares with a railroad schedule of three days and steamship schedules of four days and longer.

There is a similar schedule for the trip from Tokyo to Dairen, but it is somewhat longer because the schedule is broken by two overnight stops instead of one. In the last part of the journey, the plane leaves Keijo at 9:30 a.m. and arrives at Dairen forty minutes after noon.

The distances by air from Dairen along this route are: Heijo, 257 miles; Keijo, 382 miles; Urusan, 587 miles; Fukuoka, 736 miles; Osaka, 1046 miles; Tokyo, 1310 miles.

Fares from Dairen, in terms of Japanese yen, are: Yen 27 to Heijo; Yen 40 to Keijo; Yen 62 to Urusan; Yen 80 to Fukuoka; Yen 115 to Osaka; Yen 145 to Tokyo. The value of the yen is 50 cents United States currency.

Passengers are allowed 15 kilograms of baggage without charge. Above this limit there is a variable charge depending on weight and length of flight, ranging to a maximum of two yen per kilogram. The Company will also carry goods for one yen per kilogram if to points in Korea, and two yen per kilogram if to points in Japan. Air mail likewise is carried, the rate of letters being 33 sen (16½ cents United States currency) per 15 grams to points in Japan.

The Company has four planes registered in Dairen. These are operated by three pilots all of whom were trained in Japan. A mechanic accompanies the pilot on each flight. Planes have a capacity of six passengers, pilot, mechanic, 370 kilograms of fuel, and 45 kilograms of other load. During the year 1930, 253 scheduled flights from Dairen were effected, and 250 arrivals. 342 passengers left Dairen, and 260 passengers arrived at Dairen. The average is therefore slightly more than one passenger per flight. 16,458 pieces of mail were despatched and 7,073 pieces were received. There were no accidents in this jurisdiction.

The planes are of American make, Fokker Super-Universal type.

## Aeronautical Market

The American airplanes used by the Japan Air Transport Company appear to have been entirely satisfactory. There seems to be no immediate danger of losing this market to competitors. However, as far as this consular district is concerned, there is no immediate prospect of increasing the market.

There are no local dealers in aircraft.
