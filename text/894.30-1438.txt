Department of State

Below is a copy of communique issued by the Naval
Section of the Japanese Imperial General Headquarters on
December 24, 1939, covering naval operations for the
current calendar year.

Japanese naval forces during this year endeavored
to attain the objective of the China Affair in defiance
of every obstacle in close cooperation with army forces

In spite of climatic inridrances, the forces on
the sea continued the blockade of the entire China
coast to cut supplies lines for the enemy

The forces on the rivers controlled 500 miles
of waterway up to Yochow on the Yangtze and many
other rivers and lakes to maintain the safety of
land forces operating in China.

Milful joint operations between the navy and
the Army were often carried out at various points,
with the Japanese capturing Hainan Island and other
important places on the sea coast and various islands.

Any occasional attempt to reconstruct the
Chinese air force was crushed at every time when
Japanese naval planes heavily bombed and inflicted
great losses on the enemy air bases as well as land
forces and military establishments.

Anti-Japanese forces remaining in China which
have lost contact within themselves are now gradually
nearing their total collapse.

With the progress of the China Affair, the
Imperial Navy in the latest part of this year
realigned naval forces operating in China.

Under the command of Admiral Koshiro Oikawa,
Commander-in-Chief of the Japanese Fleet in China
waters, every unit is consolidating its firm and un-
shakable position so as to accomplish the objective
of military operations.

The highest Commanders of the separate war zones
in China are as follows:

North China: Vice-Admiral Naokuni Nomura
Yangtze Area: Vice-Admiral Umataro Tanimoto
Shanghai Area: Rear-Admiral Shuichiro Higashi
South China: Vice-Admiral Shiro Takasu

Report No. 9892
December 29, 1939

Results of operations are as follows:

1. Traffic Blockade:
Entire China coast -- 2,450 miles
Yangtze below Yochow -- 500 miles

Others include the Pearl River, Tai Lake, Manyang Lake, Tungting Lake, the Hsiang River, the Sheyang River and Baoyu Lake.

2. Strengthened Closure of Navigation:
a. Bays and Harbors: One, Fuchow, Chuanchow, Tungsha, Shandong, Shaoan, Kwatow, Hsin Hua, Wantuao, Wache, Xunang and Teichow.
b. Important Point and Points: Mainan Island, Kwatow, the Chushan Islands, Chungshan-hsien and Yunchun Bay (Manning operations).

3. Activity of Naval Places:
Reside cooperating in last offensives and clean-up operations. Japanese Naval air forces bombed the interior of China, including Wengtse, Yunnanfu, Tueow, Wenshu, Tuining, Hian, Wancow and Chungking. The enemy air forces have been destroyed to such an extent that their recovery is impossible. This various establishments for military purposes have been conclusively demolished. Chungking, the capital of the anti-Japanese regime suffered Japanese air raids more than a score of times.

DIVISION OF Lippmann seems to be learning a little
with regard to the Far East. In contrast with what he
advocated, on "appeasement" lines, in his column of
June 6 under the caption "Toward A Peace With Japan",
there appears in his column of this morning under the
caption "American Security Is Sea Power", a paragraph
as follows:

"With some modifications the same reasoning
applies in the Far East. Our concern with
the resistance of China, with the preservation
of British and French bases in the Southwestern
Pacific, with the maintenance of the Netherlands
Indies, Singapore, Australia and New Zealand,
rests on the very simple fact that as long as
the Japanese navy is not the undisputed master
of the other side of the Pacific, it is unable
to concern itself with Alaska or South America
or any place on our side of the Pacific. If there
were any way of coming to a genuine and honorable
agreement with Japan for a partnership in the
Pacific, the outlook would no doubt be still
better. But as long as Japan is engaged in conquest,
we risk less by increasing her difficulties
in Asia than by removing them."

My dear Mr. Berle:

The following information concerning Japanese activities has been received from a source considered reliable.

Two liners, of 11,500 tons each, for North Pacific service, presently are in the process of construction at Mitsubishi Shipyards, Nagasaki, Japan. They are described as being Mitsubishi-Sulzer Diesels; trial speed, 22 knots; service speed, 18 knots. One ship is named "Meike Maru"; the other probably will be named "Mishima Maru." Two liners, of 28,000 tons each, with service speed of 16 to 17 knots, are also under construction. One is named the "Kashibara Maru"; the other ship's name is unknown. The yard is said to be working at full capacity on other smaller vessels.

Manshu Kagaku Kogyo, said to be producing ammonium sulphate because of the lack of coal, was obliged to cut its production severely in 1940. In the half-year ending September 30, 1940, production was 80,000 tons. This condition is expected to improve largely due to Government subsidy on ammonium sulphate, making it possible to pay higher prices for coal.

It has been reported that a new naval base has been established on the Japan Sea. On the basis of greatly increased activity there, it was suggested that the base may be located in Niigata.

A new firm, the Yokogawa Instrument Company, has been established at Moji for the production of precision tools. The products of this firm are not comparable to American instruments due to the lack of accuracy in gauges.

Information is to the effect that Nitto Kogyo Kisen has effected a merger with the Nihon Tanka and is in a "fair way" to become Japan's leading operator of oil tankers, both for crude oil and petroleum distillates. This company in 1939 took delivery of three new tankers, the "Nichiei Maru," the "Goa Maru" and the "Hirowa Maru." In 1940 another six new tankers were ordered from various private shipbuilding companies.

The following also is being forwarded for your information although it is realized that this, no doubt, has long been in your possession:

The Yokosuka Naval Arsenal was equipped in 1934-1935 with machinery to make parts for the Navy Air Force. At the time this was done it was reported that this branch of the arsenal could handle 200 engines a year and was capable of making any parts required for their overhaul, et cetera. In 1937 new equipment was purchased, much of which came from the United States.

The capacity of the Hiro Arsenal is estimated at 150 frames a year. This would be in accordance with the usual production schedules of two engines for one completed airplane. In 1935-36 engine output in the Hiro Arsenal was rated at 200 engines a year with a frame output of between 75 to 100.

The Kasumigaura Arsenal, which is described as a repair and assembly station, is rated with an emergency output of ten to twelve engines a month.

STATE OF JAPANESE NAVAL SPECIAL TRANSPORTS

Japanese Naval Special Transports are those vessels which have been requisitioned under the necessity of the Japanese Navy and engaged in transportation of necessary commodities for the Japanese Navy.

For this reason the Japanese Navy places on board each vessel one inspecting naval officer, a few officers and a number of warrant officers and enlisted men. The inspecting officer is authorized to order and command the captain of the ship in carrying out an order or orders given by the Japanese Navy Department.

These vessels belong to the Japanese Navy and (are) mentioned in the Japanese navy ships lists.

SECRETARY OF STATE, WASHINGTON.

ORDER 27, 11 a.m.

October 27, 11 a.m.

A source believed to be of reasonable reliability states that during the past month he has witnessed intense activity at the naval base at Port Arthur. Naval vessels have been going into drydock: to have their bottoms scraped, each ship spending an average of two days in drydock. At one time, for a period of two weeks ending about ten days ago, six cruisers and one capital ship accompanied by battle cruiser were based on Port Arthur.

During the past two weeks about 50 motor torpedo boats have been going out to sea before dawn on maneuvers with only the first boat showing lights and leading the others. They have been returning after dusk. These maneuvers are apparently still going on.

Naval sentries and marine police have been greatly strengthened during the past month.

the former Japanese Embassy, Washington, D. C., stated that at the
present time shipbuilding companies in Japan are equipped to build approxi-
mately three million tons of shipping per year. In this regard, he con-
tinued, officials in this country do not believe that Japanese shipbuilding
companies are capable of producing as many ships as the United States'
facilities.

detainee formerly of the Steamship Line of Panama, stated that it is im-
possible for Japan to build three million tons of shipping per year,

However, Mr. Hiedelk did say that if Japan captures Calcutta and its
environs, the necessary materials to produce as much shipping as the
