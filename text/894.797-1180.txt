SUBJECT: TEMPORARY SHUT-DOWN OF FORD MOTOR COMPANY OF JAPAN, LTD., AND NEGATIVE PLANS FOR COOPERATION WITH JAPANESE.

Strictly Confidential

THE HONORABLE

THE SECRETARY OF STATE
WASHINGTON

I have the honor to refer to previous reports concerning the Japanese automotive industry, the latest of which accompanies this despatch, and to report the latest developments in regard to the FORD MOTOR COMPANY OF JAPAN, LTD., which operates an assembly plant in Yokohama.

Temporary Shut-Down of FORD MOTOR COMPANY OF JAPAN, LTD.

Previous reports have described the position of the FORD and GENERAL MOTORS assembly plants in the Japanese automotive industry. The Japanese authorities have been devoting great effort and much money, through the NISSAN and TOYODA companies, to the establishment of a native automotive industry which would take the place of FORD and GENERAL MOTORS. As explained in the report of this Consulate, dated February

**Proposed Sale of Food Interests.**

As reported upon previous occasions, the Japanese authorities have been trying for several years to effect an agreement with either FORD or SANTA MARIA by which the Japanese would obtain at least a controlling interest in one of the companies. Announcements have been made from time to time that one of the other company was about to sell out to the Japanese. The proposals have generally been made by the NISSAN interests, headed by Mr. Yoshisuke Aikawa, who is now head of the MANCHU. U.S. INDUSTRIAL DEVELOPMENT CORPORATION. Mr. Aikawa is still interested in such a proposition.

Negotiations have generally broken down because of opposition on the part of Japanese authorities. In addition, until the Japanese automotive industry had progressed to the point of practical independence from American sources of supply the American companies

Now that the Japanese industry can carry on fairly well, the Japanese authorities are in a position to proceed with their long-held desire to force the American companies either to sell out on favorable terms of cease operation.

Negotiations are now to introduce between FORD and the Japanese authorities and companies concerned which may result in a agreement. The initiative in proposing such an arrangement has come from the Japanese investors, the participation of NISSAN and TOYODA and the general plan having been decided by the Japanese authorities. It is understood (a) that while tentative conditions have been agreed upon, no definite understanding has been reached; (b) that the plan is contingent upon the development of relations between the United States and Japan; and (c) that in any case no additional manufacturing capacity will be provided until after the China "incident" is settled. Also, (d) that whatever is done in the earlier stages will be done while the American company retains a majority of the shares and full control of the Japanese FORD company.

The plan suggested is that a new company will be formed by FORD, NISSAN and TOYODA, by which FORD will own 40% of the stock and control the management of the company. NISSAN and TOYODA will each own 30% of the stock.

A new plant will be built capable of producing at least 50,000 units a year. The present NISSAN plant would be used for the manufacture of parts or special trucks. The present plant of TORODA would be turned over to aircraft production. The new company's product would presumably be a FORD unit with minor modifications and a different name to give the appearance of a Japanese product, such as was done by the NISSAN, which builds a truck with a Graham-raise on line. The Japanese would have, in any case, a domestic motor car of the best American design, currently incorporating the latest American improvements, which the NISSAN does not incorporate.

The proposed arrangement would probably follow in many respects the establishment of FORD branch factories in Canada, Germany, England, Spain, etc., where domestic companies have been organized and a share of the stock sold to the public. The question of management control may be hard to maintain since the Japanese will have control of the stock, but as long as the company relies on imported designs and equipment, the FORD interests would be in a strong position.

Presumably the new company would supply all the automotive needs of Japan, "manchukuo" and that part of China under Japanese control, by means of discriminatory laws. The present Automobile Manufacturing

Similar arrangements are no doubt in existence in "Manchukuo" and would certainly be put into effect in all parts of the "yen-bioc" area.

Whether or not the proposed company would also attempt to supply other markets in the Far East or in Latin America would need to make future conditions, but there is no reason to suppose that if such plants in Canada, Germany, England, etc., supply other foreign markets the proposed company in Japan would limit its activities to the "yen-bioc."

The FORD company has up to date been able to remit its dividends to the United States. Its imports have been paid for within a few months from the time they left the United States and are now paid up. (Incidentally part of the success the FORD company has had in remitting dividends to the United States is believed to be due to its purchase of ¥1,000,000 of Japanese war bonds in December, 1937. The purchase was made for the purpose of arousing a sympathetic attitude from the Japanese authorities, and the attempt was successful.)
