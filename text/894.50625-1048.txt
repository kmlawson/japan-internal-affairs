September 28, 4 p. m.

Japanese offering war risk insurance for rubber and tin cargoes transported via transpacific and Panama Canal at fifty per cent prevailing rates provided shipment made on Japanese vessels but no acceptances.

The belief that restrictions will be discontinued in the event of hostilities resulted in decline of price of rubber to twenty-one Straits Settlements cents inducing break in first-class local securities sold to cover losses.

=== Page 2 === (confidence 0.98)
September 30 1938.

In reply refer to
BA 894.50625/16

My dear Mr. Viles:

I think you may be interested in the information we have received from Singapore to the effect that the Japanese are offering war risk insurance for rubber and tin cargoes transported across the Pacific and via the Panama Canal at only 50 percent of the prevailing rates, providing the shipments are made in Japanese vessels. According to our advices, there have been no acceptances of these offers.

A report also comes from Singapore that rubber prices during the past week declined due to the belief that rubber restriction will be discontinued in the event of a European war. The decline in the price of rubber was sufficient to induce a break in first class local securities which were sold to cover losses.

I

Mr. A. L. Viles, President,
Rubber Manufacturers Association,
444 Madison Avenue,
New York, New York.
