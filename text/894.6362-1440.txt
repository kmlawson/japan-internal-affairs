Dear Dr. Hornbeck:
On April 23rd we received the following telegram from our London manager:
"Can you cable our coke deliveries ex Netherlands East Indies to northern country year 1939 1940 and estimate for 1941"
The quantities involved in answering above cable are as follows:
1939 - 1",778 tons
1940 - 11,524 "
1941 - 36,000 " (Estimated) of which 3,944 tons taken to date
We assume that the London inquiry comes from the British Government.
In our refining process at Palembang we produce a certain amount of coke and for many years we have sold this coke in Japan. It has never previously occurred to any of us here to acquaint you with this matter, but the London inquiry brings up the question, and I feel that if this matter is of interest to the British Government, you should also be in possession of the information.
Since stringent foreign exchange regulations have been in effect in Japan our customer has had more or less difficulty getting foreign exchange, which accounts for the small quantity taken in 1940; in fact they want one boat there such a last year and hold it for several days and then could not obtain their foreign exchange and the boat left. Last year their indicated requirements were 20,000 tons, of which as you see, they took slightly more than half. At the rate they have taken this year they will fall far short of their estimated requirements.
If you have any comments to make we shall be very glad to receive them.
Sincerely yours,

Berlin DEUTSCHE ALLGEMEINE ZEITUNG December 20
reports Deputy Minister Kishi in radio address announced
new coal saving measures already effective have
reduced Japanese coal consumption by twenty percent in
iron and steel industries and extension to other
industries expected cause similar reductions. Stated
saving ten percent in Japanese coal consumption would
make six million tons coal (indicating total Japanese
coal consumption sixty million tons) available for
expanding industries. Kishi urged all industrial
leaders and workmen seize every possible opportunity
to reduce use coal to minimum.

SUBJECT: Production and Transportation of Coal to Japan
under the Kailan Mining Administration

894.6362

It is understood that the State Department would be interested
to have information concerning the production and transportation
of coal under the Kailan Mining Administration. Below is
a list of data which we have available on this subject. This
information comes from sources presumed to be reliable.

The Kailan Mining Administration's principal mines are
located at:
(a) Tungshan Mining area
(b) Linsi area
(c) Kuyeh area

Production of 17,000 tons per day from the Kailan mines is a
plausible estimate. Such an estimate gives a yearly production
of 6,205,000 tons. Production in 1941 was 6,500,000 tons, and
in 1942 was 7,000,000 tons. Production for 1943 has been estimated
at 7,500,000 tons.

The Linsi area is the largest and most important of these
properties. The bombing of the Linsi mine in April, 1942 did
very little damage. It did not stop production for even a day.
One bomb came close to the power station, and, if it had been
hit, it is estimated that the mines would have been put out of
commission for at least a year.

All coal from these mining areas is hauled by double railroad
track line to Chingwantao, where it is loaded for shipment
directly to Japan. The coal trains are continuously
shuttling back and forth between the mines and Chingwantao.

At present no Americans are connected with the mines. About 10 British are now working in clerical jobs in Teng-hsien, about 40 Belgians are left at the mines.

The Chinghsing mine is located at Kantoutsan, 10 km. north of Nan Ho Tou on the Shih-chia-chuang-Taiyuan railroad. The 1941 production at the Chinghsing mine was around 2,500 tons per day of a good quality bituminous coking coal (one source says anthracite, but this is probably incorrect).

It may be possible that the Japanese are shipping coal via the Lunghai railroad only from Kaifeng to Sinpu (present name Lao-Yao) on the coast. On the other hand, it may be that coal from Chinghsing is going out through Tsingtao rather than Lao-Yao, since the distance to the former is somewhat shorter from the mine.

The coal mine at Tatungfu in northern Shansi, on the Suiyuan railroad going to Kalgan and Peking, produces high grade bituminous coal. It is reported that the Japanese have developed this mine greatly since they took it over. The 1941 production was 5,000 tons per day. The coal is transported by rail via Kalgan, and practically all is being shipped from Chingwantao to Japan and Manchuria.

A group of four mines, known collectively as the Lungyen iron mines, produces altogether about 6,000 tons a day. One source indicates that one of the mines is located between Peking and Kalgan about 100 miles north of Peking and about 50 miles from Nanko above the pass. This is an open pit mine whose output is transported to Nanko where the ore is smelted into pig iron.

Another of the mines is probably located at Hsuan Hua Fu, 30 miles southeast of Kalgan on the Suiyuan railroad. This mine may possibly be named the Lien Yuen iron mine. One source reports this mine was reopened by the Japanese and is mining about 1,000 tons of low grade iron ore per day. This ore is shipped to Japan from Tangku, where the Japanese have built special piers for handling coal and iron ore. (Great emphasis has been placed on the development of Lungyen, and Chinese sources recently reported mining at the rate of 3,000,000 tons a year - which is probably too high.)

## Secretary of State,
Washington.

5218, Eleventh.

Tokyo report dated end July in German press gives following data on Japanese coal situation: Hokkaido mines which are now producing 20% total Jap coal output were shifted end 1943 to production coking coal, thus enabling suspension imports thereof from northern China. Mitsubishi owned Daiyubari mines produce best quality with 7% ash content and 7600-8000 heat calories. Hokkaido coal while inferior to Chinese is usable in smelters and foundries and possesses advantage of shorter haul to Hokkaido ironworks and Kwanton district. Transportation facilities recently improved by increased services to Aomori-Hakodate and extension to Ominato-Hacinoe. Labor constitutes chief problem since constant change Korean workmen prevents progressive increase mine output, however, introduction compulsory labor service for Koreas now under consideration. Kuyuschu mines producing 70% total output yield inferior quality
