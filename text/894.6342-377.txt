In 1935 official imports of bullion into Japan proper
were only 33 kilograms; production was 267,000 kilograms.
Exports were nearly 3t million kilograns, an increase of
84.25% over those of 1934 and 3 million kilograms over
total imports and production for the year. Official customs
figures for all bullion imports into Chosen and Taiwan for
1935 show a substantial increase in value, although statis-
tics for quantities are not available, nor can possible
gold imports be segregated from silver. However, in view
of the known abnormal movements of silver in 1935, it is
believed that customs figures for bullion imports into
Chosen and Taiwan may be attributed in greater part to
silver.
With this assumption in mind, substantiated by
unofficial figures indicating that Chosen exported to
Japan 177 million yen worth of silver, it may not be
unwarranted to assume that a considerable portion of
the Chosen and Taiwan imports found its way to Japan and
was included in export figures for Japan proper, parti-
cularly since more than twice the silver produced in
Japan proper in the period 1931 to 1937 inclusive was
exported.
The enormous increase in exports of silver from Japan
for 1935 was undoubtedly a result of the policy of the
United States government in buying silver in accordance
