DEPARTMENT OF STATE
THE LEGAL ADVISER

May 15, 1944.

RP - Mr. Gerber:

Is this request perhaps more along your line, or for the Library, than it is for us?

So far as I can make out from the file attached, there is nothing said as to the ownership of the land on which the monument erected in September, 1927, stands. I should understand it to be the monument at Shimoda, referred to in the despatches. I think the inference is that the land remains the property of the temple, which is referred to in several of the despatches.

I don't know at all about Trinity Church; should think that a good New York City guide-book would take care of that.

I'd be interested to see the answers, if they can be routed through our office, just for my own information.

MMB
LE:W.W.Bishop

Gentlemen:-
Attention: Mr. George W. Hutchison,
- Secretary -
May I be permitted to ask for and receive the following information:
1) In September of 1927 a monument was erected near Yokohama " in Memory of Townsend Harris, American Consul-General, who by the Treaty of Yedo, July 28th-1858, opened Japan to the World". Is the land upon which the monument stands, deeded by Japan's Government to the United States? Or does Japan owns the spot?
2) If I am correctly informed, the land, upon which Trinity Church was erected on lower Broadway in New York City, was deeded years ago to the Episcopal Church of England, subject to the Church's and adjacent grounds maintenance by the Great Britain?
Thanking you in advance for kind reply, I am
Respectfully
G. Gebhardt

My dear Mr. Gebhardt:

The receipt is acknowledged of your letter of May 1, 1944, by reference from the National Geographic Society, in which you request information regarding the land on which the Townsend Harris monument in Japan was erected and dedicated on October 1, 1927, and also concerning the grounds upon which Trinity Church, New York City, was erected.

So far as the Department has been able to ascertain, the Townsend Harris monument at Shimoda was placed within the temple grounds of Gyokusenji, and the Department has found no evidence that the portion of the temple grounds occupied by the monument was deeded to the United States.

The Department has no record of the ownership of the land now occupied by Trinity Church in New York City. In this connection, however, you may wish to consult the Reverend Franklin J. Clark, D.D., Secretary to the National Council of the Protestant Episcopal Church, Church Missions House, 281 Fourth Avenue, New York, New York.

Sincerely yours,

For the Secretary of State:

E. Wilder Spaulding
Acting Chief, Division of
