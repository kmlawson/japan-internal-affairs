Brief history of the Jitsugetsutan Project.

In 1919 the result of a three year period of investigation as to the feasibility of establishing a hydro-electric plant at Lake Jitsugetsutan (Lake Candidius) brought about the organization of the Taiwan Electric Power Company, the stock in this limited liability company to be held by the Taiwan Government General and a number of private persons. The organization of this quasi-public concern was necessitated by the inability of the Taiwan Government General of obtain sufficient funds to carry out the undertaking as a government enterprise, because the loan policy of the Japanese Government at that time would not permit the adoption of a plan which aimed at the flotation of a five year loan, and as allowing this enterprise to be wholly a private undertaking was contrary to public policy.

Construction was started in that year, a period of five years being estimated as ample to complete the project, the cost being estimated at yen 48,000,000. However, various circumstances, chiefly a lack of capital and a rise in commodity costs of approximately 70 per cent, caused a suspension of construction activity in 1922. Between this time and the short period of resumption of construction in 1929 the fate of the project was the subject of considerable discussion, in both business and political circles. Eventually, however, the trend turned in favor of resumption, inasmuch as the report of a noted American consulting engineer was almost entirely commendatory of the project and its numerous engineering features.

In 1929 there was mention of what is now an assured matter, a guarantee by the Japanese Government of such foreign loans as might be necessary for completion of the enterprise, this guarantee being subject to approval of the Cabinet and the Imperial Diet as well as by the Taiwan Government

=== Page 9 === (confidence 0.99)
-3-

Government General. Coupled with this indication that the government would guarantee a foreign loan was the withdrawing of a subsidy of yen 3,000,000 which had been set aside for preservation and conservation of completed portions of the work and purchased machinery. This withdrawal, brought about by a change in government and the enunciation of a policy of retrenchment, made it more imperative that the company secure an immediate absolute guarantee of foreign loans.

**Present status of the project.**

The Governor General of Taiwan, appointed to the office in July, 1929, and Mr. Matsuki, appointed president of the Taiwan Electric Power Company in October, 1929, have been energetic in their endeavors to obtain permission to resume the undertaking, a number of trips having been made to Tokyo to lay before interested officials of the Tokyo Government the results of recent investigations. Although it has been stated that the Lake Jitsugetsutan Project has been made a political issue at various times, it now seems fairly certain that the two strong political parties of Japan, the Minshuto and the Seiyukai, are agreed as to the ultimate advisability of the enterprise, in witness whereof the 56th session of the Imperial Diet passed a bill which provided for the guarantee by the government of such foreign loans as should be necessary, stipulations being attached to this provision that a further investigation as to certain engineering features be made and the results be incorporated in the final structure.

Acting under this stipulation exhaustive investigations were carried out and such changes as seemed advisable were made in the plans, these, however, being on technical points and in nowise affecting the general layout. This matter necessitated a second trip to Tokyo on the part of the Governor General, resulting in what is believed to be an unqualified approval

=== Page 10 === (confidence 1.00)
-4-

approval by the Tokyo Government of resumption of work on the hydro-electric project as soon as the required capital is raised by a foreign loan or loans, estimated to aggregate a maximum of yen 45,890,000, to be negotiated through the agency of the Finance Department in Tokyo. Although only a formality, inasmuch as the Taiwan Government General is the largest stockholder, the question of raising this loan was discussed and approved at an extraordinary general meeting of stockholders on November 14, 1930.

While the amount of the loan might be raised in Japan, the high rate of interest on long time loans militates against such negotiation and therefore, in the first instance, the money is sought in foreign markets. Furthermore it is believed that the American money market was approached in the first instance because of the desire to create a gold credit in the United States and thus ameliorate the balance between imports and exports, both visible and invisible.

**The "Musha Affair".**

As little is known of Taiwan and as exaggerated rumors concerning the "Musha Affair" have no doubt raised some question in certain circles as to the advisability of investing in Taiwan, it is considered pertinent to discuss this situation briefly.

Lake Jitsugetsutan lies approximately 25 miles from Musha, a considerable distance when the nature of the terrain and the paths to be traversed are considered. Between Lake Jitsugetsutan and the rebellious tribes of the Musha area are a number of tribal units friendly to the Japanese, sufficient in number to form an effective buffer. These disaffected aborigines have known of the hydro-electric project for a number of years and, it is safe to presume, would

=== Page 11 === (confidence 1.00)
-5-

would have shown signs of discontent if this undertaking was or is a cause of uneasiness and discontent. Even in the catchment area where the means of living of a number of villages are in some degree menaced by the conservation methods to be enforced, there has been little or no trouble. The rebellious tribes of the Musha area are situate on the outer border of this catchment basin on the side away from the canal and tunnel leading to Lake Jitsugetsutan. As evidencing the general attitude, the savages who will be displaced by the raising of the level of the lake (in all, 27 families, approximately 120 persons) have cheerfully accepted the new sites granted them by the local government.

From what sources as are available, it would seem that there is absolutely no connection between the Musha uprising and the Lake Jitsugetsutan undertaking, in any of its ramifications. The Taiwan Electric Power Company attribute such rumors, as have reached the outside world concerning a connection between the two, to hasty and ill-advised press despatches on the part of persons having no knowledge of the local situation.

**Construction contracts and supplies.**

Construction contracts and purchases of supplies will be placed and made by the Taiwan Electric Power Company (Taiwan Denryoku Kabushki Kaisha), No. 1 Shoin-cho 1-chome, Taihoku, Taiwan.

With the development of the possibility that construction would be resumed, has come feverish activity on the part of Japanese contracting firms, each seeking the award of one or more of the various contracts to be let. Inasmuch as the Japanese Empire is now in the throes of a campaign to foster and protect home industries, it is more than likely that these contracts will be awarded to Japanese firms. A more recent

=== Page 12 === (confidence 1.00)
-6-

recent development in this situation is the pressure exerted by local firms to secure a great part, if not all, of the construction contracts, pleading the ill effects which the retrenchment policy has had upon their business. In view of the fact that the great part of the enterprises in Taiwan is under the control of the Taiwan Government, there is a great deal of truth in this plea and may have some bearing on the final awards. Petitions have been sent to the Taiwan Government General and similar representations to the company asking the exclusion of foreign and Japanese contracting firms. (By Japanese is meant firms of Japan Proper)

The greater part of the machinery required for the project has already been purchased, having been stored in various warehouses during the periods of suspension of construction. A considerable part of the income of the company, ordinary and extra-ordinary revenue, has been applied to the preservation of such supplies. By far the greater part of this equipment and machinery is of American manufacture, the investment therein being approximately yen 6,000,000, the next largest item consisting of supplies from Germany. However, a certain amount of machinery and supplies remain to be purchased and according to Mr. Yamanaka, Vice President of the Taiwan Electric Power Company, these will be obtained largely from the United States. Such purchases will not be made direct but will be negotiated through such importers as the Mitsui Bussan Kaisha, the Mitsubishi Trading Company, Okura &amp; Company, etc., all of which firms maintain offices in the United States (New York City) and with which offices American firms should negotiate.

According to information supplied by the company, construction will be resumed in 1931, pending the outcome of negotiations for loans.

General outline of the project.

In general, the Jitsugetsutan Project involves tapping the waters of the upper Dakusui-kei (Muddy Water River), fed by a catchment area of some 179 square miles, conducting the flow of water from the intake at Shimaigahara through open canals and tunnels to Lake Jitsugetsutan, this to be made into a reservoir by construction of dams at Suisha and Tosha, a pressure tunnel from the lake to Suirikei where the first power plant will be built, the flow from this unit to be diverted into a second pressure tunnel for utilization at a second power plant at the foot of Taihei Mountain or to be re-diverted into the lower stream of the Dakusui-kei for irrigation purposes.

As complementing this undertaking is the construction of a settling pool, 1,080 ft by 240 ft, between the intake and the lake. This feature is necessary in view of the heavy detritus carried by the waters of the river, a maximum of 1 part in 230 and a minimum of 1 part in 8384 (calculated on the basis of 216 cubic feet equalling 1 ton of solid matter), or an average of 1 part in 1000. The construction of such a pool will reduce the quantity of solid matter entering the lake to 1 part in 5000.

Five generators, each of 20,000 kilowatts, will be installed at the first plant, the second plant to be constructed and equipped as the necessity develops. At the terminals of these lines there will be transformer sub-stations, the capacity at Taihoku being 40,000 kilowatts, the capacity at Takao being 60,000 kilowatts.

Taiwan Electric Power Company Seeking Internal Loan.

Reference is made to the telegram from this office sent by United States Naval radio from Anoy China about October 27, 1932, to the effect that the Taiwan Electric Power Company is seeking a loan from the Tokyo government. The loan is for twenty million yen and the money, if procurable, is to be used to buy up the five and one half per cent gold debenture bonds of the company, which were floated on the American market in July 1931. A certain amount of these debentures are held in Japan by Japanese investors, and the company in order to reduce the heavy interest charges, which have become particularly burdensome due to the fall in yen exchange, has been negotiating with Tokyo since July of this year, for an internal loan at a low interest rate, the proceeds to be used to purchase such bonds as may be held in Japan, and the surplus if any to be used to buy the bonds available on the American market, which are believed to be currently quoted at a fifty to sixty per cent discount.

Details of the American loan were reported by this office on October 14, 1931 under the title "Side Lot for Construction Work on the Jitangetsutan Hydro-Electric Project".

Negotiations Conducted Secretly.

Negotiations have been going on very secretly and it is only lately that the plan became known, when the Japanese Minister of Communications, Mr. Minami, made a statement to the press, partially revealing the plans for aid to the large electric power companies in Japan, which have foreign loans. The Taiwan Electric Power Company, is believed to be the only large power company in the Japanese Empire, of which the foreign loan has been guaranteed by the Japanese government. Mr. Minami, the Minister of Communications in the Saito

Saito cabinet, was for a short period just prior to his appointment to his present post Governor-General of Taiwan, and he is therefore cognizant of and sympathetic with the situation facing the Taiwan company. The company, although nominally a private company is practically a semi-government enterprise, the Taiwan Government General being heavily interested in it financially, and to a certain extent exercising control.

**Interest Rate of New Internal Loan.**

It was originally planned to secure the loan of approximately twenty million yen from the Deposit Department of the Finance Department in Tokyo, but it is reported that the Japanese Government Industrial Bank was later approached. The internal loan will bear an interest rate of 2% to 3%, and if a substantial amount of the 5% gold debentures can be retired it will result in a great saving to the company in interest charges.

**Brief Review of Jitsugetsutan Project.**

The foreign loan of $22,500,000 was successfully negotiated in July 1931, and the proceeds of this loan are being used to construct a large hydro-electric plant of 100,000 kilowatt capacity at Lake Jitsugetsutan near the center of the island; the power to be generated will be sufficient, it is thought, to supply the needs of all Taiwan. Work, which originally was commenced in 1919 was suspended in 1922, due to financial difficulties, but after a survey by a firm of well known American* engineers in 1928, it was decided to go on with the project providing funds could be obtained. With the floating of the loan, this obstacle was overcome and in October 1931 active work began on the construction of the dam. As reported previously the contracting firms

firms are all Japanese or Taiwan companies, (no foreign construction companies being allowed to bid). The proceeds of the $22,500,000 loan of 1931 will be used for actual construction work, and for paying local importers of the (American) machinery (referred to).

**Present Progress of Construction.**

The work is estimated to take three years from the date of resumption on the project in October 1931, so that in October 1934 the plant is expected to be completed and in operation.

Work is reported to be progressing satisfactorily, and on September 20, 1932, 3,098 men were actively employed at the dam site. Cement for the dam is all locally manufactured, (being supplied from the Takao plant of the Asano Cement Company.) The total sum to be paid contractors amounts to yen 10,504,781 of which yen 3,216,530 was paid up to August 20, 1932 or 30.6% of the total. It is roughly estimated that on October 25, 1932 the construction work is 35% completed.
