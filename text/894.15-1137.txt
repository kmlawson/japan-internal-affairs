SIR:

I have the honor to transmit herewith under separate cover five copies each of two publications descriptive of the reconstruction of the city and harbor of Yokohama which was made necessary by the almost complete destruction of both in the great earthquake of September 1, 1923. It is believed that these booklets will be of interest both to the Department and to the Bureau of Foreign and Domestic Commerce as indicating the complete recovery of the city and harbor from the devastation caused by that great catastrophe. The new Yokohama and its harbor are in every way far superior to the old.

The first booklet, prepared by the City Office of Yokohama, consists only of a series of photographs of some of the important buildings, parks, streets and bridges constructed since the earthquake, while the second, issued by the Municipal Harbor Bureau, gives a fairly complete description of the reconstructed harbor works, accompanied by

No. March 20, 1936.

Sir:

Under instructions from my Government, I have the honor to request that you be good enough to transmit to the Government of the Philippines the following statement which has been made at the instance of that Government.

With regard to contracts for the construction of Government buildings and other public works undertaken by the Japanese Government, the regulations of Japan adopt the principle of free competition by means of public bidding as shown in the articles hereto attached. Thus any person, foreign or Japanese, meeting certain qualifications, may participate in the bidding, and there is, of course, no discrimination against the citizens of the United States or the Philippines.

Accept, Sir, the renewed assurances of my highest consideration.

The Honorable Cordell Hull,
Secretary of State.
Washington.
