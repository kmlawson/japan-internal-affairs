Dear Dr. Remer:

Sometime ago you asked me to give you a short memorandum on the properties in Korea held by the Japanese, saying that I might put it in a letter form.

On my return to New York office, I gave the matter considerable thought but found that I did not have sufficient help to get up a through study on the subject. However, I was able to secure some figures which were used as a bases for some other calculations and from those I have made up a short summary.

Any attempt to segregate the properties of the Japanese in Korea from those of the Koreans, is difficult. The Japanese have so integrated themselves into every phase of Korean economy that their property holdings make up items in every category of physical assets of the country. Moreover, Japanese corporate enterprises include investments of some Koreans-- estimated at about eleven to twelve percent. Accordingly, what is set down here as Japanese industrial investments in Korea is net after Korean participation has been deducted.

The tasks of even estimating certain kinds of property holdings of the Japanese is far more of a problem than it would be in other colonial set-ups where only the major industries are controlled by the alien rulers. The Japanese in Korea, on the contrary, have engaged themselves in all kinds of business -- from small hot cake vendors

GOVERNMENT PROPERTIES

For the purpose of this report, Korean properties have
been divided into two classes. Government and private. Gov-
ernment properties are taken to include the following :---

1) Agricultural and Forest lands taken from the former Korean
government in 1910, experimental stations, stock farms,
Monoply lands, buildings equipment, live stock etc.

2) Government railways consisting of some 3,250 miles of stand-
ard gauge lines together with the rolling stock, motove power,
repair shops, supplies, stations, warehouses, communication
systems, docks, hotels employees' residence etc etc.

3) Communication Facilities including telephone, telegraph and
submarine cable lines, radio stations post offices and all
equipment pertaining to these.

4) Monoply holdings consisting of tobacco stocks, warehouses
manufactured stocks, machinery, processing plants; Ginseng
and salt stocks and all physical properties used by the Bureau.

5) River and Harbor installations, jetties, wharves, custom
houses, air ports and all equipment and fixtures there belonging.

6) Military, police and Provincial offices including barracks,

PRIVATE PROPERTIES

For the purpose of this report, the values of private properties in Korea, as of 1936 is taken. Begaining 1937, War Economy was enforced in Korea and, investments by Japanese in Korea is considered War Spending and may not be considered as investments benefiting the economy of the country, as indeed very little was ever invested, except for war purposes. Koreans have always considered that the Japanese forced themselves into Korea by might of or -- at their own risk -- and since Koreans have always been at war with the Japanese, any property loss that individual Japanese may sustain in Korea should be considered as the fort out of war, just as any investor now in German occupied territories must take his chances of losing them when the Germans are driven out.

1) Farm and forestry lands. Japanese farm families in Korea number little over 10,000. Corporate and independent Japanese land holdings in 1936 were about 24% of the best rice land, 15% of other fields and a goodly proportion of the forest lands. The value of these holdings including buildings and appurtenances is estimated at about ¥1,132,000,000. It should be explained here that most of the best rice lands were acquired by the Japanese at low costs to them through forclosures on

high interest mortgages, sales of lands due to untenable irrigation projects forced upon the small farmers who could not meet assessments. Much of the forest land and upland farms were acquired by the Japanese through government grants, which favored Japanese, as for instance; 3,500,000 acres of forest lands were given out as timber claims, i.e. on the pretext of re-forestation, to Japanese colonists and large companies. Some 750,000 acres of other forest lands were arbitrarily given to Japanese settlers to turn into farms. According to the wording in the "Annual Report of the Government General of Chosen (Korea)", these were given to "Captains of Industry" for development. Lumbering industry is here included.

Most of the reclaimation projects, similar to the Shider Zee in Holland, where the sea water is dammed out, goes to the Japanese corporate interests. This kind of land should be valued on cost bases and the unearned increment should revert to the Korean people through Governmental allocation.

2) Korean Fisheries in 1936 had an output of ¥130,000.000 in value on an investment of about ¥15,000.000 in boats and equipment. Most of these were Japanese owned.

3) Manufacturing industries at the same time had an investment, excluding Korean participation, of ¥634,000,000.

4) Private Railways mostly narrow gauge lines totalling some 700 miles had Japanese investment of ¥54,000,000.

5) Mining has been well developed and had an investment of some ¥74,000,000 including stocks of metals on hand.

6) Gas and Electric undertakings including 60 miles of tramways, bus lines, Hydro-electric generating plants, transmission lines, transformers etc had an investment of ¥166,000.000 by 51 companies.

7) Private Banks and Loan Associations. The eleven Banks in Korea had paid up capital of ¥66,000.000 mostly held by Japanese, and Loan associations were capitalized at ¥11,500.000.

8) Small shops, commercial agencies, private houses, licensed quarters, unrecorded structures belonging to some 50,000 Japanese families are extimated to have cost ¥75,000.000.

Recapitulation: According to these estimates the total Japanese properties privately held, amounts to ¥2,227,500,000. as follows:

1. Agricultural and forest lands including buildings and the Lumbering industry — $1,132,000,000
2. Fisheries investment — 15,000,000
3. Manufacturing industries — 634,000,000
4. Private Railways — 54,000,000
5. Mining investments — 74,000,000
6. Electric and Gas undertakings — 166,000,000
7. Banks and loan associations — 77,500,000
8. Small shops, residences etc. — 75,000,000

Net Japanese holdings Excluding Korean participation — ¥2,227,500,000

Some writers have stated that in 1930 the Japanese investment in Korea was about ¥2,000,000,000. They estimate that it is far greater sum at present. The figures given here are based on 1936 government reports with Korean participation deducted. Also Government properties are not included.

You did not tell me Dr. Remer, what you were going to use these figures for, but I presumed they are for comparative purposes. If you would be good enough to let me have your comments and any changes that should be made on our estimates, I would appreciate it very much. You have access to figures which it is difficult for us to obtain, and it is important that our records be accurate.

Hoping that you will give me a word soon after you receive this, and with kind personal regards,

Yours very sincerely,
