JAPANESE NAVAL VESSELS IN KURILE ISLAND AREA - FISHERY
PATROL

For many years, the Japanese Navy has maintained a "fishery patrol" in northern waters during the fishing season. The purpose of this patrol is to protect the Japanese fishing fleet from the Russians, to assist fishing vessels which may get into difficulties, to familiarize officers and men of the Japanese Navy with the Kurile Islands and the Siberian coastal area, and to obtain information of Soviet activities. The patrol usually consists of 1-2 divisions of destroyers based on Ominato.

This year, according to the Soviet Naval Attache in Tokyo, the patrol has been greatly enlarged, and according to information which he has received from Siberia, the naval units known to be operating in the Kurile Island - Kamchatka area are as follows:

2 CL - Kuma Class
12 DD - Kamikaze - Akikaze Class
8 SS - I - 53 Class.

Several of these vessels have approached Russian naval operating areas, and have appeared in Russian territorial waters, but have always withdrawn when approached by Soviet aircraft or naval units. So far this year, there have been no incidents between the two forces.

The Soviets are unable to give any reason for the unusually large number of Japanese Naval vessels in the north this year since Soviet-Japanese relations in general appear to be improving. They believe that the Japanese are merely utilizing the summer months to familiarize more men with this area. However, they are very much on the alert for any unexpected Japanese moves, and are increasing their vigilance to prevent Japanese observations of strategic coastal areas.

In this connection, the Soviet Naval Attache expressed the opinion that Japanese submarines, and perhaps surface craft, were probably more numerous than usual in the Aleutian-Alaska area. He stated, however, that this was merely an opinion, and was not based on any definite information.
