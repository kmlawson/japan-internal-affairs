In accordance with Article 21 of the Convention of 1931 for limiting the Manufacture
and regulating the Distribution of Narcotic Drugs, the Secretary-General has the honour
to communicate herewith to the parties to the Convention the above-mentioned report.
The report is also communicated to other States and to the Advisory Committee on Traffic
in Opium and Other Dangerous Drugs.
(For the form of annual reports, see document O.C.16oo.)

A. General.

N.B.—As the Japanese Government abolished its extraterritoriality in "Manchukuo" on

(Foreign Office Ordinance No. 12, June 3oth, 1936) were thereby repealed.

II. Administration.
I. There is nothing to report.
2. Control of addicts.
The total number of drug addicts reported by physicians in accordance with the provisions
of Article XIX of the Regulations for the Control of Narcotic Drugs was 2, 3oo at the end of
1937. Principal drugs were such as morphine, pantopon and narcopon.
As stated in the report for 1936, these addicts were given medical attention by the pre-
fectural governments concerned in co-operation with local associations of physicians, dentists
or pharmacists, or of various social welfare organisations.
It may be added that the clinic established by the Metropolitan Police Bureau of Tokio
(Musashino Hospital), accommodating these addicts during the year under review (January-
December), took care of 531 patients, of whom 522 were fully cured, the average period of
their confinement being 12.63 days. As regards the Koreans who have been fully cured, they
are being sent back as far as possible to their native land.
III. Control of International Trade.
I. The system of import certificates or export authorisations proves effective for the
control of international trade, and is giving satisfaction.
2. The official responsible for issuing import certificates and export authorisations
continues to be the Minister for Home Affairs.
It being the policy of the Government not to authorise shipment to Japan Proper of goods
not destined to Japan Proper but for diversion elsewhere, no diversion authorisations are
issued. As for freight on transit, its control is exercised by requiring the import certificate
or diversion authorisation attached thereto to be shown to the Director of the Custom-house
upon arrival of the vessel at the port. No transit certificate or anything like it is issued.
(N.B.—Following the creation of the Ministry of Welfare on January 11th. 1938, import certi-
ficates and export authorisations are henceforth issued by the Minister for Welfare.)
IV. International Co-operation.
With regard to the smuggling affair the Heian Maru (vide V. Illicit traffic), the Japanese
Government, in conformity with the principles of the arrangement with the United States
Government in 1929 for the exchange of information relating to the seizure of illicit narcotic
drugs and to persons engaged in this traffic, sent Shinji Taniguchi, inspector in the Metro-
politan Police Bureau of Tokio, to the United States in September 1937, with a view to
making consultation with the American authorities on the subject matter.
V. Illicit Traffic.
I. The Government, maintaining close contact with the authorities concerned at various
ports, is doing its best to stop the practice by blacklisting habitual offenders, posting guards,
organising special police forces, keeping strict watch over those boarding or leaving ships,
and co-operating with shipping companies which have voluntarily inaugurated systems of
private detective service. Thanks to the rigid control exercised and the above-mentioned
efforts in particular, illicit trafficking is recently on the decline.
Further illicit traffic on board Japanese ships on the Pacific service has disappeared
subsequent to the case of the Heian Mariu. This was the case of illicit traffic in which twenty-
five pounds of morphine concealed in five casks of " shoyu " shipped for the United States
were discovered on board the Heian Maru, which had arrived at Seattle on March 31st, 1937.
The principal offender, Seiichi Amasaka, was arrested at Seattle by the United States
authorities on the spot. Simultaneously, a thorough-going enquiry was conducted by the
Metropolitan Police Bureau of Tokio, in co-operation with the Police Departments of Kanagawa
and Hyogo Prefectures. Consequently, the accomplice of the above-mentioned offender,
called Hamakichi Nonaka, was arrested at Dairen on May 29th, 1937, and was brought to the
Metropolitan Police Bureau, where the investigation of the matter was in progress. In
order to demonstrate the spirit of international co-operation for the suppression of illicit
traffic, the above-mentioned inspector of the Metropolitan Police Bureau, who had been
in charge of this affair, was sent by the Japanese Government to the United States with
a view to making necessary consultation on the matter.

C. Manufactured Drugs.
X. Internal Control of Manufactured Drugs.
1. (a) To manufacture drugs, sanction by the Minister for Home Affairs must first
be obtained. Accordingly, before sanction is granted, the authorities take into careful con-
sideration the quantities of the drugs needed for medical, scientific, conversion and export
purposes, as well as the required reserve stocks, with a view to limiting the total of such quantities
to the estimated quantities for home consumption.
Following the development of the present Sino-Japanese conflict, which broke out in
July 1937, and the subsequent occupation by the Japanese army of the wide area in China,
the manufacture of narcotic drugs has been more or less on the increase in order to meet the
demands in that area for legitimate medicinal purposes.

I). Other Questions.
XI. Chapter IV of the Hague Opium Convention of 1912.
Following the outbreak of the Sino-Japanese conflict in July, 1937, the Japanese Army
has virtually occupied a wide area of the north and central parts of China. Except Suiyen, the
area under occupation, particularly Central China, does not contain opium-producing land;

Southern China.

II. Administration.
I. (a) There is nothing specially to be mentioned under this head.
(b) The administrative authority responsible for the control of opium and narcotics
in Chosen is the Police Bureau of the Government-General, under which respective governors
of prefectures are carrying out a thorough-geing surveillance. Since January 1937, one
secretary and one expert have been added to the staff of the Bureau of Police.
(c) No difficulties have been encountered in the application of any of the Conventions.
2. The special administrative measures adopted by the Government for the control
of the abuses of narcotic drugs and the prevention of drug habit were described in the reports
for the years 1934 and 1935. These measures wete pursued during the year 1937, and,
strenuous efforts were made towards both cure and prevention. In the course of the year,
altogether 394 persons were admitted to various prefectural government clinics, of whom

V. Illicit Traffic.
I. Outline of illicit traffic.-Illicit traffic originates from the fact that non-licensed
smokers of prepared opium, who find it difficult to obtain Government-made prepared opium,
must necessarily purchase smuggled prepared opium or raw opium, of which the country
of origin is presumably China. The trade is generally carried on by Chinese and natives
of Taiwan, who bring in the contraband on junks to the open ports, Keelung, Takao, Tansui.
Ampei, etc., and dispose of it through intermediaries. But the illicit traffic is sometimes
carried on on board steamers by low-class seamen, who are natives of Taiwan, or is
discovered at the non-open ports, such as Koryu, which are frequented by junks.
Raw opium is imported for the purpose of clandestine manufacture of prepared opium,
for which discovery is made before being manufactured or after pursuing search into the
discovered prepared opium illicitly manufactured. In the latter case, many connected cases
of illicit traffic in the past may be traced.
This was the state of affairs prior to the outbreak of the Sino-Japanese conflict. Following
the outbreak of the present conflict, the junks which frequently plied between China and Taiwan
have completely disappeared. Illicit traffic by means of junks has accordingly disappeared.
It may be noted that, in the year under review, the number of cases, as well as the quantity
of illicit imports, was on the decline.
Illicit traffic in narcotic drugs, as shown in Table 3, containing the number of prosecutions
and convictions, is small, except in the case of morphine. Illicit traffic in morphine is carried
on simply to meet the local demands of addicts for narcotic drugs, the quantity of which is
extremely small in comparison with the large number of cases involved.
In the course of 1937, the purchase of narcotic drugs for medical treatment made by a
native medical practitioner in Taiwan was unusually large in quantity, so that he was suspected.
After careful investigation, it was found that the said medical practitioner, in collusion with
his employee, engaged in illicit traffic, in which the former made a profit of 8oo yen and the
latter 4oo yen. Apart from this, no important cases in illicit traffic in narcotic drugs were
reported.

the intercourse with China has been interrupted. with the consequent decrease of smuggling

A. GENERAL.
I. Laws and Publications.
It was provided by Notification No. 28 of the Ministry of Welfare
of April 1, 1938, that from April 1, 1938, the quantity of morphine
contained in opium and the amount of compensation to be given in
accordance with Art. 4 of the Opium Law shall be as follows:—Yen
2.80 per kilogramme of opium containing 1'; or more but less than
2': morphine;. for morphine exceeding 2';, Yen 2.80 shall be added
for each 2'.
II. Administration.
1. (a) There is nothing to report.
(b) With the establishment of the Ministry of Welfare on
January 11, 1938, all matters concerning opium and narcotic drugs
were placed in the charge of the Medical Section of the Public
Health Bureau of the Ministry of Welfare.
(c) Nil.
2. Control of Addicts.
The total number of drug addicts was 3,600 at the end of 1938.
Principal drugs were such as morphine, pantopon and narcopon.
As stated in the Report for 1936, these addicts are given medical
attention by the prefectural governments concerned in cooperation
with local associations of physicians, and of pharmacists, and various
social welfare organizations.
It may be added that the clinic established by the Metropolitan
Police Bureau of Tokyo (Musasino Hospital), accommodating these
addicts during the year under review (January—December), took
care of 316 patients, of whom 310 were fully cured, the average
period of their confinement being 15.62 days. As regards the Koreans

III. Control of International Trade.
1. The system of import certificates or export authorizations
proves effective for the control of international trade, and is giving
satisfaction.
2. The official responsible for issuing import certificates and
export authorizations is the Minister of Welfare.
It being the policy of the Government not to authorize shipment
to Japan Proper of goods not destined to Japan Proper but for di-
version elsewhere, no diversion authorizations are issued. As for
freight on transit, its control is exercised by requiring the import
certificate or diversion authorization attached thereto to be shown to
the Director of the Custom-House upon arrival of the vessel at the
port. No transit certificate or anything like it is issued.
3. There is no change in the conditions upon which import
certificates and export authorizations are issued.
4. Copies of export authorizations, as provided for in Art. 13
of the Geneva Treaty of 1925, are regularly returned on the whole.
5. No case is reported of forgery or alteration of import certi-
ficates or export authorizations.
6. No inconvenience has been experienced concerning tranship-
ment or bonded warehouses.
7. No transaction has taken place with a country which has
not adopted the system of import certificates.

In conformity with the arrangements made with the United
States and Great Britain for the exchange of information relating to
the seizure of illicit narcotic drugs and to persons engaged in this
traffic, the Japanese Government are exchanging information with
the Governments of these countries.

police forces, keeping strict watch over those boarding or leaving
ships, and co-operating with shipping companies which have volunt-
arily inaugurated systems of private detective service. Thanks to
the rigid control exercised and the above-mentioned efforts in parti-
cular, illicit trafficking is recently on the decline.

2. Main provisions of the above-mentioned regulations.
The amendments referred to in (a) and (b) headings are as fol-
lows:
Hitherto licensed opium retailers obtained raw opium from the
Monopoly Bureau and manufactured prepared opium from it, and as
a result prepared opium of inferior quality appeared on the market,
illicit sale was made, and led to illicit manufacture of narcotic drugs.
Therefore, in accordance with the provision of Art. 1, paragraph
two, of the First Opium Convention signed at Geneva on February
11th, 1925, monopoly of opium prepared by the Government was put
into force, according to which opium is manufactured by the Mono-
poly Bureau and the quantity allotted to each opium retailer is
limited to the minimum quantity required by the addicts. Thus
the illicit sale caused by the over-production of prepared opium is

=== Page 66 === (confidence 1.00)
prevented, and the opium addicts are prohibited to buy opium from
other retailers than the one designated for each addict, and the quantity
allowed for each addict is strictly fixed and stringent regulations are
enforced in regard to the disposal of dross and tubes. Hitherto the
rights to issue permit for smoking opium and license opium retailers
were vested in the Ambassador to Manchoukuo, but these rights
were transferred to the Governor of the Kwantung Leased Territory,
who is better versed in the knowledge of the state of affairs of the
region. The control of opium and its addicts is still under the
supervision of the Ambassador, and strict enforcement of control is
carried out with a view to exterminating opium addicts.
The amendment referred to in (c) is that while opium prepared
by the Kwantung Monopoly Bureau was formerly packed in tubes,
it has been amended and the opium is now packed in paper.

1. (a) Smuggling of opium and narcotic drugs was hitherto
prevented by cooperation between the Police and the Customs author-
ities. In order to enforce the prevention still more strictly, regula-
tions relating to the Kwantung Monopoly Bureau were amended by
the Imperial Ordinance No. 88 of February 19, 1938, and eight full-
time superintendents were newly appointed.
(b) The special administration referred to in Article XV of the
Limitation Convention of 1931, is in charge of the Department of
Judicial Affairs of the Kwantung Bureau.
(c) No difficulties were encountered in the application of any
of the Conventions.
2. The drug addicts treated by physicians were 1,454, of whom

prevention of smuggling having been tightened up, prepared opium
was made more difficult to obtain, and also to the fact that as regards
Japanese subjects, Korean immigrants have increased, and as regards
Manchurians and Chinese, many opium addicts have taken to the use
of narcotic drugs. Destitute persons among the addicts are treated
free of charge by the Police physicians at fixed periods.

SPECIAL ANNUAL REPORTS CONCERNING PREPARED
OPIUM FOR THE YEAR 1938.
I.
TAIWAN.
I. New Legislative and Administrative Measures taken during
the year 1938.
(1) Amendment of Ordinance.
There is nothing to be specially mentioned under this head.
(2) Administrative measures.
Two measures for opium smokers are in force, as described in
the previous Report, namely of cure by compulsory treatment at the
Taihoku House of Correction on the one hand, and of punishment of
offences on the other, besides special police surveillance exercised over
those suspected of opium-smoking.
The number of those who were ordered to undergo corrective
treatment at the Taihoku House of Correction during the year was
261.
Provisional measure was taken under which the natives of
Taiwan residing in China who came back to Taiwan owing to the
Sino-Japanese conflict and who are opium smokers are supervised as
opium addicts subject to correction. (Vide Annual Report for the
year 1938, Taiwan, A. General, II.)
(3) Working of the system of control.
The total number of licensed smokers at the end of 1938 was
10.884, showing a decrease of 1,179 as compared with the preceding
year. These licensed smokers are being put under close police sur-
veillance.
Clandestine smokers and consequent illicit traffic, not yet totally
eliminated, continue to exist in various localities. Control of these
cases is extremely difficult, but the utmost efforts are being exerted
toward rounding up the offenders through the co-operation of the
police and the customs authorities.
Owing to the outbreak of the present conflict, the intercourse with
China has been interrupted with the consequent decrease of smuggl-
ing of opium. Accordingly clandestine smoking and illicit traffic have
more or less decreased temporarily.

to helping them to control themselves not to recommence smoking.

is difficult to obtain opium owing to the strict control.

China Affair, a number of opium addicted Chinese have been deported

grated to Manchoukuo, some have been cured, and some have died,
with the result that at the end of 1938, there were altogether 21,373

2. Control of Addicts.
The total number of drug addicts was 3,6oo at the end of 1938 Principal drugs were
such as morphine, panitopon and narcopon.
As stated in the report for 1936,1 these addicts are given medical attention by the prefec-
tural governments concerned in co-operation with local associations of physicians, and of

It may be added that the clinic established by the Metropolitan Police Bureau of Tokio

their confinement being 15.62 days. As regards the Koreans who have been fully cured, they
are being sent back as far as possible to their native land.
III. Control of International Trade.
I. The system of import certificates or export authorisations proves effective for the
control of international trade, and is giving satisfaction.
2. The official responsible for issuing import certificates and export authorisations
is the Minister of Welfare.
It being the policy of the Government not to authorise shipment to Japan Proper of goods
not destined to Japan Proper but for diversion elsewhere, no diversion authorisations are
issued. As for freight on transit, its control is exercised by requiring the import certificate
or diversion authorisation attached thereto to be shown to the Director of the Custom-house
upon arrival of the vessel at the port. No transit certificate or anything like it is issued.
3. There is no change in the conditions upon which import certificates and export
authorisations are issued.
4. Copies of export authorisations, as provided for in Article 13 of the Geneva Treaty
of 1925, are regularly returned on the whole.
5. No case is reported of forgery or alteration of import certificates or export
authorisations.
6. No inconvenience has been experienced concerning transhipment or bonded
warehouses.
7. No transaction has taken place with a country which has not adopted the system of
import certificates.
IV. International Co-operation.
In conformity with the arrangements made with the United States and Great Britain for
the exchange of information relating to the seizure of illicit narcotic drugs and to persons
engaged in this traffic, the Japanese Government is exchanging information with the Govern-
ments of these countries.

private detective service. Thanks to the rigid control exercised and the above-mentioned
efforts in particular, illicit trafficking is recently on the decline.

crop is gradually decreasing.

2. Main provisions of the above-mentioned regulations.—The amendments referred to
in (a) and (b) headings are as follows :
Hitherto licensed opium retailers obtained raw opium from the Monopoly Bureau and
manufactured prepared opium from it, and, as a result, prepared opium of inferior quality
appeared on the market, illicit sale was made and led to illicit manufacture of narcotic drugs.
Therefore, in accordance with the provision of Article 1, paragraph 2, of the First Opium
Convention, signed at Geneva on February 11th, 1925, monopoly of opium prepared by the
Government was put into force, according to which opium is manufactured by the Mono-
poly Bureau and the quantity allotted to each opium retailer is limited to the minimum quantity
required by the addicts. Thus the illicit sale caused by the over-production of prepared
opium is prevented, and the opium addicts are prohibited to buy opium from other retailers
than the one designated for each addict, and the quantity allowed for each addict is strictly
fixed and stringent regulations are enforced in regard to the disposal of dross and tubes.
Hitherto the rights to issue permits for smoking opium and license opium retailers were
vested in the Ambassador to "Manchukuo", but these rights were transferred to the Governor
of the Kwantung Leased Territory, who is better versed in the knowledge of the state of affairs
of the region. The control of opium and its addicts is still under the supervision of the
Ambassador, and strict enforcement of control is carried out with a view to exterminating
opium addicts.
The amendment referred to in (c) is that, while opium prepared by the Kwantung
Monopoly Bureau was formerly packed in tubes, it is now packed in paper.

V. Illicit Traffic.
I. The Kwantung Leased Territory having much in common with " Manchukuo " and
China in its geographical, economic and racial aspects, and the traffic between Kwantung and
"Manchukuo " or China being quite free, a large number of Manchurians and Chinese travel

frequently fled into Kwantung, and they carried with them opium and narcotc drugs, thus
causing a great increase in the quantity of drugs smuggled. Moreover, since it was promul-
gated by the amendment of the Opium Ordinance in February 1938, that prepared opium
should be manufactured only by the Monopoly Bureau, a tendency has appeared for the
smuggled raw opium to increase. The superintendents of the Monopoly Bureau above
referred to are co-operating with the police and Customs authorities for the suppression of
smuggling, and the cases of illicit *raffic tend to decrease in number as mentioned in the later
part of the present report. The drugs handled in the illicit traffic bear no marks or labels,
and there is no way of ascertaining their origin. But, presumably, all the drugs are of clandes-
tine manufacture and of illegal origin.
Illicit traffic is mostly carried out through the channels of North or South China or
"Manchukuo" by smugglers who carry the contraband goods on their persons or sewed up
in their clothes.

SPECIAL ANNUAL REPORTS CONCERNING PREPARED OPIUM
I. TAIWAN
I. New Legislative and Administrative Measures taken during the Year 1938.
(1) Amendment of Ordinance.—There is nothing specially to be mentioned under this head.
(2) Administrative Measures.—Two measures for opium-smokers are in force, as described
in the previous report—namely, cure by compulsory treatment at the Taihoku House of
Correction on the one hand and punishment of offences on the other, besides special police
surveillance exercised over those suspected of opium-smoking.
The number of those who were ordered to undergo corrective treatment at the Taihoku
House of Correction during the year was 261.
Provisional measure was taken under which the natives of Taiwan residing in China
who came back to Taiwan owing to the Sino-Japanese conflict and who are opium-smokers
are supervised as opium addicts subject to correction (vide annual report for the year 1938,
Taiwan, A. General, II).
(3) Working of the System of Control. —The total number of licensed smokers at the end
of 1938 was 10,884, showing a decrease of 1,179 as compared with the preceding year. These
licensed smokers are being put under close police surveillance.
Clandestine smokers and consequent illicit traffic, not yet totally eliminated, continue
to exist in various localities. Control of these cases is extremely difficult, but the utmost
efforts are being exerted towards rounding up the offenders through the co-operation of the
police and the Customs authorities.
Owing to the outbreak of the present conflict, the intercourse with China has been
interrupted, with the consequent decrease of smuggling of opium. Accordingly, clandestine
smoking and illicit traffic have more or less decreased temporarily.
II. New Activities during the Year against the Use of Prepared Opium.
There is nothing to be mentioned.
III. Scientific Research.
The researches mentioned in the previous report were continued as follows :
(1) Statistical enquiry into opium-smokers in Taiwan (third report) by Dr. Sōmei To;
(2) Effect caused in the catarrase of the blood and internal organs of a rabbit by
opium aikaloid (second report) by Dr. Syōren Tyo.
IV. Other Information.
(1) Compulsory Treatment and After-care of Addicts.—As regards the corrective measures
for opium-smokers as described above, a closer connection is being maintained with local police
authorities, with a view to preventing persons cured at the Taihoku House of Correction from
resuming the habit of smoking opium and to helping them to control themselves not to recom-
mence smoking.
(2) Statistical Classification of Opium-smokers.—There has been no great change in the
classification of opium-smokers from that mentioned in the report for the year 1933.1
(3) System of Control of Dross and Disposal of Dross.—Dross is destroyed by the licensed
smokers according to the direction of the police officers who have inspected. Where analysis
of dross is considered necessary, research is being made by experts.
(4) Illicit Traffic.—During the year under review, 6,877 grammes of prepared opium,
19,95o grammes of raw opium, 221 grammes of morphine and 183 grammes of other narcotic
drugs were confiscated (vide annual report for the year 1938, Taiwan, A. General, V).
(5) Narcotic Drugs used as Substitutes for Prepared Opium.—There is a tendency of mor-
phine being used as a substitute, as it is difficult to obtain opium owing to the strict control.

been cured, and some have died, with the result that at the end of 1938, there were altogether
