Discussion Japanese Newspapers in China:

In response to protests from Japanese newspapers and correspondents in China against what they allege is unwarrantable interference from the Chinese authorities, the Foreign Office is understood to have despatched instructions to the Japanese Legation in China to negotiate with the Nationalist Government to settle the matter. The newspapermen charge that the Chinese authorities have illegally attacked or seized Japanese newspapers and banned their mailing. They are also alleged to have obstructed communication of Japanese correspondents, even forbidding the Rengo News Agency from using either telegraphs or telephones in Chinese territory. The Chinese state that they have been led to interfere because of the unfairness of Japanese interpretation of Chinese events. The dispute reached serious proportions with an attack on the Nanking office of the Tokyo Nichi Nichi Shimbun and the wounding of Mr. Kusazo Chihara, correspondent of the Asahi Shimbun. On April 18th a protest was filed with the Chinese Minister in Tokyo by a group of newspaper representatives.

DEPARTMENT OF STATE
DIVISION OF FAR EASTERN AFFAIRS

December 27, 1932.

MEMORANDUM

MANCHURIA SITUATION

Misinformation issued by Japanese News Agencies in China.

Reference: Peiping's telegrams No. 1321 of December 13, No. 1332 of December 15, No. 1350 of December 17, and No. 1367 of December 21, 1932.

Although anti-American propaganda in Japan has recently lessened, due largely, the Embassy at Tokyohas reported, to influence exerted by Ambassador Debuchi during his recent visit there, telegraphic reports from the Legation at Peiping indicate that Japanese news agencies in China are now issuing apparently unfounded reports which may tend to revive ill-feeling. These reports relate principally to alleged American activities in connection with the Chinese military; namely, alleged sales of armaments to Chinese leaders and alleged efforts to gain influence through employment of American officers in Chinese forces.

As typical of some of the news items published and disseminated in English by the Japanese information bureau, the Legation has reported the following examples: purchase of thirty airplanes and thirty anti-aircraft guns from the United States by Chang Hsueh-liang; purchase of twenty anti-aircraft

aircraft guns from the United States by Chu Ching-lang, alleged director of the activities of the "Volunteers" in Manchuria, and pending contract by Chu for twenty airplanes; purchase from the United States by the Nanking Government of twenty airplanes and other war material; anticipated arrival of more airplanes and war material; decision of Chinese military at Nanking to engage more American instructors of aviation and chemical warfare; and employment of Americans for study of poison gas and smoke screens in the Military Chemical Institute at Nanking. The Legation has also reported that the Rengo (Japanese) News Agency published a story to the effect that, according to an unconfirmed report, China has obtained a loan of $15,000,000 (gold) from the United States for the manufacture in this country of 500 airplanes, in return for which the United States will be allowed the use of two aviation bases in China in time of war.

The report with regard to the use by the United States of two aviation bases in China in time of war is obviously ridiculous. With regard to the employment of American instructors, the Department has knowledge only of a group of thirteen Americans engaged by the Chinese Government to assist in the organization of an efficient Department of Aviation. These Americans

Americans have no official status. According to figures supplied by the Department of Commerce, the value of exports of war material from the United States to China during the first ten months of 1932, exclusive of raw cotton, was only $391,155, of which amount $277,903 was for twenty-two airplanes, five of which have not yet been delivered, and $1,560 for twelve machine and heavy ordnance guns and carriages. From these figures it would appear that the foregoing press reports are exaggerated and misleading. During the same period, the value of exports of war material from the United States to Japan, exclusive of raw cotton, was $270,509, not significantly less than the value of the war materials exported to China. With reference to the sale of war materials by the United States to Japan and China, Japan rather than China is favored, for the reason that there are no restrictions in respect to the sale of war materials to Japan while the sale to China is restricted by regulations based on the President's proclamation of March 4, 1922. With regard to the alleged loan of $15,000,000, the Consul General at Nanking has reported that an employee of the Ministry of Finance characterized this story as highly improbable.

It is suggested that the information contained in this memorandum might be brought informally to the attention of Ambassador
