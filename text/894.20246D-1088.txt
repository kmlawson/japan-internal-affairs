Subject: Japanese Spies at Singapore.

The despatch below, dated December 9th, is printed in "The Naval and Military Record" of London of December 13th, under the heading "Grave Espionage Revelations: More Japanese Deported from Singapore. Governor's Appeal".

In connection with this despatch which follows, it appears that these revelations will come rather inopportunity for the British element which advocates a return to the Anglo-Japanese alliance, and that the revelations must have a ridiculously disconcerting effect on the Japanese mission in London which is playing golf with British statesmen and attempting to smooth the way of diplomatic negotiation. The despatch follows:

"Revelations following upon the drive against spies in Singapore, where the large scale Navy, Army, and Air Force maneuvers begin on December 20th, are stated to have proved "so sensational that to make them public might have political repercussions".

"Sir Shenton Thomas, the Governor of the Straits Settlements, has appealed to a conference of Press representatives whom he called together to refrain from publishing any further statements regarding the alleged Japanese espionage story.

"Officials in Whitehall are in possession of the available facts, and any further statement must come from them. The police here still refuse to make public the results of the post-mortem examination on Mr. Nishimura, president of the Singapore-Japanese Association, who dropped down dead at the police station when about to be questioned by C.I.B. officers. He was buried last Tuesday, December 11th.

"Two Japanese residents of Singapore, who were detained by the police following investigations by secret service men, are being deported. They will thus follow the two men, who were first detained by the police when they arrived here from Europe, but were later allowed to leave by cattle head. These last two men were the Japanese who called on Mr. Nishimura, who was the representative of a big Japanese concern controlling iron works in Hawaii.

"This latest deportation follows a raid on a Japanese hotel, and is the most recent phase of an intense enquiry among the Japanese in Singapore. The enquiry is believed to be connected with espionage, and possibly espionage connected with the Singapore defense.

On December 5, 1934, Mr. Yoshio Nishimura, a well known Japanese resident of Singapore, aged about 41 years, was called to the Police Station and upon arrival he collapsed while talking to a Police Inspector and died shortly afterwards. According to newspaper report Mr. Nishimura called at the police station in connection with inquiries concerning a certain trunk which was allegedly left in his care by two Japanese travellers on an N.Y.K. boat. It is said these travellers were interrogated by the police while in Singapore, but they were allowed to proceed. Previous to this account the "SINGAPORE FREE PRESS" of December 8, 1934, stated that

Two Japanese have been detained by the police in Singapore. Their names are given as Mr. Kokubo, an engraver, of Bencoolen Street, and Mr. Ohki, sub-editor on the staff of the Singapore Japanese newspaper the Nippo.

This article goes on to say that "it is stated that the two men are being detained in custody pending a private enquiry with a view to their leaving the country on banishment warrants.

The Coroner of Singapore rendered an open verdict of death by poisoning and stated that the evidence showed that the poison was probably self administered but it was not sufficient to justify a finding of suicide.

The Japanese Consul General told the writer that Nishimura undoubtedly committed suicide; that he had got mixed up with two bad men, but did not say just what the bad men were doing in Singapore or what they did to Nishimura in order to make him commit suicide.

Japanese Spy Case in Singapore - Intelligence Service Throughout the World

It is learned from a reliable source that the "well known and highly respected" Japanese referred to in this article from Singapore was the head of the Ishihara Trading Company branch in Singapore, and was also the head of the Japanese intelligence service in that place. He is now known to have had some 20 operatives under him, and he had in the course of several months obtained complete plans and photographs of the Singapore Naval Base. He was planning to send these to Japan by two agents who were passing through Singapore, and he made an appointment to meet these agents at a Japanese inn. On his way to this appointment, he left a small handbag containing all of the plans and photographs in a taxicab in which he was riding.

The driver, on discovering the bag, delivered it to the police station as a "lost article". It was opened there, and as soon as its contents were known, the case was turned over to the intelligence police. The Japanese owner's name was soon discovered, and he was called to the police station for questioning. Fearing that he might in the course of being questioned, reveal much that would harm the Empire, he took poison and died in the police office. However, the police had been able, through the documents, to learn all about the Japanese organization in Singapore.

As a result of this incident the British in Singapore have passed regulations closing parts of the settlement to visitors, restricting the landing of foreign sailors, etc.

Little has been published in Japan in regard to this case and it is learned that the two Japanese agents who were to act as messengers were landed in police boats at Kobe before the vessel in which they were embarked had docked.

It is believed that while the Japanese were making every effort to obtain the details of the Singapore Naval Base, and therefore had a larger number of agents in the Malay Straits than in other places, that similar intelligence organizations—largely civilians—exist throughout the world. It is noteworthy that the Japanese involved was the head of a large and reputable firm, and that he was, at the same time, the head of the intelligence organization.

DETAILS OF THE SINGAPORE SPY MYSTERY

Photographs of the New Naval Base Discovered by Secret Police
From Our Own Correspondent

Singapore, Dec. 18.

There has been more than enough to occupy our minds down here of late - entirely apart from our frenzied preparation for Christmas and for snatching a few brief days's leave. The sudden death of a prominent Japanese, a rumor that photographs of our Naval Base have been taken by unauthorized persons, fleet manoeuvres including what has come to be known as the Battle of Singapore, and finally a majestic showing of the flag to the peoples of this island by the Navy. These things have certainly provided food for thought for those among us who look beyond the things of to-day and fear the possibilities of the future.

Why Did He Take Struchnine?

We were startled to read that a well-known and highly respected Japanese resident, had fallen down dead in a local police office whither he had come to answer certain questions. The inquest was lengthy and the Coroner found that the poison had been self-administered but could not say why. Certain incidents that have transpired since, however, and which have not been published, seem to show why all too clearly. The dead Japanese was the leader of his community in certain matters. Some of his friends were under suspicion. He was to be questioned about them. Rather than face the inquiry, it is now alleged, he took poison and so died. As a result of his death we have noted various phenomena which seem to show that there is a good deal that only the secret police know. One rumor that is circulating is that the police have now got hold of a series of unauthorized photographs of the Naval Base, but that so far they have not been able to get hold of the negatives for these photographs. This is undoubtedly a serious matter. Very significant, too, is the fact that at a certain local Club here, a Club where there are a great many Japanese members (as well as members of all other races), there has been a notable absence of all Japanese since the alleged suicide. It is openly being said that the dead Japanese gentlemen controlled a large body of his countrymen who owe allegiance to something higher than any more local interest. If all this is true it is no wonder that we are glad of the presence in our midst of British warships.

Battle of Singapore

During the past week-end there have been combined manoeuvres between the various defensive and attacking branches of our local forces together with the ships that have been temporarily stationed here from the China Station. Few of us have seen aught of these manoeuvres, nor has it been possible to read much about them; some information has, however, been gleaned, and it would seem that while the attackers successfully landed on this island, it is not at all sure what would have happened under conditions of actual war. Most of us only knew that manoeuvres were in progress if we happened to live by the coast and were kept awake all night by the drone of planes overhead. Others of us looking out to sea on a clear night might have seen tiny fireflies in the distance - planes setting out to their appointed tasks. The Volunteers, of course, had all about flying spent a very strenuous time, and nowadays the Volunteer certainly earns the respect of us all on account of

his manifold activities week in and week out.

## Navy Shows the Flag

Finally, when the battles had died down, when we had recovered somewhat from the spate of rumours, we were privileged to see the Navy as Singapore has seldom seen it before. Usually we merely see our warships as they lie at anchor far out in their special anchorage. There is nothing of nearness, of familiarity, about the presence of these ships. It was arranged, however, that on Sunday morning last H.M.S. Cumberland should head a procession of Destroyers through the narrows which separate Singapore from one of its nearest satellite islands, and thousands of us turned up to see the unique spectacle. Many were rather disappointed at the apparent smallness of these vessels of war. They would have preferred a procession of ships of the Queen Mary type I gather! But to those of us who understood at any rate something of what we were looking the sight was both memorable and impressive, and it was a tossup whether those who were on a level with the warships saw better than those of us who had taken up our places on Mount Faber, a hill that commands every side of Singapore island. The majesty of that slow steam-past will always remain in my mind, that allied to the peaceful setting of the whole affair. One hoped and prayed that this was how we might always see such ships of war. We have no desire to test their martial qualities!

## Air Announcements

Here is an item of news that may or may not be true, but which taken in conjunction with what I have been writing already is surely highly significant. Says a local paper: "A message from Tokyo states that preparations are being made for the establishment of the Japanese Zeppelin services to Manchoukuo and Singapore and the Netherlands East Indies, and that the services will begin next year. Of the L 2,000,000 capital of the company, it was stated that L 1,500,000 had already been subscribed."

In many ways we should welcome a closer link with China and Japan. Nowadays we can send our letters to Australia by air, and our letters to Europe by air. A letter to you, however, still takes as long as ever it did. But, will the British authorities allow this new service to operate within our areas? Shall we not try and do something about it ourselves?

One asks this question because of the very significant pronouncement of our new Governor, Sir Shenton Thomas, recently when he was speeding on its way the newly-opened air link with Australia. "The day of local air services", he said, "is not far off. I predict that before long we shall be sending our local letters by air, and I shall do all I can to make this possible, because if we do not provide the service ourselves, others will forestall us." I happen to know that all details of such a local air service, especially for the carriage of mails, have long ago been worked out. Does H.E.'s pronouncement mean that now, at long last, these pigeon-holed plans are to be seen in full daylight? We all devoutly hope so.

## BACKGROUND

Since the outbreak of Sino-Japanese hostilities in 1937, Singapore Island has become the stage of a bitter propaganda contest between Japanese agents on the one hand and a combined communist-Chinese patriotic movement on the other. Essentially, communist and Japanese aims are similar in that they envisage the eventual overthrow of British power in Malaya, the one through a communist anti-Imperialist revolution throughout Malaysia and India and the other by the union of Asiatic peoples under Japanese leadership and control.

In both cases ultimate objectives are subordinated to the exigencies of the war in China, the outcome of which may well determine which of the two ideologies will have a free hand in its activities. A Japanese victory in China would involve a definite set-back to communist influence and serve as the basis for continued southward expansion on the ideological lines set forth above. On the other hand a Chinese victory would remove the menace of a Japanese-controlled Pan-Asiatic movement, leaving the field clear for communism with its prestige enhanced through cooperation in the ultimate victory.

The significance of these factors with respect to Singapore Island is indicated by the fact that over 77x percent of its population is made up of Chinese who also comprise over 40x percent of the inhabitants of British Malaya

## COMMUNISM

Accordingly, the small but highly organized Malayan Communist Party has identified itself with Chinese nationalism, acquiring control of various patriotic societies with specific view to intensifying the anti-Japanese boycott and securing contributions for the 8th Route Army in China.

This control is exercised through domination of the "Malayan Overseas Chinese All Circles Anti-Enemy Backing-Up Society", an anti-Japanese organization made up of various subsidiary groups with an estimated total membership of 30,000. Facilitating this move is the fact that many of the subsidiary groups are the outgrowth of communist-sponsored organizations whose names and objectives were revised to attract adherence of patriotic Straits Chinese. The organizational evolution is illustrated in Table I, /-

The labor movement, largest and most important of communist subsidiaries, has been virtually transformed into a collection agency for the 8th Route Army in China. In the desire to assure a steady stream of funds, labor disputes leading to a cessation of work and income, while not actively discouraged, do not receive any positive stimulus from communist sources. However, strikes, once initiated, have received support as in the cases of the recent Traction Company, X Rickshaw Pullers, XX and F.M.S. Railway XXX strikes. The Vocational Workers Backing-Up Society, as it is now called, has considerable influence in most lines of trade, and is reported to have a membership of 20,000 in Singapore alone.

Cooperation of patriotic Chinese youth has been attracted through transformation of the "Malayan Communist Youth", formerly a training school for the Communist Party, into an anti-Japanese society actively engaged in arousing patriotic consciousness and aiding the Chinese cause even to the extent of sending and equipping military volunteers. The success and widespread character of the movement is indicated by the adherence of nearly all clerks and organizations in Malaya serving Chinese youth.

The former Communist Party Special Branch, now the Special Service Corps of the Backing-Up Society, plays an important although not public part in the Society's activities. It gathers information about and takes action against those dealing in Japanese goods or who are otherwise considered lukewarm to China's cause. When threats do not succeed, resort is often had to destruction of property and to the instigation of riots against offending persons. Chinese merchants are not infrequently blackmailed into contributions by the threat of being branded as unpatriotic. It is not uncommon to see in the Chinese press advertisements by merchants denying accusations against their patriotism and proving it by publicizing substantial contributions toward China relief.

To counteract such activity the Straits Settlements and Federated Malay States authorities have recently issued instructions that all relief funds must apply for registration under the Societies Ordinance. Under this Ordinance societies are not permitted knowingly to receive any donation which has been offered directly or indirectly as an inducement to anyone not to interfere with the person, property or vocation

Controlling the whole structure is the Malayan Communist Party Central whose membership is also that of the "Head Association" of the Anti-Enemy Backing-Up Society in Singapore. Through its authority over the Communist Party organizations in each of the Malay States which have similar relationships to the local branches of the Backing-Up Society, this control is centralized throughout British Malaya.

The recent arrest and exposure as communists of five of the twelve members of the Head Association is believed by the Singapore Police to have seriously affected the Party's domination of anti-Japanese activities. Not only do the remaining seven members find it difficult to direct the Society's activities while in hiding, but many non-communist Chinese leaders are believed ready to withdraw their support of the movement now that its communistic character has been revealed.

At the same time, however, the significance of this change in communist policy cannot be minimized. Police activities notwithstanding, the close identification of communism with Chinese national aspirations, both in China and Malaya, assures a certain sympathetic receptivity to communist doctrines, thus making the movement an important factor for the future.

# JAPANESE PROPAGANDA

Placed on the defensive by the communist-Chinese nationalist propaganda and visualizing the eventual strategic as well as economic advantages to be secured from the development among Malayan Chinese of a favorable attitude to Japanese aspirations, Japanese undercover activity in the Straits Settlements has been diverted from espionage to an intensive propaganda campaign among the Chinese. As already indicated, the objectives are first to break the Japanese boycott and stop the flow of contributions to the Chinese National Government and thereafter the development of a sympathetic attitude to Japanese Pan-Asianism with particular view to the growth of anti-British sentiment.

Apparently underlying this emphasis and propaganda is the feeling that Chinese nationalism is an ephemeral phenomenon; and that local Chinese leaders, most of them paying only lip service to anti-Japanism in deference to prevailing sentiment, are more alarmed over the potentialities of communistic influence than of Japanese control, and can eventually be purchased. With respect to those whose patriotism is considered genuine and who cannot be bought, attempts are made to utilize the traditional Chinese distrust of their leaders, and to discredit them. x

x As an example, my informant cited the following case concerning which he had definite information from an undercover agent. A well-known and respected member of the Chinese community whom he knew quite well was being cultivated by a formerly estranged brother now secretly in Japanese pay. They are constantly seen together in public and the impression given that they are friendly not only socially but in business matters. According to the undercover agent, the brother will in the near future order a cargo of Japanese goods of which the character will be divulged upon arrival, thus calling forth an anti-Japanese boycott demonstration. The brother will then flee Singapore, supposedly in fear of reprisals from the local Chinese, and suspicion will fall on the leader in question as being a partner in the transaction, thus thoroughly discrediting him with his people despite denials and proof to the contrary.

In addition to subversive activities among local Chinese leaders, considerable sums of money are expended on written propaganda originating in South China and Singapore. Pro-Japanese literature published in Chinese by the South China puppet governments is distributed through the Japanese Consulate General in Singapore while anti-Chinese Nationalist Government material attacking Chiang Kai-shek and other Chinese leaders as traitors in Communist pay is secretly printed by local agents and distributed surreptitiously among the Chinese masses.

Designed for Europeans and the better-educated Chinese are the Singapore Nippo and the Eastern News, both daily publications presenting news from the Japanese point of view (chiefly Domei), and distributed gratis on an unsolicited basis. Plans are now being made for the publication of another Japanese-subsidized newspaper to be called The Singapore Herald and edited by a young American* of Japanese blood, who had formerly served in Seattle as Secretary to the present Japanese Consul General at Singapore, Issaku Okamoto.

To avoid the impression of biased reporting given by the Singapore Nippo and Eastern News, both served by Domei, efforts are being made to utilize non-Japanese news services such as Reuters or possibly the Associated Press.

That the large sums expended on propaganda have had no appreciable success in checking nationalistic enthusiasm among the Malayan Chinese is indicated by the 70 percent drop in 1938 imports of Japanese goods and the constantly expanding volume of contributions to Chinese armies.

However, local Police and Military officials, not at all confident that anti-Japanese sentiment among the Straits Chinese would outlast a final victory in China by the Japanese forces, envisage with concern the possibility of ultimate cooperation in Malaya between the two peoples. The danger of such a development to the security of Singapore is indicated by the predominantly Chinese character of the population of Singapore Island. It is believed that Japanese propaganda has as its ultimate aim the development of anti-British sentiment, which can be achieved only after present antagonism to Japanese aims in China is dissipated.

As stated by my informant, definite proof of such intentions would justify the Colonial Office in permitting the Singapore Police to take drastic action against the whole Japanese undercover structure. In view of the intensive Chinese propaganda, contributions to the Chinese Nationalist Government and Japanese boycott, proof only of counter Japanese propaganda would not be considered sufficient by the Colonial Office to permit drastic measures.

Accordingly, having what they considered sufficient information to believe that local Japanese were actually fostering anti-British sentiment among the Chinese, the Special Branch Police, Political, on January 18, 1939, made a series of raids on Japanese hotels, associations and business houses known to be active centers of Japanese propaganda. The results were negative in so far as proof regarding anti-British propaganda was concerned, but considerable material was collected throwing light upon the organization and methods used in local Japanese propaganda activities.

# STRICTLY CONFIDENTIAL

## REVIEW OF COMMUNISM: THE MALAYAN COMMUNIST PARTY.
(Prepared by Nigel G. Morris, Chief of the Special Branch, Political, of the Straits Settlements Police.)

### 1. General Policy

The policy of the Malayan Communist Party has not been altered since the last meeting of the Standing Advisory Committee was held in January 1938. The Anti-Japanese policy has been pursued and nearly all the branches of the Party such as the Malayan General Labour Union and the Malayan Communist Youth have altered their titles in order to emphasise the single-mindedness of their aims, and to camouflage their identities under the patriotic national salvation movement. The Malayan Communist Party Central, has taken full control of an organisation known as the Malayan Overseas Chinese All Circles Anti-Enemy Backing-Up Society (name in Chinese characters) or the "Federation of Overseas Chinese Anti-Japanese Associations". The objects of this organisation are to collect money for the 8th Route Army in China, to intensify the boycott by all means at its disposal, and to educate the masses to take an active part in national salvation work. This organisation apparently advocates Anglo-Chinese friendship and urges all overseas Chinese not to violate the laws of the country; but actually it resorts to various forms of intimidation of Chinese who are suspected of dealing in Japanese goods, and to acts of violence against other so-called "Traitors" and "Trotskyists" who do not follow the orders of the organisation blindly.

### 2. Personnel and Strength

The membership of the Malayan Communist Party is reported to have increased considerably during the last eight months, but this is not strictly accurate. The Malayan Communist Party now controls an organisation whose branches are numerically far stronger than those controlled by the Party heretofore, but the new members are mostly persons who believe in the United Front against Japanese aggression, but who do not necessarily agree with the fundamental principles of the Malayan Communist Party (i.e. Revolution in South Seas). In Communist Party secretarial records the names of the members of the Backing-Up Society, and the names of the Party are kept distinct and separate, and no members are admitted to the inner ranks unless they believe in the communist ideology itself. The numerical strength of the Malayan Communist Party does not therefore show a great increase, but whereas it formerly influenced a number of societies which were designated "grey" organisations, it now uses the "Backing-Up Society", and through it exercises a "controlling" influence over many of its subsidiary branches.

The actual numerical strength of the Malayan Communist Party and the Anti-Enemy Backing-Up Society in Malaya is not available, but from Malayan Communist Party Secretarial Records which have come into the hands of the Special Branch it is estimated that the membership of the Malayan Communist Party is about 500, and that the total membership of the Anti-Enemy Backing-Up Society is about 30,000.

3. Activities
(1) Malayan Communist Party Central Committee. This Central Committee has continued to direct the activities of all its subsidiary organisations, and has been responsible for remitting the funds collected by the Backing-Up Society, to the 8th Route Army in China. Since the last meeting the

Party has not maintained its liaison with the Communist Parties of Siam, Burma, and Indo-China, but has been in constant touch with the Southern Committee of the China Communist Party in Hong Kong. A representative of Malayan Communist Party Central was recently sent to Hong Kong with a full report on the Malayan Communist movement. This report was handed over to a responsible member of the China Communist Party for despatch to Moscow. The Malayan Communist Party Central Committee is awaiting the reply. Documents and pamphlets signed by Malayan Communist Party Central have been issued on communist anniversaries, but as a general rule Central has remained in the background during the last few months.

(ii) Malayan Communist Youth. (Malayan Overseas Chinese Juveniles Anti-Enemy Backing-Up Society). Formerly the membership of Malayan Communist Youth was small, and it was only the training school for the Malayan Communist Party. The new Juveniles Backing-Up Society has attracted an enormous number of youths of all classes, and except for the Vocational Workers Branch, this is now the most active and numerically the strongest of all the branches of the Backing-Up Society. The patriotic sentiments expressed by the Society have appealed to young Chinese all over the country, and nearly all the clubs and associations which cater for Chinese youths, have established branches of the Anti-Enemy Backing-Up Society.

(iii) Malayan General Labour Union. (The Malayan Overseas Chinese Vocational Workers Anti-Enemy Backing-Up Society). The chief function of this branch has been the collection of funds for China relief purposes, but it has not neglected the cause of the labourers. In the rules of the organisations it is specifically stated that it strives for the improvement of the livelihood of the masses, and it has found this a little difficult to reconcile with its policy of co-operation between capitalist and labourer so that labour disputes shall not interfere with the work of collecting money for China relief. None of the recent strikes in Singapore can be attributed directly to Malayan Communist Party agitation or to the Anti-Enemy Backing-Up Society, but, when labour disputes have occurred, the Communist Party and the Backing-Up Society have given their moral support to the strikers, and in the case of the Traction Company strike considerable active help was given. By this method the Backing-Up Society assists the labourers and at the same time ostensibly adheres to its policy. The Vocational Workers Backing-Up Society is ubiquitous and wields enormous influence. There is no trade in which a branch has not been established. The actual strength is difficult to determine, but it claims to have 20,000 adherents in Singapore alone.

(iv) Malayan Proletarian Writers Association. This name has not come to the notice of the Special Branch during 1938, and its members have been engaged on national salvation propaganda work. Several bodies have applied for registration such as the "Chinese Writers Association" and the "Chinese Journalists Association", many of whose members were formerly members of the Malayan Proletarian Writers Association. Ex-members are also found in the ranks of registered Dramatic Societies.

(v) Malayan Racial Emancipation League. This name has fallen into disuse, although the Party still makes strenuous efforts to recruit non-Chinese members. There is an Indian Branch in Singapore which has direct contact with Malayan Communist Party Central and is reported to have 300 members. There is also an Indian Branch in Malacca.

(vi) Malayan Communist Party Special Branch. (Special Service Corps of the Overseas Chinese All Circles Anti-Enemy Backing-Up Society). Formerly the Special Police of the Communist Party, this is now a most important branch of the Backing-Up Society. It is responsible for collecting information concerning persons trading in Japanese goods, daubing shops with tar, and instigating crowds to acts of violence against "Trotskyists" and traitors. As in former days the personnel of this branch is limited, and their identities are kept strictly secret. The start of the Penang riots in July can be traced to this branch.

(vii) Malayan Seamen's General Labour Union. This branch of the Malayan Communist Party provides seamen couriers for carrying documents and letters to and from Communist Parties of neighbouring countries. The only point of interest in this connection is that letters were recently exchanged between the Malayan Communist Party Central Committee and the Communist Party of Australia. The liaison was effected by a member of the Malayan Seamen's General Labour Union.

(viii) Red Aid Society. The name of this branch in Selangor has been changed, and it is now known as the "Distress Relief Committee". The only case in which money is known to have been collected by relief of political prisoners in Singapore was after the arrest of the four leaders of the Backing-Up Society in July 1938. The Backing-Up Society branches collected subscriptions all over Malaya, and about $3,000/- Chinese currency was handed over to the prisoners when they were banished from Singapore.

(ix) General. The local Committees of the Malayan Communist Party up-country have continued to function, and in each particular State exercise the same control over the local branches of the Anti-Enemy Backing-Up Society as does Malayan Communist Party Central over the "Head Association" in Singapore. The constitution of the "Head Association" is purely communist. The twelve persons who were known to form the "Head Association" were all concurrently members of the Malayan Communist Party, but this was not known to the majority of the lower ranks of the Backing-Up Society. The arrest of five of these men has had beneficial results. Four of them have been recommended for banishment, and one has been able to prove Straits birth. He will be released shortly. The other seven have gone into hiding and have been unable to control the activities of the Society to the same extent as they did before the arrests were made. It is now known to the rank and file that the five men were arrested for communist activities, and the other leaders realise that their connection with the Malayan Communist Party has been exposed to the many non-communist members of the Backing-Up Society. A large number of leading members have until recently given their passive support, but it has become increasingly clear that this support has been withdrawn since strong action has been taken by the Police.
