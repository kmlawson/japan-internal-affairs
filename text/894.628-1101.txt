United States, as it has resulted in increasing the American fur-seal
herd from about 130,000 animals in 1911 to over 1,600,000 animals in
1936, and during this period over 700,000 pelts have been taken from
surplus male seals.
In considering visits of the Japanese Government vessels Hakuyo
Maru and Hakuho Maru to the Pribilof Islands and various ports in
western Alaska in recent years, the records of this Department show
the following:
1936. The Hakuyo Maru visited the Pribilof Islands and
Kiska Harbor.
1935. The Hakuho Maru visited the Pribilof Islands end
Attu Island.
1934. The Hakuyo Maru visited the Pribilof Islands and
Dutch Harbor.
1933. The Hakuyo Maru visited the Pribilof Islands and
Dutch Harbor.
The Hakuho Maru visited Dutch Harbor and cruised
along Aleutian Islands to investigate the migra-
tion routes of fur seals.
1930. The Hakuyo Maru visited the Pribilof Islands and
Dutch Harbor.
It is thus shown that for several years either the Hakuho Maru or
the Hakuyo Maru has visited the Pribilof Islands; on such calls there
have been friendly contacts between Japanese officials and representa-
tives of this Department in matters pertaining to the management of the
fur-seal herd.
As it is understood, upon the basis of informal information, that
it has not been deemed appropriate to grant permission for the Haluyo
Maru to visit the Pribilof Islands in 1937, the question arises as to
whether the Japanese Government may not feel it has been unduly de-
prived of a privilege extended without question in a mumber of previous
years.

=== Page 54 === (confidence 0.98)
-3-
So far as the Department of Commerce is concerned, there is
no objection to either the Hakuho Maru or the Hakuyo Maru visiting
the Pribilof Islands as heretofore. The Japanese Government has
a direct interest in the fur-seal herd under the terms of the con-
vention of l9ll and, therefore, may feel it enjoys the right to
have its representatives visit those islands.
There seems to be no real basis upon which the Japanese Gov-
ernment can expect the privilege of having the Hakuho Maru visit
Chichagof Harbor, Constantine Harbor, or Nazan Bay in the western
Aleutians in 1937 to study the migration routes of fur seals.
There would seem, however, to be reasons to believe that the Japa-
nese Government might expect permission for one of its vessels to
visit the Pribilof Islands in 1937. If the Department of State
decides to deny permission for the Hakuho Maru to visit the three
harbors mentioned, perhaps the earlier request of the Japanese
Government might be reconsidered and permission be granted the
Hakuyo Maru to visit the Pribilof Islands this year. If this is
deemed inadvisable, perhaps a visit to the Pribilofs by the Hakuho
Maru may warrant attention.
Another matter which causes this Department to think it the
course of wisdom to deal as sympathetically as possible with re-
spect to any reasonable request of the Japanese Government for
permission to visit certain places in Alaska, is the threatened
invasion of our salmon fisheries on high sea waters off the coast
of Alaska, particularly in Bristol Bay. American interests are
greatly alarmed and, as you know, are pressing for action which
will insure protection of the high seas coastal fisheries of
Alaska from exploitation by foreign vessels.
The extent, if any, that this situation may have bearing upon
the granting of permission for Japanese Government vessels to visit
the Pribilof Islands or elsewhere in Alaska, appears to merit con-
sideration.
