DEPARTMENT OF STATE
DIVISION OF FAR EASTERN AFFAIRS
July 24, 1934.

It appears to me that the action of the Mayor of Dairen, Inviting American and other foreign firms to contribute towards the purchase of equipment for the air defenses of Dairen raises a somewhat more serious question than one would assume from a reading of the attached despatch.

The commercial treaty with Japan provides in Article I, that nationals of one party shall be exempt "from all contributions imposed in view of personal service, and from all forced loans or military exactions or contributions".

Vincent points out that the reason the foreign companies have not declined to make contributions is that they fear retaliatory measures against their business. One might well sympathize with that fear, but it seems to me that it is incumbent on Vincent and on the Embassy at Tokyo to take a positive attitude in the matter, in view of the treaty provision above quoted.

Any representations that might be made either at Dairen or at Tokyo would of course be set aside with the statement that the

It could be pointed out in rejoinder that the treaty takes cognizance of the impropriety of imposing upon American nationals equal responsibility with Japanese nationals in the matter of contributing to the national defenses of Japan; that the action of the Mayor of Dairen in soliciting voluntary contributions does violence to the spirit of the treaty, and that it is incumbent on the Japanese Government to cause the Mayor to withdraw his notice to American nationals.
