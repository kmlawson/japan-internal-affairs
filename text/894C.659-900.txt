AMERICAN CONSULATE
Dairen, Manchuria, November 26, 1932.

CONFIDENTIAL

SUBJECT: Sulphate of Ammonia Plant at Dairen.

The Honorable
Joseph C. Grew,
American Ambassador,
Tokyo, Japan.

Sir:

I have the honor to refer the Embassy to the frequent press reports which have appeared during past months concerning plans of the South Manchuria Railway Company to establish a plant in Manchuria for the large scale production of ammonium sulphate.

As the Embassy is aware, sulphate of ammonia is now produced in limited quantities at the Anshan iron mills as a by-product of the blast furnaces, at the Fushun colleries in connection with the extraction of oil from oil shale, and at the various gas plants throughout Manchuria. The South Manchuria Railway Company reports that the average annual production for the past five years has been 12,000 long tons. The product is exported, largely through the Mitsui Bussan Kaisha, to Japan, China, the United States, and the South Seas.

Yesterday Baron Shiba, a Director of the South Manchuria Railway Company informed me in confidence that the Company, at a meeting of its Directors on the 19th of this month, had finally reached a decision to establish

=== Page 2 === (confidence 0.99)
- 2 -

an ammonium sulphate plant in Dairen, capable of an annual production of 90,000 tons. The decision and plans were then referred to General Muto at Changchun and received his approval. With reference to this information, which was given to me by Baron Shiba in the course of a conversation concerning other matters, Baron Shiba requested and received my promise that I would treat his statements as strictly confidential until the information was released for publication in the press. He explained that there was so much speculation as to the Company's plans, both with regard to the location and character of the plant, that the Company was anxious to complete all arrangements before making the plans public. It is respectfully requested therefore that the Embassy treat the information as confidential for the time being and guard Baron Shiba against disclosure as the source.

The plant is being placed in Dairen because it is a convenient half-way point for assembling the coal dust from Fushun and sulphur from Japan to be used in the manufacture of the ammonium sulphate. A nitrogen fixation process, probably similar to that known as the "Haber process", will be used. It appears that there was considerable agitation to have the plant located in Mukden which in a measure accounts for the desire not to disclose the decision as to its location in Dairen for the present. It is also believed that the Company was influenced by opposition in Japan to competitive industrial development in "Manchoukuo" in making the decision to locate the plant in the Leased Territory where it technically cannot be considered as an industry of "Manchoukuo".

Significance

=== Page 3 === (confidence 1.00)
-3-

Significance is attached to the decision to establish a plant here because of the controversy among Japanese leaders as to the formation and scope of a so-called "Economic Bloc" between Japan and "Manchoukuo", some leaders opposing any industrial development of Manchuria which might offer competition to Japan and others favouring an unrestricted industrial development along lines best suited to Manchuria's needs and potentialities. The Embassy may recall that Mr. Nagai, the Minister of Overseas Affairs, recently summoned leading Japanese financiers and economists to a conference on this subject. Baron Shiba was present at the meeting and, in answer to Dr. Yamamoto of Kyoto Imperial University, who was opposed to an "economic bloc" in any form, is reported to have said that

although, as Dr. Yamamoto urged, it is but proper that Manchoukuo should be allowed to develop her industry, it does not seem proper that it should be developed as a country with the same industries as Japan from the beginning. With regard to the iron, sulphate of ammonia, aluminum and magnesium or other special industries of that country, they can be helped forward as they give promise of future development.

Baron Shiba pointed out during our conversation that ammonium sulphate is imported into Japan from England and Germany and that Japanese factories therefore had no good grounds for opposing the establishment of a plant at Dairen. Furthermore, it seems probable that the South Manchuria Railway Company will be more interested in the foreign market for sulphate of ammonia than in the Japanese market. There is practically no demand for sulphate in Manchuria.

Respectfully

Respectfully yours,

American Consul.

The Manchuria Chemical Industries, Ltd., under the control of the South Manchuria Railroad, is building a large plant near Dairen to manufacture sulphate of ammonia and other chemicals. One of the largest ventures ever launched by the South Manchuria Railway Company is the Manchuria Chemical Industries, Limited, at Kanchingtzu (suburb of Dairen), to manufacture sulphate of ammonia and certain other chemicals. An attempt is being made to guard the chemical reactions and equipment of the plant with the greatest secrecy, but there are strong indications that the Company is making provisions to produce materials useful in war such as ammonium nitrate and nitric acid. One Dairen firm, in a position to know, states that the manufacture of soda ash (for which a large demand exists in Manchuria) is to be entrusted to a separate company which will probably be located nearby.

**Organization**

The Manchuria Chemical Industries, Limited, the plans for which took definite form only about two years ago, is now capitalized at about 25 million yen. The Company will be under the control of the South Manchuria Railway Company, owning 60 per cent of the stock. Other large Japanese interests have subscribed the balance of the stock, among them the Farmers' Purchasing Guild of Japan.

**Date Operations Commence and Output of Ammonium Sulphate**

THE MANCHURIA DAILY NEWS (October 22, 1934) reports that since the formal opening of the factory by firing the coke furnace on October 9th the Manchuria Chemical Industries, Limited, is making haste with its incidental equipment. This paper further states that the operation of the sulphuric acid factory at Kanchingtzu will commence from

=== Page 8 === (confidence 0.99)
- 3 -

from November 15th. A German engineer employed at the plant states that when operations commence one third of planned capacity of ammonium sulphate will be produced. One reliable source of information states that the nominal planned capacity of the Company is 400 tons daily. It will be remembered that the South Manchuria Railway Company has announced that the annual production of sulphate of ammonia will be 180,000 tons. It is probable that the plant will not be operating to capacity before the spring of 1935.

**Construction and Personnel at Kanchingtzu**

Kanchingtzu is across the bay from Dairen and has the advantage of being the site of a large coal pier and an electric power plant (now under construction) with a capacity of 54,000 kilowatts. It is stated that this power plant will furnish 20,000 kilowatts to the chemical factory. The chemical products building is being constructed by Takaoka Komusho, the electric power plant building by Obayashi Gumi and the machinery shop by Fukusho Company - all of Dairen. A new pier at a cost of Yen 1,067,000 and branch line of the railway have been completed; an important building to house a converter of German make is practically finished; and girders for a 70 ton overhead travelling crane have been put into place.

Some 400 men are now working at the plant including 18 German engineers in the liquidation section and 4 Germans in the high pressure section. In order to preserve certain secrets the engineers are forbidden to move

**Market for Ammonium Sulphate and Price**

As stated above part of the capital of the Company is subscribed by the Farmers' Purchasing Guild of Japan, which presumably will buy a great percentage (perhaps 22% of the demand in Japan) of the sulphate of ammonia produced. An outlet for the rest will be sought in China and abroad. It is reported that the price of the sulphate will be about 46 Yen a ton at the pier at Kanchingtzu. At this price it is expected to undersell in Japan the products of Japanese manufacturers who use electrolysis instead of the treatment of coke-oven gas for the derivation of hydrogen, and also to undersell foreign imports there - even if a change in exchange rates were to remove at any time the disadvantage under which foreign exporters are at present laboring.

In 1932 and 1933 Mitsui Bussan Kaisha and Mitsubishi Trading Company, as sales agents for the South Manchuria Railway Company, disposed of ammonium sulphate that was produced at the Anshan Iron Works and the Fushun Colliery. Prices ranged in the former year from Yen 49.50 to Yen 80.00 f.o.b. Dairen, per short ton. In 1932 8,000 tons and in 1933 8,600 tons of sulphate of ammonia were exported

ported to the United States. The Consulate has learned of no exports of this commodity to America during 1934.

It is interesting to note that Manchuria has a very small area suitable for paddy cultivation and that its soil is highly chemical. There is, therefore, no demand for sulphate of ammonia in this region, nor is one likely to be created - at least for years to come.

**Process of Manufacture**

The method of deriving the ammonia is to be the Uhde Process. In this process, on burning coal ammonia gas and coke gas will be driven off and coke left behind. The small percentage of ammonia gas produced can be recovered by sulphuric acid thus making ammonium sulphate. On treating the coke water gas will be obtained from which hydrogen is derivable. Hydrogen will also be taken from the coke gas driven off in the first stage mentioned above. The nitrogen is to be obtained by liquefying air by cold and pressure and then boiling off the nitrogen. To aid in combining the hydrogen and nitrogen to form ammonia a catalyst obtainable from Manchuria is to be utilized. The catalyzing agent is stated to be a secret known only to certain German chemists and officials of the Manchuria Chemical Industries, Limited. An important phase of the reaction in joining the hydrogen and nitrogen is that a compression of 150 atmospheres is to be used. It will be noted that such a compression is lower than in the Haber Process.

The raw materials required are coal and pyrites, of which

## Machinery and Countries of Origin

The following expenditures for machinery have been
planned: ammonia plant, Yen 9,000,000; finishing plant,
Yen 4,500,000; electric and water service plant, Yen
4,500,000; and Yen 7,000,000 for miscellaneous equipment,
machinery and land. According to one source the Chemical
Company contemplates spending up to 50 million yen on
machinery of which 50% will be purchased in Japan and the re-
mainder in Germany and other countries. It is very doubtful,
however, if this sum will ever be expended.

Since the chemical process to be employed has been
evolved in Germany and since Japan is just starting to
make the requisite machinery, Germany has been and probably
will continue to be in the most advantageous position
to sell machinery to the Kanchingtsu plant. Moreover,
it is possible that the fact that Germany has an
unfavorable trade balance with "Manchukuo" has been taken
into consideration.

As of interest in this connection, I have been informed by a reliable American engineer that the Manchuria Chemical Industry Company is at present negotiating for the construction near the ammonium sulphate plant of a very large factory for the production of aluminum sulphate (alum). Construction plans are now completed. The plant under contemplation is reported to be planned to produce 165 tons of alum per day, as well as 25 tons of aluminum which are to be used for naval purposes.

According to my informant plans are likewise being discussed for the erection of a caustic soda and soda ash factories by the same company. This is confirmed by the MANCHURIA DAILY NEWS of April 11, 1935, which states the companies are to be known respectively as the Manchuria Lead Mining Company, to be established in Dairen, and the Manchuria Soda Ash Company to be erected in Mukden. The News also reports that the former company will have an authorized capital of four million yen subscribed exclusively by the South Manchuria Railway Company and by the Nippon Mining Enterprises of Tokyo. If the plans for the manufacture of soda ash in Mukden mature it will be a serious blow to the Imperial Chemical Industries, limited, as soda ash constitutes the principal import into Dairen of that British company.
