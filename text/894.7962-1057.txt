Commercial Airport Projected at Nagoya

It has been reported recently in the local press, and confirmed from other sources, that it has been definitely decided to construct a civil airport at Nagoya, to be located on land to be reclaimed adjacent to the southern part of the already reclaimed areas which now form the eastern side of the inner Nagoya harbor at Atsuta. The airport as now projected will have an area of 280,000 tsubo (229 acres). The cost of construction is estimated at around Yen 1,700,000 ($844,000 at par). The construction work will be supervised and carried out by the Aichi prefectural authorities. Reports indicate that the work will be spread over a five year period and that it will be inaugurated in 1933, with an initial expenditure of Yen 100,000 during that year. Although details are lacking it is supposed that the cost of construction will be borne jointly by Aichi Prefecture, the City of Nagoya and the Japanese Government.

The exact location of the proposed airport has not yet been definitely decided upon, but it is reported that a site adjacent to sections 8 and 9 of the harbor area (see attached sketch map) will be chosen. The area to be reclaimed now consists of mud and sand flats lying slightly below extreme low tide level. A fill of five to ten feet over the acreage to be reclaimed will be required. It is presumed that this project is linked with the fourth period improvement program of the port of Nagoya, which

=== Page 3 === (confidence 0.98)

which, according to a recent report, contemplates the further widening of the harbor entrance channel from its present width of 120 ken (716 feet) ot 140 ken (836 feet), and the increasing of its depth from 30 to 34 feet at low tide, as well as various other dredging operations designed to increase the mooring capacity of the inner harbor.

Pending the completion of the projected airport it is reported that Section No. 7 of the Nagoya port area may be devoted temporarily to airport uses. Only a limited amount of grading work would be required to fit this area for airport landing purposes.

The provision of convenient airport landing facilities at Nagoya will enable it to be made an intermediate stopping point on the direct Tokyo - Osaka passenger airplane route of the Japan Air Transportation Company. It is likely also that the important Mitsubishi and Aichi Tokei airplane factories, located respectively at Sections 6 and 1 as indicated on the attached map, will find the existence of an adequate landing field adjacent to their works both convenient and valuable for test uses. The nearest field now available is the army airfield at Kagamigahara, about twenty-five miles north of this city.

Projected Commercial Airport at Nagoya.

Referring to this Consulate's report of November 29, 1932, concerning a project to construct a commercial airport at Nagoya on land to be reclaimed adjacent to the port of Nagoya at Atsuta, it has just been announced in the local press that on March 13, 1933, the Governor of Aichi Prefecture addressed a communication to the City Assembly of Nagoya requesting sanction of the Prefecture's plans to reclaim certain land, indicated on the attached map, to be devoted, upon completion of the work, for use as an airport. The City Assembly signified its approval of the plan at a meeting held on March 14, 1933.

The project, as previously reported, embraces the reclamation of 280,000 tsubo (229 acres) of land at present lying below low tide level. The location now definitely decided upon is south of Section 10 of the harbor area. The estimated cost of construction is approximately Yen 1,700,000 ($844,000 at par). It is reported that construction work will be commenced in the near future.

2. More Naval Air Bases.

The press reports that in consideration of the menace from the strong Soviet air force, and in accordance with its first and second replenishment plans, the Navy has decided to add five new air bases, one at Maizuru facing the Japan Sea, one at Kisarazu, Chiba Prefecture near to Tokyo, one in the neighborhood of Kogan, Nemuro, Hokkaido, one near Kagoshima in Kyushu, and one at Chinkai in Korea. The need to have more air control over the Japan Sea has long been felt, and these new bases are calculated to fulfill that purpose.

A commercial airport has been opened at Nagoya. Passenger service to Tokyo and Osaka commenced October 1, 1934, on a temporary landing field. The air-mail from Tokyo calls, but not the Osaka air-mail, for as yet there are no facilities for night landing.

Details follow:

Location: Nagoya harbor district, four miles from the city.

Area: 330,000 square feet to be reclaimed and built up.

Landing area: Width, east to west, 555 feet; north to south, 755 feet.

Water landing: To be dredged at south east.

Facilities: Aerodrome, Liding stands, signal posts, oil tanks.

Additional: Roads and bridges to field, and breakwater enclosing field, being constructed.

Passenger service:
Nagoya-Yokyo; 180 miles flying; 1 hour, 50 minutes; fare Yen 20.00.

Nagoya-Osaka; 93 miles flying; 50 minutes; fare Yen 12.00.

Other local fields: There are no other commercial airports near Nagoya. But there is a very large military air field north of the city.

SUBJECT: Construction to be Begun on New Landing Field at Kamitsu, Hyogo Prefecture.

THE HONORABLE
THE SECRETARY OF STATE,
WASHINGTON.

SIR:

I have the honor to report that according to the KOBE SHIMBUN of November 25, 1936 the purchase of a parcel of land for a new public landing field to be constructed at Kamitsu, Kawabe-gun, Hyogo Prefecture, was effected by the Prefectural authorities acting on behalf of the Ministry of Communications on that date. Authorization for the purchase was originally received from the Ministry on September 2, 1936, but negotiations were protracted because of the demands of the villagers for greater compensation for their land. It is understood that of the sum of ¥1,076,800 appropriated for the entire project about one-half was used to purchase the land. Work on the field will be begun on December 3, 1936, and opening to air traffic is scheduled for June of 1937.

The field will be located on the Osaka plain bordering the Ina river about seven miles inland from Osaka bay and roughly halfway between the hot spring resort of Takarazuka and

It is regarded by Japanese aviation engineers as the most suitable location in Japan for a public airport for three reasons: first, it is one of the largest truly level sections of land in the entire Empire; second, it is entirely free from the fog conditions which have hindered air transportation at the Osaka Kizuhama field and other places in Japan; third, there are no power lines or horizon obstacles as in the case of the Kizuhama field and the Fukuoka Najima field, the two main airports in western Japan.
