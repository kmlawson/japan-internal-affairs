SEE 756.9411/1 FOR Despatch #1226

FROM Latvia (Skinner) DATED March 20, 1933
TO NAME 1-1127

REGARDING: Japanese naval base for Submarines. Discovery of, on the Mortlock Islands.

Under the title "Japanese Naval Base for Submarines Has Been Discovered on the Mortlock Islands" a further DAILY HERALD despatch is quoted to the effect that the captain of an English vessel had announced that he had by chance discovered a Japanese naval base on one of the Mortlock Islands, 460 miles from British New Guinea, in the Caroline Island group. The captain is reported to have stated that a Japanese submarine was in the harbor and that "there can be no doubt that the location is suitable for a base."

Dairen's despatch of May 13, 1933, to Ambassador Grew encloses a copy in translation of the revised port regulations for Port Arthur as they appear in the Kwantung Government Official Gazette of April 28.

The water area of the port of Port Arthur is divided into three areas. These areas are indicated on the attached map. War and commercial vessels, both Japanese and foreign have free access to the third area; only vessels belonging to the Japanese navy are allowed to enter the first and second areas without special permission, except vessels coming out of the third area and passing through the second again to enter the third area. Most of the regulations are very technical in nature and not of immediate interest to FE.

Aviation within the limits of Port Arthur, and surveying, photographing, et cetera, are prohibited unless permission is
