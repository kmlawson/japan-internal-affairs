GENERAL HEADQUARTERS
SUPREME COMMANDER FOR THE ALLIED POWERS
Diplomatic Section

The Diplomatic Section presents its compliments to the Australian Mission and has the honor to state that the Japanese Government, which up to the present time has been required to provide supplementary food rations to foreign nationals residing in Japan, will no longer be required to do so after May 1, 1949.

The foreign nationals' ration was established in 1946, as a supplement to the Japanese basic ration, to provide the necessary nutritional requirements for foreign nationals at a time when the Japanese food ration provided only like calories per day for the normal consumer and when there was no free market in which supplementary foods of any type could be purchased.

The Japanese food ration has now materially changed. The Japanese basic ration, including cereals, sugar fries, oils, miso and shoyu, together with rye and fries and vegetables, now provides 1500 to 1600 calories per day to the American consumer. In addition a wide selection of food rations, the Japanese food ration is to be used in the food rations provided for foreign nationals in the Japanese Government, as a supplement to the Japanese basic ration, to provide supplemental food rations for foreign nationals residing in Japan.

It has the honor to say that the food ration is to be used in the food rations provided for foreign nationals residing in Japan. It may also be mentioned in this connection that all foreign nationals residing in Japan have a special food ration. Sirs:

I have your letter of April 1 requesting information regarding the present cost of living in Japan compared with 1937, and regarding the current rate of exchange for dollars in Japan.

I regret that the Department does not have figures on the current cost of living in Japan. State Department personnel have formed part of the occupation personnel in Japan and it has accordingly not been necessary for the Department to secure such figures. If you will write to the Economic and Scientific Section, Headquarters of the Supreme Commander for the Allied Powers, APO 500, c/o Postmaster, San Francisco, California I am confident that you will be able to obtain the information you desire.

In response to your second inquiry, the present official rate of exchange in Japan is 270 yen to the dollar.

Sincerely yours,

Max P. Pishon
Chief
Division of Northeast Asian Affairs

Dear Sirs:

We are trying to arrive at a figure for the cost of living in Japan at the present time as compared with 1937. Can you give us any approximate figures or suggest how they might be arrived at?

I am interested also to learn the current rate of exchange for dollars in Japan - the rate available for American civilians living in Japan.

I shall appreciate any assistance you can give me.

Sincerely yours,
