SUBJECT: Japanese Activities in Macau and Portuguese Timor
STRICTLY CONFIDENTIAL

THE HONORABLE
THE SECRETARY OF STATE
WASHINGTON.

SIR:

I have the honor to report that the Japanese Government is still endeavoring to obtain industrial and shipping concessions in Macau. It will be recalled that early in 1935 Japanese interests endeavored to purchase the waterworks at Macau, as reported in a strictly confidential despatch, No. 853e.151/2, 127 of May 17, 1935, regarding the reorganization of the Macau Waterworks Company.

About two or three months ago the Japanese Government submitted a proposal to the Macau authorities through the Japanese Consul General at Hong Kong offering to establish two or three industrial enterprises at Macau (the precise nature of which is unknown to this office) and also to establish a fast steamship service between Macau, Canton and Hong Kong. They also offered to establish a regular steamship service between Macau and Timor. There is no trade between Macau and Timor and what possibility there would be of developing

Some months ago the Japanese Government endeavored through Portuguese residents of Macau to buy up certain reclaimed land at Macau and even went so far as to deposit the money in a Macau bank but as soon as the Japanese interest appeared the Macau authorities dropped the matter.

According to the Macau authorities there is only one Japanese national resident there but this office is reliably informed that several Japanese are believed to be residing in Macau in the guise of Chinese. The one Japanese national is Dr. Shinzo Wada who has been practicing as a dental surgeon in Macau for the past six or seven years but who is generally recognized as being in charge of the Japanese Intelligence in South China, including Hong Kong. Dr. Wada does practically no work professionally and is always to be seen in the company of high ranking Japanese naval officers, when the latter visit Macau. He is kept under surveillance by the Hong Kong Police.

The new Governor of Macau, Senhor Tamagnini Barbosa, who arrives at Hong Kong on April 10th en route to his post, is definitely known to be anti-Japanese.

anti-Japanese, as are the majority of officials in Macau. However, not all of them take the same view, some believing that Japan should be encouraged to make investments in Macau as an offset to British influence. No foreign capital, other than British and Chinese, is invested in Macau. The leader of this faction, which is undoubtedly in the minority, is the Attorney General of Macau, Dr. Alves Ferreira.

British investments in Macau are now limited to the electric light plant, known as the Macau Electric Company. The Green Island Cement Company, a Hong Kong British owned concern, has only recently disposed of its Macau plant, as mentioned on page one of a report from this office entitled "Hong Kong Industrial Enterprises During 1936", dated March 30, 1937.

There have been rumors for the past four or five years that the Swedish Match Company contemplated establishing a plant at Macau but these reports are believed to be without foundation.

The Consulate General is reliably informed that Dr. Wada has been actively negotiating regarding oil wells in Timor and that working with him is a Portuguese national, Delfino Ribeiro, a resident of Macau who retired from the police department of that city a few years ago

Both Wada and Ribeiro have been expecting to leave for Timor for the past two months but have for some unknown reason delayed their journey. The purpose of the visit is to survey the property and to decide on the terms of a contract with the Government of Timor. Probably a Portuguese company will be formed with Japanese capital which is stated to be unlimited.

Mr. Wada has stated that he will visit Timor every two months to inspect the work and that about May this year he intends to go from there to Australia regarding the purchase of some iron ore mines, which at present are known as "Haisa &amp; Company", and are owned by an Australian, whose Christian name is Norman, but whose surname is unknown.

It is also understood that Mr. Wada made a special trip to Tokio by plane in November 1936 regarding this project.

The information contained in this despatch has been furnished chiefly by Mr. F. J. Gellion, a British subject who has resided in Macau for years and is manager of the Macau Electric Lighting Company. Mr. Gellion is Honorary British Vice Consul at Macau. He is well known to the writer and his comments as reported in this despatch may be accepted without reserve.

Hong Kong, April 15, 1937.

SUBJECT: Japanese Activities in Portuguese Timor.
THE HONORABLE
THE SECRETARY OF STATE
WASHINGTON.

SIR:

I have the honor to refer to my strictly confidential despatch No. 518 of April 9, 1937, regarding Japanese activities in Macao and Portuguese Timor and to report that Mr. F. J. Gellion, Honorary British Vice Consul at Macao, has furnished some additional information on the same subject.

It is understood on excellent authority, according to Mr. Gellion, that negotiations have practically been concluded for the purchase by some Japanese of a large Portuguese company in Timor at present owned by the family of the late Celestino da Silva, at one time Governor of Timor. The purchase price is understood to be two million florins.

Dr. Sales Luiz, a member of this family, was enroute to Japan to conclude the matter when he received instructions to come to Macao and await further instructions, but he expects to sail for Japan shortly.

The
SPECIAL ASSISTANT TO THE SECRETARY
JUN 1 1937

SIR:
I have the honor to refer to my strictly confidential despatch No. 518 of April 9, 1937, regarding Japanese Activities in Macao and Portuguese Timor, and to report that in the second line on page two of that despatch the word "Macao" should read "Timor". The statement as made in the despatch under reference was, of course, an obvious error as all of Macao's trade passes through Hong Kong.

It was intended to state that, so far as this office has been able to ascertain the trade of Timor moves through Singapore.
