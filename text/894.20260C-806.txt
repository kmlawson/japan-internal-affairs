**Subject:** Japanese Propaganda in Poland.

**For the** report No. 0028 (3850)

The following is a translation of an interview with General S. S. S., Japanese Military Attache in Warsaw, published this morning by the "EXPRESS JOURNEY".

## Translation.

"The Shingoku Army exists no longer."

What comes now: ... of ...?

**Special Interview of "Express Journey"** with Gen. S. S. S.

The eyes of the whole world are turned towards the Far East.

Nanking's fall may be expected any minute, - properly speaking its fall is already achieved. It has been occupied by the victorious troops of General Matsui. The conquest by the Japanese of the northern capital of China - 27 days after having driven the Chinese army out of Shanghai - is not only an extraordinary achievement of the 100,000 strong Japanese corps which made in so short time 280 kilometers under combat conditions and in spite of difficulties of the terrain, but it is also a critical moment in the armed conflict in Asia.

What are Japan's intentions? What course will the events in the Far East take now?

Replies to these questions were granted us by General S. S. S., Chief of the Japanese military mission in Poland, and former chief of the Japanese mission in Manchuria.

The statements of this prominent expert of Japanese policy in China possess therefore a particular significance.

" The total occupation of Nanking and vicinities by our army is now only a question of a few hours" - said General Sawada. - "The first 4 months phase of military operations is closed".

- "Now, we shall again address to China the question: peace of war? We are prepared for two eventualities. However, we can negotiate only with China alone. This is our condition that we two sit alone at the conference table. We shall not agree to any intervention of other powers."

- "Our other condition will be - checking of communist propaganda in China. If it was not for that propaganda, it would not have come to such a conflict which was caused only by Bolshevik agitation".

- "Should China reject our conditions, we shall continue war. Operations in the direction of Hankow will be commenced. We will attack it from two sides. In the North along the railroad track, and from the side of the Yangtze-kang river."

- "The most difficult phase of the war is already behind us. The army of Marshal Chang-Kai-Sheek is entirely defeated. There are still regiments of provincial generals the combat value of which is not menacing."

- "The richest parts of China are occupied by us. The occupation of Shanghai has cut off Marshal Chang-Kai-Sheek from his financial resources. I do not think therefore that the Marshal would now agree to continue war. It would be a hopeless fight, - senseless, in my opinion."

- "The result of the war is thus predetermined, we have won it..."

After these sensational explanations the question was asked whether it was true that the Japanese Government, according to information from Tokyo, is going to refuse further recognition of Marshal Chang-Kai-Sheek's government as a legal one?

- "This information is not official. However, I do not consider it impossible. The Cabinet of Marshal Chang-Kai-Sheek was not a government in the sense of European ideas. The Marshal maintained his power because he had the army on his side and was in possession of money."

- "Now he has lost the army defeated by us under Nanking and Shanghai, as well as the money. So he has no means of keeping his power. It is very doubtful whether the Chinese themselves will still consider him their Chief."

A last question, General. What are at present the probabilities of an extension of the war to other countries in Asia?

"The critical moment is already past. Now that Marshal Chang-Kai-Sheek is vanquished, it is not thought that a third party would be desirous of attacking us", - said General Sawada.

As a supplement to this statement the words of Colonel Takahashi, Chief of the Chinese section of the Japanese Staff, said at the time of his stay in Warsaw should be recalled:

"Japan will not start war. The Soviets will surely try to prolong the Sino-Japanese war by helping the Chinese. But if the Soviets will continue to carry on communistic activities, particularly in northern China, a conflict may become unavoidable. The Soviets must choose: either an open military intervention, or renouncement of all interference."

Batavia - Java.
February 18, 1934.
CONFIDENTIAL.

Visit to Java of Major General Itagaki of the Imperial Japanese Army.

I have the honor to refer to a confidential report to this Consulate General made by the Consul at Surabaya (copy of which was furnished the Department) dated January 15, 1934, in regard to the visit of Major General Seishiro Itagaki, of the Imperial Japanese Army, to the Surabaya consular district, and to state that I have endeavored discretely to ascertain the purpose of the visit of Major General Itagaki to Java. For this purpose I approached Admiral Osten, Director of the Department of Marine of Motherland India, since the Department of War of Motherland in is is at Bandoeng. Admiral Osten first informed me that he had no information concerning the visit of this Japanese general but that he would be glad to see the necessary inquiries and to inform me later of any significance which might be attached to the visit of this officer.
