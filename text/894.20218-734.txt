894.20218 January 28 1932.

STRICTLY CONFIDENTIAL.
No. 160

The Honorable
Charles C. Eberhardt,
American Minister,
San Jose.

Sir:

The Department has received information from various
sources that individuals of the Japanese race have in
recent months been engaged in what appear to be topo-
graphical surveys along the Pacific and Caribbean coasts
of certain Latin American countries. In some cases such
surveys appear to have been undertaken by individuals
alleged to have represented themselves as fishermen.

At the request of the Director of Naval Intelligence,
the Department desires you to investigate discreetly and
report promptly upon these and such similar activities
as may come to your notice. Comments are desired as to
the appearance of any Japanese fishermen and their vessels:
do they seem to be bona fide fishermen, or are they more
highly educated men in disguise? Are the vessels equipped
with survey, sounding, or other scientific instruments?

You

You are authorized, in your discretion, to request the cooperation of the principal American consular officers in the country to which you are accredited, taking care to impress upon them the importance of being discreet in their investigations.

Very truly yours,

For the Secretary of State:

FRANCIS WHITE
