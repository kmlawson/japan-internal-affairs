Tokyo, Japan. February 2, 1933.

Data regarding foreign fire insurance companies.

THE WESTERN
THE SECRETARY OF STATE
WASHINGTON

IN:

I have the honor to acknowledge the receipt of the Department's instruction of December 21, 1932 (P. O. 600. 5061/15) directing this immediate general to procure and transmit to the Department blank copies of any fire insurance policies that may be obtainable from insurance companies operating in the supervisory consular jurisdiction of this office. It is understood that these blanks are desired by the Honorable George Holder Tinkham, Member of Congress, who addressed the Department in a letter dated November 14, 1932.

There are transmitted herewith blank policy forms used by the three foreign companies operating in Japan, namely, American, British and French, and also the policy form of a Japanese company. The forms of the foreign companies are identical in so far as general conditions are concerned and are a modified version of the so-called "F.G.C. (foreign) Standard Policy", adopted to meet local conditions.

The

The Japanese companies use varying conditions but although wordings may differ slightly, the extent of cover is the same and the sample forms are believed to be representative. The Japanese companies' policies differ in essentials from the foreign companies' policies only in that one covers forest fire risk and the other excludes it. In practice, however, since additional sites are charged for the cover in localities where it is sold, governments remove the apparent discrepancy altogether.

As regards local rating practice with respect to dwelling houses and other general risks, the fundamental principle followed is to grade rates according to the fire loss record of the locality in which the risk is situated. In some cases a flat rate applies to a whole province, in others there are many "divisions" recognized within cities. For instance, the whole of Mara-ken is in the same rating division, but the City of Tokyo is split into no less than eight rating divisions.

Within each rating division, suitably graded rates apply to the various classes of construction; and in the congested areas of the country very substantial discounts are given for isolated risks. There are some penalties for poor construction, such as shingle roofs, but in general such risks as dwelling houses are treated as being approximately of the same hazard and the object is to make rates for the entire class pay for the class losses, penalizing by additional charges only those risks which are greatly below the class level. In this respect local rating follows the British "Class" method, rather than the American system.
