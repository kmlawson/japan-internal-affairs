# Japanese espionage in Netherland India.

Local officials allege that Japanese espionage activities are increasing, both in frequency and seriousness. For example, during the latter half of April, 1936, Lieutenant Colonel Takanobu Manaki of the Japanese General Staff visited Medan and, in addition to other things, inspected the local electric power, water supply, telephone and telegraph plants. Colonel Manaki then came to Batavia, Buitenzorg, Bandoeng, Surabaya, and other cities,

Japanese goods. Colonel Manaki then expressed the hope that high military and other officials of Netherland India would visit Japan more frequently for the purpose of studying Japanese life and customs, and particularly the Japanese Army and Navy. At this point Mr. Lovink told Colonel Manaki that efforts in the past to do this had proved to be fruitless. Colonel Manaki then replied that officers of the Netherland Indian Army would even be commissioned as officers in the Japanese Army and be given command over troops in order that they might study conditions in Japan more thoroughly. Mr. Lovink replied to this statement rather icily by saying that he hoped Netherland Indian officials would be received with a little more cordiality than they had been in the past.

In connection with espionage and propaganda, which are alleged to be carried on in Netherland India by the Japanese, reference is made to my despatch No. 266, dated November 4, 1935, File No. 800, concerning the alleged efforts of the Japanese to spread Buddhism among the natives of Java and the Outer Possessions. I was informed today that the house of Mr. van Dienst and the house of his assistant, Mr. de With, were raided very recently, and that the officials making the raid discovered many Japanese army uniforms, a considerable supply of

Furthermore, a Japanese naval officer who recently came to Netherland India for the purpose of finding a position of some sort locally was followed very carefully by the Secret Service and was finally asked to leave the country. The Japanese Consul General immediately protested on the ground of unfair discrimination against a Japanese national. When confronted with evidence, however, to the effect that the Japanese national was a naval officer in disguise, the Consul General withdrew the protest. When the officer in question left Netherland India, he preferred to sail from Surabaya rather than from Batavia. Needless to say, he was under constant police surveillance between Batavia and Surabaya. Detail of Japanese naval officer for the study of languages in the Netherlands East Indies.

1. It has been learned from a reliable source in the Ministry of Defense that recently the Foreign Minister of Japan informed the Foreign Minister of the Netherlands via the Japanese Charge d'Affaires here in The Hague that the Japanese Navy in April would send a Lieutenant-Commander of the Japanese Navy to the Dutch East Indies for the purpose of studying the Dutch and Japanese languages. The headquarters of this officer will be in Batavia.

2. My informant pointed out that if the Japanese officer desired to study the Dutch language, the place for him to study it was here in the Netherlands, but as the Japanese stated that this officer would also study Javanese, this made it mandatory for him to be stationed in the Netherlands East Indies, which fact appears to give the officials of the Ministry of Defense some concern.
