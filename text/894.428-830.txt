MAY 21 1938

Hon. Cordell Hull,
Secretary of State,
Washington, D. C.

Dear Mr. Secretary:

Knowing your firm belief that good international relations must
rest on knowledge I want to tell you, briefly, of how fully I
share that belief and of one or two things I've been doing to give
it practical effect.

When I was in Japan three years ago Viscount Ishii suggested that,
in a talk I was to make before the Japan-America Society, I should
outline ways by which Japan could be better understood in this country.
I endeavored to do this by describing the highly successful
work of the British Library of Information and I ventured to suggest
that Japan might find it to her advantage to establish a Library
on similar lines. This suggestion was received with great
favor. The late Baron Dan asked me to meet a group of important
business leaders to consider the matter. When they asked me about
costs I told them I could easily get the budget of the British Library,
on my return to America, and send it to them. This I did
and the whole plan was quickly taking final form when the Manchurian
incident broke and everything was postponed; but the plan is in no
wise abandoned. Indeed interest in it is active and Prince Tokugawa
and Prince Konoye talked the plan over with me when they were
here and I feel confident that the plan will shortly be carried into
effect.

This leads me to raise the question as to the value and timeliness
of this country having a United States Library of Information in
Tokyo; and, in due course, in London, Paris, Berlin, Rome and in
other strategic capitals. Such Libraries, I confidently believe,
would spread the kind of light that would do much to dispel war clouds.

You knew, of course, of the Pan-American Library established here last
January. It was my privilege to be closely associated in that undertaking
and it is a pleasure to tell you that it has already completely
justified itself.

If the suggestion I offer seems to have validity I'll be delighted

My dear Mr. Houston:

In Mr. Hull's absence, the receipt is acknowledged of your letter of August 6, 1934, in regard to your suggestion that there be established a United States Library of Information in Tokyo and, in due course, in London, Paris, Berlin, Rome and other capitals. Although we know of no governmental funds that might be available for such enterprises, we assure you that we share your view that the setting up of such libraries would be likely to be productive of beneficial results and that the establishing and maintaining of institutions of this character by private initiative and effort would receive our unofficial good wishes.

I am sending a copy of your letter to Mr. Grew, American Ambassador to Tokyo, for his information and comment.

It is regretted that through inadvertence there has been delay in replying to your letter under reference.

Sincerely yours,

Mr. Herbert S. Houston,
70 Fifth Avenue,
New York, New York.

AUG 31 1934
Department of State

Dear Mr. Secretary:

Thank you very much for your kind letter about the suggestion I submitted to Secretary Hull about a United States Library of Information in Tokyo and others in important capitals. I am greatly pleased that you have sent the whole matter forward to Ambassador Grew and I shall be very glad to know what his general reaction is.

Having been familiar with the work of the British Library of Information from the beginning, and now seeing the fine results that are already being accomplished by the Pan-American Library of Information, which I had something to do with getting under way, I am more than ever convinced that this method of spreading light and knowledge in a befogged world is the best that has yet been devised.

For your confidential information I may say that I had a conference at the Harvard Club yesterday about a plan for a German Library of Information.

With assurances of my warm personal regards, I am, as ever,

Yours faithfully,

Herbert S. Houston

September 11th,
1934.

894.428/9

Hon. William Phillips,
Acting Secretary of State,
Washington, D. C.

Dear Mr. Secretary:

Some very important information reached me from Japan yesterday that I want you to immediately have. It is to the effect that the Japanese Foreign Office has approved the plan for establishing a Japanese Library of Information here in New York, along the lines of the British Library, and they are arranging to have a substantial sum in the next budget to cover the cost of initial expenses and yearly maintenance.

You very likely have this information from our Embassy in Tokyo but I felt that it was so timely and important that I wanted to transmit it to you the moment it came to my attention.

It seems to me, my dear Mr. Secretary, that it would be a great stroke in improving good relations between this country and Japan if an American Library of Information could be established in Tokyo at the earliest convenient moment.

Another interesting piece of information has just reached me to the effect that Japan has invited the World Federation of Education Association to hold its biennial convention of 1935 in Tokyo. This is assuredly a happy circumstance and I have reason to believe that this invitation from Japan is likely to be accepted. I am a member of the Board of the World Federation and I certainly shall use my influence to that good end.

With hearty personal regards,

Yours faithfully,

Herbert S. H. H.

September 12, 1934

Dear Mr. Houston:

I am very glad to have your kind letter of yesterday with regard to the establishment of the Japanese Library of Information in New York and I agree wholeheartedly that it would be a fine thing if we could establish in Tokyo reciprocally an American Library of Information.

I also thank you for letting me know that Japan is encouraging the World Federation of Education Association to hold its biennial convention of 1935 in Tokyo.

With kindest regards and renewed thanks for your letter,

Sincerely,

Mr. Herbert A. Houston,
70 Fifth Avenue,
New York, New York.
