X. MISCELLANEOUS.
(a) "Greater Tokyo".

On October 1 the city limits of Tokyo were extended to incorporate within the city 82 towns and villages and increase the total population from slightly over two million to over five million. The city authorities now claim that Tokyo is second only to New York among the large cities of the world.

THE HONORABLE
THE SECRETARY OF STATE.
WASHINGTON.

SIR:

I have the honor to advise the Department that under separate cover appropriately marked are being forwarded three copies of a publication in English, "Greater Tokyo", issued by the Tokyo Municipal Office. The publication, besides containing interesting municipal statistical data and profuse illustrations, has also a map showing the communication facilities of the city, and should prove of value as a reference book.

One copy is marked for the Library of the Department of State. It might be deemed desirable to send the second copy to the Congressional Library. The third copy should prove valuable to some Bureau interested in municipal planning and city administration. Its disposition is left to the discretion of the Department.

Respectfully yours,

SUBJECT: FISCAL RELATIONS BETWEEN THE CENTRAL GOVERNMENT AND THE CAPITAL CITY.

The Honorable
The Secretary of State,
Washington.

Sir:

I have the honor to refer to the Department's telegraphic instruction No. 195, July 14, 7 p.m., forwarded by radio to Shanghai and thence by mail to Tokyo, requesting information in regard to the fiscal relations between the Japanese Central Government and the City of Tokyo, in connection with a contemplated study of fiscal relations between the United States Government and the District of Columbia by the Bureau of the Budget.

This

=== Page 10 === (confidence 0.99)
-2-

This Embassy, promptly upon receipt of the instruction in question, left with the Foreign Office a memorandum (a copy of which is enclosed) requesting the information desired by the Bureau of the Budget. Under date of August 18, 1936, the Foreign Office delivered to the Embassy a memorandum (a translation of which is enclosed), giving such information as could be supplied by the Ministry of Finance in reply to the questions contained in the Embassy's memorandum.

The Foreign Office official who delivered the memorandum to this Embassy stated that the questions could not be answered accurately because the Japanese Central Government does not treat the Municipality of Tokyo any differently than it treats other municipal and local bodies in Japan. The Central Government pays no especial subsidies or contributions to the Municipality of Tokyo because of the fact that the Central Government's offices are situated in the City of Tokyo. There are, in other words, no especial fiscal relations between the Central Government and the capital city in Japan.

The official further stated that it was difficult to give any information as to the number of Central Government employees in the national employ in Tokyo, as such information would require several months of investigation. In this connection he explained that, due to the fact that in Japan all policemen, gendarmes, postal employees, telegraph employees, telephone employees and railway employees, are in the national employ instead of in the employ of the municipality
