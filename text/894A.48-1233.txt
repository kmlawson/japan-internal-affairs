SUBJECT: Message in Taiwan by Typhoon of August 30, 1940.

THE HONORABLE
THE SECRETARY OF STATE
WASHINGTON

SIR:

I have the honor to report that the Taihoku Weather Bureau has informed me that the typhoon which struck Taiwan on August 30, 1940 was the worst in the past nine years, which in turn was the worst in the previous nine years. The Bureau stated that wind velocity averaged some 25 meters per second (55 miles per hour) for ten hours, and at one time reached a Taiwan record of 40 meters per second (90 miles per hour). It followed a path up the East Coast from Karenko through Keelung, but its effect was felt all over the island. While it has reportedly not yet been possible to calculate the loss, sugar interests roughly estimate that some Yen 2 millions of damage was done to cane. SIR:

I have the honor to report that the worst typhoon in the last 26 years struck Taiwan on the afternoon of September 29, 1940 and did not die down until the evening of September 30. The center of the typhoon was in the southern part of Taiwan and the extent of damage is not yet known, but was considerable to the second rice crop which is within a month of being harvested, and to sugar cane part of which is nearly fully matured and would shortly be ready for cutting, and part of which was planted this summer and would normally mature a year hence. It is at present roughly estimated that from 10% to 20% of agricultural crops were destroyed in this latest disaster by both wind and flood damage. There was also considerable property destruction and some loss of life. The wind velocity reportedly reached a peak of 48 meters per second (108 miles per hour).

SUBJECT: Typhoon and Tidal Wave on Shinnan Gunto.

THE HONORABLE
THE SECRETARY OF STATE
WASHINGTON

SIR:

I have the honor to report that the local press has recently been giving prominence to the disaster in the form of a 50 metre typhoon and an 18 ft. tidal wave which on December 5, 1940 swept over Taiwan's newest acquisitions, Shinnan Gunto (Spratley Islands), located some 800 miles south of Taiwan. According to reports all buildings were demolished, some 8 people killed, several score wounded and about 40 are missing. When it is considered that the highest land in the islands is only some ten feet above sealevel, and only some 300 Japanese reside there, mainly on Naga Shima, the magnitude of the disaster from their standpoint can be seen. So complete was the destruction that news of the disaster did not reach Takao, Taiwan until a week after it had occurred. In answer to the appeal for medical, food and other supplies
