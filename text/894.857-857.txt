34, there called at the Nagasaki Consulate a delegation of

The "No. 8, Sumiyoshi Maru" of 49 tons of Tsubaki Mara, Toku-
shima Ken, Shikoku, had left the port of Tama-no-ura in the Goto
Islands, the most westerly of the Kyushu group, for the fishing
grounds on April 18th and was returning to Tama-no-ura in company
with its sistership "No. 5, Sumiyoshi Maru" on the night of May
8th when the ships lost sight of each other in a storm. Losing
its course in the storm the "No. 8" struck a sunken rock near
Hashima, a amall island about 25 miles Northwest of Nagasaki.

Due to the violence of the impact the vessel soon broke up and
left the master and the ten members of the crew to cling to the
still floating forecastle. In the darkness one of the men
became lost.
The United States Steamship "City of Birmingham", 6l94 tons,
of the Isthmian S. S. Co., Bertrand M. Green, Master, which had
left Nagasaki bound for Dairen about eleven o'clock on the morning
of the ninth, sighted the wrecked fishermen about two in the after-
noon. The Captain immediately radioed the Japanese Steamer Kofuku
Maru (32o9 tons) passing near by, bound for Nagasaki and the two
ships then joined in the rescue work. The Japanese vessel brought
the ten survivors into Nagasaki about 5: 30 P. M. on the 9th. One
of the ten died later from exposure.
There are enclosed copies of telegrams sent by the owner of
the "No. 8" and the fishing association to Captain Green at Dairen.
It is believed that the head office of the Isthmian Steamship Com-
pany, 50 Trinity Place, New York, will be pleased to learn of this
rescue work.
Incidentally it will be of interest to the Department to note

notice of Your Excellency in advance.

With regard to the question of
application of the 12-mile limit
to the RASHIN MARU Case:
The United States, in searching or
arresting foreign vessels within the l2-mile
limit, was not necessarily exercising a
right as sovereign over the waters in which
such vessels were found. It now is settled
in the United States and recognized else-
where that the territory subject to its
jurisdiction includes (in the language of
the United States Supreme Court) "a
marginal belt of the sea extending from
the coast line outward a marine league,
or three geographic miles". Within such
marginal belt of the sea the United States
is sovereign; while beyond that belt, and
extending from the coast line to a distance
which could be traversed in one hour by
a vessel suspected of seeking to smuggle
or otherwise to evade payment of revenue,
there exists  in the view of the United
States but not of all countries  an area
within which the arrest or search of a
suspected foreign vessel is permissible.
In order that there should be raised no
question of legality in the event of search
or arrest by the United States of suspected
