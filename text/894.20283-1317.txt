I have the honor to transmit herewith as of possible interest copy of a report which I was confidentially shown by an official in charge of intelligence work here and which was submitted to him in March of 1939 by a counter-espionage agent in his service. He showed it to me because the reported clash between Peru and Ecuador had brought it to mind. The curious phrasing is due to the agent not being British.

The remark regarding the Frenchmen in the Canal Company fits in with what the same source told me some months ago, namely that before the war the then Japanese Consul in Port Said had been assiduously cultivating an intimacy with Mr. Douin, "Chef du Transit" of the Canal, a very high official at Ismailia subordinate only to Baron de Benoist (Douin, incidentally, has declared for de Gaulle since the British entered Syria). Enclosure to despatch No. 123 from American Consul at Port Said, Egypt, dated August 3, 1941. Source confidential.

Copy

March 17, 1939

I beg to report that by yesterday's Japanese "YASSUKUNI MARU" (left today, 19.7.39) arrived in Egypt coming from Palao of Peru via Japan, the ex-Prime Minister of Peru, Senor J. DELLA RIVA' AGUERO (Professor) and disembarked in our Port. The Japanese Consul has been ordered by the Japanese Foreign Office to meet him and to render all facilities should he be in need in P. Said.

Senor RIVA' AGUERO, accompanied by the Japanese Chancellor, Mr. Tanabe, after having visited the town on the Consulate's car, came in the Consulate accompanied with his secretary (a young man from Switzerland) and had a long talk with the Japanese Consul Mr. M. Ohno, who closed the doors of his office by key.

Then, the Consul presented me to Senor RIVA' AGUERO and to his Secretary, saying that I am his right hand.

From informations I could have from Japanese sources, as well as from Japanese friends of him of the "YOUSUKUNI MARU", I understood that the Japanese Government is very much interested in Peru and generally in South America, wishing to counter-balance the American influence in this region. Also, it is said, that the Japanese Government asked him whether he is disposed to come back in the Power after his tournee of lectures in Egypt, Italy, etc. The result of his answer has never been known, but it seems to me that for making him so many ceremonies and compliments and honours, it must be something. In Cairo, he will meet Mr. Yokoyama, Japanese Minister in Egypt, as well as the British Ambassador, Sir Miles Lampson. Later, the Japanese Consul told me that Senor RIVA' AGUERO should serve very well the Japanese Empire with not too much money, as some FRENCHMEN of the Canal Company asked. The Japanese movements in South America as well these of Germany are to weaken the American Democratic Influence and propaganda, as well as to affect the Lima's Conference (Peru).

Copied by: JPS
