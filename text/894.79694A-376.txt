## Beginning of Air Mail Service

On the arrival at Taihoku on October 8, 1935, of a Fokker plane from Fukuoka via Naha, capital of the Ryukyu or Loochoo Islands, the Japan Air Transport Company inaugurated a preliminary weekly air mail service between Japan and Taiwan. According to present announcements, a regular air mail and passenger service will be started in April, 1936, with four Douglas DC fourteen-passenger planes making the trip three or four times a week. Exact schedules for the mail and passenger service have not yet been determined.

From now until the beginning of the passenger service in April, the Japan Air Transport Company will use Fokker F7 3M, three 200 HP motors, on the air mail and express service.

The air mail and express service between Fukuoka and Taihoku has been established as follows from October 8, 1935.

## Mail and Express Rates

The air mail postage rates from Taihoku to Japan and Chosen are as follows:

Letters ... Yen 0.33 each 15 grams or fraction thereof
Postcards .. Yen 0.165
Parcels ... Yen 2.27 up to 500 grams (for Japan only).
Yen 2.42 registered.

The express rates are calculated by weight, but no parcel may exceed 1' X 1' X 2' in size. Charges are Yen 2.00 per kilogram from Taihoku to Fukuoka, and Yen 2.50 per kilogram from Taihoku to Tokyo and Osaka.

## Passenger Service

It has also been announced that when the passenger service is started the fare will be Yen 110.00 from Taihoku to Fukuoka.

## Subsidy by Taiwan Government

The budget of the Taiwan Government General as published in the Official Gazette on April 1, 1935, for the 1935 fiscal year which ends March 31, 1936, provides the sum of Yen 180,000 as a subsidy to an air service between Taiwan and Japan. The budget further provides as follows:

"Terms and conditions:

Aeroplanes shall be provided in this air route as prescribed by orders, such aeroplanes having the capacity of over 600 kilograms in weight for freight and passengers and of over 10 cubic feet of measurements for freight and passengers.

"As a rule three return trips every week shall be conducted in this route.

"When considered necessary the Government may order a change of the capacity of aeroplanes or a change in the number of flights.

"Rates for passengers and freight shall be fixed upon approval by the government.

"When considered necessary the government may require a change in rates for passengers and freight.

"When considered necessary for public interests the government may purchase or use the aeroplanes used on the route, by paying reasonable compensation.

"In case of emergency the government may use the aeroplanes, aerodromes, other equipment, crew and staffs on land duty of this route, provided, however, that in such a case due compensation shall be paid.

"When the recipient of the subsidy is a corporation, government's approval is necessary to appointment of directors or auditors, alteration of articles of association, disposition of profits, flotation of debentures, amalgamation or dissolution.

"Subsidy will be granted as prescribed by orders, provided, however, that when the number of flights is reduced or when aeroplanes not complying with the capacity mentioned in the foregoing are used, the amount of the subsidy may be reduced.

"In case of violation of the provisions of the order, a penalty may be imposed on each violation according to the gravity of act.

"When the party to the contract has transferred obligations to other person; when, without justifiable reasons, non-flights in a year exceed the number of non-flights prescribed in the order; when the party to the contract refuses the government's attempt to buy or use aeroplanes for public interests; when the party to the contract refuses the government's attempt in case of emergency to use the aeroplanes, aerodromes, other equipment, crew and staffs on land duty; when the party to the contract alters the articles of association, disposes profits, floats loans, amalgamates or dissolves, or appoints directors or auditors, without permission of the government; or when a foreigner is made a stockholder; the government shall require revocation of the contract, discontinuance of the subsidy, and refund of the subsidy granted already during the year involved.

"The one year mentioned in the preceding clause means from the period from April 1 of that year to March 31 of the following year.

In view of the terms and conditions of the Government subsidy, it is not known what sum is now being paid to the skeleton service inaugurated on October 8, 1935. The service announced to begin next April would appear to meet the conditions of the budget.
