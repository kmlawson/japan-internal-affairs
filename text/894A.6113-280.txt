Subject: Earthquakes in Taiwan affect
the large dam at Uzanto.

From: Vice Consul Charles S. Reed II

Taihoku, Taiwan, Japan. Date of preparation: December 30, 1930.
Date of mailing : December 31, 1930.

FEB 6-1931

=== Page 2 === (confidence 0.97)
-1-

The central and south central regions of the western coastal plains of Taiwan, notably in the districts surrounding Tainan and Kagi, have been visited by a number of earthquakes, unprecedented in number and violence. The first series be inning on the 8th of December and lasting through the spring of the 1960s. The second, of only one day, is the 21st.

There has been considerable property damage from these quakes, numerous residential and public structures having crumbled under the repeated tremors, earth cracks having rendered some sections of roads somewhat impassible, weakened foundations having endangered the safety of a number of bridges.

From the beginning of these seismic disturbances, there has been a great deal of apprehension as to the affect upon the large dam at Uzanto, the reservoir of the Kahan Irrigation Project. Within a few days of the start of the first series of shocks, it was observed that nearly 4,000 cubic yards of earth had slid from the upper face of the dam into the reservoir. By the 10th it was estimated that almost 8,000 cubic yards of earth had collapsed under the repeated shocks and, although the resident engineers stated that the effects of the quakes were to settle the structure rather than to endanger its solidity, preparations were made to drain the reservoir and to repair the damages.

This resolution was strengthened by the effects of the stronger shocks on the 21st which brought about a number of radiating cracks over the entire surface, inner and outer, of the dam.

It is now estimated that the cost of repairs to the

-2-

dam will amount to yen 1,000,000 inasmuch as Mr. Hatta, Chief Engineer of the Taiwan Government General and designer of the undertaking, believes that one-half of the dam should be restored and strengthened. Lack of funds may compel the restoration of those parts only where actual masses of earth have slid down into the reservoir. In the meantime the reservoir is being emptied at the rate of 800 cubic feet per second, complete drainage being arrived at on or about January 6, 1931.

This draining was an important economic repercussion in that there will be no water for irrigation purposes until the latter part of March or early in April. Sugar and rice will be seriously affected unless some remedy can be offered.
