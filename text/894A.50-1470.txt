At the invitation of the Governor General of Taiwan, a party of 11 representatives of leading Japanese banks spent two weeks during the month of November investigating economic and industrial conditions in the island. This visit and the report which the bankers will later make are expected to contribute concretely to the present basic policies enunciated so frequently by the Government General, namely, expansion to South China and the South Seas, and the industrialization of Taiwan. It is recognized that Japanese capital will be required to carry out new industrial projects and for this reason, the Government General is extremely desirous that Japanese bankers interest themselves in the economic development of the island.

It has been often stated that Taiwan has been neglected in the process of Japan's overseas development and although the superiority of resources in Chosen and "Manchukuo" is admitted, local authorities hope that Japanese financiers can be made to recognize the special nature and importance of Taiwan's economic and agricultural resources. They point out that the contributions which Taiwan can make to the Japan-China-Manchuria bloc are unique and that the extension of Japanese influence in China and the South Seas has opened up new and valuable opportunities. Although the bankers stated that a detailed report to the Government General would be made only subsequent to their return to Japan and after mature consideration, various opinions were expressed, both individually and at round table conferences held in Taihoku. For purposes of clarity, the comments of the bankers regarding conditions in Taiwan are summarized as follows:

**Favorable Factors in Taiwan's Economic Situation**

1. Taiwan is favored geographically, from the points of view of national security, economic expansion southward, and abundance of tropical agricultural products.

3. Taiwan is efficiently administered. Economic control is easier to exercise than in Japan.

3. The zeal of local officials in promoting industrial and economic expansion is a far more severe.

4. Scientific experiments and research which have been carried on in Taiwan are praiseworthy.

5. Taiwan enjoys agricultural prosperity. The plains in the western part of the island are an advantageous factor. The lot of the Formosan farmer is far better than that of the Korean or Manchurian farmer.

6. Taiwan is fortunate in its coal resources and in its potential water power for the manufacture of electricity.

**Unfavorable factors in Taiwan's Economic Situation**

1. Transportation facilities in Taiwan are inadequate. Main line railways are not yet completely double-tracked. Port facilities are inadequate.

2. There is a labor shortage in Taiwan.

3. Resources other than agricultural are unexpectedly poor.

4. In many parts of the island the soil is poor. This places more dependence upon cultivation methods and fertilization.

5. Irrigation projects are insufficient.

**Recommendations**

=== Page 7 === (confidence 0.94)

## Recommendations

1. Railway construction, including double-tracking of present lines and replacement of railway through a heavier type.
2. A 110 mm division of 1000 liters fort project.
3. Further and more thorough scientific research and investigation by young experts from Japan.
4. Irrigation projects, especially in the east of Taiwan, where rivers are plentiful but still remain in their natural state.
5. Increased use of machinery to offset labor shortage.
6. Concentration on manufacturing projects which will utilize available agricultural products (such as manufacturing pulp, fiber, bagasse, alcohol, sugar, all from sugar cane).
7. Utilization of cheap power and labor in Taiwan plus raw materials from South China and the South Seas to manufacture products for re-export to South China and the South Seas.
8. The bankers warn against over-emphasis on new industries at the expense of existing ones.

A-995, November 30, 2 p.m. Jananese Economic-Political.

Potentialities of Formosa - In an article entitled "Within the Interior Zone", the KRAKAUER ZEITUNG of November 1, 1944, stated the following facts relative to the economic and geopolitical importance of Formosa:

In view of Formosa belonging to the realm of the "interior administration" of Japan, the recent American action against that island is not only of military but also of considerable political importance. Formosa (in the words of Otani, the President of the Central Union of Japanese Industries) was to constitute "the central point within a radius of 4,000 miles of the Great Asiatic Prosperity Sphere under Japanese dominion". The American thrust at the heart of this zone, has now finally prompted the "long-postponed activity" of the Japanese navy.

In surveying the economic potentialities of the island, the rice harvest of 1940/1941 is quoted as 17,500,000 da (Doppelzentner=100 kilograms). Certain statistics regarding pre-war imports and exports are also quoted, which, "though not necessarily valid any longer", are considered indicative also of present trends.
