The Honorable
The Secretary of State,
Washington.

Sir:

I have the honor to acknowledge the receipt of the Department's instruction No. 814733/54 655 of December 21, 1929, directing the Embassy to investigate a statement of Professor George H. Blakeslee, which appeared in the "Christian Science Monitor" of December 2, 1929, to the effect that the Japanese Government "had induced the various

=== Page 2 === (confidence 0.98)
-2-

various press organizations in Japan to accept a radical cut in deferred rates".

I have been reliably informed that agreement for reduction of regular press rates and the institution of a deferred press rate was arrived at in New York City on October 15, 1929, through the efforts of the Pacific Cable Company and the Radio Corporation of America, in telegraphic communication with the Japanese Government. Regular press rates and theretofore been 36 cents a word and there had been no deferred press rate. By this agreement this rate was reduced to 18 cents and a deferred rate of 9 cents was instituted. Announcement of these rates was made shortly before the conference of the Institute of Pacific Relations opened at Kyoto.

By the phrase "press organizations", which appears in Professor Blakeslee's statement, is apparently meant the various news agencies and associations which are represented here.

I have the honor to be, Sir,

Your obedient servant,

Edwin L. Neville

Chargé d'Affaires ad interim.

The Honorable

Washington, D. C.

My dear Mr. Secretary:

Receipt is acknowledged of your letter of the 8th instant, reference FE 894.733/6, transmitting copy of despatch No. 1395 from the American Embassy at Tokyo concerning an agreement for reduction of press rates and the institution of deferred press rates between this country and Japan involved in the Pacific Cable Company and the Radio Corporation of America.

This Department has no information on the subject.

Inquiry at the Federal Radio Commission has been made but information on the subject is lacking there. Also inquiry has been made at the Interstate Commerce Commission with like result.

The only correspondence this office has had with reference to reduced press rates to China is contained in a letter from the State Department dated January 30, 1930, reference SO 811.91293/180. This involves the Press Wireless Incorporated having its head offices in Chicago.

Very truly yours,

E. M. Morgan
Acting Secretary of Commerce.

January 14, 1930.

Dear Mr. Tate:

Commander Fisher of the Division of Naval Communications, telephoned me this morning that a settlement of the dispute with the Japanese radio station over the frequency 19.8 kilocycles, has been amicably adjusted and requested that no further action be taken and that steps be taken to prevent any representations to the Japanese Government by our Embassy at Tokyo.
