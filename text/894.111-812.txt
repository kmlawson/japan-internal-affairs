In a pamphlet prepared by the Foreign Office and dis-

"The old ordinance (Ordinance No. 1, Ministry

has, therefore, been revised, with due regard to

kept in view in making this revision has been to
facilitate and make more effective the protection
and control of foreigners staying in Japan".
Undoubtedly the new legislation is partly responsive
to a need felt for the stricter control of aliens living
in Japan under conditions of national mobilization and
partly to give precise legal definition to various methods

15 days or more, and aliens arriving for "transit",
a sojourn of less than 15 days.
2. The existing procedure whereby a prefectural
governor may grant special permission for "entry" or
"transit" has been given legal definition in the
ordinance. Fees are charged for such permission more
or less on a basis of what would have been charged had
the normal visa procedure obtained.
3. The new ordinance provides for stricter control
over the sojourn of aliens in Japan for a period of
thirty days or more, requiring special permission for

setting a maximum limit of one year for sojourns
granted under such circumstances.

report of residence to the Chief of the competent

landing, modifying in this respect an ordinance of
1899 which only required reports of aliens desiring
to sojourn for 90 days or more.

7. The powers of the prefectural governors in respect

TOURIST TRADE IN RELATION

In view of the fact that Japan had, previous to
the coming into power of the present Government Administra-
tion, represented by the Hamaguchi Cabinet, been in
the midst of a prolonged business and industrial depres-
sion, this Government decided from the beginning to make
economic retrenchment and rehabilitation of the country's
financial position a definite part of its policy. One
of the first steps taken by the new Government toward
achieving its object was to plan the lifting of the
gold embargo which had become the subject of study and
attack by an influential section of the financial and
business community. As to do this called for efforts
to improve Japan's position in relation to its unfavor-
able trade balance, an investigation group, known as the
International Trade Balance Commission was created by
the Government. The possibilities of increasing Japan's
tourist trade, and the probable favorable effects there-
of on the foreign trade balance, was investigated by
this Commission which subsequently recommended the
establishment of an organ within the Government to
foster tourist trade in an active way. It was also re-
commended that an advisory commission composed of
individuals prominent in public or business life be
organized.
31

FUNCTIONS AND PLANS OF
NEW BOARD OF TOURIST INDUSTRY
The newly organized Board of Tourist Industry
is not intended to take over functions of the previously
organized Japan Tourist Bureau. There is, and will
continue to be limited duplication, but the Tourist
Bureau will operate largely as before. It will be
relieved, however, of much of its expensive burden
of foreign advertising and tourist trade promotion.
On the other hand, the Board of Tourist Industry
represents Government recognition of the tourist industry
as a national affair, and constitutes an organization
through which the Department of Railways, for the
National Government, will give substantial financial
support to the promotion of the industry in question.
The plans that have been drawn up by the new board
indicate the nature of its proposed activities.
According to a statement issued by its Director,
the Board of Tourist Industry was created within the
Department of Railways in June, 1930, for the purpose
of improving the tourist trade in Japan. The chief
features of its program are set forth as follows:
1. Supervision and assistance of corporations
participating in the tourist industry.
2. Collection and compilation of material and
statistios necessary for promoting the business.
3. Improvements in facilities at pleasure resorts
and sightseeing places.
4. Improvement of hotel accommodations.
5. Support and improvement of other enterprises
involved in the tourist trade.
6. Systematization of advertising Japan abroad,
so that advertisers may be in touch with each
other.
It

It is not expected that the work of the Board
will interfere with the activities of the Japan
Tourist Bureau. The sphere of activity of the Board
tends mostly to instruction, support, and mediation
in connection with those who participate in the
tourist industry.
To help improve hotel accommodations the Board
will advance loans to hotels on easy terms, with sums
loaned by the Finance Ministry. Attention will also
be given toward improving such native products as
meet with favor among tourists. The Board will undertake
to regulate the tourist guide business, to improve
and unify guide activities. The establishment of
national parks and conservation of the scenic beauties
of Japan will be another feature of its activities.
Commission of Tourist Industry:
As an advisory organization, the Commission of

Japan is possessed of the factors necessary
to attract foreign tourists, namely, unique scenery,
an abundance of places of historical interest,
contrasting customs and manners, shrines, temples,
and festivals that provide entertainment. Hence
there is ample reason for the promotion of tourist
trade.
The American people are known in Japan as the
greatest travelers in the world. As a result much
of the effort in question will be exerted toward
the United States. With improved means of attract-
