Washington, D. C.

Sir:

I have the honor to inform the Department that the local press of February 26, 1933, reports that the Mexican Government has granted permission for the Japanese gunboats YOKUMO and INATE, training ships of the Imperial Japanese Navy, to make courtesy calls at Mexican ports, and the news article states that according to information obtained from the Ministry of Communications both vessels contemplate arriving at the port of Acapulco on April 30th and leaving the port of Manzanillo on May 29th. The news item also mentions that a commission of Mexican naval cadets will be sent to Acapulco to extend a welcome to the Japanese cadets, and invite them while in Acapulco to visit Mexico City.

It

=== Page 2 === (confidence 1.00)
-2-

It will be recalled that both the vessels named visited the port of Manzanillo in 1925 and at that time a commission headed by General Arturo Bernal was sent by the Mexican Government to Manzanillo to extend a welcome to the officers and cadets of these two training ships.

During the course of a conversation which I had with the Minister for Foreign Affairs on February 22nd, Doctor Puig stated that he had been informed by the Japanese Minister, Mr. Hori, regarding the proposed visit of the Japanese naval vessels, and that the Japanese Minister proposed giving a reception at the Japanese Legation in Mexico City in honor of the Admiral in command of the squadron. Doctor Puig told me that Mr. Hori had inquired whether it would be correct to invite the President of Mexico to the occasion, and that he had replied that there was no objection to inviting the President.

Mr. Hori informed me yesterday that as there were about three hundred midshipmen on this cruise, and as the transportation facilities between Mexico City and Acapulco are limited, he was making arrangements for only the Admiral and his Staff to come to Mexico City.

Respectfully yours,

Arthur Bliss Lane.

With reference to the Embassy's despatch No. 2338 of March 2, 1933, in which the Department was informed that the Mexican Government had granted permission for the Japanese gunboats YAKUMO and IWATE, training ships of the Imperial Japanese Navy, to make courtesy calls at Mexican ports, I have the honor to inform the Department that the two training ships mentioned arrived at Acapulco at 10:00 a.m. on the morning of April 30, 1933. On April 28, 1933, according to an article in EL UNIVERSAL of April 29, 1933, a Japanese transport, the TAIBUN MARU, carrying fuel for the training ships, arrived at Acapulco.

Chapital of the Mexican Navy, who had been commissioned by the Mexican Department of War and Marine as an Aide to Vice Admiral Hyakutake and sent to Acapulco to extend to the Vice Admiral, his officers and cadets a welcome to Mexico on behalf of its Government and people. Upon the arrival of the ships at Acapulco, the Mexican gunboat BRAVO returned the customary salute of the visiting Japanese vessels and the usual courtesy calls were exchanged between the visiting Japanese naval officers and the officers of the port of Acapulco.

Vice Admiral Hyakutake, Captain Kasuke Susuki, Commander of the IWATE, Captain Masaichi Niimi, Commander of the YAKUMO and 36 officers and cadets left Acapulco in automobiles for Mexico City early on the morning of May 1st, arriving at the Capital at 9 p.m. that evening.

"It gives me great satisfaction to have the opportunity to pay my respects to His Excellency the President of the Republic on this occasion of the visit of the Training Fleet to Mexico, a brother country with which Japan maintains the most cordial and friendly relations.

"This Training Fleet has as its principal object the practical instruction of the cadets and at the same time to acquaint them with the customs and geography of the countries which they visit, thus fulfilling their

I attribute this situation to the virtue of the noble Mexican nation which has identified itself with our own.

"The Japanese people appreciate and respect the excellent virtues and qualities which are characteristic of the Mexican nation, such as bravery, sacrifice and perseverance, for which, together with a profound love of country, the young cadets of the Military School died, and it is my earnest desire to pay my tribute to the Boy Heroes.

# MEMORANDUM

The personnel of the Japanese naval training ships IWATE and YAKUMO, commanded by Vice-Admiral Zengo Yoshida, which anchored in the Port of Manzanillo on September 20, 1936, arrived in Mexico City on September 22 and were given an enthusiastic reception at the station. The Vice-Admiral was accompanied to Mexico City by Lieutenant Commander Takashi Kanoya; Captain Haijime Miyata, Chief of Marines of the squadron; Captain Fikuzo Harada, Chief Medical Officer; Captain Shinji Fujita, Chief Paymaster; Captain Toshisa Nakamura, Commander of the Flagship YAKUMO; Captain Kakuji Kakuda, Commander of the IWATE; twenty-six medial officers, 280 cadets, and 18 sailors.

A large crowd gathered at the Colonia Station at 5 o'clock to receive the visitors, but the special train which brought them from Manzanillo did not arrive until nine in the evening. Among those present were: a committee delegated by the Ministry of War and Navy and headed by Commander Coello, Chief of the Navy Department; a group of naval officials; officers of the local garrison; Minoru Izawa, Second Secretary, Translator and Chargé d'Affaires ad interim of the Japanese Legation; the personnel of the Japanese Legation and members of the Japanese Colony in Mexico. Japanese and Mexican women carried bouquets of flowers.

The Japanese officers had been met in Manzanillo by Commodore Luis Schaufelberg and Lieutenant Carlos R. Berzunza of the Mexican Navy, who have spent several years in Japan.

The War Department band, which was at the station, played the Japanese and Mexican national anthems, after which the cadets paraded up the Paseo de la Reforma and thence to the hotels where reservations had been made for them.

Mr. Izawa translated the short conversation between Vice Admiral Yoshida and representatives of the press. The Vice Admiral is said to have an extensive knowledge of Mexican history and he stated that from his youth he had been attracted toward Mexico. He was very glad, he said, that the traditional relations between Japan and Mexico, far from being disrupted, are being strengthened.

"I hope," he said, "that the cadets will learn much in spite of the fact that we are going to be here such a short time, as they have prepared themselves on the history and geography of this nation. I regret that owing to the enforced absence of the President of the Republic I cannot greet him, but I shall present my respects to the Secretaries of War and Foreign Relations and shall express my gratitude for this undeserved reception which has been rendered us."

The Vice Admiral has seen thirty-five years of constant service. "The first combat at which I was present", he told newspaper men, "was the Battle of the Sea of Japan, under the orders of Admiral Togo. In that same war of 1904 these

=== Page 26 === (confidence 1.00)
-2-

these ships which are today under my command did great service."

On the following day, September 23, Vice Admiral Yoshida, accompanied by Captain Miyata, the Commanders of the two vessels, Commodore Schaufelberger, Lieutenant Berzunza, Mr. Izawa and Captain Kamanoka, Naval Attaché to Mexico, made a courtesy call at the Ministry of War and Marine, and at the Ministry for Foreign Affairs. They also mounted guard at the shrine to Mexico's heroes at the Monument of the Independence. At two in the afternoon a banquet was given in their honor by the Ministry of War at the Club France. Lieutenant Berzunza, who served as interpreter at the courtesy visits, gave a toast in Japanese, which was answered by Vice Admiral Yoshida.

At noon, the sailors made a brief courtesy call at the Department of the Federal District. The Chief of the Department, Señor Cosme Hinojosa, being absent, they were received by Secretary General Antonio Villalobos and Chief Clerk Adolfo Ruiz Cortines, with whom the sailors conducted a short conversation through an interpreter.

The high officials of the Department delegated Major José Escudero Andrade, Private Secretary of the Secretary General, to repay the visit of the Japanese sailors by serving them a "champagne lunch" at the Hotel Regis.

That evening the Japanese Chargé d'Affaires held a formal reception at the Legation, attended by the Diplomatic Corps and numerous Mexican officials.

On September 24, Admiral Yoshida gave a banquet at his hotel to the Minister for Foreign Affairs, high Army and Navy officers, and other officials of the Mexican Government. He seized the occasion to express his gratitude for all the manifestations of friendship of which his companions and subordinates had been made the object during their stay in Mexico City, not only on the part of the naval, military and civil authorities, but by the people in general; and said that in leaving they were taking with them a pleasant impression of Mexico and its traditional hospitality which they would never forget. Secretary of Foreign Relations Hay thanked him on behalf of the Mexican Government for the kind sentiments expressed by him and recalled his own stay in Japan, where, he declared, "Mexicans are also received with open arms." He rendered homage of admiration to the Japanese Navy and expressed his best wishes for a happy return to the visitors' native land. General Blas Corral of the Ministry of War, on behalf of the Secretary and Assistant Secretary of War, spoke a few words in praise of Japan and begged Vice Admiral Yoshida to convey the special greeting of the Mexican Army and Navy to those of the Empire of the Rising Sun.

The visitors left Mexico on the evening of September 24 in a special car attached to the regular train to Guadalajara. Numerous naval and military delegations, representatives of the Ministry of Foreign Affairs, and members of the Japanese Legation appeared at the station to bid them farewell. Vice Admiral Yoshida and his men were cheered and applauded by the crowd as the train departed.

The end of their stay in Mexico City was saddened by the news received through the Japanese Legation on the morning of the day of their departure, that a luanch carrying drinking water to their vessels in Manzanillo had capsized and that three sailors had drowned. For this reason there was no music at the banquet given by the Vice Admiral and all plans for further entertainment were cancelled.

Washouts near Ateniqueque, 460 kilometers from Mexico City, detained the visitors in Ciudad Guzmán, and Vice Admiral Yoshida offered the Superintendent of Railroads the sea aid of his sailors in repairing the track, though they were without suitable equipment. Two hundred cadets and officers handled wheelbarrows and rolled stones into place, with the result that traffic was restored to normal in two hours. The correspondent of EL UNIVERSAL in Guadalajara reported that "very laudatory comments are being made on the conduct of Vice Admiral Yoshida and his sailors."

Upon arriving at the Port of Manzanillo on September 25, the Japanese sent a cordial message of thanks to several Mexican officials. They also sent a message to the President of the National Railroads of Mexico, thanking him for his kindness in placing a special train at his disposal to carry the men from Manzanillo and back again and lauding the efficiency of the railroad service.

The two training ships weighed anchor the same day for Honolulu.

Sir:

I have the honor to advise the Embassy that I have been informed that the S. S. ITSU KUSHIMA MARU is expected to arrive at Tampico between the 25th and 30th of March and that while at this port it will take on fuel oil at the Aguila wharf. From here it will proceed to Tuxpam where it will take on Poza Rica crude from the Petromex. It has not been possible to ascertain where this vessel will proceed to after leaving Tuxpam.

The S. S. ITSU KUSHIMA MARU is said to be chartered by the Centralamer, Ltd., of which a Mr. D. A. Thomas is the managing director. The office of this concern is in the Hotel Reforma at Mexico City. This company has also chartered the S. S. JANE MAERSK and the S. S. CAROLINE MAERSK for the purpose of taking on cargoes of Poza Rica crude from the Petromex. The S. S. Caroline Maersk loaded 86,500 barrels on February 25th last, destined, it is said, for Antwerp. The Petromex is said to have very heavy supplies of crude on hand, approximately 300,000 barrels in its own tanks and over 1,000,000 barrels in tanks belonging to the Aguila Company and unless it manages to dispose of this oil it will be placed in a difficult position. In addition the Petromex is reported to the Aguila Company over 500,000 pesos for work done and facilities supplied by the latter company.

Very respectfully yours,

(Signed:) L. S. Armstrong,
American Consul.
