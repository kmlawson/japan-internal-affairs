January 31, 1935.

Senator Arthur Vandenberg,
Senate Office Bldg.,
Washington, D. C.

Dear Senator:

Mr. Charles Staff of the Larrowe Milling Company, Detroit, Michigan, is interested in securing detailed information regarding the Japanese sugar industry and particularly Japanese production of beet pulp.

It seems that some Japanese beet pulp is being imported into the United States. This pulp comes in bales of 130 pounds, instead of sacks, and the information we have is that while the quality is good, the imported pulp is hard as rock and has to be sawed to pieces before using.

The information which we have been able to secure from the Department of Commerce, is not detailed and covers sugar production rather than beet pulp. We are advised that an official request from you to the Secretary of State would assist us in securing a special report through the United States Commercial Attache at Japan, which report would be requested of him by the United States Department of State.

The Larrowe Milling Company is apprehensive because of the shipment of the beet pulp to the United States from Japan and from a business standpoint they desire to keep posted as to possibilities that might follow from this competition. They desire to know how much beet pulp is produced in Japan and its possessions; how much is consumed in Japan and how much is exported; the amount of beet pulp exports to the United States from Japan, and also what the Japanese have done in past years with their dry beet pulp.

Trusting that you will assist us in securing this important information by addressing the Secretary of State in our behalf, we are,

# Production of Beet Sugar and Pulp

Production of sugar beets, beet sugar and pulp in Japan is annually increasing and according to plans for the future, noted below, production will be doubled by the growing season of 1936-37. There are appended hereto statistics upon production of Japanese-grown sugar beets for the years 1930 to date. It will be noted that in the season just ended, 1934-35, which extends from April, the month of seeding, until February, the month in which manufacturing processes are usually completed, 24,623 acres were seeded to sugar beets, producing 501,466,800 pounds of beets. From this amount sugar to the total of 77,698,700 pounds was produced with 32,663,800 pounds of pulp. Practically all sugar beets raised in Japan are grown in the northern island of Hokkaido, which is climatically suited to such production. There are only two large manufacturers of beet sugar in Japan, the Hokkaido Seito Kabushiki Kaisha located at Obihiro, Tokachinokuni, Hokkaido, with a plant in the same city with a daily slicing capacity of 700 short tons, and the Meiji Seito Kabushiki Kaisha with head offices at 8 Kyobashi 2-chome, Kyobashiku, Tokyo, and with a factory at Shimizu Kojo, Shizu, Tokachinokuni, Hokkaido. This factory has a daily slicing capacity of 600 short tons. The same corporation likewise is engaged in refining cane sugar. Each of the corporations plans to establish an additional factory with a daily slicing capacity of 600 short tons to be located in Hokkaido.

## Uses and Export of Sugar Beet Pulp

Beet pulp is used in Japan for the feeding of milch cows raised in the territories adjacent to sugar beet factories. Until the beet sugar year of 1933-34, beet pulp was wholly consumed in the Hokkaido, but in that year, owing to the remarkably good crop, efforts were made for the first time to export the surplus. The first shipment of beet pulp was made on consignment in early November of 1934, totaling 500 bales, each bale containing 100 Kin or 132 pounds. After this first sample consignment, the unit quantity of the bales for export was changed to 100 pounds, although the unit quantities for domestic transactions are still 100 Kin or 132 pounds.

Since the latter part of November 1934 and up to the end of January 1935, 3600 long tons of beet pulp have been shipped to the Atlantic Coast of the United States and 25 long tons to the Pacific Coast. The foregoing shipments were all handled by the Mitsubishi Shoji Kaisha (Mitsubishi Trading Company), which maintains offices in the United States at the following addresses:

Equitable Building,
New York, New York.

55 New Montgomery Street,
San Francisco, Calif.

1703-1707 Exchange Building,
Seattle, Washington.

No shipments have been made to any other country with the exception of a sample consignment sent to European countries by a British Trading Company located in Tokyo, the Leybold Shokwan K. K.

## Expansion of Sugar Beet and Beet Pulp Industry

An agreement has been reached between the two mentioned sugar beet manufacturing concerns to increase production in the growing season beginning April 1935 to 35,700 acres as compared with 24,623 acres seeded during the season just past. In the growing season beginning April 1936, 49,000 acres will be seeded, at which time it is believed that the two new factories now contemplated will be completed. Until that time the increased production of sugar beets will be handled by working full time shifts in the two factories now in operation. It is believed probable, therefore, that Japanese exports of beet pulp will continue at the present rate and will probably increase unless natural factors such as crop failures prevent. It is not expected that there will be any exportation of beet sugar as the total amount is consumed in Japan.
