The Honorable
The Secretary of State,
Washington.

Sir:

I have the honor to report that I have been approached by Mr. Shingoro Takaishi, Editor in Chief of the OSAKA NICHI and TOKYO NICHI, one of the largest newspaper in Japan, in regard to an equestrian exhibition in Tokyo and Osaka to which it is desired to invite American Army equestrians to take part, together with Japanese riders, before the end of June, or after August, 1935. Mr. Takaishi informs me that he has obtained the approval and encouragement of Prince Higashi-kuni, an Imperial Prince in command of

=== Page 2 === (confidence 0.99)
-2-

of the Fourth Army Division in Osaka, and of Lieutenant General Oshima, President of the Nippon Equestrian Society, and hopes to obtain the definite cooperation of the Japanese Army authorities. Mr. Takaishi states that the newspaper will assume the responsibility for the arrangements, and defray all the expenses in connection with the proposed plan. This includes, I am informed, transportation to and from Japan as well as expense while in Japan.

I can see no objection to American Army officers participating in these events provided the Japanese Army gives its approval and ensures the participation of Japanese officers. Visits to Japan by foreign athletic teams and associations have been decidedly helpful in creating good feeling, at least with a certain section of the public, and while public equestrian exhibitions by Army officers are not quite in the same category, I believe that they might be of distinct value. The public interest and good feeling arising in the United States out of Lieutenant Baron Nishi's brilliant performances in Los Angeles in 1932 might well be duplicated by American military riders in Japan. Major Crane, the Military Attaché of the Embassy, concurs in this view.

A copy of Mr. Takaishi's letter to me of December 24 is enclosed. It will be noted that five or six American army riders are desired and that a reply to the invitation is requested at an early date. A subsequent letter of the same date, copy of which is also enclosed, states that late information indicates that the United States War Department is

THE TOKYO NICHI-NICHI
Tokyo, December 14th, 1934.

Dear Mr. Grew,

With reference to my conversation with you this morning, regarding the plan to invite American army eques-trians to Japan for exhibitions with Japanese experts in the same field, I am very grateful to note that you will be willing to help us by approaching unofficially the Department of War, or whatever necessary authorities, of your government to have their a proval to send five or six military equestrians over here.

It is with pleasure to state that if the plan were materialized, it will not only increase the interest of equestrian sports as a whole, which already finds great popularity here in Japan, but will serve to help in promoting the friendship between your country and mine.

We have already approached some leading Japanese equestrian organizations with the proposed plan, and they have all expressed their approval of it and their willingness for close cooperation. H.I.H. Prince Higashi-kuni, Commander of the Fourth Army Division in Osaka, expressing his keen interest, has given me a strong encouragement. Lieutenant-General M. Oshima, President of the Nippon Equestrian Society, has also given me words to actively cooperate with us. We are getting in touch with our army authorities to obtain their definite cooperation for it. Depending, therefore, mostly upon the approval on the part of the American authorities, I feel sure that

=== Page 5 === (confidence 0.99)
-2-

that it will make quite a success and will do a great deal of good in creating better feelings between the peoples of the two countries.

I wish to add that the Osaka Mainichi and the Tokio Nichi-Nichi will assume the whole responsibility and defray all the expenses in connection with the proposed plan. The exhibitions will be held under the auspices of both papers, probably with some equestrian organizations or public institutions as co-sponsors. We would like to have them held some time before the end of June, or after August, 1935, mainly in Tokio and Osaka.

Thanking you for your kind attention, and hoping to hear from you favourably soon, I remain,

Yours very faithfully,

(Signed) Shingoro Takaishi,
Editor in Chief.

Joseph C. Grew, Esq.,
American Ambassador,
Tokio.

THE TOKYO NICHI-NICHI
Tokyo, December 24th, 1934.

Dear Mr. Grew,

Following up my previous letter of today, I have just received words from my New York correspondent to the effect that the State Department of Washington had kindly conveyed to the War Department our request for approval of inviting some American officers for equestrian exhibitions to be held in Japan, but that the War Department is not in favour of the proposal.

I am very much disappointed at this report, but we have not yet given up our hope and wish to have the plan materialized in some way or other. In the meantime, therefore, I shall greatly appreciate if you will be so kind as to communicate to Washington our proposed plan as mentioned in my previous letter and ask their further consideration.

With kindest regards, I am

Yours very faithfully,

(Signed) Shingoro Takashi.

Joseph C. Grew, Esq.,
American Ambassador,
Tokio.

The honorable

The Secretary of State.

Dear Mr. Secretary:

With reference to your letter of February 1, 1935 (FE 894.4065/1), transmitting a copy of a despatch from the American Ambassador at Tokyo, with its enclosures, the Secretary of War requests me to advise you as follows.

The War Department deeply appreciates the courtesy of the generous invitation of Mr. Shingoro Takaishi, Editor in Chief of the Osaka Mainichi and Tokyo Nichi Nichi, to American Army officers to participate in an international equestrian exhibition which may be held in Japan this year. Unfortunately, the proximity of the 1936 Olympic Games, and the preparation necessary therefor, precludes sending a suitable representation of army horses and riders to Japan this year.

Sincerely yours,

General,
Chief of Staff.
