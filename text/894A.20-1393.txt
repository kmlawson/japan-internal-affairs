December 11, 1941

MEMORANDUM

Since April, 1941, the Province of Takao (S. Formosa) has been closed to foreigners, even resident Spanish missionaries having been required to leave. An Italian freighter which had been at refuge in Takao since June, 1940, was ordered by the Japanese naval authorities to move to Kobe. The whole of the southern half of Formosa has become a military garrison (strength of effectives not ascertainable); the beaches are fortified against possible attack, and the harbor of Takao has been steadily improved for troops embarkations (the transports themselves being based at the Pescadores since Takao is open to heavy seas throughout the summer months and is not suitable for basing large numbers of transports).

Throughout the Island there has been almost continuous air raid drill. This consists largely of training the Formosan public to cooperate in extinguishing fires and controlling lights. Anti-aircraft guns have not been visible (or audible) on any of these occasions and it is impossible to say if and where they exist. Searchlights have been of antique pattern.

Up until the end of July, Japanese war planes seen in the sky over North Formosa were of not very modern design and their speed seemed very moderate. From the end of July, planes of more modern design and higher speed were seen. Planes based on the northern airdrome at Taihoku were used for bombing the China coast (Fukien and Chekiang Provinces), the main defense and striking force being concentrated in the south of the Island, opposite Hong Kong. It is presumed that the authorities consider that any hostile air attack would come from the south and that it could be broken up before getting farther north.

There are few air raid shelters in the principal towns, and much publicity was being given to the need for building more. Japanese householders in many cases being canvassed and ordered to construct one for each group of houses, but these facilities were (up to the end of July) quite inadequate. Experiments were going on for the conversion of drain-water to drinking water in case of emergency. "Emergency, emergency, emergency" and "preparedness, preparedness, preparedness" were the constant cries of the newspapers and the armies.

In (?) may, the Governor General introduced a volunteer system for Formosans. A similar system had been introduced into Korea in 1938 and had met with some success, the young Koreans having become saturated with the fervent nationalism taught in the Japanese Army. The nebulous mysticism which is the core of this spirit is essentially Oriental in character and it is a mistake to suppose that a governed people like the Koreans and Formosans, in spite of the subordinate positions in which they are deliberately held, regard it as alien and compulsory. In the hands of the Japanese educationalists, as they have been for a quarter and a half a century respectively, their minds have been methodically conditioned to this doctrine and the young generation now rising will be a tremendously powerful instrument in the Japanese nationalists' hands unless steps are taken now or very soon to break the Japanese military power and discredit their doctrines in the minds of the young.

If it is left much longer, action may already be too late. The jubilation and martial air of the Formosan children when marching in procession to celebrate the opening of the army ranks to Formosan volunteers was not assumed to order, although the processions themselves were, of course, organized. It cannot be emphasized too strongly that the conditioning of the young for national expansion is older and more fanatical (vide their school textbooks and, for adults, popular literature) than anything Germany has known.

The intention is quite definitely to strive to throw the white man out of the Far East, including Germans, Italians and what have you. Any temporary modification of their program is purely a matter of expedience--a marking time until the moment is ripe. What the military will not, and indeed cannot, give up is the program on which they have embarked.

The Japanese as a nation are not anti-British or anti-American--they are anti-White and they, themselves, have no love racially for the Germans, but cannot help themselves, hence we get the backlash.

Canadian and American citizens of Japanese ancestry cannot be trusted. I have definite knowledge of where they have betrayed the countries of their citizenship--and when caught their excuse is they were forced by Japanese authorities against their own desires--highly debatable!

# EMBASSY OF THE UNITED STATES OF AMERICA

No. 134. Chungking, China, July 21, 1943.

Subject: Information Concerning Formosa.

CONFIDENTIAL

The Honorable
The Secretary of State,
Washington, D. C.

Sir:

There are enclosed translations of the documents containing confidential information, principally of a military character, with respect to Formosa entitled "Increasing Significance of Formosa in International Problems" and "Further Establishments of Air Force Bases in Formosa", obtained from Formosans in Chungking associated with the Taiwan Revolutionary League (台籍革命同盟會).

It is stated in the first of these two documents that the Japanese anticipate that the United States will employ a strategic plan involving an attack on Formosa instead of an island to island offensive and that the Japanese are making preparations to meet such an attack. It is also stated that Formosan production (presumably agricultural) has been reduced 20% to 30% since the outbreak of the Pacific War as a result of conscription of approximately 280,000 men, reduction of farm land through its use for military installations and roads and through the excessive butchering of farm cattle.

The second enclosure contains a list of airfields in Formosa together with information concerning each (a copy of this enclosure has been furnished to General Stilwell's Headquarters.)

The Embassy is not, of course, in a position to verify the information contained in these two documents and cannot vouch for the reliability of the persons who furnished them.

Respectfully yours,

George Atcheson, Jr.
American Charge d'Affaires a.i.

Enclosure No. 1 to despatch No. 134 of July 21, 1943, from George Atcheson, Jr., American Charge d'Affaires a.i. at Chungking, China, on the subject of Information Concerning Formosa.

(Translation)

CONFIDENTIAL DATA OF (金山) RESEARCH OFFICE

Formosa News No. 223.

1. INCREASING SIGNIFICANCE OF FORMOSA IN INTERNATIONAL PROBLEMS.

Ever since the enemy's discovery of the American strategic plans for the Pacific theater along the following lines: "counter-offensive in the South Sea islands would not be as satisfactory as outright occupation of Formosa to cut off the entire Japanese naval forces in the South Seas from their own country, so that the latter naval forces may be rendered entirely useless and a comparatively timely result may be obtained", he has decided to prepare new harbors to serve as naval bases and to strengthen the defense of the southern and northern tips of Formosa, i.e. the submarine bases in (小清水) and Central (蘇湖). Because of the recent recovery of Attu Island by U.S. forces and the incessant attacks on Japanese ships by submarines of unknown nationality in Hongkong Bay and north of Pescadores Bay (崇湖), the enemy's conviction that he would be able to crush U.S. naval forces has been shattered. He is therefore openly instigating anti-American feelings among Formosans. The important members of the captives of alien nationality originally interned in Formosa have gradually been escorted to Japan. According to the public opinion of Formosa in general, it is believed that American attack on Formosa is quite possible. And it is hoped that China, the fatherland, will intensify various works in connection with the recovery of Formosa.

2. THE ENEMY'S PLAN TO PUT POSSIBLE WAR OPERATIONS IN THE SOUTHERN PLAIN OF FORMOSA UNDER COVER HAS BEEN COMPLETED.

The decisive battle for the occupation of Formosa will be fought on the southwestern plain of that island, where 70% of Formosa's inhabitants live. When the enemy first occupied Formosa, the region was a wilderness with dense forests. The Formosans, taking advantage of this natural cover, had engaged in guerrilla warfare against the enemy. As a result, the enemy adopted a policy of eradicating the menace, burnt all the forests, and turned the wilderness into cultivated lands, so that nothing might be left for use as guerrilla battlefields. At the commencement of this war, the enemy was convinced of the absence of cover for war operations on the plain and ordered southern Formosa to carry out the tree-plantation movement, in which the villages, farm lands and various roads were planted with trees. As a result of this, 20% of farm land has been lost. At the beginning, the Japanese chanted the Formosans and stated that the tree-plantation aimed at

3. SHARP DECREASE OF FORMOSA'S PRODUCTIVE POWER.

The reasons are as follows:

a. More than 280,000 producers and able-bodied male adults have been conscripted for military service. Of these men 70% are scattered in the South Seas regions while 30% are scattered in the occupied areas of China.

b. Farm land has been expropriated for airfields, places for military use and for the use of communications. Farm land has now been reduced by 25%.

c. Farm cattle have been butchered as much as possible to meet the requirements of Japanese forces on the front. Land cultivation power has been greatly affected.

From the outbreak of the Pacific War up to the end of last year, the total production of Formosa was decreased by 20%. It is generally estimated that this year's production will be reduced by 30%.

CONFIDENTIAL MATERIAL FROM (合二) RESEARCH OFFICE

News from Formosa No. 224.

FURTHER ESTABLISHMENTS OF AIR FORCE BASES IN FORMOSA

There are at present eight airfields in the island, classified under the following three categories:

1. Airfields for both civil and military uses:

One in Taiju (合中) and another in Tainan (合南) located in the suburbs and ordinarily used for passengers and civil transports. In time of necessity, these may be utilized as army airfields. In view of the small areas of and geographical restrictions to these airfields, they are not much valuable use.

2. Army's exclusive airfields:

A. (松山) airfield located in the northeastern suburb of Taihoku (合武). The adjacent village is called (松山). Thus it is called (松山) airfield. A brigade of troops is stationed here. Planes ordinarily parked here consist of more than 50 in number consisting of large-type bombers, fighters and reconnaissance planes.

B. (桃園) airfield. Built in 1940. The largest of Japanese airfields (the Japanese themselves often claim it to be the greatest airfield of Japan). Number of planes ordinarily parked there is approximately 100. Located in the east of (番票) railway station in Sinjigu (新行) County.

C. (嘉義) airfield. Built in 1936. The 14th Brigade of Japanese Army is now stationed there, together with fliers training corps. Number of soldiers stationed there is 1600. Number of planes is more than 60 including training planes. Located in the southwestern corner of (嘉義). The area is approximately 10,000 Japanese acres.

3. Navy's exclusive airfields:

A. (岡山) airfield. Built in 1938. Located in the southwestern corner of (岡山) railway station in Takao (嘉穗) County. This airfield is of a new type similar to that of (嘉義) airfield. 2,000 odd troops are ordinarily stationed here. Number of planes is above 100 including torpedo bombers, bombers, fighters, et cetera.
