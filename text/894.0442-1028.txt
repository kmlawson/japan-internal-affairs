Article 175 of the Code of Civil Procedure of Japan, according to deBecker's translation (1928), reads as follows:

"Service to be effected in a foreign country is effected by the Presiding Judge entrusting the matter to the competent authorities of that country, or to the Japanese Ambassador, Minister or Consul resident there."

LAW NO. 18

The civil code shall be revised as follows:

Paragraph 1, Article 45 shall be revised as follows:

A juridical person must, within two weeks from the date of its establishment effect registration at the location of its main office, and within three weeks, at the location of other offices.

In paragraph 3 of the same article "within one week" shall be revised to read "within three weeks at the location of its office".

Paragraph 2, Article 46, "within one week" shall be revised to read "within two weeks at the location of the main office and within three weeks at the location of other offices".

Paragraph 1, Article 48, shall be revised as follows:

When a juridical person has removed its main office, registration of such removal must be made at the former location within two weeks, and the registration provided for in article 46, paragraph 1 must be made at the new location within three weeks. When it removes other

other offices, registration of such removal must be made at the former location within three weeks, and the registration provided for in Article 46, paragraph 1, must be made at the new location within four weeks.

Article 77: "Within one week" shall be revised to read "within two weeks at the location of its main office, and within three weeks at the location of other offices".

Article 97, No. 2: A declaration of intention may be made by means of a public announcement in case the person making the declaration is unable to know the other party or his domicile.

The public announcement of the preceding paragraph shall be made in compliance with the provisions of the law of civil procedure concerning the conveyance of the public announcements and the court shall post the announcement on the bulletin-board, and shall publish at least once in the official gazette and newspapers the fact that such announcement has been made. However, the court may, if it finds such action suitable, give orders that a notice be posted on the bulletin-board of the city hall, town office, village office, or corresponding offices, instead of publishing the fact in the official gazette and newspapers.

Declaration of intention by means of a public announcement shall be deemed to have reached the other party after the date on which the last publication was made in the official gazette or newspapers, or after two weeks from the first day after posting on the official bulletin board instead of publication. However, should the declarant be negligent in his ignorance of the other party or the domicile of the other party, the above reception of the declaration shall

Procedure in connection with the public announcement shall be under the jurisdiction of the district court of the place of domicile of the declarant when the latter is ignorant of the other party, and under the jurisdiction of the district court of the last place of domicile of the other party when the declarant is unable to determine the domicile of the other party.

The court shall require the declarant to pay the expenses of the public announcement in advance.

Article 174, No. 2. The prescriptive period of rights as determined by final judgement shall be ten years even in instances where there is an established period of less than ten years. The same shall apply to rights determined by legal reconciliation, arbitration, or others which have the same effect as final judgement.

Provisions of the preceding paragraph shall not apply to credits for which the time of repayment has not matured by the time of decision.

Supplementary Regulations.

The date of the enforcement of this law shall be fixed by Imperial ordinance.

If a question of registration should arise prior to the enforcement of this law, the period of such registration shall be determined by previous examples.

The provisions of No. 2, Article 174 shall also apply to rights determined prior to the enforcement of this law, provided that the rule shall not apply to rights on which prescription became effective prior to the enforcement of this law.

Tokyo's no. 2947 of May 23, 1938, transmits the Embassy's translation of an amendment to the Japanese "Law for Reciprocal Aid in Compliance with the Requests of Foreign Courts" together with a copy of a translation of the original law by a well-known Japanese legal scholar.

The amendment provides that regulations or stipulations of treaties or similar acts may set aside the requirements prescribed.

The Embassy interprets this amendment to mean that the Japanese government may be considering the advisability of establishing a legal basis for treaties which would grant the right to take testimony for use in foreign courts through other channels than the Japanese courts.
