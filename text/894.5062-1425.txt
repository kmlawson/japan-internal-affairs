Dear Mr. Drew:

I very much enjoyed reading your most recent article, appearing in the National Geographic.

You state, "The Japanese mercantile marine was one of the finest in the world". I wonder if you have any information has to how Japan handled insurance for its merchant marine. I would be everlastingly grateful to you if you could give me a few points of information on this subject, such as how the insurance was handled, whether by the government, private companies or foreign companies.

I recognize that you are a very busy man, but maybe you could dictate just the barest of information upon which I could build an article.

Please be assured that I appreciate your courtesy in reading this, and that I will be grateful for a reply.

William E. Healy,
William E. Healy

My dear Mr. Healy:

Thank you for your letter of April 3, 1944. I am very pleased that you enjoyed reading my article which appeared in a recent issue of the *National Geographic*.

As regards your request for information as to how Japan handled insurance for its merchant marine I trust the following comments will prove helpful to you.

Marine insurance was the first form of insurance established in Japan, the first company being incorporated in Tokyo in January 1879. The moving spirits in this venture were Eiichi Shibusawa and Yatoro Iwasaki, the founder of the Mitsubishi interests. The initial capital was ¥650,000.

At the end of the 1938-1939 fiscal year (March 31, 1939), there were probably 20 Japanese insurance companies writing marine policies but the bulk of the marine business was in hands of about 12 large companies.

Total claims paid during 1938-1939 amounted to 30,367,000 yen; payment by contract other than claims, 2,948,000 yen; business expenses 12,774,000 yen; other expenses, 18,189,000 yen; or a total of 64,279,000 yen. New contracts during this year numbered 7,182,000 valued at 14,434,316,000 yen. The number of contracts in force at the end of the year was 1,599,168 valued at 3,447,604,000 yen.

All

Mr. William E. Healy,
Associate Editor, Pacific Northwest Underwriter,
Northern Life Tower,
Seattle 1, Washington.

All insurance business in Japan is in the hands of private companies, mostly Japanese concerns. In 1937-1938, seventeen foreign marine insurance companies operated in the country. During that year these companies had on deposit with the Japanese Government 4,947,000 yen. They received 2,341,000 yen in premiums and paid out 1,179,000 yen in claims. The number of new contracts written was 180,616 and the value of these new contracts was 570,128,000 yen. Contracts in force at the end of 1937-1938 (March 31, 1938) numbered 15,610 to the aggregate value of 51,038,000 yen.

During the period from 1938 to 1941 the number of foreign insurance companies declined substantially due to more rigid Japanese Government insurance regulations and to mounting shipping and exchange difficulties.

The above statistical data has been compiled from the 1940-1941 edition of the Japan Year Book and if it is used in any publication, attribution to that source should be made and not to the Department or myself.

Sincerely yours,

Joseph C. Grew
Special Assistant to the Secretary

You may wish to note in the attached letter of April 17, 1941 from Mr. Brooks Parker of Parker and , Aviation and General Insurance, 1616 Walnut Street, Philadelphia, that a Japanese insurance company recently offered to insure a cargo valued at $125,000 in Japanese bottoms from Calcutta to New York via Kobe against normal and war risks, including British seizure, for a premium of 3 percent in advance; that the prevailing rate in the American market for the same risk excluding British seizure is 3-1/2 percent; that Mr. Parker deduces the implication that the Japanese underwriters do not consider that there is likely to be any immediate difficulty with the British which would involve the seizure of these cargoes.

(The letter from Mr. Parker was received with a personal letter addressed to Mr. Edward L. Reed of CO, who knows Mr. Parker intimately, and is making personal acknowledgment. No further action by the Department is expected.)
