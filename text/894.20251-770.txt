Paris, November 23, 1932.

Serial No. W. L. 1144
CONFIDENTIAL REPORT
By Warrington Dawson
Special Assistant

SUBJECT: Japanese Propaganda in France

A further instance of manifest Japanese paid propaganda in the French press appeared in "THE PARIS OF November 21, 1932 on the economic and financial page, under the title "The Japanese Monetary Problem."

The tone of the article tends to restore confidence in the economic situation of Japan and is very possibly preparing the way for a new loan of some description, although it may merely be calculated to stimulate French business in Japan.

The article concludes as follows:

"Within approximately the last five months the balance of foreign trade, helped somewhat by the raising of certain customs tariffs, has become favorable, that is to say, it is now on the credit side.

"Such

"Such an assemblage of facts, taken along with the policy of deflation observed by Japan as well as the elimination of all monetary inflation should bear fruit sooner or later. It should be added that the end of the world crisis must accelerate an improvement to which the country seems disposed to apply itself with the exceptional energy characteristic of the national temperament."

The words about the policy of deflation and the avoidance of inflation, in any form, as evidences of improvement in the Japanese monetary situation, would appear to be significant if taken in conjunction with reports by the French press to the effect that the new Administration will very possibly apply a policy of inflation, to solve America's budget problem.

Very respectfully,

Carrington Dawson
Special Assistant
