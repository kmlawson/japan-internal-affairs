# REGULATIONS GOVERNING THE ENTRY OF ALIENS INTO JAPAN.

(Ordinance No. 1 issued by the Department of Home Affairs on January 4, 1918)

Revised under Ordinance No. 1 issued by the same Department in April, 1911, and under Ordinance No. 10 issued by the same Department in April, 1914.

Regulations regarding the entry into Japan of foreigners are established as follows:

ARTICLE 1. Alien arriving in Japan who are considered as included in any one of the following classes may be prohibited from landing by Prefectural Governors (in the case of Tokyo-fu, by Superintendent of the Metropolitan Police Office):

1. Those persons not in possession of a passport or certificate of nationality.
2. Those who act against the welfare of the Empire or who are suspected of acting in the interests of the enemy.
3. Those who are likely to disturb public peace or to subvert public morals.
4. Paupers and professional beggars.
5. Persons suffering from contagious diseases or other diseases dangerous to the public health.
6. Inbeciles, feeble-minded persons, indigent persons, and those likely to become public charges.

The passport or certificate of nationality mentioned in Item 1 of the above paragraph must have a photograph of the bearer attached; must have been issued by the authorities of the bearer's country, and must have been visaed by an Ambassador, or

or Minister, or consular officer of the Empire stationed abroad, within a period of one year prior to landing in Japan.

ARTICLE 2. The stipulations of Item 1 of the first paragraph of Article 1 shall not be enforced against the subjects or citizens of those countries which do not require passports or certificates of nationality, of Japanese subjects. The stipulations of the second paragraph relating to visas in Article 1 shall not be enforced against the subjects or citizens of those countries which do not require Japanese subjects to present passports or certificates of nationality, visaed by the authorities of that country.

ARTICLE 3. An alien, upon his arrival in Japan, shall exhibit his passport, or certificate of nationality to the police authorities upon their request, and must make true statements in reply to their inquiries regarding matters mentioned in the several items of the first paragraph of Article 1, and other pertinent matters.

ARTICLE 4. An alien who violates the provisions of Article 3, or uses a passport or certificate of nationality, bearing the name of another person, or has his passport or certificate of nationality visaed by fraudulent means may be forbidden to enter, or be deported from, Japanese territory by Prefectural Governors.

ARTICLE 5. The provisions of the present ordinance shall not be applied to Ambassadors, Ministers, their suites, consular officers and staffs, and their families of foreign countries stationed in the Empire, nor to those who are on official business of a foreign country. The above provisions shall also not be applied to the crews of ships of foreign countries entering Japanese ports.

## Supplementary Rules.

The present Ordinance shall be enforced from the date of promulgation. However, the provisions relating to passports or certificates of nationality shall be enforced from February 1, 1918.

In the case of a foreigner who is on his way to Japan before February 1, 1918, and who cannot obtain a passport or certificate or naturalization, or the visa prescribed in Article 1, of the present Ordinance, the requirements of Item 1 of paragraph 1 of Article 1 shall not be applied to him.

MATTERS RELATING TO THE ENTRY OF
ALIENS INTO THE SOUTH SEA ISLANDS

(Ordinance No. 1 issued by the South Sea Administration office on February 2, 1925.

Regulations relating to the entry into the South Sea Islands of foreigners are established as follows:

ARTICLE 1. Aliens arriving at the South Sea Islands who are considered as included in any one of the following classes may be prohibited from landing by the Chief of the branch office of the South Sea Administration office:

1. Those persons not in possession of a passport or certificate of nationality.
2. Those who act against the welfare of the Empire, or who are suspected of acting in the interests of the enemy.
3. Those who are likely to disturb the public peace or to subvert public morals.
4. Paupers and professional beggars.
5. Persons suffering from contagious diseases or other diseases, dangerous to the public health.
6. Imbeciles, feeble- and persons, indigent persons, and those likely to become public charges.

The passport or certificate of nationality mentioned in Item 1 of the above paragraph must have a photograph of the bearer attached; must have been issued by the authorities of the bearer's country, and must have been visaed by an Ambassador, or Minister, or consular officer of the Empire stationed abroad within a period of one year prior to landing at the South Sea Islands except in case the bearer arrives at the Islands from other districts of the Empire.

ARTICLE 2. The stipulations of Item 1 of the first paragraph of Article 1 shall not be enforced against the subject or citizen of those countries which do not require passports or certificates of nationality of Japanese subjects. The stipulations of the second paragraph of Article 1 relating to visa shall also not be enforced against the subjects or citizens of those countries which do not require Japanese subjects to present passports or certificates of nationality of Japanese subjects, visaed by the authorities of that country.

ARTICLE 3. An alien, upon his arrival at the South Sea Islands, shall exhibit his passport or certificate of nationality to the police authorities upon their request, and must make true statements in reply to their inquiries regarding matters mentioned in the several items of the first paragraph of Article 1, and other pertinent matters.

ARTICLE 4. An alien who violates the provisions of Article 3, or uses a passport or certificate of nationality bearing the name of another person, or has his passport or certificate of nationality visaed by fraudulent means may be forbidden to enter, or be deported from, the South Sea Islands by the Chief of the branch office of the South Sea Administration office.

ARTICLE 5. The provisions of the present ordinance shall not be applied to Ambassadors, Ministers, their suites, consular officers and staff, and their families of foreign countries stationed in the Empire, nor to those who are on official business of the government of a foreign country. ## Supplementary Rules.

The present ordinance shall be enforced from the date of promulgation (gazetted on May 13, 1925).
