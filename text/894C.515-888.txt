AMERICAN CONSULATE, DAIREN, MANCHANA, DECEMBER 16, 1935.

SUBJECT: ANNOUNCEMENT OF PROHIBITION OF SMALL CHINESE SILVER COINAGE BEGINNING APRIL 1936.

EDWIN L. NEVILLE, ESQUIRE,
AMERICAN CHARGE D'AFFAIRES AD INTERIM,
TOKYO, JAPAN.

SIR:

I have the honor to refer to the Consulate's despatch of December 10, 1935, in regard to the effect on the Kwantung Leased Territory of the amendment of the Foreign Exchange Control Law of October 5, 1933, and to state that the evening edition of the MANSHU NICHI NICHI SHIMBUN of December 14, 1935, carried a story to the effect that the Chief of the Kwantung Bureau had announced on that afternoon that beginning April 1936 the use of the small Chinese silver coins would be prohibited within the Kwantung Leased Territory.

From the announcement as reported in the press it appears that the authorities realize the difficulties in the sudden withdrawal of such coinage and therefore intend to put the prohibition into effect gradually. The announcement states that by degrees it is hoped to substitute for contracts, or payments,

Respectfully yours,

Stuart W. Crummon,
American Consul.

## Imperial Ordinance

The following Imperial Ordinance is taken from the
Official Gazette of December 21, 1935.

Imperial Ordinance No. 313
December 20, 1935.

Subject: Imperial Approval is Given to the
Prohibition of the Circulation of
"Hsiaoyang-chien."

## Article I.

The circulation in the Kwantung Province after
April 1, 1936, of the small silver coins, known as
"Hsiaoyang-chien," shall be prohibited.

## Article II.

At the time fixed for the prohibition of these
coins to go into effect, the Government shall purchase,
on the demand of the holders of such coinage, the coins
at places to be decided upon by the Japanese Ambassador.

## Article III.

Obligations made prior to March 31, 1936, may be
settled in either the small silver coins or in Bank of
Chosen notes or coins in accordance with the Coinage
Law, but those made after April 1, 1936, can only be
paid in Bank of Chosen notes or coins in accordance
with the Coinage Law.

## Article IV.

The necessary particulars in regard to the carrying
out of the prohibition of small silver coins shall be
decided upon by the Ambassador.

## Addendum.

This Ordinance goes into effect from the date
of its promulgation.

Enclosure No. 2 to despatch to Embassy, Tokyo, dated December 23, 1935, from Stuart E. Grummon, American Consul at Dairen, Manchuria, entitled "Prohibition of Circulation of Small Silver Coinage in Kwantung Leased Territory after April 1, 1936."

Kwantung Bureau Ordinance No. 73

December 21, 1935.

Subject: Regulations Regarding the Purchase of "Hsiaoyang-chien."

Jiro Minami
Ambassador Extraordinary of Japan.

Article I.

In accordance with Imperial Ordinance No. 313 of December 20, 1935, the places of purchase of the small silver coins shall be designated separately but it shall be by banks and exchange companies.

The buying commission which shall be given to the banks and exchange companies, mentioned above, shall be decided upon separately.

Article II.

The period during which the said coinage may be purchased shall be from April 1, 1936, until June 30, 1936.

Article III.

The value and quantity of the small silver coins to be purchased as well as the market price will be announced later.

Addendum:

This Ordinance shall go into effect from the day of its promulgation.

Enclosure No. 3 to despatch to Embassy, Tokyo, dated December 23, 1935, from Stuart E. Grummon, American Consul at Dairen, Manchuria, entitled "Prohibition of Circulation of Small Silver Coinage in Kwantung Leased Territory after April 1, 1936."

Kwantung Bureau Ordinance No. 74

December 21, 1935.

Subject: Regarding the Prohibition of Imports of "Hsiaoyang-chien."

## Article I.

The importation of, or the preparations to import, small silver coins into Kwantung Province is prohibited.

## Article II.

Persons who violate the foregoing provision shall be imprisoned at hard labor, for less than one year, or fined less than Yen 200.00, or both imprisoned and fined.

## Article III.

Should representatives of a legal person, or legal persons themselves, or their agents, employees or persons of like nature violate the provisions of Article I, in addition to the punishment of the actual violator, the legal person or the person for whom the act was done, shall be punished in accordance with the foregoing provisions.

## Addendum.

This Ordinance shall go into effect from the date of its promulgation.

SUBJECT:

MAN:

Dairen's attached despatches of December 10, 1935, and December 16, 1935, have to do with the subject of currency in the Kwantung Leased Territory. For the purpose of a closer control of foreign exchange and of the currency, an attempt will be made by the Kwantung Government to take up the silver coinage (mostly Chinese) which is in circulation in the Kwantung Leased Territory. The use of paper money will be introduced gradually.
