Sir:

The Department has received information from various
sources that individuals of the Japanese race have in
recent months been engaged in what appear to be topographical surveys along the Pacific and Caribbean coasts
of certain Latin American countries. In some cases such
surveys appear to have been undertaken by individuals
alleged to have represented themselves as fishermen.

At the request of the Director of Naval Intelligence,
the Department desires you to investigate discreetly and
report promptly upon these and such similar activities
as may come to your notice. Comments are desired as to
the appearance of any Japanese fishermen and their vessels:
do they seem to be bona fide fishermen, or are they more
highly educated men in disguise? Are the vessels equipped
with survey, sounding, or other scientific instruments?

CONFIDENTIAL

From: The Naval Attache and Naval Attache for Air, Guatemala City, Guatemala.
To: The Director of Naval Intelligence.
SUBJECT: Intelligence data: Japanese activities in Central America.
Reference: (a) Ltr DNI Op-16-B-16 AS-6/EF37/EF49, Serial No.1744, 23 Jul 37.

1. In connection with reference (a), the Naval Attache has to report that on 23 July, 1937, the new Japanese Minister to Mexico, Mr. Gaishiro Hoshida, also accredited to the Central American Republics, arrived in Guatemala City by Pan-American Airways airplane from the South. He was accompanied by his Secretary, Mr. Minoru Izawa. On 30 July the Minister left Guatemala City to visit the city of Chichicastanango, Guatemala, leaving his Secretary here, returning to Guatemala City on 2 August. No information has been received as to his mission in Chichicastanango. On 3 August, 1937, Mr. Funio Niura, Secretary of the Japanese Embassy in Brazil arrived in Guatemala City by Pan-American airplane and joined the Minister's party. On the same date the Japanese Minister presented his credentials to the President of Guatemala. On 9 August, the Japanese Minister, accompanied by the Secretary of the Japanese Embassy in Brazil and the Minister's Secretary, departed Guatemala City by Pan-American airplane for Brownsville — it being stated that the Minister and his Secretary would take train from there to Mexico City where he will reside.

2. From my investigation it would appear that the Japanese Minister was desirous of securing two things from the Guatemaltecan Government: (1) concessions on the coast; and (2) a trade treaty. From reliable sources it has been ascertained that he failed in both objectives with the Guatemaltecan Government and that he secured no concessions nor agreements of any kind from that Government. My investigation further develops no evidence of any nature of Japanese connection with oil concessions or developments in this country.

3. In the course of my investigations it has developed that the Guatemaltecan Government and Guatemaltecan people are very strongly anti-Japanese. It has been stated from a source considered reliable that the President of Guatemala has stated that he would under no consideration grant concessions to the Japanese on the coast of Guatemala and that as far as a trade treaty is concerned, he is also agreeable to such a treaty only if the Japanese would buy in Guatemala an equal value of goods for export as was imported, — to which, of course, the Japanese are not agreeable. In this connection it has been learned that a few years ago the Japanese flooded the Markets of Guatemala with cheap goods which did not give satisfactory service and that since that time the people of Guatemala and especially the Guatemaltecan Government are wary of Japanese made articles.

Further in this connection, it has developed that the Government of Guatemala possesses approximately double the usual duties on Japanese goods entering the country.

4. Following the call of the Japanese Minister on President Ubico, it was learned that the President made a remark to the effect that the Japanese were becoming very wary with him as they sent him this time a Chinaman as their Minister,--referring to the fact that the new Japanese Minister has the Chinese appearance rather than a Japanese. In this reference to personalities that might be fitting to state that it would appear that the Japanese Minister's Secretary, Mr. Minoru Izawa, who is a large and powerfully built man as compared to the small Minister, has a strong liking for alcoholic drinks.

5. In line with this report the Naval Attaché almost daily hears remarks by Guatemaltecans that would indicate that their whole-hearted sympathy is with the Chinese in the present crises in the Orient, and feel that the Japanese invasion of China is proof of the correctness of their distrust of the Japanese.

6. I am of the opinion, as a result of my investigation, that the present government in Guatemala is very distrustful of the Japanese in all matters broached by them.

7. As soon as my office is organized and time permits I will investigate Japanese activity in the other Central American Republics to which I am accredited.

F. H. Lamson—Cribrar.
