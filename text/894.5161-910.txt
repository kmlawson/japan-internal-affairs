Raising of the Discount Rate:

On October 6th the Bank of Japan announced the rise of its official discount rate the new rate going into effect the previous day. The new rates are as follows:

1. Discount rate for commercial bills, - 16 rin per diem.
2. Interest rate on loans on the security of national bonds, over 17 rin per diem.
3. Interest rate on loans on the security of negotiable papers other than national bonds, - over 18 rin per diem.
4. Interest rate for over-draft account and for overdrafts by correspondence, - over 20 rin per diem.

Mr. Hijikata, Governor of the Bank of Japan is quoted as declaring that the present raise in discount rate is intended to check the outflow of gold. There has been much criticism among banking and business men of the action of the Bank of Japan, but both Mr. Hijikata, Governor of the Bank, and Mr. Inouye, Minister of Finance declare that there will be no bad effect on financial circles.

On October 5th a further increase of two rin a day per one hundred yen was announced, the Minister of Finance stating that although the present rise is admitted to be too high and may cause much hardship to business men, it is necessary for several reasons, the chief among them being the determination of the Government to maintain the Gold Standard.

V. GOVERNMENT FINANCE.

On April 6 the Bank of Japan and the semi-official banks lowered the discount rate on commercial bills and the interest rates on loans and overdrafts by an average of about .37% annually. The Finance Minister stated that this was the first step toward the general lowering of interest rates in Japan. The Governor of the Bank of Japan explained that another reason for the step was to make the interest rate on Government bonds more attractive than those of bank deposits and in this way to create a market for future issues of Government bonds. Indications became even clearer during the month that Mr. Baba, the Finance Minister, would abandon the Takahashi policy of gradually reducing Government bond issues. On April 18 the Finance Minister converted a quantity of 5% Government bonds into 3.5% bonds,* this move having been made possible by the prior lowering of the discount rate on commercial bills and the interest rates on loans and overdrafts by the Bank of Japan and the semi-official banks.

In an interview on April 6 with a correspondent of the ASAHI, the Finance Minister was quoted as having said that he doubted whether he would be able to give an explanation of taxation readjustment at the coming Diet session and that his taxation program would be completed by next October. This newspaper stated that the Finance Minister hoped to have taxation measures ready to be applied in the fiscal year 1937-1938.
