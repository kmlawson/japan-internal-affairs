TO : IMPERIAL JAPANESE GOVERNMENT

THROUGH : Supreme Commander for the Allied Powers

1. Representatives of your Government have expressed an interest in the purchase of United States surplus property. In its discretion, the Office of the Foreign Liquidation Commissioner will sell to Imperial Japanese Government agencies, as surplus, such civilian type military supplies in Japan as are approved by the Supreme Commander for the Allied Powers. Payment is to be obtained in dollars on the same terms as presently apply to approved imports by the War Department from the United States.

2. The quantities and type of such surplus property, the prices thereof and other terms of sale, including provisions for exchange of property, are matters for agreement between the Office of the Foreign Liquidation Commissioner, or its Field Commissioners, and the representatives of your Government as negotiated through and with the approval of SCAP.

3. If these terms are agreeable to your Government it is requested that you indicate its acceptance thereof by signing and returning to me the inclosed duplicate original of this letter. When this has been done I shall inform my Field Commissioners as to the terms in order that they may be appropriately incorporated or referred to in any contracts for the sale or exchange of surplus property which may be executed between my Field Commissioners and representatives of your Government as provided above.

MEMORANDUM for: LP-Mr. Hale T. Shenefield

FROM: OFLC-E. F. Smith

SUBJECT: Proposed report to NAC Staff Committee

The following suggested alterations are offered in connection with the draft report received in this office 3 December:

1. The figure 597 in the next to the last line on page 1 be corrected to read 59.

2. The words "Answer (B)" on page 2 be removed from their present position and inserted at the 9th line and the language beginning "This question" to and including the words "of SCAP?" be inclosed in parenthesis. This will make the reading uniform throughout.

3. On page 2 a reference should be inserted as an introduction to the quoted part of the answer. The present reading does not indicate the source of the quotation.

4. On page 3 the word "or" toward the end of the quotation in the second question should be "of", and a parenthesis mark should close the sentence.

5. The answer to question 4 on page 4 does not appear to satisfy the question. It is felt that a direct answer in dollars and cents should be furnished to indicate the value of items to be returned to the United States whether of surplus or not. The answer as it now stands is a definition of the nature of the needed items as distinguished from their extent. If Colonel Davis is unable to supply exact or even general information on the subject, such negative information should be given. Having supplied the answer to the question, there is no reason why a brief statement should not be made to indicate that the committee felt the subject of returned items was irrelevant to the subject of surplus.

6. On page 5 the answer supplies the figures $16,900,000. In the light of recent cables and in view of the use of the figure $92,560,000 under question 7, it is suggested that $16,900,000 be changed to $20,000,000.

State Dept.
Foreign Liquidation
Commissioner's Office
From Smith

AGREEMENT BETWEEN THE UNITED STATES AND JAPAN FOR THE SALE OF CERTAIN SURPLUS PROPERTY

This Agreement between the Government of the United States of America, hereinafter called "United States", acting through the Assistant Field Commissioner for China and Eastern Asia, Office of the Foreign Liquidation Commissioner, Department of State, and the Imperial Japanese Government, acting through the Board of Trade with the approval of the Supreme Commander for the Allied Powers, hereinafter called "Japan",

WITNESSETH:

WHEREAS, the Congress of the United States has by the Surplus Property Act of 1944, as amended, authorized the disposal of surplus property by the United States; and the Assistant Field Commissioner for China and Eastern Asia, Office of the Foreign Liquidation Commissioner, Department of State, has been charged with the responsibility for that disposal in Japan; and

WHEREAS, Letter Agreement dated 3 September 1946 signed by the Central Field Commissioner for Pacific and China, Office of the Foreign Liquidation Commissioner, Department of State, and accepted 5 November 1946 by Japan with the permission of the General Headquarters of the Supreme Commander for the Allied Powers provided terms and conditions under which Japan may purchase surplus property from the United States, said Letter is by this reference incorporated herein and made a part hereof; and

WHEREAS, it is estimated that approximately Fifty Million ($50,000,000) Dollars, U. S. currency, at United States procurement cost, of surplus property located in Japan, of which over one-half has already been declared surplus, is desired by Japan; and

WHEREAS, it is the desire of the parties hereto to express more explicitly certain terms, conditions, and understandings as to the transfer of property under that Agreement;

NOW, THEREFORE, in consideration of the premises and mutual agreements and covenants hereinafter stated, it is agreed that:

Article 1. By these presents the United States agrees to sell and Japan agrees to purchase certain properties, to be hereafter selected by Japan, owned by the United States on the effective date hereof but surplus to its needs, subject, however, to the following conditions and limitations:

=== Page 8 === (confidence 1.00)
a. Surplus property selected shall be subject to approval of the Supreme Commander for the Allied Powers.

b. This sale is to be in the nature of a bulk sale, and unless specifically excepted by mutual agreement, the entire quantity of all items listed on an individual Form SPA-3 will be accepted by Japan. Japan will be permitted to make inspections at such times and under such conditions as may be prescribed by the military authorities. No labor will be furnished for such purpose, and in no case will failure to inspect be considered ground for a claim of adjustment or rescission. The selling price stated in Article 4 below reflects these and other conditions of sale and the average condition and usability of the property to be purchased.

c. Surplus property selected on this contract shall not include fixed installations, aircraft or equipment peculiar to aircraft, ships and other maritime equipment, and non-demilitarized combat materiel.

d. Selection of property shall be made by Japan from property which has been or will be declared surplus by the United States owning agency, pursuant to the Surplus Property Act of 1944 and amendments thereto, and which is located within Japan.

e. No property is to be sold hereunder which is already under firm commitment for sale or transfer.

Article 2. Property hereafter selected as provided for in Article 1 above shall be specifically itemized and listed, with the number of units, purchase price of each unit, the total purchase price for the number of units, and location, on schedules to be attached hereto subsequent to the execution and delivery of this contract. The schedules shall be numbered consecutively, the first schedule being No. W-FLC(CH-II)-500-BT-1. They shall be dated in the upper right hand corner of each page and the date shall be the date of the last signature, and shall be initialed by a representative of Japan and a representative of the United States on the bottom of each page, and signed by such representatives on the last page of each schedule. When so initialed and signed, title to property listed on such schedules shall pass as of the date of the schedule, as provided in Article 3 below.

Article 3. All right, title and interest of the United States, together with the right to possession, of the property that the United States agrees to sell and Japan agrees to purchase under the terms of this Agreement shall pass to Japan as of the date of each schedule attached hereto itemizing and listing the property which is transferred under the terms of this contract.

Article 4. Surplus property selected shall be paid for as provided in Letter Agreement dated 3 September 1946 referred to above, and the purchase price is to be thirty (30%) percentum of the procurement cost of the selected property. The total amount due the United States under this Agreement is to be the sum total of the purchase prices as shown on the individual schedules attached hereto, less any payments or adjustments made.

-2-

=== Page 9 === (confidence 1.00)
Article 5. a. All surplus property listed in the schedules attached hereto is sold "as is"; the United States makes no guarantee, warranty, or representation, express or implied, as to the kind, size, weight, quality, character, description or condition of any of the surplus property, or its fitness for any use or purpose, other than warranty as to title.

b. Unless otherwise specified the quantities of various items listed in schedules attached hereto are approximate only. If it is shown that any variation existed between the quantity stated for an item sold on a per-item basis and the quantity of such item actually received by Japan at the time of delivery, an adjustment for shortages or overages on the basis of the quoted unit price for such item will be made between the parties hereto.

Article 6. The property is sold "where is" at the location indicated on the attached schedules. Removal of the property, including all crating, conditioning, handling, loading and transportation, shall be the responsibility and at the expense of Japan. Unless Japan removes or causes the property to be removed from its location within sixty days (unless said 60-day period is specifically extended in writing) after the date of acceptance of the attached schedules, the United States may at its option:

a. Sell, destroy, or abandon, the property and hold Japan responsible for any loss incurred or damage suffered by the United States as a result of Japan's failure to remove the property;

b. Store the property at its present location or remove and store the same for, in the name of, and for the account and at the risk of Japan, and the cost of said removal shall be paid by Japan to the United States in addition to the consideration and value stated for the property to be sold, and the cost of the storage shall constitute a lien thereon in favor of the United States or other person, firm, or corporation undertaking the storage thereof.

Article 7. Until sixty (60) days after the date of the attached schedules, or until Japan removes the property, whichever shall first occur, the United States shall give said property the same care and protection as is accorded its own property of like character, but shall not be liable for any loss, damage, or deterioration.

Article 8. Japan will use its best endeavors to insure that property transferred pursuant to this Agreement will not be imported into the United States in the same or substantially the same form, if such property was originally produced in the United States and is readily identifiable as such, unless such property is to be imported into the United States on consignment to a person or firm in the United States for the purpose of reconditioning for re-export to Japan.

Article 9. No duty, tax, excise, or other governmental exaction is included in the price, and if any such duty, tax, excise, or governmental exaction is levied or found to be payable, Japan shall pay such sum in addition to the purchase price of the property sold. The United States represents that no duties, taxes, excises, or other governmental exactions are due to the Government of the United States.
