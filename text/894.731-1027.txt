104, January 26, 1926.

Japanese authorities announced last evening that coded commercial messages passing through the cable offices will be subject to censorship. The procedure proposed would delay messages and require disclosure of their content and of commercial codes. I have informed the Japanese Consul General that I cannot admit the right of the Japanese authorities to censor commercial code messages of American firms and nationals. I shall support this position if necessary by citing the fact that the Japanese refused to permit Chinese censorship of Japanese language messages sent by Japanese cable from Shanghai to Japan. Meanwhile an effort is being made to find a practical solution and the chief censor is understood to have agreed with the cable companies to follow the procedure heretofore in force under the Chinese censorship. That procedure permitted acceptance of coded commercial messages if bearing a foreign consular seal or in the case of banks and large firms having numerous messages if a consular
