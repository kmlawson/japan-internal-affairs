AIR MAIL

STRICTLY CONFIDENTIAL

No. 671

To the American Ambassador,

Buenos Aires.

The Secretary of State quotes for the strictly confidential information of the Embassy the following excerpts from a letter dated February 3, 1941 which has been received from an official agency of this Government:

"As of possible interest to you, information has been received from a reliable, confidential source to the effect that Osaka Syosen Kaisya, Whitehall Building, New York City, New York, informed their office at Avenida Roque Saenz Pena 616, Buenos Aires, Argentina, that it had been noted that the Captain of the M.S. 'Brazil Maru' refused to accept Mr. Paul Mutter and Mr. W. Leverman as passengers, giving no particular reason other than the present international situation.

"It was further stated that the passengers' passports and visas were already in order and the passage fares had already been received by Osaka Syosen Kaisya, $300.00 having been collected from each at New York for first class passage.

"It is reported that Osaka Syosen Kaisya also informed the Buenos Aires office that they had further noted that the Captain of the 'Brazil Maru' was kind enough to accept Mr. and Mrs. Jaeger as passengers, who were also among the group refused by the Captains of previous sailings, thus making Mutter and Leverman the only passengers remaining.

"It was further stated that although Osaka Syosen Kaisya had not gotten any written information from the Japanese authorities as to a change of policy in the acceptance of persons of belligerent nations, it has been quite apparent that they are granting visas even for those passengers of military age."
