(Translation.)

The London and the
(Communist) on March 15 of this year published reports from the Exchange Telegraph Agency concerning a clash alleged to have taken place between the Soviet frontier guards and some units of the Japanese Army on the "Siberian Border." According to the same reports, a Japanese aeroplane, alleged to have flown across the border at the station of Kogranichnaya, "was surrounded by Soviet aeroplanes and forced to land."

This has been informed in authoritative quarters that the reports of the Exchange Telegraph Agency published by the above-mentioned newspapers are not true. There has been no collision between the frontier guards of the U.S.S.R. and Japanese troops, and no approach of Japanese troops to the Russo-Chinese border (Manchuria) has thus far been observed anywhere. Likewise, no flights of Japanese aircraft over the territory of the U.S.S.R. have been recorded.

Moscow, January 28, 1936.

Subject: Forced Landing of Japanese Military Plane on Soviet Territory.

The Honorable
The Secretary of State,
Washington, D.C.

Sir:

With reference to my Despatch No. 1271, of January 17, 1936, mentioning the forced landing on January 9, 1936, of a Japanese military plane on Soviet territory, I have the honor to report that the incident has now been declared closed.

Mr. Sakoh, Counselor of the Japanese Embassy at Moscow, informed a member of my staff on January 23rd that on the same day Mr. Stonemyshev, Assistant Commissar for Foreign Affairs, had received the Japanese Ambassador Ohta and had communicated to him

=== Page 7 === (confidence 0.99)
-2-

him that the Soviet Government had agreed to return the pilots and the plane. Mr. Ohta expressed thanks for the speedy and favorable resolution of the incident.

A TASC statement published in the Soviet press on January 24th was less complimentary to the Japanese in its report of the conversation between Mr. Stomonyakov and Mr. Ohta. According to TASC the Japanese Ambassador was informed that the guilt of the pilots had been fully confirmed. The MOSCOW DAILY NEWS, in its version of the TASC announcement, states that "Nevertheless, taking into consideration the regret expressed by the Japanese Minister of Foreign Affairs, and the request of the latter and of the Ambassador himself, and also taking into account the circumstance that as a result of their aggressive actions the Japanese pilots suffered seriously, being wounded, the Soviet authorities, meeting the request of the Japanese Government, have agreed to return immediately the pilots and the plane."

The foregoing marks the high point so far in the graph of recent recurring frontier incidents. The facts that the plane belonged to the Japanese Army and that the pilots opened fire on approaching Soviet frontier guards did not help to fortify the Japanese position. On the other hand the refusal of the Soviet authorities to allow the Japanese Consul General at Vladivostok to communicate with the interned pilots was interpreted by the Japanese Embassy at
