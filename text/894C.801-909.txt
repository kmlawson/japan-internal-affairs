SUBJECT: Exclusion of Foreign Vessels from the

Edwin L. Neville, Esq.
American Charge d'Affaires ad interim,
Tokyo, Japan.

Sir: FEB 1 1936

I have the honor to report that the often mooted
question of foreign vessels engaged in coasting trade
between Dairen, Japan proper, and the intervening
Korean ports has recently arisen again here, in-
volving an important American interest. The matter
is reported in its present status in order that the
Embassy may be informed should its assistance later
be necessary.

The Texas Company which sends some of its petro-
leum products to Korea from the plant at Dairen de-
sires to engage Chinese vessels for their transportation. On November 16, 1935, the S.S. YUNG SAI
sailed from Dairen under charter by the Texas Company
with 425 tons of petroleum products for Shingishu,
Korea, and 200 tons for Chinampo. The cargo for
Shingishu was permitted to land without hindrance.

That for Chinampo, on the other hand, was held up for some time on the ground that foreign vessels could not engage in carrying cargo between Dairen and Korean ports. Permission to discharge however was at length granted.

On November 29, 1935, a similar cargo was shipped on the same vessel to the ports of Fusan and Fusan. Special permission was granted after some discussion and reference to Keijo to discharge the cargo.

The local manager of the Texas Company has now been apprised by the Keijo office of his company that shipments in other than Japanese bottoms will not be permitted again. This is apparently contrary to the terms of Imperial Ordinance No. 236 of August 31, 1906. With higher freight rates on Japanese vessels and difficulty in pursuing the latter to take petroleum cargoes on account of the fire hazard, as well as increasing stocks in Dairen and declining stocks in Korea, the matter is of some importance to the American company concerned.

The Chief of the Marine Affairs Bureau at Dairen is of the opinion and has so stated in writing that the provisions of the Imperial Ordinance of August 31, 1906, above referred to provide the possibility of foreign cargo ships plying between Dairen and Japanese ports, as that decree excludes such vessels from being considered as engaged in the coasting trade. This treatment of such vessels was moreover confirmed in a

letter

-3-

letter from the Vice Minister of the Colonial Department to the Chief of the Kwantung Government under date of November 27, 1930. Copies of both communications referred to are attached for the Embassy's information.

At the request of the representative of the Texas Company, I discussed the matter with the Chief of the Foreign Affairs Section at Fort Arthur at the time that the first shipment referred to was refused permission to discharge, and was informed that he could see no reason for the cargo being held up. Yesterday, I again took up the matter with him in view of the definite refusal to permit further shipments as reported from Keijo, and he informed me that in view of the general interest which the matter has for the Kwantung Government, as well as because of the American company specifically involved, he has requested an explanation from the Korean Government.

Inasmuch as the manager of the Texas Company now desires to obtain instructions from the Company's head office at Shanghai before proceeding further with the matter, no new developments are to be expected until his instructions are forthcoming or until an explanation has been received by the Foreign Affairs Section from the Korean Government.

Respectfully yours,

Stuart E. Grummon,
American Consul.

27-th Nov. 1930.

Masshir Ota, Esq.,
Chief of Kwantung Government.

Re: Transportation of Passenger and Cargo by foreign vessels navigating between Dairen port and Japan and her territory:

With reference to the above matter, it should be done in accordance with the notice that the Japanese Foreign Minister communicated to the foreign representatives residing in Japan in August 1906. A copy is attached for your reference.

In connection with the affair of the Chinese ship "Chukago" during August of this year, I have made inquiries by letter of the Vice-Minister for Foreign Affairs. Until definite policy has been decided upon I am instructed to advise you to treat foreign vessels as empowered to transport passengers and cargo without the formality of special permission.

I may here state in passing that it has been decided at a meeting of all the Chief of Custom Houses of the whole country that the treatment of this matter should be in accordance with the above mentioned notice issued by Japanese Foreign Minister.

Marquis Kinichi Komura
Vice Minister of Colonial Dept.

Encl: Dairen/Shanghai Letter No. 468.

January 24, 1936.

WTT: 1936 FEB 14 AM 10 59
RGM:
MCH:

Dairen's attached despatch of December 11, 1935, on the possible exclusion of foreign vessels from the carrying trade between Dairen and Korean ports, reports that certain Chinese vessels chartered by the Texas Company to carry petroleum products from Dairen to Korea have been delayed by port officials in Korea on the ground that foreign vessels could not engage in carrying cargo between Dairen and Korean ports. The vessels were finally allowed to discharge their cargo. The Korean Government has been asked for a ruling, and as yet there is no indication that the decision will be against participation in such carrying trade by foreign vessels.

Comment by CC:

Japanese Imperial Ordinance No. 236, August 31, 1906 (enclosed with the despatch), includes foreign merchant vessels as qualified for navigation between the Japanese Empire and the Kwantung Leased Territory. Japanese officials heretofore interpreted this ordinance as permitting foreign vessels to transport passengers and cargo between the Kwantung Leased Territory and the Japanese Empire. If such trade is to be stopped it is probable that the ordinance

=== Page 9 === (confidence 0.99)
-2-

ordinance will be superseded by a new one defining such voyages in a manner which would cause them to fall under the heading of Japanese coasting trade, to which foreign vessels are not admitted. The United States Government might then contend that the Kwantung Leased Territory is simply leased and is not Japanese territory, and that American vessels should be allowed to navigate between it and ports of the Japanese Empire because such voyages are not coasting. Whether the United States would desire to make this contention would presumably depend in part on the interest which American ships have in such trade, on a thorough consideration of the status of the Kwantung Leased Territory since the establishment of "Manchukuo," and on a careful study of treaties bearing on the matter. Certainly interference with Chinese vessels only, even though chartered by American companies, would not justify American representations.

The despatch is addressed to the Embassy in Tokyo and no doubt will be given the Embassy's attention without further action at this time by the Department.

Sir:

I have the honor to refer to the Embassy's instruction of February 8, 1936, and to the despatch to
the Embassy of the Consul at Seoul dated February 14,

by the Texas Company in attempting to ship cargo from
Dairen to Korean ports in Chinese bottoms.

I am informed by the local manager of the Texas
Company that he has recently made arrangements with the
Matsuura Kaisen Kabushiki Kaisha, a Japanese shipping
firm, to send another cargo to Jinsen on the S.S. PEI
HAI, a Chinese flag vessel operated by the Japanese
firm mentioned and that the cargo will leave Dairen
on either the 19th or the 20th of February. The
Japanese firm in question has accepted the business,
stating that it has obtained the "toleration" of the
Korean authorities for this one shipment.

Inasmuch as the Texas Company is still desirous of
utilizing
