Enclosure No. 1 to report to the Embassy, Tokyo, from C. H. Stephan, American Vice Consul, at Nagoya, Japan, dated December 8, 1938, on the subject: News Items from Nagoya.

Source: NAGOYA SHIBUN
December 2, 1938.

Training Engineers to Facilitate Soundant Productive Power.
New Aviation Department in Nagoya Technological College

With a view to establishing stabilized productive power against the prolongation of the war (in China), the Department of Education has drafted a plan for the education of as many engineers as possible in a short term. For this purpose all educational activities in the country are to be mobilized. The estimated cost for the coming fiscal year is calculated at Yen 5,700,000. This estimate, it may be noted, includes the cost of a new aviation department in the Nagoya Technological College and the engineering department of the proposed new Nagoya Imperial University.

Enclosure No. 2 to report to the Embassy, Tokyo, from C. H. Stephan, American Vice Consul, at Nagoya, Japan, dated December 8, 1938, on the subject: News Items from Nagoya.

Source: SHIN AICHI
Nagoya, December 3, 1938.

Aviation Department to be established in the Nagoya Technological College

The long awaited aviation department is to be established in the Nagoya Technological College early in 1939, funds for this purpose having been included in the estimates of the Department of Education.

It is expected that the new department will take 40 students at the beginning, and the school has already made tentative arrangements for classrooms, dormitory accommodation, etcetera.

Enclosure No. 4 to report to the Embassy, Tokyo, from C. H. Stephan, American Vice Consul, at Nagoya, Japan, dated December 8, 1938, on the subject: News Items from Nagoya.

Source: OMAKA MAINICHI SHIMBUN
Nagoya Edition
December 7, 1938

(Full translation)

Nagoya's Export Trade to China Boosted in November

The export trade with China, which had declined sharply since the outbreak of the incident last summer, recorded a complete recovery in November, following the fall of Hankow and the successful development of long-term reconstruction measures all over the (occupied) Chinese territories.

According to returns of the Nagoya Customs for the month of November, exports to China totaled Yen 1,522,000, as against Yen 81,000 for the month of August 1937, the first month of the hostilities, or roughly 20 times greater.

Vessels bound for China -- carriers of Made-in-Nagoya goods -- also increased during November to 120 runs, regular and irregular, taking chiefly wheat flour, woolens and beer, as well as certain machinery and hardware; thus promising a prompt restoration of industries in various places.

Enclosure No. 1 to report to the Embassy, Tokyo, from H. Stephan, American Vice Consul at Nagoya, Japan, dated December 21, 1938, on the subject: "Military Industries and Labor in Nagoya (News Items)".

Source: OSAKA MAINICHI SHIMBUN
Nagoya Edition
December 14, 1938.

(Full Translation)

Rapid Growth of Nagoya Industries in 1938

The Industrial Department of the Nagoya City Office has recently completed an investigation of industrial enterprises established, dissolved, capital increased, et cetera, covering the ten month period ended October 31, 1938, from which it was learned that the total authorized capital of companies operating in Nagoya as of that date amounted to Yen 845,000,000. This record high figure represents an increase of Yen 125,169,000 since January 1, 1938, and is accounted for principally by (wartime) business expansion in the heavy industries in such plants as the Aichi Clock Company (airplanes), Okamoto Industrial Company (bicycles, motor vehicles, airplane parts), Japan Vehicle Company (locomotives, railway rolling stock and military supplies and equipment), Okuma Iron Works (machinery, munitions and military supplies and equipment), et cetera.

During the period covered by the investigation there were 211 companies newly established, while 330 were dissolved, and 199 others increased their capital. The large number of dissolutions are accounted for by mergers or absorptions of many small establishments in conformance with the national aim.

Source: OSAKA MAINICHI SHISBUN
Nagoya Edition
December 10, 1938.

(Full Translation)

Organization of War-Labor Control Committee

Consonant with the long-term (Continental) construction objectives, which can be realized only by close collaboration between military and civil authorities and the public, and with the aim to maintain steady productive power for munitions and military supplies, a War-Labor Control Committee, under the presidency of the Governor, Mr. Tanaka, has been established in Aichi Prefecture.

The attention of the authorities has been directed to the fact that the services of munitions and military supplies workers in this district are not entirely satisfactory, and that there have recently been reported an increasing number of absences on account of illness due apparently to overwork and unsatisfactory living conditions. It was learned that low-salaried workers particularly are experiencing increasing difficulties in finding houses for rent, within the city or nearby, according to their living standards. Efforts will therefore first be directed to providing new small houses to be supported preferably by a community dining room for the supply of nourishing food.

103, February 21, 7 p.m.
Press comment.
One. The discussions in the United States over
such questions as the sale of military planes, naval
expansion, fortifying of Guam, et cetera, have been
widely reported in the Japanese press which agrees in
concluding that obviously the United States is moving
away from its isolation policy and is definitely in
process of aligning itself with Great Britain and France
against the totalitarian states. The press was unanimous
in declaring that the American representations concerning
the occupation of Hainan Island were actually only for
the purpose of making common and concerted action with
Great Britain and France. The future development of
this tendency toward common action merits, it is said,
the closest observation.
Two.

2-#103, From Tokyo, Feb. 21, 7 p.m.

Two. On February 19, the ASAHI carried in the most prominent position a long dispatch from its New York correspondent stating that the United States plans to use its financial resources to close the markets of South America to Japan, Germany and Italy, and, by thus making more difficult acquiring of foreign exchange, to hinder acquisition of necessary raw materials by these countries. This, it was said, was to be carried out by making loans to South American countries and by granting subsidies to American manufacturers. The resignation of Assistant Secretary of the Treasury Taylor was said to have cleared the way to the employing of monetary policy as an "instrument of diplomacy".
