AMERICAN CONSULATE
Yokohama, Japan, October 15, 1938.

Subject: TREATMENT ACCORDED SICK AND DISABLED SEAMEN BY FOREIGN GOVERNMENT.

The Honorable
The Secretary of State
Washington

Sir:

I have the honor to refer to the Department's mimeographed instruction of September 3, 1938, File No. 196.7 Medical Relief /14, and to state that upon inquiry, local government officials advised that there do not exist in Japan government hospitals to supply medical services to sick and disabled seamen of foreign vessels.

Respectfully yours,

Beppe R. Johansen,
American Vice Consul.

DEPARTMENT OF STATE
DIVISION OF FAR EASTERN AFFAIRS
March 31, 1930.

P: ☑

This is an excellent report on "The Japanese Press" considered from nearly every angle, - historical background, distribution, circulation, composition, management, and the ethics and influence of the press, - with an appendix including a detailed description of each of the principal newspapers which should be very useful for reference.

Some of the conclusions relating to the influence of the press may be considered by some to be over-optimistic but as a whole the report is well balanced and instructive.

I see no reason why the report should not be shown to Mr. Hulen, or to any other responsible correspondent who may be interested, with the usual reservation as to quotation or reference.

RSM: ZLIF
