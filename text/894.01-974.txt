FROM State Department (Stimson) DATED January 5, 1933.
TO Secretary NAME 1-1157

SECRETARY'S STATEMENT TO JAPANESE AMBASSADOR.

REGARDING:
"I reminded the Ambassador that just before he went away he told me that the Japanese Government was in the control of a group of younger officers, none of them of a higher rank than a Lieutenant-Colonel, and I said to him that he must recognize that as long as that situation lasted I could not regard Japan as a normal Government and must make my own conclusions as to information coming from her. He said he remembered that situation but he found that when he got back to Japan it had somewhat changed and that Saito was getting into better control and that, as he expressed it, this incident at Shanhaikwan he regarded as a test of whether that was so."

Tokyo's 2141 of November 14, 1936, contains a statement handed to the press by a committee representing the army and navy setting forth the official views of the fighting services on the much discussed question of reorganizing the administration of the government.

The recommendations appear on pages 2-3. Note that in addition to administrative reforms the army and navy advocate revision of the laws to provide for "renovating the Diet". In another despatch of the same date as this, this proposal for reform of the Diet is discussed in detail.

In reference to the Embassy's despatches No. 2070, October 3, 1936, and No. 2117, October 31, 1936, on the subject of proposed administrative reform of the Japanese Government, I have the honor to report that on November 9 the Vice Ministers of War and Navy submitted to a conference of four Cabinet Ministers, sitting as a committee to consider administrative reform, a joint recommendation with regard thereto. The joint recommendation was incorporated in an announcement to the press on the same day, and serves as the most authoritative statement, up to the present time, of

of the official views of the fighting forces on the much-discussed question of reorganizing the administration of the government. Chief points of the joint recommendation are:

Under the jurisdiction of the prime minister should be an organ of general inquiry, supervision, and coordination. Its head, whether or not a cabinet member, should be fully able to assist the prime minister and at times should be authorized to attend cabinet meetings in the capacity of a cabinet minister. (This point embodies a concession to the opposition which has been expressed to the original plan of making the head of this office a cabinet minister without portfolio.)

Under the jurisdiction of the prime minister there should be an organ for the control of personnel. (This proposal apparently contemplates something of the nature of a unified civil service.)

The Foreign Office and the Ministry of Overseas Affairs should be combined in the interest of a unified external policy.

An organ should be created in the cabinet to have control of Korean, Formosan, and South Seas affairs.

The Ministry of Agriculture and Forestry and the Ministry of Commerce and Industry should be combined.

Foreign trade, fuel policy, electric power, and related matters should be controlled by a unified office of expanded powers.
