MEMORANDUM

To the: U.S. Representative on the Far Eastern
Commission.

Subject: Announcement of Admission Limited Powers
of Attorney to Japan.

The attached press release is to announce a change in
theater regulations in Japan which has been communicated to
the Department of State by the Supreme Commander for the
Allied Powers. Under modified theater regulations, limited
powers of attorney may be admitted to Japan through the
diplomatic channels for the purpose of permitting nationals
of members of the United Nations not presently in Japan,
to designate agents to secure the return of property which
was confiscated, blocked, or wrongfully transferred by the
Japanese during the war.

The information contained in this press release is
hereby furnished the Far Eastern Commission for circulation
to the representatives of other member governments.

J. H. Hilldring
State Department Member
State-War-Navy Coordinating Committee

30 September 1947

Mr. O. J. McDiarmid, Chief
Finance Section
Division of Occupied Areas
Economic Affairs,
Department of State,
Washington, D. C.

My dear Mr. McDiarmid:

Letters are now coming to me from my old staff (Japanese) in Tokyo and Yokohama, advising that they are ready and anxious to restore the business office of Frazar Shokai, Tokyo.

To do so, however, they advise me that there is necessity first of securing permission from the U.S. Army authorities. To do this, a limited special power of attorney must be prepared which will have the approval of the Dept. of State.

I have a copy of the form issued by the U.S. officials covering powers of attorney for restitution of property, but at the present time do not wish to apply for restitution of these properties, but only to open the office of Frazar Shokai, to enable some of my old staff to make me reports and surveys.

Before making application for a limited power of attorney, I want to make sure that it will be in an acceptable form. I, therefore, would greatly appreciate your advice as to whether this can be done. If you are in a position to make the necessary inquiries and let me have your advice I shall be very appreciative. In other words, what I should like to give is a simple limited power of attorney to enable my Japanese office man to rent a room in Tokyo and to hang out the sign of Frazar Shokai.

Yours very sincerely,

E. W. FRAZAR

OCT 7 1947

In reply refer to
OE

My dear Mr. Frazar:

Reference is made to your letter of September 30, 1947, in which you inquire regarding the acceptable form for a Power of Attorney to be used by your representatives in Japan, for the purpose of reopening your Japanese office.

Aside from the limited Power of Attorney for the restitution of property, with which you are already familiar, there is not to my knowledge any particular form required for other types of Powers of Attorney admissible to Japan. As you may know, however, transactions and business arrangements between persons in Japan and foreign concerns are subject to the specific approval of General MacArthur's Headquarters. I personally see no objection to the program outlined in your letter, but suggest that you address a communication along similar lines to the Civilian Property Custodian, General Headquarters, Supreme Commander for the Allied Powers in Tokyo, for consideration there by appropriate officials. If you so desire, you might enclose with that communication the limited Power of Attorney which you desire to grant to your Japanese representative, stating therein the name of the grantee and the services you desire him to perform for you. Such a document does not require scrutiny by the Department of State, and may now, in view of the opening of transactional communications with Japan, be readily transmitted through the mails.

Sincerely yours,

O. J. McDiarmid, Chief
Finance Section
Division of Occupied Areas
Economic Affairs

Mr. Everett W. Frazar,
3 Brooklands, Apt. 1-H,
Bronxville 8, New York.
