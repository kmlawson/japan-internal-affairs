Vicente Lopez, plantation owner at Iloilo. Allen stated that Lopez is generally believed to have helped the Japanese in many ways as soon after the invasion as possible. They placed a sign on his gate which meant not to harm him.

Joaquin Silos, resident of Iloilo, is a real Fifth Columnist, according to Allen, and cooperates one hundred per cent with the Japanese.

Yater Mills Alles advised that the following persons were cooperative with the Japanese:

Governor Fermin Caram, a Syrian Filipino and Japanese puppet governor of the Island. Before the Japanese occupation, Caram was a physician and a member of the Senate residing at Iloilo. Allen stated that Caram tries to "play ball" with both sides, but for the most part, he cooperates with the Japanese. Caram has told the Filipinos that the Americans will never return to the Islands, and in public speeches urged them to cooperate with the Japanese. He has made speeches urging that resistance to the Japanese stop.

Vicente Ybiernas, Filipino, former representative from Iloilo, now Mayor of Iloilo, has told the Filipinos that the Americans would never return to the Islands and has urged cooperation with the Japanese and has made public speeches urging that resistance to the Japanese stop. He has given a number of large parties for the Japanese.

Fortunato Ybiernas, brother of the above individual, a lawyer and resident of Iloilo, cooperates with the Japanese one hundred per cent and acts as an interpreter for them.

Simplicio Lizares, Spanish Filipino and owner of a large plantation, cooperates with the Japanese and made his home available to them for their headquarters.

The above data are being furnished for your information and appropriate consideration.

DEPARTMENT OF STATE
Memorandum of Conversation

DATE: January 16, 1946.

SUBJECT:

PARTICIPANTS: Mrs. Elsie Wallace Moore
Mr. E. W. Mill (PI)

![img-0.jpeg](img-0.jpeg)

COPIES TO:

Mrs. Elsie Wallace Moore, who was detained by United States Army Counter Intelligence in Manila on suspicion of collaboration with the Japanese, and on whom the Consulate General in Manila has submitted a voluminous report, called at the State Department today. She called ostensibly to inquire regarding payment for war damages suffered in the Philippines. I advised her that she could obtain information on the subject as well as the necessary WDC-PL Forms No. 2 from the War Damage Corporation at 811 Vermont Avenue NW. I also told her that Senator Tydings had introduced a bill, S. 1610, calling for payment of war damages for certain categories of losses and suggested that she obtain a copy of this bill.

Mrs. Moore mentioned in passing that she had heard that she was on some black list in the Department, but when I made no attempt to continue the conversation along this line she changed the subject.

She said also that she wanted to talk to Mr. Grew, and mentioned that she had already talked to Mr. Gauss and Mr. Salisbury.

PI: Mill:hg
