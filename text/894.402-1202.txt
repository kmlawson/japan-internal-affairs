Subject - Japanese Language Study.
After a careful examination of the officer personnel
of the Department of State as shown in the Government Manual, I
have decided to address this inquiry to you inasmuch as your
office would seem to be best prepared to give me certain inform-
ation which I am seeking. However, I sincerely trust it will not
894.402
work any hardship upon your office in so doing.
I am now completing my second term at the local night
school class of the Y.M.C.A. with respect to Japanese language
study and am desirous of having your recommendation as to a
practical, but not too voluminous, textbook of the kanji (Chinese
characters) used by the Japanese. If possible, I would like such
a book which has an alphabetical arrangement under the English
word or Japanese, preferably the former, for ready reference.
Of course, I would want the kanji, the English, and the Japanese
words so arranged as to permit close association for memorizing.
I served in the Consular Service in the Orient during
the years 1920-1923 at Shangha1 during which time I was continuing
my study of the Russian language in spare time.
It is quite possible that I may return to the Orient
one of these days and,if so, I intend to have a practical grounding.
at least in the spoken language of Japan. For many reasons I
consider a practical knowledge of the kanji as quite essential.
Although a war veteran, it is my understanding that
there is little probability of an opportunity for re-entrance into
foreign service such as I had in the Orient, above menticned,so
I expect to make other use of my past training when the opportunity
presents itself.
