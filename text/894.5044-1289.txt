THE HONORABLE
THE SECRETARY OF STATE
SIR:
I have the honor to refer to previous reports
in regard to retirement allowance difficulties being
experienced by various local firms, including the
Kobe branch of the States Steamship Company.
Mr. Robert McClelland, manager of the local branch,
reports that the States Steamship Company has agreed
to pay local employees of the firm a retirement allow-
ance of one month's average salary per year of service,
or, in other words, one-twelfth of the total salary
received by an employee during his entire period of
employment, plus salary for the month of August.
Mr. McClelland states that this agreement was
arrived at after numerous conferences between himself,
his employees and the police authorities and that the
decision represents a compromise. His own stand was
that Japanese enter the employ of foreign firms at
higher salaries than are paid by Japanese firms with
the

the understanding that they shall receive no retirement allowances. The employees, backed by the police authorities, requested that retirement allowances be paid at the rate of two months' present salary for every year of service.

Mr. McClelland also states that he has now been asked by his company for recommendations in regard to closing the local branch of the States Steamship Company and is recommending that the office be closed immediately as there is no longer any possibility of doing business here.

In connection with the payment of retirement allowances by foreign firms, the general arrangement to which many firms have agreed in compliance with police supervision on behalf of employees, is that one and one-quarter months' salary per years of service, figured at the present salary rate, be paid to employees who are dismissed. It may be mentioned that many foreign firms are making an even more liberal arrangement with their employees in paying up to two months of present salary per year of service.

Respectfully yours,

Gerald Warner
