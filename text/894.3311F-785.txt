COPY

Governor Schley's Luncheon.
May 15, 1933
Balboa, C.Z.

Hon. Governor Schley, and Gentlemen:

It is, indeed, a source of profound satisfaction that upon our arrival in Panama Zone, we are receiving such a sincere and enthusiastic reception. I am very grateful to you, one and all, for this expression of generosity and friendship.

It is particularly gratifying to us to be here and to meet the prominent citizens of this Zone who, by their energy and wisdom, have made their Canal what it is today, and who, in the true sense of the term, are the leaders of the real activities of this wonderful work.

We are here on a friendly visit and to express to you our earnest (sic) desire to cooperate with you in fulfilling our common mission in the Pacific.

During the last several years, we have been accustomed to the fact that the Pacific has a commanding position in international trade and diplomacy. More and more is it apparent that the age of the Pacific is approaching; that the Pacific Ocean will be the great new highway of the world.

The Ocean was not placed there for us to quarrel over. It is there for our benefit and advancement.

We are happy to see, so far as America and Japan are concerned, that there has been a steady growth of understanding and appreciation, and while I do not deny that there have been a few irritating experiences in the past, I am convinced that, in the final analysis, it will be found that such irritation has been merely superficial, temporary, and without solid foundation.

Finally, it is my privilege to believe that the generosity of this reception and the friendly atmosphere now prevailing is a guarantee that our mission is fulfilled and understood.

In behalf of the officers and men of our Imperial Training Squadron, and for myself, I thank you with all my heart.

MEN:
Mexico's despatch No. 2338 of March 2, 1933, reports that the Mexican Government has granted permission for the Japanese gunboats YOKUMO and IWATE to make courtesy calls at Mexican ports. (The United States has also granted permission for visits by these vessels to American ports). The Japanese Minister at Mexico City intends to give a reception to the Admiral and his staff.
