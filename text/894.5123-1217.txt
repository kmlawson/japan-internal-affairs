DEPARTMENT OF STATE
ADVISER ON POLITICAL RELATIONS
February 20, 1940.

Mr. Secretary:

Two points of great immediate interest are brought out in the underlying report from Consul Gourley at Kobe, as follows:

1. There was an acceleration in gold shipments from Japan to the United States during the latter part of 1939, attributed to Japan's desire to make purchases of goods prior to termination of the treaty of 1911.

2. Japan's gold reserve is believed to stand now at yen 369,780,000 (approximately $120,000,000) [which figure tallies almost exactly with estimates which have been made by Mr. Luthringer of this Department].

Stanley K. Hornbeck

Reference Consul Gourley's report entitled "Japan Speeds Up Gold Exports and Reduces Gold Bank Reserves During Latter Part of 1939", Kobe, December 22, 1939.

There was a noticeable tendency on the part of Japan to speed up gold shipments during the latter part of the year 1939 in the opinion of Consul Gourley. The chief purpose of this unusual flow of gold was to purchase commodities and to export them from the United States before the termination of the commercial treaty between Japan and the United States on January 26, 1940.

Gold shipped from Japan in 1937 amounted to 851-1/2 million yen; shipments in 1938 amounted to 686-1/2 million yen; and gold exports in 1939 through December 15 amounted to 653.3 million yen. Total shipments since March 1937 have been 2.2 billion yen. The Bank of Japan's gold reserve was still officially shown as 501,287,000 yen. However, according to information obtained by Consul Gourley from local banking sources the specie reserve of the Bank of Japan has been reduced to 369,780,000 yen. According to a recent report from the Acting Commercial Attaché at Tokyo, the Japan Times and Mail published that figure for several days following December 19 but later stated that the figure
