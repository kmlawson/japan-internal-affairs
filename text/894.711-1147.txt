No. 633

POST OFFICE
Tientsin, 9th April, 1938.

Sir,

With reference to your complaint of the 28th ultimo and in continuation of my communication No. 629 of the 7th instant concerning a letter posted by the American Embassy, Peiping, to the address of Mrs. Chang Fong Mee c/o American Consulate General, Tientsin, which you state bears signs of having been tampered with, I have the honour to inform you that very diligent enquiries were made both at the Tientsin and Peiping offices but nothing could be ascertained showing or leading to respect that the alleged irregularity occurred while the letter referred to was in the hands of the Post Office.

While it is true that one of the end flaps of the envelope bears signs of having been pulled out and subsequently resealed, the slight damage to the seal may have been caused while the letter was under the date-stamping machine or by friction while in the bag.

The cover was cleared from a Pillar box at Peiping and being "ordinary" was treated without any record being kept.

The envelope is returned herewith.

I have the honour to be,

Sir,

Your obedient Servant,

POST OFFICE
No. 634
Tientsin, 9th April, 1938.

Sir,

I have the honour to acknowledge the receipt of your letter of April protesting that certain pages have been torn out of each copy of the magazine "Readers Digest" for April, 1938 addressed to yourself and members of your staff, and adding that the wrappers bore no censor's stamp.

The case has been the subject of a most careful investigation, but, much to my regret, it has failed to disclose where the irregularity occurred.

The magazines referred to were received included in the U.S. mail which reached Tientsin at 6.30 p.m. on the 5th instant and were delivered as soon as they were released by the censors. They being "unregistered" no record of them was kept.

I have the honour to be,
Sir,
Your obedient Servant,

August 29, 1938

Reference Tokyo's 2156 of July 27, 1938.

The contents of this despatch, which need not be read, relate to a protest by the Iranian Charge d'Affaires in Tokyo to the Japanese Government over the opening by the Japanese postal authorities of mail addressed to the Iranian Legation. The Embassy observes that the tone of the protest and lack of evidence for it are indicative of Iranian policy and methods in international affairs.

No action is required.

=== Page 13 === (confidence 1.00)
-2-

Minister for Foreign Affairs formally protesting the opening by Japanese postal authorities of mail addressed to the Iranian Legation.

The violent tone of the protest over this trivial incident together with the fact that no evidence whatever is adduced to show that the letter was opened by the Japanese postal authorities, or that it was more than an isolated case, will no doubt appeal to the Department as significant of Iranian policy and methods in regard to matters of international import touching not only Japan but the United States as well.

This incident is perhaps more keenly appreciated by this Embassy for the fact that it had in its possession a cover, addressed to the American Consul at Taihoku on the back of which are five red wax impression seals, two of which bear the impression "American Embassy, Tokyo" and the other three the impression "American Consulate General, Tokyo". It may be stated for the Department's information that this cover was exhibited informally to an official of the Foreign Office with the remark that nothing of a confidential nature was ever sent by open mail to our Consul at Taihoku. (See our despatch no. 2989 of June 7, 1938.)

Respectfully yours,

Joseph C. Grew

Annexe à la Circulaire No. 10.

Karuizawa,
19 July, 1938.
No. 245/17.
Monsieur le Ministre,

I have the honor to call the attention of Your Excellency to the following:

A few months ago I received a parcel from Iran through the Azabu post-office. As examination showed the parcel to be in an open condition I protested to Your Excellency's predecessor, Mr. Hirota, and received positive assurance that the individual responsible would be severely reprimanded. From the post-office of Azabu ward I further received that the misconduct would not be repeated; therefore it is with deep regret I have to inform Your Excellency that in spite of the fullest assurances given I was today offered by the Karuizawa postal officials a registered letter, No. 420, from Williamstown, Victoria, Australia, and plainly addressed to the Imperial Iranian Legation, yet which was in a completely open condition; consequently I refused acceptance.

Now, such repeated violation, which is a denial of private rights, of International Law, of diplomatic prerogatives and immunities, throws serious responsibility upon the Imperial Japanese Government.

This further breach of rights is the more regrettable since our countries exist on the most cordial terms, and our respective people enjoy much in common. I therefore again beg to lodge formal protest, and request that

(1) the official responsible be dismissed;
(2) the letter in question be handed me with an official apology; and
(3) that formal assurance be given that such action will not be repeated.

In case the Imperial Government of Japan fails to give adequate consideration to the foregoing three requests the Government of His Imperial Majesty Shahinshah of Iran will be urged, in retaliation, to reserve to itself full right to open all mail matter addressed to the Imperial Japanese Legation in Teheran.

I avail myself of this opportunity to renew to Your Excellency, Monsieur Le Ministre, my highest consideration.

Iranian Chargé d'Affaires.

His Excellency
General Ugaki
His Imperial Japanese Majesty's
Foreign Minister,
Tokyo.

Enclosure no. 2 to despatch
no. 3156 of July 27, 1938,
from the Embassy at Tokyo.

The Dean of the Diplomatic Corps, the Belgian Ambassador,
Baron de Bassompierre, to his colleagues. (Translation)

CIRCULAR NOTE NO. 10
***

The Dean of the Diplomatic Corps presents his compliments to his honorable Colleagues.

At the request of the Iranian Chargé d'Affaires he has the honor to transmit to them herewith a copy of a letter addressed July 19 by Mr. Sheybany to His Excellency Mr. Ugaki, Minister for Foreign Affairs.

The Iranian Chargé d'Affaires has asked the Dean whether he did not consider it necessary to make representations in the name of the Diplomatic Corps in general on this subject.

The Dean is provisionally of the opinion that there is no reason for him to take action. He would perhaps modify his opinion if other cases of the secret violation of diplomatic envelopes were brought to his attention by others, lending to the belief that it was a question of a system and not of an isolated and perhaps accidental incident.

Tokyo, July 22, 1938
BELGIAN EMBASSY

(seal)
