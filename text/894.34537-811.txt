208 Garrard Street,
Covington, Ky.,
Feb. 26, 1934.

Mr. W. H. Moran,
Secret Service Department,
Washington, D. C.

Dear Sir:-

I received your letter of Feb. 21st. May I suggest that you get in touch with a Dr. Louis (?) Loasses in Camagüey or Sr. Paco Mirando of the same city? I do not expect to return to Cuba for two or three months and if they could be made to talk I believe you could find out more about the sale of a naval base by Cuba to Japan from these parties.

It has been a year or more since I was informed of the transaction and I believe it was one or the other of these two parties who told me. National and international politics were freely discussed in our home by Cuban friends most of whom were radical revolutionaries.

Dr. Loasses was formerly superintendent of the government agricultural school at Camagüey but lost his position because of his bitter hatred of Machado. He speaks excellent English, was a graduate from one of the American state universities in the west, is intensely interested in politics but talks too much.

Mr. Paco Miranda is an old time politician who is voluble with friends but reticent and suspicious with strangers. For the past few years, when he has not been imprisoned for his anti-Machado activities he has edited secretly a revolutionary newspaper in Camagüey.

The party who originally informed me of the sale of a naval base site by Cuba also informed me that the few officials who made the deal pocketed most of the money and kept the transaction out of most of the records and he doubted that the transaction was legal.

When I return to Cuba I shall make inquiries from friends and see if I can find out anything more about this subject.

I

I wonder if you would be interested in such topics as where the arms are purchased by the A.B.C.s, where they are hidden in Camagüey province, who is furnishing the capital to carry on the Cuban revolution and who is buying the arms in the U. S. Probably these are of importance only locally in Camagüey. Our friends accept me as one in sympathy with the Cuban revolution and frequently I pick up bits of information in their conversations.

I would be glad to furnish you with information if it would be of value to you but I would not care to arouse the suspicions of my husband or jeopardize my own welfare for the sake of my two children. When I return to Camagüey if I find out anything of importance I will send it to you without either my address or signature and I am going to request that you not answer any letters that I might send you as my husband occasionally opens my mail.

Very truly yours,

MRS. GAIL HALL DE MONTEJO.

208 Ganard St.,
Covington, Ky.,
Feb. 18, 1934.

Secret Service Dept.,
Washington, D. C.

Sir:

A few months ago while living in Camagüey, Cuba,
I was informed that Japan secretly purchased the rights
to establish a naval base on Cuban soil. This transaction
took place during the regime of President Machado.

It was recently called to my attention that
perhaps the United States Government was unaware of this
purchase which was in violation to the Platt Amendment.
I do not know anything about the details but offer you
this bit of information for whatever value it may have
to you. I am a Cuban by marriage but an American by
birth and my country comes first. It might be well to
investigate the authenticity of this transaction because
of Japan's warlike attitude towards various countries of
the world.

If you do not already have more information about
this transaction than I, it might be possible for me to
get the full details when I return to Cuba. The difficulty
would be to get information to your department.

When

When I lived in Cuba I only succeeded in getting two letters out of the country from November 1932 to May 1933. Several letters from the U. S. were never received and at least two letters had been opened and resealed. Why this should have occurred is still a mystery but in view of this fact I could not send any information of importance while on Cuban soil.

Very truly yours,

MRS. GAIL HALL DE MONTEJO.
