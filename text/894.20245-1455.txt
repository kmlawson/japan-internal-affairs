The Japanese have launched what may prove to be a major propaganda campaign directed at India.

The keynote of this campaign may be found in a statement by Premier Tojo in his policy address to the Japanese Diet on June 16 as follows:

"In contrast to the nations and races within the GEA Sphere who are achieving manly advancements, the people of India are suffering heavily for their independence under the shadow of cruel crimes by Britain who is taking control of India. For them I express my heartfelt sympathy. Yet I also cannot help but feel utter disappointment. (Applause)

"Imperial Japan has determined to do her best for helping the people of India attain their independence and to establish the great India Empire for which the people of India have been longing for a long time. (Applause) I am hoping to see that day dawn as soon as possible, even a day sooner, when prosperity and freedom so burningly desired by the people of India, will be realized by removal of the British yoke." (Applause)

The campaign was dramatically opened and the identity of its leader revealed in a series of broadcasts over radio Tokyo June 19 when it was announced that "Subhas Chandra Bose, fiery Indian patriot, second only to Ghandi," had recently arrived in Tokyo from Germany and would make a series of radio speeches and a tour of the south in connection with the Indian problem. In these broadcasts, listeners were reminded of Premier Tojo's statement in regard to India and of Bose's long career as a leader of the independence movement in and out of India. In the course of the publicity campaign which began June 19 in behalf of Bose, an effort was made to dramatize him by (1) referring to and again denying reports of his death in an aeroplane accident in 1942 and (2) speculating whether he had made the long journey from Germany by submarine or by aeroplane. Moves to increase his prestige included reported promises of support from "Rash Behari Bose, leader of the Indian Independence League in East Asia", who is also in Tokyo, and from "Ba Maw, the Burmese leader.

The arrival of Bose from Germany at the time of the Diet session (or suppression of the news of his arrival until June 19) suggests that the propaganda campaign he is leading was carefully prepared in advance to capitalize on Tojo's statement before the Diet. A Manila broadcast giving a detailed program of speeches by Bose to be beamed on the Philippines is indicative of the thoroughness with which his speeches are probably being publicized to listeners throughout the Far East.

It is too early to determine whether the propaganda campaign will continue to be carried out systematically and with determination or will assume the less organized pattern which has been used in the past in propaganda directed to India. It would seem, however, that the campaign should be closely watched because (1) as already pointed out, it seems to be carefully prepared; (2) after a year and a half of control over conquered territories Japan may feel able to devote the major propaganda effort to India; and (3) the campaign involves an open call to rebellion as illustrated by the following statement by Bose: "Civil disobedience must develop into an armed struggle."

J.W.B.

FE: Johansen:MLS
