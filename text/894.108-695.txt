government is shown by the fact that they have persuaded
the International Statistics Association to hold its next
convention in Tokyo in September of this year. A Japanese

the government will do its utmost to render every possible

Japanese Police.
The polioe in Japan are under the control of the
Central Government in Tokyo. There are no looal police
supported by municipalities but all the police throughout
the country with the exoeption of the Tokyo Metropolitan
Police are under the Police Bureau in the Ministry for Home
Affairs. The Chief of the Metropolitan Police Board of Tokyo
reports directly to the Ministry for Home Affairs. The fact
that polioe methods in voguo in different cities of the
Empire must necessarily be almost identioal and must meet the
approval of the controlling authorities in the Capital
makes possible great unanimity in the form of statistics
returned, resulting in the easy compilation of national
statistios. The duties of the police in Japan are con-
siderably varied. They deal with the protection, prevention
and suppression of crime, the enforcement of sanitary and
health laws, the inspection of shops, boarding houses, peri-
odicals, eto. This wide range of duties requires complete
familiarity with the laws of the country and the organiza-
tion of the government. Consequently the men at the head
of the police are government officials of reaatively high
standing.
The central government is reproduced in miniature as
far as necessary in each Prefecture and the governor has
representatives of each Department on his staff.
Probationary work is part of the police duties. Much
effert is expended in keeping track of ex-offenders and in
endeavoring to prevent their relapse into orime. Any member
of a family once convioted of a serious orime will have a

As fast as information is colleoted by the police it
is summed up in daily totals which are submitted to the
