# MEMORANDUM

JAPANESE NEWSPAPERS PUBLISHED
IN BUENOS AIRES

Argentin Djijo, founded May 25, 1924 - oldest Nipones publication in Argentina. Office - Uspallata, 981, Buenos Aires. Director and proprietor - T. Midjo. He is the Nipones leader in Constitution, Boca, and Barracas. He is a active in other groups in Buenos Aires. He is a director in "La Asocion ponesa en la Argentina," and is the principal supporter of the Japanese School.

Principal problem of the paper is the lack of paper, making it necessary to turn to a smaller size and fewer pages. At the present time the paper is composed of four pages of tabloid size. The paper used is of very poor quality. Price per copy - 10 cents (Argentine). Circulation - 1,200 copies. Circulated in the City of Buenos Aires and throughout the Province of Buenos Aires. It is the organ of the industrial class - is for the common people.

Connection with the Japanese Embassy - It is the organ of the Japanese Embassy and the principal Japanese agents in Argentina. Assistant to the Director - - - Tekama.

Nighia Gjiji, founded August, 1933 - office, Humberto 1499, 1st floor, Buenos Aires. Size and number of pages - four pages of tabloid size. Paper of very inferior quality.

Director - proprietor - JS. Niiruma, also known as Yasumiko Niimura. He is considered a very intelligent Japanese. He seems to be actually responsible for the strength of the Japanese importer, Tsuji. This paper is also having a difficulty in obtaining paper to continue its printing. It also sells at 10 cents per copy. Circulation - 1000 copies in and around Buenos Aires.

Nan - A, founded February 1, 1936. Office - Mexico 2044, Buenos Aires. Size - four pages. Before May, 1942, paper was of good quality.

Director - owner, Takaichi Shintaku. Was active in the extreme party in Japan. His paper is presently suspended by the Japanese Embassy. Circulation - 800 copies. In Buenos Aires, Avellandesa, San Martin, Haedo, and other communities near the Caital. It is preferred in the working sections.

Nichi Nichi, founded August, 1940. Office, Esmeralda, 1084, Buenos Aires. Size - four pages tabloid size inferior paper. Director - manager - T. Suzuki. It is said that Suzuki is really an instrument of Sr. Shinya. He occupies a place of importance in "Maison Satama."

Pretends to be an organ of the higher "burguesia." Officially respects the tendencies of Japan. At present it is suspended from the mails in Argentina because of certain articles published. Circulation - 600 copies. 10 cents a copy. Mostly distributed in Buenos Aires, Argentina.

DECEMBER 4, 1942.
STRICTLY CONFIDENTIAL
The Honorable
The Secretary of State,
Washington.
Sir:
I have the honor to enclose a translation of an international telephone conversation reported in El Argentin Djijo of December 6, 1942 as having taken place on December 4, 1942 between Mr. Iwashita, Buenos Aires reporter for that Japanese language sheet, and Mr. Yamauchi, Tokyo branch manager of the Manchuria Daily News, in commemoration of nearly one year of the Greater East Asia War. With the institution since that date of the strengthened Argentine Communications Control Law, prohibiting the use of the Japanese language in international communications, it should not be possible for such further conversations to take place in Japanese, and in the future strict control of the six languages permissible in communications should effectively limit the flow of news from Argentina detrimental to the United Nations.
It will be noted that the local newspaper report of the conversation merely mentioned, without going into details, that subjects covered in the 20 minute talk included (1) American reports of the Solomons Sea Battle; (2) American wartime structure and increased production of military supplies; (3) Morale and actual fighting strength of the American Army which "invaded" French North Africa. The contents of any one of these subjects might well have constituted a revelation of military secrets.
Mr. Iwashita reflects current local Japanese press opinion when he observes that both Argentine and Chile are maintaining neutrality toward the Axis in name alone, and envisages the possibility of a formal break in relations with the Axis.

Tokyo-Buenos Aires International Telephone Conversation.

IF WE LOSE ONE SHIP WE BUILD TWO.

THE VOICE OF THE PEOPLE OF THE MOTHER COUNTRY
CALLS FOR VICTORY.

On December 4, in commemoration of nearly one year of
The Greater East Asia War our reporter, Mr. Iwashita spoke
for twenty minutes by international telephone to Mr. Yamauchi,
Tokyo branch manager of the Manchuria Daily News. Subjects
covered were:

1) American reports of the Solomons Sea Battle;
2) American Wartime Structure and Increased Production
of Military Supplies;
3) Morale and actual fighting strength of the American
Army which invaded French North Africa;
4) Recent internal conditions in France.

After talk on these and other subjects, Mr. Yamauchi
in Tokyo asked: How has the daily livelihood of Japanese
over there been affected by the reported neutrality oscillations
of Argentina and Chile?

Our Mr. Iwashita replied: The wavering of Argentina
and Chile on the brink of neutrality is a fact and both
countries actually are neutral toward the Axis in name
alone, the kernel having been thrown away. This tendency
has become conspicuous lately. Argentina and Chile are
just barely following a policy of preserving neutrality
in name, even internal conditions in the countries being
aggravated by pressure from America. Thus, until there is
a formal break of relations with the Axis countries, I do
not think there is cause for anxiety regarding the daily
livelihood of Japanese here.

Mr. Yamauchi then said that as Japan enters the second
year of the Greater East Asia War the people are determined
to gain victory and are tightening their helmet cords.

Mr. Iwashita: How do people in Japan regard the United
States and Great Britain?

Mr. Yamauchi: Since the outbreak of the war Japan
has given strictly correct and proper treatment to enemy
nationals. Despite this, our nationals in America and
Britain have received extremely cruel and inhuman treatment.
As this has become clear our people have become
extremely angry. The government cries out to the people
to grasp the true character of these countries and to burn
to the bitter end with determination to destroy America and
Britain. The government is making a hit with this directive policy.

SUBJECT: THE THREE JAPANESE NEWSPAPERS OF ARGENTINA
INCREASE THEIR SUBSCRIPTION RATES
CONFIDENTIAL
The Honorable
The Secretary of State,
Washington
Sir:

I have the honor to enclose a translation of a joint advertisement appearing in the three Japanese language newspapers of January 28, 1943, notifying readers that the subscription rate is to be Pesos 2.50 per month for each paper beginning February 1, 1943, an increase of Pesos 0.50 per month over the former rate. The reasons given for the increase are a quadrupling in price of newsprint since the war began, the extreme difficulty of obtaining paper, and the desire to continue publication on a daily basis (except Mondays and holidays) rather than to reduce publication to two or three times per week and thus retreat from their duty of counteracting enemy propaganda.

The Japanese language newspapers advertise no rate for single copies, and, at least recently, none has been found on sale at a news stand, and they are apparently to be had only by monthly subscription. During the past month there have been numerous trial balloons in the form of letters from subscribers discussing the pros and cons of amalgamation into one paper, cutting down on the number of issues, and raising subscription rates. The last course was finally decided upon. However, if the newsprint situation becomes further aggravated the other courses may have to be followed. Even now all three papers use a very poor and varying quality of paper, but it is not known from what sources it is obtained. El Argentin Djijo, in its twentieth year, prints but one

# NOTICE TO ALL READERS

The price of newsprint was risen four times
over its pre-war price since the outbreak of the
present world war. In addition it is extremely difficult

Subject: Coverage of International News by Japanese Press of Argentina for May and June, 1943.

STRICTLY CONFIDENTIAL

The Honorable
The Secretary of State,
Washington.

Sir:

I have the honor to report that the three Japanese newspapers published in Buenos Aires carried the following coverage of international news during May and June, 1943. This news came mainly from Japan via Domei in Spanish and was retranslated into Japanese for publication. However, by the end of June the papers were printing an increasing number of news reports received directly from Japan in Japanese by shortwave radio, thus circumventing Argentine cable restrictions.

1. Japanese press reports gave considerable emphasis to Japan's so-called new China policy which Premier Tojo announced was based on equal and mutual assistance in prosecution of the war for the liberation of Asiatics from American and British oppression. The last of the international settlements was handed back to Chinese control early in June. The Japan-China Treaty was scheduled for revision. The Japanese alleged that great numbers of Chungking officers and troops were changing their allegiance to Nanking. It was said that Nanking was being given increasing control of Chinese affairs. The Japanese are apparently endeavoring to curry favor with the Chinese in the belief that Japan is in full control of the situation and stands to gain by liberality.

2. Japan noted with apprehension the increase in American air strength in China during the past few months, and were preparing their cities for anticipated raids. The keynote in Japan is defense, an impregnable iron ring around conquests and a determined struggle to consolidate gains.

3. The 82nd Japanese Diet met for three days in mid-June to beat the tom-tems a bit and passed several government prepared bills designed to strengthen Japan's wartime structure. Premier Tojo promised the Philippines and Burma their

As of possible interest to you, information is being furnished herewith concerning subject, who is manager of the Domei News Agency in Buenos Aires, Argentina. It will be recalled from previous communications that the Domei News Agency is a Japanese organization. This information was received from a confidential source and has been made available to the American Embassy in Buenos Aires, Argentina.

Information has been received that Tsuda has received instructions to send extracts mainly of articles which appeared in United States papers and magazines to the Japanese Government. It has been stated that special interest is shown for the following publications: Time, New York Times, Wall Street Journal and the New Republic. According to information received these publications are available in Tokyo but are received late. It has been stated that most of the United States magazines reach Japan via Russia and India. Two days after the publication of the "United States White Book" Tsuda got a cable which read as follows:

"Rush five hundred words white book far as Japan concerned".

Tsuda is reported to submit material for Japanese propaganda.
