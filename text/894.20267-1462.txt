Paraphrase of telegram from Sir M. Petersen, Ankara to Mr. Eden, dated November 27, 1944.

I have no objection at all to the proposed demarche.

2. We have been discussing this subject here for some time and there is undoubtedly opinion that dislocation of Japanese Intelligence Service in Turkey would offer real advantages.

3. From the point of view of Turkey's own interests and the position vis-a-vis the U.S.S.R. there would seem to be additional advantage in going as far as a declaration of war which would make her a co-belligerent with Americans and us in a sphere which the U.S.S.R. have not yet entered. But there is admittedly little Turkey could do to give effect to a declaration of war except possibly the despatch of her few modern destroyers to the Red Sea. It may well be that the Turks themselves will not appreciate this point. But should they do so, I should like to know whether I need discourage them.

4. Number of Japanese involved has been increased by the arrival in Istanbul of their Missions from the

Balkan/

Paraphrase of telegram from Mr. Eden to Lord Halifax dated November 24, 1944.

Previous objections to idea of requesting Turkish Government to break off relations with Japan have now disappeared. Roumania and Bulgaria under pressure from His Majesty's Government and United States Government have set a good example in breaking off relations with Japan, and Soviet Government have made it clear, particularly at Moscow Conference, that they are not inclined to be tender towards Japan.

Rupture of Turco-Japanese relations would therefore offer many advantages and I am in favour of a combined Anglo-American request to the Turkish Government to take this step. This would be outside the scope of Anglo-Turkish alliance but could be based on desirability of the Turks making concrete contribution to Allied victory against Japan and thus helping to shorten world war. We could point out that apart from consideration described above Turkey lies not far off the route between Great Britain and the Far East and by rupture of relations with Japan could remove important Japanese/
