I have the honor to inform the Department that in the past several weeks an increasing tendency to glorify Japan, thus indirectly deprecating the United States, has been shown by Moroccan newspapers and periodicals, More and more emphasis is being placed on the virtues of the Axis powers in articles which attempt to explain their aims and viewpoints. Even the youth of Morocco is not free from this propaganda and in the August 2, 1942 issue of "JEUNESSE", the official weekly paper of the Moroccan Youth Movement, an article appeared on "Japan wins its battles by its established system of Church and State", written by Pierre CORDIER, a well-known collaborationist attached to the Sécurité Publique at Rabat. A translation is attached.

# TRANSLATION

## "JAPAN WINS ITS BATTLES BY ITS ESTABLISHED SYSTEM OF CHURCH AND STATE."

The Japanese are a great family of which the Emperor is the head, "Son of Heaven, Keeper of the Mirror, of the Sword, of the Jewel", symbols of fundamental virtues: "Wisdom, Courage, and Goodness". He governs his people paternally, still grouped in three classes as in medieval times, warriors, farmers and tradesmen; but now on an equality. Distinctions were suppressed by the national spirit of the country taking its inspiration from Montesquieu and Rousseau, producing another revolution of the 4th of August. In passing it should be noted that the character of the Japanese is always to progress toward better things: only the flowery, shadowed expressions of the language and its exquisite politeness show the respect and the consideration which the lower classes have for even the most important. Unjust taxes and debts have disappeared but spiritual elevation has continued; this was what counted.

Aided by different forms of philosophy, the State has created a form of socialism which is both temporal and spiritual. It is the State in the Church, and the Church in the State, but in spite of all the political views and administrative methods that Japan has adopted from the West, the country has fiercely defended its thousand-year-old practices and beliefs: the unshakeable foundations of the Empire. They are the motivating powers of the country.

And, what adds to its strength is that all life in Japan is at the same time temporal and spiritual. The Emperor is both head of the State and head of the Church. His people are demi-gods. The rites of the Emperor before the altar of domestic gods are followed in exactly the same manner by each head of the family in each household.

When the Emperor sends a proclamation to his people it has the sound of a papal encyclical studded with religious expressions: "We son of Heaven... Our Imperial ancestors built the Empire on a large and indestructible base where they have planted virtue deeply and solidly".

"The path traced here is the doctrine willed to us by our imperial ancestors to be observed as well by their descendants as by their subjects, infallible at all times and true everywhere. It is our wish to take it seriously to heart in common with our subjects so that we can all develop the same virtues." (Extract from the speech of the Emperor Meiji October 30th 1890).

What majesty, what greatness! How far we are from the dry speeches of the Western nations.

In itself this phrase explains the importance of the individual and his insignificant position: the importance of the individual in his acts and thoughts for the Empire - and his individual insignificance. It is the grain of sand of the Bible. Only that which remains is worthy of consideration and it is the destiny and the existence of the Empire founded by the gods that the descendants are working for today and will continue to work for tomorrow. Man is available only a fraction of time to collaborate and maintain the destinies of the Empire. Let death come. If he has done his duty well, he will rejoin his ancestors and those dear to him in Paradise.

Virtue - this word already twice repeated and which a long time ago disappeared from our legislative language. This word really means saintliness for the priesthood, perfection for the artisan and the peasantry, courage for the warrior. This word is born of the community but of a community which strives for better things - perfection - a word unsuited to individualism, for if it is used by the individual it is because of pride and therefore without positive results - a word which today denotes courage, self-denial and the gift of oneself.

These are the virtues of the Japanese nationalists and to which they have applied themselves from the earliest age. Exposed to cold and hunger, their mental development is completed by stories of the life and death of heroes (most of them suicides).

What is more natural than suicide for a Japanese? "The Japanese who kills himself because ordered to do so by his conscience is no different from Socrates who drank hamlock" (Nitobe).

When in the fire and heat of battle a European pilot dives on his objective blowing it up together with his plane, he has succeeded. We admire him but people would state that this is foolish heroism. A European would never voluntarily shut himself in a human torpedo either for a trial or for a battle. A Japanese aviator takes off from his base with his plane fully loaded, cold-bloodedly, calculatingly, and goes to his death without hesitation for the honor of his empire and himself. The enemy may dispose of as many ships as he wishes but the best tactics are to remain far away (unless it is possible to hide under a smoke screen). Nothing will stop a warrior from completing his work, not even women. Beliefs and traditions outweigh all other things. For he knows that soon he will meet his gods and his wife in Paradise. His wife? Yes, because at the news of his death she will have collapsed on the sword of the Samuri.
