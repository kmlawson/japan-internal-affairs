To: Mr. Frederick B. Lyon
Chief
Division of Foreign Activity Correlation
Department of State

From: John Edgar Hoover - Director, Federal Bureau of Investigation

Subject: HESAGOSHI ESHIMA YASUMAGA, with aliases
Colombia

As of possible interest to you, attached is a report received from a reliable source which reflects the facts concerning the activities of the above subject which were presented through diplomatic channels to the Colombian National Police on August 8, 1944, for their consideration as to the internment of the subject. Further, the report reflects that information received on November 20, 1944, from the Colombian National Police indicated that in the opinion of that organization, the evidence presented was considered insufficient for the purpose of interning or deporting Yasumaga. Yasumaga is presently employed as an instructor at La Escuela Normal Superior, Bogota, Colombia.

This information has been made available to the American Embassy, the Military and Naval Attaches at Bogota, Colombia.

Enclosure
cc - Assistant Chief of Staff, G-2
War Department, Washington, D. C.

Navy Department, Washington, D. C.

January 17, 1945

RE: HESAGOSHI ESHIMA YASUMAGA,
with aliases: Hisayoshi Eshima
Yasunaga, Fernando H. Eoima;
Bogotá, Colombia

This investigation is predicated upon information
to the effect that HESAGOSHI ESHIMA YASUMAGA, a Japanese subject who was born in Tokio, Japan, on May 2, 1910, was for a time employed in the file section and as a translator in the Japanese Legation at Bogotá, Colombia.

Searches of YASUMAGA'S home were made with negative results; however, it was reported that he was traveling for the purpose of procuring information for the Spanish Legation.

All of the details concerning the investigation of YASUMAGA were made available to the Colombian National Police on August 8, 1944, at which time they advised that a thorough study of the evidence concerning this individual would be made.

Thereafter, on November 20, 1944, information was obtained from Source C that a review of the evidence had been made, and it was considered insufficient for the purpose of
interning or deporting YASUMAGA. It was further ascertained from this source that he is presently employed by the La Escuela Normal Superior at Bogotá, Colombia, as an instructor.
