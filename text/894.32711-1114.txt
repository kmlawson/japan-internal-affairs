Sir:

It is the desire of the Navy Department to order Lieutenant (jg) Forrest R. Biard, U. S. Navy, to Tokyo, Japan, for the purpose of acquiring a knowledge of the Japanese language. Lieutenant Biard will report to the Naval Attache, American Embassy, Tokyo, for administrative purposes only and will not form a part of the official staff of the Embassy.

Lieutenant Biard graduated from the U. S. Naval Academy in June 1934 and the date of his present rank is 29 May 1937.

It is intended to have Lieutenant Biard report for duty about 1 October, 1939, and it is requested that the appropriate diplomatic officers be so informed.

Respectfully,

Charles Cross
Acting Secretary of the Navy

The Honorable,
The Secretary of State.

Muly 31 1939

Eugene H. Dooman, Esquire,
American Charge d'Affaires ad interim,
Tokyo.

Sir:

The Department advises you of the receipt of a letter from the Navy Department stating that it is the desire of that Department to order Lieutenant (jg) Forrest R. Biard, United States Navy, to Tokyo, Japan, for the purpose of acquiring a knowledge of the Japanese language. It is stated that Lieutenant Biard will report to the Naval Attache to the American Embassy at Tokyo for administrative purposes only, and that he will not form a part of the official staff of the Embassy.

In this relation, reference is made to your telegram of December 13, 1934 concerning the decision of the Japanese Government that it would discontinue according diplomatic status to military and naval officers assigned to the Embassy for study after January 1, 1935.

The Navy Department has advised the Department that Lieutenant Biard graduated from the United States Naval Academy in June 1934, that the date of his present rank is May 29, 1937, and that it is intended to have him report for duty about October 1, 1939.

Very truly yours,
For the Secretary of State:

JUL 29 3 1939PM
FRANCIS B. DAVIS

DEPARTMENT OF STATE
DIVISION OF FAR EASTERN AFFAIRS
August 17, 1933.

TAIHOKU'S despatch of July 14, 1933, reports the visits to Taiwan of three Japanese royal princes with the Japanese fleet. Two admirals were quoted in the press as having made very patriotic statements about the importance of Taiwan in the first line of national defense, one of them saying, "as viewed from the military standpoint, the relations between Japan and the United States have recently become strained a great deal of care must be exercised." The rest of the despatch is not of interest to FE and a copy of it was sent to the Naval Attache at Tokyo who, it is supposed, has forwarded to the Navy Department the parts of interest.
