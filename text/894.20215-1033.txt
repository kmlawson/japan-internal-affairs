The Department has received information from various sources that individuals of the Japanese race have in recent months been engaged in what appear to be topographical surveys along the Pacific and Caribbean coasts of certain Latin American countries. In some cases such surveys appear to have been undertaken by individuals alleged to have represented themselves as fishermen.

At the request of the Director of Naval Intelligence, the Department desires you to investigate discreetly and report promptly upon these and such similar activities as may come to your notice. Comments are desired as to the appearance of any Japanese fishermen and their vessels: do they seem to be bona fide fishermen, or are they more highly educated men in disguise? CONFIDENTIAL - Foreign activities on coasts of Honduras.

At the request of the American Minister at Tegucigalpa a discreet investigation has been made of the reported questionable activities of Japanese or other foreigners on the coasts of Honduras.

The American Consuls on the north coast have made investigations and report that there are no boats, the movements of which could be questioned.

The Naval Attaché visited the south coast and obtained information that no foreigners have been engaged in the fishing business in that section and no boats other than the usual commercial craft have appeared in those waters. The fishing industry in the Gulf of Fonseca is unimportant and is carried on by a few natives using dugout canoes, and the entry of foreigners into the business or the appearance of larger craft could easily be detected.

A report was received from a reliable local Chinaman to the effect that a small boat owned by a Japanese was operating on the north coast of Honduras, however, reliable information from Puerto Cortes, Tela and the Bahia Islands fails to verify the presence of any such boat in that vicinity.

Structure No. 1 to despatch No. 901 of September 22, 1933, from the Legation at Tegucigalpa.

(TRANSLATION)

From "El Cronista" of September 20, 1933.

A MYSTERIOUS VESSEL IS MAKING INCURSIONS INTO THE GULF OF FONSECA AT NIGHT TIME.

From the investigations which are being made it appears that it is the act of Japanese espionage.

SUSPICIONS ARE AWAKENED.

A mysterious vessel is making nocturnal incursions into the waters of the Gulf of Fonseca in the territorial zone between El Salvador and Honduras and principally near the mouths of the rivers.

INVESTIGATIONS.

From investigations which are being undertaken by means of agencies outside of Honduras it has developed that the vessel in question is registered in San Francisco as a fishing boat.

SUSPICIONS ARE AWAKENED.

The maneuvers of the mysterious ship have aroused suspicions in the neighboring republic of El Salvador where an attempt is being made to watch it as closely as possible.

A SPY BOAT.

In the beginning it was suspected to be a vessel carrying contraband of arms for El Salvador or for Honduras but subsequently the idea gained ground that it was a floating center of Japanese espionage and that its incursions into the Gulf were for the purpose of making soundings and gathering maritime data which possibly would be destined for the Ministries of War and Navy of the Empire of the Rising Sun.

ATTITUDE OF THE UNITED STATES.

It appears that the notice of the presence of this mysterious ship in territorial waters of Central America has already reached the United States, which will result in the vessel in question being soon identified and its incursions into the Gulf being put an end to.

Translated by L. H. Typed by C. O.

September 22, my strictly confidential despatch No. 901/ of September 22 and the telegram of September 27,/5 2 P.M. from the American Chargé d'Affaires at San Salvador, repeated to the Department, I have the honor to report that yesterday's edition of El Cronista, leading local newspaper, carried another article with regard to the mysterious vessel which has been operating in the Gulf of Fonseca. It states that

=== Page 15 === (confidence 0.96)
-2-

that it has been seen by a number of persons on other boats in the Gulf, who describe it as being about one hundred and fifty feet long, painted white, but without the usual red paint at the waterline; they add that it does not approach the coast, at least in the daytime, does not whistle, nor observe maritime regulations. It is wondered how it refuels and provisions. It is said also that it was recently in the waters of the Pacific Coast of Guatemala, and when put under observation by an airplane, went out to sea at great speed. It is no longer thought to be a smuggler but generally believed to be an espionage vessel getting hydrographic data in violation of territorial waters.

An assistant editor of *El Cronista* confidentially informed the Legation that when he was recently at La Union, the Gulf port of El Salvador, the military commandant there told him that he had placed a cannon on a hill above the shore to fire on the "mystery ship" should it come within range.

Unfortunately neither El Salvador nor Honduras have any naval or coast guard vessels to send to investigate the unknown, and as it did not make any known landing and kept a good distance from other boats its identity was not ascertained. According to the Legation at San Salvador's telegram, it left the Gulf of Fonseca on September 25 northbound.

The Legation would be most interested to know anything that the Department may learn about this strangely acting vessel.

Respectfully yours,

Julius C. Lay
