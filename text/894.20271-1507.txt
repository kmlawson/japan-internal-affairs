Subject: Japanese Activities in Rumania

Submitted: Japanese Activities in Rumania

The Japanese in Bucharest have enjoyed considerable liberty within the city despite the diplomatic break in relations between the two countries. Arrangements were made for them to return to their homeland by way of the Trans-Siberian Railroad. An American agency planned to remove from a warehouse 10 tons of documents which the Japanese intended to take with them. However, the documents were burned by the Japanese before our raiding party could seize them. This was done when the Japanese were informed by the Russians that they would have to leave by plane.

Contacts with many agents is being maintained by Lt. Colonel Tsuneyuki Shimamuke, former Military Attaché in Bucharest. A number of these agents were formerly employed by Germans. Shimamuki has made arrangements whereby all personnel of the U.S. can be identified upon arrival in capital. Also he has instructed his agents to increase their activities concerning Allied Missions. This information goes via Madrid to Munchen as well as Tokio, it is reported.

Increase of Jap legation expenses has gone up greatly during past month. Almost 2 million JPI per week is being spent.

EUK

He resided principally in Rumania until August 1944, when he fled Bucharest by automobile, together with Eugene BOTTA, an engineer whose nationality is unknown. At the time of their flight, these men were reported to have been carrying gold and currency for Miahi ANTONESCU.

It is believed that he has been trying to proceed to Stockholm, in order from there to return to Japan. In Stockholm he has connections with Miron GIROIU and Paul LAHOVARY of the Rumanian Legation in that city.'

"The Legation is instructed to verify the report of CONSTANTINESCU's residence in Switzerland late in 1944 and to report such additional information on the subject as may be available in Switzerland."

The Legation is instructed to report as fully and as promptly as possible on the activities of CONSTANTINESCU, Miron GIROIU and Paul LAHOVARY.

A-1276, December 26, 1945.
Reference is made to the Department's A-213, March 17, 1945, copy of which was forwarded to Stockholm and Bucharest. Reference is also made to Bucharest's A-22, April 10, 1945, to the Department, repeated to Bern.
Ion Constantinescu, the subject of the reference airgrams, and his Rumanian attorney and associate Dr. Lumitru Chisalicescu recently applied to this Legation for a visit to transit the American zone for Rumania to carry with them medical supplies for that country. Their application was supported by the Rumanian Legation in Bern. Constantinescu and Chisalicescu were interviewed at this Legation on December 4. The full recital of the interview will be forwarded by the Legation in despatch form. In substance, both denied the allegations contained in the reference airgrams and other reports which, in the course of the past two years, have come to this Legation.
Constantinescu swore that he has never been in Germany and has never had any relations directly or indirectly with German or German-controlled enterprises at any place or at any time. Between 1936 and August 1941 he resided in Japan bearing an honorary title as Commercial Counselor to the Rumanian Legation at Tokyo and serving as Mitsui agent exclusively for traffic between Japan and Rumania. He asserts that between September 1939 and the time of his departure from Japan he effected only one transaction between Mitsui and Rumania: the despatch of 5,000 tires to his native land, which he understands were seized enroute in Russia. He left Japan in August 1941 with a visa issued at the United States Embassy, Tokyo, August 4. He was in the United States from October through December 1941. He was interrogated at Bermuda in January 1942 and released.

He proceeded via Lisbon and Madrid to Bucharest to resume management of his trading company, Contcomin, allegedly engaged in barter trade between Rumania and Hungary. He denies that shipments at any time were made by his company under his direction to Germany or that any materials were imported by his company from Germany.

He denies membership in the Iron Guard and contends he assisted and protected Jews. He claims he disregarded Antonescu anti-Semitic decrees by retaining as export manager of Contcomin a certain Tucar, said still to occupy that position in Bucharest. Constantinescu further contends he facilitated and paid for passage of Tucar's son from Bucharest to Palestine during the war. He also contends he rendered financial assistance to two Jewish families from Brasov by the name of Grama (Grumberg) and provided shelter for them at No. 8 G Street, Bucharest for four years.

Constantinescu first entered Switzerland during the war on April 8, 1944, returning again on July 11 and again on August 20, 1944 with a commission from the Rumanian Ministry of Industry to purchase twenty tons of tin and 260 radios. Orders for latter valued at 2,700,000 francs were placed by him with Zellweger A.G., Zurich. Orders for tin valued at 1,200,000 Swiss francs were placed with F. Straub and C. Meier Company, Basel. No deliveries were made and the francs still remain on deposit with the Rumanian Legation, Bern. Constantinescu has remained in Switzerland since August 1944. He cites his application to return to Rumania as proof of willingness to defend himself against all charges of collaboration which may be leveled against him there.

In September 1945, he founded "Trading S.A." of Zurich in conjunction with Prince Nicholas for Swiss import Export business.

With respect to Department's A-213 of March 17, 1945, Constantinescu denied all knowledge of Eugene Rotta. He contends he left Bucharest in August 1944 solely in company of Dr. Chisalicescu. The latter proceeded directly to Stockholm possessed of a power of attorney from Constantinescu to continue negotiations the latter had begun there in March 1944. Negotiations involved effort to sell 5,000 tons Rumanian oil to A/. Transfer, believed to be subsidiary of Enskilda Bank. The oil was to be delivered from Rumania in exchange for commodities from Sweden. No contract was signed

Subject: Yokohama Specie Bank Deposit of Rumanian National in Switzerland.
The American Minister at Bern has the honor to transmit herewith as enclosure No. 1 a request addressed to an officer of this Legation by Ion Constantinescu, a Rumanian national resident in Switzerland, with respect to the latter's credit of Swiss francs 312,800 with the Yokohama Specie Bank in Toyko. It is the Legation's understanding that this credit arose from a transaction in which Mr. Constantinescu engaged prior to his departure from Japan in August 1941. For information concerning Mr. Constantinescu's employment's attention is invited to the Legation's airgram No. A-1276 dated December 26, 1945. He has recently applied to the American Consulate, Bern, for a visa to transit the United States en route to China where he previous to associate himself with the Asia Impex Company, 89 Foa Road, Shanghai. A visa has been issued to him by the Chinese Legation at Bern. In view of the circumstance that Mr. Constantinescu has been acquitted by Swiss Courts of all charges brought against him by the Rumanian Legation at Bern, the Legation perceives no basis on security grounds to urge unfavorable action by the American Consul at Bern in respect to Constantinescu's application for a United States transit visa. In this connection the Department's attention is invited to the Legation's airgram No. A-553 of December 1946.

Mr. Constantinescu has been advised by the Legation that his request for release of funds credited to his account with the Yokohama Specie Bank in Tokyo is being forwarded to the Department and that in view of the numerous questions with respect to Japanese external assets and satisfaction, foreign claimants against Japanese enterprises which have still to be resolved, it is unlikely that any definitive response to his petition may be made prior to his departure for China.

Dear Sir:

It is with pleasure that I recall various conversations we had in past times and in connection with topics then discussed I take the liberty of coming to you with a pressing problem of mine.

As you probably are still well aware, I lived in the Far East, mainly in Japan and China, where my commercial interests were partially located in prewar days. As a result of a transaction then brought to a good end of tires bought in Japan and resold in Japan to the Mitsui Bussan Kaisha, there is a sum of Swfrs. 312'800. on my account with the Yokohama Specie Bank in Tokyo.

On this question, the following cable was sent to the same bank on July 25th. 1946:

"PLEASE CABLE NOW SEEED I DISPOSIT FROM MY CREDIT ACCOUNT NUMBER 8115 ARRIVED FROM GOOD TRANSACTION BEFORE THE WAR MEANS MONEY CASTED FROM 3128 TYPES STOP PLEASE RESPECT ONLY MY WRITTEN DISPOSITIONS - CONSTANTINESCU EDENOTEL ZURICH --

The answer on this cable, dated 25.10.46 reads as follows:

"REPLACING YOUR CABLE 25TH JULY AND 21ST AUGUST (1) CREDIT 8115 ALREADY EXHAUSTED AND RELATIVE EXCHANGES SETTLED (2) REPURCHASE OF 3128 TYPES BY MITSUI AND CO IRRELEVANT TO THIS CREDIT (3) FOR ABOVE SETTLEMENT MITSUI CONTRACTED US EXCHANGES SWISS FOS 312800 EXECUTION THEREOF NOW PROHIBITED BY ALLIED HEADQUARTERS - YOKOHAMA SPECIE BANK --

The last passus of the above cable indicates clearly the existence of this amount of Swiss francs, but as being prohibited for transfer by the Allied Headquarters in Japan.

I beg you to consider kindly the facts:

- that despite being a Rumanian national, I have not relations of any kind with this country or its present regime, on the contrary, I am considered undesirable there owing to my anglo-american sympathies.

- that I am refugee in Switzerland having done no business, neither directly nor indirectly with any axis power or person

- that
