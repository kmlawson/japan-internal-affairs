AIR MAIL
SOCIETY CONFIDENTIAL

Walter A. Foote, Esquire,
American Consul General,
Batavia, Java, Netherlands Indies.

Sir:

The Department has been informed that a Japanese organization called "The Union of South Sea Associations", the announced policy of which is to promote Japan's so-called "co-prosperity sphere" program, is planning, with the support of the Japanese Government, to hold a "Greater South Seas" exhibition in Tokyo, October 15-25, 1941. The Department understands that participation in the exhibition by the South Sea islands under Japanese mandate, French Indochina, and Thailand has already been arranged and that negotiations are now under way with the Netherlands Indies, the Philippines, Borneo, Australia, Burma, et cetera, toward the end that these areas be represented in the exhibition.

The Department desires that you endeavor discreetly and informally to obtain from the appropriate authorities any information which may be available in regard to the reported negotiations concerning the "Greater South Seas" exhibition and that you submit promptly to the Department by

=== Page 2 === (confidence 0.94)
-2-

by air mail a report in regard thereto. For your information, and discreet use should you deem it advisable, the Department has read and the United States High Commissioner to the Philippines to inform the Commonwealth Government that, in the opinion of the American Government, participation in the proposed exhibition by the Commonwealth Government or any of its agencies would be undesirable in view of the fact that the exhibition has as its announced objective the promotion of a scheme which appears to envisage the placing of areas in the southern Pacific, including the Philippine Commonwealth, under Japanese political and economic hegemony.

Very truly yours,

For the Secretary of State:

Sumner Waller

PARAPHRASE OF TELEGRAM SENT

FROM: Secretary of State, Washington, D. C.
TO: United States High Commissioner, Manila
DATED: June 16, 1941, 8 p.m.
NUMBER: 238

With reference to telegram no. 803, June 11, 1941, 11 a.m., from Tokyo which is being repeated to you.

The Department desires that unless you perceive some objection the Commonwealth Government be acquainted by you with the contents of Tokyo's telegram and in such a manner as you may consider suitable, and that you state that it is felt by this Government that it would not be desirable for the Commonwealth Government or any of its agencies to participate in the proposed exhibition. In your discretion you may desire to point out that the objective announced by the sponsors of the exhibition is the promotion of the so-called co-prosperity sphere program of Japan, which seems to envisage the placing of areas in the southern Pacific, the Philippine Commonwealth included, under the economic and political hegemony of Japan; that the Commonwealth's participation in exhibitions of this nature would serve only to further such a plan and therefore would not be in the Commonwealth's best interests. It is requested that the Department be informed of the action taken.

HULL

284, July 8, 11 a.m.

Reference the Department's telegram no. 238, June 16, 5 p.m., the Secretary to President has been informed orally of contents. STRICTLY CONFIDENTIAL

SUBJECT: Proposed "Greater South Seas" exhibition in Tokyo of
THE HONORABLE

O.N.I. AND M.I.D.
THE SECRETARY OF STATE,
WASHINGTON.

SIR:

I have the honor to acknowledge the receipt of the Department's
strictly confidential instruction of July 9, 1941, in regard to
plans by a Japanese organization called "The Union of South Sea As-
sociations" to hold a "Greater South Seas" exhibition in Tokyo, October 15-25, 1941, and concerning the Department's desire that an
endeavor be made to obtain from the appropriate authorities here any
available information relating to reported negotiations with Burma
for participation in the proposed exhibition.

In reply I have to report that I have been informed by the Secretary of the Defence Department of the Government of Burma that the
authorities here were notified telegraphically by the British Government of the plans for the Tokyo exhibition, but that to date "no
approach has been made on the part of the Japanese as far as Burma
is concerned". In connection with the question of Burma's participation in such an exhibition, the Secretary said: "We would not
think of such a thing."

Respectfully yours,

Austis C. Brady
American Consul

894.607 SOUTH SEAS/5
SEP 18 1941
COMMRCIAL AFFAIRS
Department of State

I have the honor to refer to the Department's strictly confidential instruction of July 9, 1941 expressing the desire that the Consulate General endeavor discreetly and informally to obtain from the appropriate authorities any information available in regard to reported negotiations concerning the proposed "Greater South Seas" Exhibition to be held in Tokyo, October 15-25, 1941, and requesting that a prompt report in regard thereto be submitted to the Department by air mail.

Upon receipt of the Department's instruction I discussed the matter informally with the Colonial Secretary, who informed me in confidence that while no Japanese approach as regards participation in the exhibition had been made to the Government here, instructions had been received from London that no such participation should be considered. The Colonial Secretary assumed from the instructions received from London that a Japanese approach had in fact been made in London, though he was without positive information in this respect.

This despatch is being transmitted by the first available air mail subsequent to the receipt of the Department's instruction.

An extra copy of this despatch is enclosed, against the possibility that the Department may wish to forward it to London.

Respectfully yours,

K. S. Patton
American Consul General
