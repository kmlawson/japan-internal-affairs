1/ I have the honor to forward herewith the Embassy's translation of an amendment, promulgated on March 19, 1938, to the Law for Reciprocal Aid in Compliance with the Requests of Foreign Courts.

The law under reference does not specifically deny to consular and diplomatic officials in Japan the right to take testimony or depositions to be used in foreign courts, nor does it declare that such evidence shall be taken only by the Japanese court having jurisdiction; however, this law has been interpreted

interpreted by the Foreign Office and the Ministry of Justice as granting only to Japanese courts the right to take evidence which is to be used in foreign courts, subject to certain prescribed conditions which must be fulfilled before any assistance of a legal nature may be rendered a foreign court. The amendment which is added to section 2 of Article 1 of the law provides that regulations or stipulations of treaties or similar acts may set aside the requirements prescribed and shall be enforced even when they are contrary to the conditions above referred to.

This amendment does not affect the affirmative provision of the first section of the original law requiring that local courts shall give aid to foreign courts upon request for such aid.

However, in view of the amendment specifically mentioning treaties and like documents as having precedence over the provisions of the law as above noted, it is believed that the Government may be considering the advisability of establishing a legal basis for treaties which would grant the right to take testimony for use in foreign courts through other channels than the Japanese courts. Such an amendment could eliminate the basis on which the Japanese Government has insisted that under the laws of Japan, only Japanese courts were allowed to take testimony to be used as evidence in courts of law.

There is also enclosed the translation by a Japanese legal authority of the original law which was forwarded to the Department with the Embassy's despatch No. 1272, May 3, 1935. # LAW FOR GIVING JUDICIAL AID UPON REQUEST OF FOREIGN COURTS
(Law No. 63 promulgated on March 15th, 1905 as amended by Law No. 7 of 1912.)

## ARTICLE I.
- Courts of Law shall give judicial aid to Law Courts of foreign countries in connection with the service of documents and the examination of witnesses and the taking of evidence in civil and criminal matters, upon the request of law courts of foreign countries.

Judicial aid shall be rendered by the Local Court having jurisdiction over the place where the steps requested to be taken will have to be executed.

## ARTICLE 1 bis.
- Judicial aid shall be given only in cases satisfying the following conditions:-
1. The request must be made through the diplomatic channel.
2. In case the service of documents is requested, the letter of request must state the name and nationality as well as the permanent or temporary residence of the person on whom the document is to be served.
3. In case where the taking of evidence of the examination of witness is requested, the letter of request must state the names of the parties to the litigation, the kind of evidence to be taken or examined, the names and nationality as well as the permanent or temporary residence of the persons to be examined, and the questions on which such persons are to be examined. In criminal cases, in addition to the foregoing, a summary of the nature of the pending criminal proceedings must be given.
4.

4. The letter of request as well as documents accompanying the same, if not executed in the Japanese language, must be accompanied with Japanese translations.

5. The country to which the Court making the requisition belongs must guarantee reimbursement of expenses required in giving effect to the letter of request.

6. The country to which the Law Court making the request belongs must give the assurance that in all similar cases the Courts of that country will give judicial aid upon request of the Courts of Japan.

ARTICLE II. - When the matter requested to be executed falls within the competency of another Court that Court to which the letter of request is addressed shall transfer the same to the proper Court having jurisdiction over the matter.

ARTICLE III. - The matter requested to be done shall be performed in accordance with the laws of Japan.

(ARTICLE IV. has been deleted by the amendment above referred to.)

The Department transmits, with its enclosures, a letter addressed to you by the American Consul at Kobe, Japan returning, for the reasons stated, an unexecuted commission to take testimony in the case of Mitsubishi Shoji Kaisha, Limited, versus the United States.

It may be noted from the statements contained in the Consul's letter to you that, under the laws of Japan, neither diplomatic nor consular representatives of foreign governments nor any individual may execute commissions to take testimony issued by foreign tribunals. Such testimony should be obtained through letters-regular.
