DEPARTMENT OF STATE

The Honorable
The Secretary of State,
Washington.

Sir:

Press reports, I have the honor to report, indicate that Japanese capital is being encouraged to turn its attention to Persia for investment and that some headway has been made.

These reports are appeared in different forms in the Teheran press during the last few days. I have not been able to see my Japanese colleague to question him on the authenticity of these news stories but would expect him to minimize their importance, although it would seem that he has been active in trying to promote increased trade relations between Japan and Persia.

# SETARIK'YE DJHAME:

It is understood that a group of Japanese financiers and industrialists are coming to Persia for the purpose of making preliminary studies to embark on economic and industrial enterprises of great importance.

This group consists of the representatives of the following three companies: San-Kio, possessing one of the largest chemical factories; Okura, a big firm engaged in the exploitation of mines; and Mitsubishi, one of the greatest navigation companies of Japan.

# ETTELLA'AT:

While it is noted that as a result of the monopolization of foreign trade a movement has been started for the organization of native firms it should not be neglected that it is in our interest to develop our trade by inviting foreign capital. The representative of the Japanese Company San-Kio has for some time been conducting negotiations with Madji Mirza Habibollah Amin, the holder of the exclusive monopoly of opium, for effecting an important opium deal. Encouraged by Mr. Amin, the representative of the above-mentioned Company associated itself with two other big Japanese firms, Okura and Mitsubishi. Thus a syndicate has been formed for purchasing Persian opium. In view of the keen interest which the Japanese Minister entertains for developing Persian-Japanese trade relations, the Syndicate has agreed not to confine itself to the opium business but to send representatives to Persia for establishing general commercial relations involving imports and exports.

While we have no detailed information as to the nature of the work done by the Syndicate, it goes without saying that it will purchase and export Persian raw products and in return therefor will furnish us with the commodities that we need. We expect nothing else save a trade balance and the more such syndicates are formed, whether foreign or domestic, the more we will be delighted.

It is understood that this Syndicate, which will be called the San-Kio Syndicate, will be organized for the sole purpose of promoting trade between Persia and Japan.

We are extremely happy over the formation of this Syndicate, and we are confident that thanks to the goodwill and interest which the honorable Japanese Minister has displayed in developing the political, commercial and economical relations between Persia and his country, the Syndicate will take long strides in the interest of both parties. The credit will go to Hadji Mirza Habibollah Amin and the Japanese Minister who have rendered invaluable assistance for the success of this cause.

Enclosure No. 2 with Despatch No. 529
of April 16, 1931.

IRAN:

It is reported that an exhibition will shortly be organized in Japan to introduce Persia and the Balkans as the archway of eastern and western civilization. The Persian Government has been invited to take part. The purpose of the exhibition is to make known the conditions in Greece, Persia, Yugoslavia and other Balkan countries constituting an archway between occidental and oriental civilization. In view of the unknown geographic importance of said countries, the city of Osaka which is a commercial and industrial center of Japan, has decided to arrange the Perso-Balkan Exhibition in the Foreign Trade Museum at the Municipality Hall of the city during the whole month of October, 1931.

The Director of the Museum and other members of the staff are most energetically making arrangements for a Persian soirée. The representative of each participating State will deliver a lecture concerning his own country. The national hymn of each country will be sung. There will also be three delegates from Greece, one from Yugoslavia, one from Turkey, one from Iraq, and one from Sicily. It is understood that Mr. Mana-Outa (spelling uncertain), the Director of said Museum, will proceed in a few days to Tokyo to consult with the Japanese Assembly attached to the League of Nations in regard to the above-mentioned lectures and other affairs concerning the exhibition. The exchange of picture and handicraft of the primary and middle schools of the aforesaid countries on the occasion of the inauguration of the exhibition is being contemplated.

The Honorable

Sir:

I have the honor to acknowledge the receipt of the Department's instruction No. 109 of June 4, 1931, enclosing a copy of despatch No. 529 of April 16, 1931, from the Minister at Teheran. The despatch referred to Japanese interest in trading and industrial enterprises in Persia, upon which the Department desired reports particularly in regard to opium.

The Japanese opium monopoly in Formosa and in Dairen has for years purchased Persian opium. Latterly too the manufacturers

=== Page 10 === (confidence 1.00)
-2-

manufacturers of morphine and its salts in Japan have drawn their supplies, in part at least, from the same source. It has been the practice of the Monopoly Bureaus to make their purchases rather through different trading companies than to attempt to purchase direct.

It is quite possible that Japanese trading firms are becoming interested in direct trade with Persia, now that diplomatic relations exist between the two countries. Sankio, or Sankyo, referred to in the despatch is perhaps the largest pharmaceutical concern in this country. It has a working arrangement, I understand, with Parke Davis and the Takamine Laboratories in the United States and has also a connection with the Aluminum Company of America. It got into the aluminum business apparently through the sale of aluminum utensils in its retail drug stores. It would not be surprising if Sankyo should come to some arrangement with Persian opium sellers to obtain supplies.

The Mitsubishi interests, referred to in the Minister's despatch, are the largest stockholders in the Nippon Yusen Kaisha, which operates ships to the Persian Gulf. The trading company of this group is known as the Mitsubishi Shoji Kaisha. I have not yet had an opportunity to investigate the extent of the Mitsubishi interest in Persian trade.

Okura and Company are a large concern who for many years have affected interest in new ventures. I do not know to what extent they would be prepared to back a Persian trading venture.
