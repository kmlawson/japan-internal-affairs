Vancouver, B. C., Canada
April 11, 1933.

Visit of Japanese Training Squadron

I have the honor to report that following an exchange
of official calls at Victoria on the 11th of June, training
squadron, consisting of two men, the TANGO and the TATO,
under the command of Vice Admiral John C. Houtche, arrived at
Vancouver on the morning of 11th June and that their departure
for San Francisco at 3:30 pm was approved by the
Consulate General to the Chief of Staff of the Thirteenth Naval
District, Seattle, Washington, in a telegraphic reply to a letter
of reply. I in which he requested that information as to the
exact time of the departure of those men. From Vancouver he so
regarded to him, in order that he in turn might inform the
Navy Department as to the probable time of the arrival of the
ships in San Francisco.

At the time of the Squadron's arrival Vice Admiral Hyakutake
issued the following statement:

"We are here on a friendly visit to express
to you our earnest desire to co-operate with you
in fulfilling our common mission in the Pacific,

"The principal duty of our training Squadron, as its name shows, is to give training to the young midshipmen for their future career, yet I wish to give our crews the opportunities of elevating their experiences and knowledge by getting acquainted with Canada and her people, in whom they are so much interested.

"The ocean was not placed there for us to quarrel over. It is there for our benefit and advancement. We are happy to see, so far as the Dominion of Canada and Japan are concerned, that there has been a steady growth of appreciation and understanding.

"Towards the Dominion of Canada, we have always had a natural feeling of friendship and admiration, so that our present visit is altogether congenial.

"It is my earnest wish, as it is my greatest hope, to promote better and friendly relations between this country and that which I have the honor to serve."

On the evening of the 5th Vice Admiral Hyakutake and the officers of his staff and of the TAUO and the TATE were tendered a banquet by the Mayor and City Council of Vancouver at which the other guests, with the sole exception of the American Consul General, were representative Canadian and Japanese residents of Vancouver, Canadian officials, naval and military predominating, and the Japanese Naval Attachés at Ottawa and Washington.

Japanese and Canadian speakers at the banquet in their exchange of expressions of goodwill in the name of their respective countries emphasized the importance of the maintenance of cordial relations between Canada and Japan as Pacific neighbors.

On April 5, following the banquet, the acting Japanese Consul held a reception in the ballroom of the Hotel Vancouver at which there was a large attendance of Dominion and Provincial officials, consular officers and representative citizens of British

=== Page 4 === (confidence 0.98)
British Columbia.

On the afternoon of April 6 Vice Admiral Hyakutake and his staff received a considerable number of invited guests on board the IWATE and on the following day these visiting officers were guests at a luncheon tendered them by the Japan Society of Vancouver.

The popular interest in the visit of the Squadron with its complement of more than fifteen hundred officers and sailors was evidenced by the sightseeing and other arrangements that had been made for the entertainment of the visitors and by the large crowds that daily at all times thronged the docks alongside the ships and visited the ships during the hours that they were open to the public.

The successful outcome of this visit is apparent from a VANCOUVER SUN editorial of April 6, as quoted below:

"OUR PACIFIC NEIGHBOR CALLS"

"As official representatives of the Japanese people — our great and good neighbors across the Pacific — the warships YAKUNO and IWATE, Vice Admiral Hyakutake, his officers and men, are exceedingly welcome in Vancouver.

"However much we may disagree with Japan's present policy in Manchukuo, that disagreement does not by one jot abate the warmth of our regard for the Japanese people.

"This is a call of courtesy by a people who are the very personification of courtesy.

"And as we receive them, we remember with gratitude the utmost graciousness with which the Japanese have always greeted and treated our own Canadian visitors in Japan.

"We remember, too, the part Japanese warships played in protecting this coast from German raiders during the Great War.
