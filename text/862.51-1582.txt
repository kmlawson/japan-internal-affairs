NEW YORK 5 July 9, 1947.

The Honorable George C. Marshall,
The Secretary of State,
Washington, D. C.

Dear Sir:

Reference is respectfully made to the undersigned's letter to you dated May 6, 1947 with regard to the position of American holders of various Dollar Bonds issued by obligors located in countries which were at war with the United States or were enemy-occupied.

At the time the above-mentioned letter was written, the undersigned had just received certain advice relating to three such issues of Dollar Bonds guaranteed by the Japanese Government and as a result, our letter dealt primarily with such issues. In the schedule which was attached to our letter of May 6 there were included issues of German and Polish foreign Dollar Bonds, which prior to the war, had been offered in this country by groups headed by the undersigned. Another copy of that schedule is sent to you herewith.

The undersigned respectfully calls to your attention the issues of German and Polish Dollar Bonds appearing on the schedule which issues were not discussed at length in our May 6, 1947 letter. We would very much appreciate any information which you can furnish to us with respect to the steps being taken to protect the interests of American holders of these German and Polish Dollar Bonds.

Yours respectfully,
