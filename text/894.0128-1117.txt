AMERICAN CONSULATE GENERAL,
Tokyo, Japan, August 28, 1935.

SUBJECT: Revision of Japanese Passport Regulations.

THE HONORABLE
THE SECRETARY OF STATE,
WASHINGTON.

SIR:

I have the honor to enclose, as of possible interest, an original copy and translation of a press release of the Japanese Foreign Office in regard to the revision of the Japanese Passport Regulations. The revised regulations were published in full in the Official Gazette of July 22, 1935. The Consulate General has been informed by a member of the staff of the Japanese Foreign Office that the only changes of importance are those mentioned in the stated press release.

Perhaps the most significant change is the addition of South and Central American countries to those for which passports valid for more than one journey may be issued. The Japanese announcement states that formerly only the United States of America, British India, and the Netherlands East Indies, "countries of chief commercial important to Japan", were on the list of those for which "special" passports might be issued.

TRANSLATION.

Revision of Japanese Passport Regulations and
Supplementary Rules.

July 22, 1935.

The existing passport regulations and supplementary
rules were promulgated in May, 1929, and as there have
various points which necessitate revision, the
following changes have been promulgated to take effect
on and after September 1, 1935.

1. Revision of the passport regulations.

2. Notification of the Ministry of Foreign Affairs
in regard to regions for which passports valid
for more than one journey may be issued.

3. Procedure for the carrying out of the passport
regulations.

4. Revision of Section 4 of Article I of the Regu-
lations for the enforcement of the Immigrant
Protection Law.

Of the foregoing, the first, second, and fourth are
published in detail in today's Official Gazette, while
the third section was issued as an order of the Ministry
of Foreign Affairs to the various local governors, the
Governor of the Kwantung Leased Territory, and the heads
of the Japanese Government offices abroad.

The most important points in which revisions were
made are as follows:

According to the Immigrant Protection Law, an intend-
ing emigrant must have, in addition to a passport, per-
mission from his local administrative authorities to go
abroad. According to the above mentioned law an "immi-
grant" is a person who is going abroad for the purpose
of labor and all other persons going abroad are classed
as "nonimmigrants". However, according to the supple-
mentary rules in connection with passport regulations an
immigrant is defined as a third-class steamship passenger.
The present revision makes the supplementary passport
rules conform to the definition of "immigrant" as found in
the Immigrant Protection Law.

Formerly

=== Page 14 === (confidence 0.99)
-2-

Formerly a passport was issued valid for three years, for the convenience of those persons who in the course of business find it necessary to make many trips back and forth between Japan and certain countries. The countries to which such passports might be issued for repeated journeys have been confined to those countries which have important commercial relations with Japan, such as the United States of America, British India, and the Netherlands East Indies. According to this revision the countries of Central and South America shall be added to the list of countries for which such passports may be issued.

In the Certificate of Personal Status to be presented by both immigrants and nonimmigrants in applying for passports, it was formerly necessary to have the statements regarding military service, rewards, and punishments, payment of taxes, certified to by city, village, town and ward officer as well as by the chief of the local police department. Under the new regulations the certification of the police chief is no longer necessary.

In short, the present revision has been made for the purpose of clearing up doubtful points in the law and supplementary regulations and to make the procuring of a passport more convenient for intending voyagers.

Translated: J. A.
