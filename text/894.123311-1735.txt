No. 364

Subject: Repatriation to the United States of Ashes of Allied Dead from Mass Graves In Japan

The Honorable
Secretary of State,
Washington.

Sir:

With reference to the Department's instruction No. 288 of December 16, 1946, concerning the repatriation to the United States of the ashes of Allied dead buried in mass graves in Japan, I have the honor to report that upon receipt of the instruction a note dated January 5, 1947 was transmitted to the Netherlands Ministry for Foreign Affairs requesting the Ministry to obtain permission from the appropriate authorities for the cremated remains of the decedents to be brought to the United States for final burial.

Repeated oral inquiries have been made to the Foreign Ministry in an effort to expedite action on the Department's instruction but in each instance, Dr. A. Adriaanse, the Chief of the Juridical Section of the Foreign Ministry, upon checking with the appropriate official in the Ministry of Overseas Territories, has informed the Embassy that the decision which rests with the Netherlands East Indies Government has not been reached. When the matter was discussed with Dr. Adriaanse today he expressed deep regret at the delay, which he admitted was inexcusable, and promised to press the Ministry of Overseas Territories for an immediate reply.

Respectfully yours,
For the Ambassador:
Philip W. Bonsal
Counselor of Embassy

16 November 1948

Chief, Division of Protective Services
Department of State
Room 605, 515 22nd Street, N. W.
Washington, D. C.

ATTENTION: Mr. William McCahon

Dear Sir:

We have been requested by the father of the late Staff Sergeant Solomon H. Groner, ASN 32 818 450, now in the United States Military Cemetery Yokohama #1, Japan, to have his remains removed to Palestine, for final burial.

Under Public Law 368, 80th Congress, we are required to comply with the wishes of the next of kin of the decedent since Palestine is his homeland.

Therefore, it is requested that the Department of State initiate action to secure permission from the appropriate authorities for final burial of the decedent's remains in a private cemetery located at Jerusalem, Palestine. Mr. Max Groner, father of the decedent, has designated Rabbi Zalmon Grossman, Even Yehoshua, Jerusalem, to receive the body.

Sincerely yours,

GEO. W. HINMAN, JR.
Colonel, QMC
Memorial Division

Secretary of State,
Washington.
A.160, June 28, 1949.

The assistance of the Embassy has been requested by Mr. James Craigie, 23 Queen Street, Wellington, in determining whether or not the body of Captain Edward Glenn WALKER, United States Marine Corps, who was killed in action in November 1942 at Tarawa, has been returned to the United States for repurial. Captain Walker was a native of Lebanon, Tennessee, and is believed to have been attached to the 2nd. Marine Division at the time of his death. No further information concerning him is available.

Mr. Craigie states that he was a close friend of Captain Walker's and would like to know the location of Captain Walker's grave.

It is requested that the Department endeavor to ascertain the information requested by Mr. Craigie.

SCOTTEN
