Sir:

From information obtained from the finance department of the Kwantung Government, I have the honor to make the following report on the proposed 1933-34 fiscal year budget for the Government of the Kwantung Leased Territory. As the Embassy is no doubt aware, the budget has been approved by the Cabinet; and local officials of the Kwantung Government seem confident that it will be passed without alteration when the diet next meets.

The budget for the coming fiscal year (April 1, 1933, to March 31, 1934) calls for an expenditure of Yen 25,864,792. This is an increase of Yen 3,128,776 over the budget for the current fiscal year. The budget for 1932-33 amounted to Yen 22,736,016, and that for 1931-32 to Yen 22,171,316. It is worthy of note that the budget is virtually unaffected by the depreciation in the gold value of the Japanese yen.

An intelligible explanation of the meaning and divisional basis of "ordinary" and "extraordinary" expenditures seems impossible to obtain. Also the same item will be included in both ordinary and extraordinary expenditures without, in many cases, any clear reason for the division. The appropriation for meteorological observatories is made, of course, on behalf of military and commercial aviation in the Territory and Manchuria. Prior to September, 1931, there was no air transportation service in Manchuria. Non regular service is maintained between the time of the important cities of Manchuria and connections are possible through Dairen and Korea with Japan. The proposed meteorological observatories will be of considerable service to military and commercial aviation in furnishing more exact information concerning weather and flying conditions.

One fact is clear from a study of the budget. The intimacy and lack of clarity with regard to functions which characterizes the relationship of the Kwantung Government, the Kwantung Army, and the South Manchuria Railway Company are reflected in the Kwantung Government's budget. This is the first annual budget of the newly organized Kwantung Bureau which under the new system inaugurated last year administers the Japanese governmental establishments in Manchuria, including the Kwantung Lease Territory. In the South Manchuria Railway Zone, the Kwantung Bureau administers police offices, justice posts and telegraphs, as did the Government of the Leased Territory heretofore and most other civic services and public utilities and public works continue to be operated by the South Manchuria Railway Company.

The budget balances at Yen 24,738,165, as compared with the figure of Yen 22,911,058 for the budget of the previous fiscal year of the Kwantung Leased Territory Government. Of the ordinary revenues, amounting to Yen

Receipts from State undertakings yield 59.3 per cent, the principal items being receipts from the monopoly and receipts from communications (posts, telegraphs and telegrams). Of the extraordinary revenues amounting to Yen 7,939,015, the largest item is surplus from last year's account, amounting to Yen 4,915,289, which illustrates the Japanese fiscal practice of estimating budgets conservatively.
