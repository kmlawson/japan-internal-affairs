RE ACCOUNT OF PISAL PANITE TRADING COMPANY WITH SUMITOMO BANK

Sir:

We beg to acknowledge receipt of your letter of July 28, instructing us, in accordance with the memorandum for your Ministry from GHQ, SCAP, 004.2 (25 July 1947) CPC/FP., to submit a report on the outstanding balance of the account of Pisal Panite Trading Company, Siam, with this Bank, Osaka.

In reply we would inform you that through our records etc. the following facts have been ascertained:-

In March, 1941, our New York Agency received the sum of $6455.00 from Banca Commerciale Italiana, New York City, for account of Pisal Panite Trading Co. by the order of "Bankasia" but having had no instructions from Japan on the matter at that time, they cabled their Head Office, Osaka, advising the receipt of this money and requesting to investigate and confirm the matter. In the meantime they placed the sum of money received to their "Head Office Account".

On receiving the above telegram, our Head Office, Osaka, had looked into the matter but could not trace it and they had no instructions from any one in this respects. Meanwhile the circumstances changed and the money was frozen in New York by the U. S. Government Order.

According to our records received from our New York agency, the money had not been paid by them to any one on or before Oct. 23, 1941, but we can not trace its fate after Oct. 23, 1941, as no report has been received from New York on the matter, and it is certain that the money was not transferred to this country, and all our offices in Japan have no account with Pisal Panite Trading Co., Siam. The above is all we can say at the moment and we would suggest that if you will be able to investigate the records and documents of our New York agency, now in the hand of the liquidator designated by U. S. Government, the matter can be made clear.

Trusting the above will meet your requirement.

Yours truly,

The Sumitomo Bank, Ltd.

s/ Ken'ichiro Mizugaki
(Ken'ichiro Mizugaki)
Manager, Liaison Department

The files of this Office contain no further information with respect to the credit balance of Pisal Panite Trading Company with the Sumitomo Bank, Ltd., New York Agency, than is contained in the Siamese Ambassador's letter of May 27, 1947, a copy of which was enclosed with the State Department transmittal note under reference. We have been advised by our New York Office that the file of the New York Agency of Sumitomo Bank, Ltd. relating to this matter has not been found and that many of the records of the New York Agency were destroyed before the War. Information taken from the files of Banca Commerciale Italiana, however, discloses that the sum in question represented the unutilized portion of a credit in favor of Pisal Panite Trading Company, which was transmitted on February 27, 1941, by the Torino Office of Banca Commerciale Italiana to the Osaka Office of Sumitomo Bank via the New York offices of the respective banks. The amount in question was actually received by Sumitomo, New York, from Banca Commerciale Italiana, New York, and credited by it to the Osaka Office of Sumitomo Bank, Ltd. Whether Osaka ever actually accepted the credit in question does not appear from available records. It does appear, however, that Osaka requested Sumitomo, New York, to repay the amount to the Torino Office of Banca Commerciale Italiana, but that Torino refused to accept the repayment and so advised Sumitomo, New York, via Banca Commerciale Italiana, New York.

As matters stand, the records of Sumitomo, New York, do not reflect any amount due and owing to Pisal Panite Trading Company or to anyone else with respect to the transaction. The fund receiv-

We note, however, that Sumitomo Bank, Osaka, has advised that it has no record of having received the credit balance in question.

In his letter of May 27, 1947, the Siamese Ambassador requests information from the Department of State with respect to the formalities required for filing a claim to recover the credit balance in question. Ordinarily one of two procedures for filing a claim could be followed in a matter of this kind, one against the New York Agency of Sumitomo Bank, Ltd. in Liquidation, and the other against the Office of Alien Property under the provisions of Public Law 671, 79th Congress. It would appear, however, that at the present time Pisal Panite Trading Company is barred from filing a claim under either procedure.

The time limit within which claims against the New York Agency of Sumitomo Bank were to have been filed expired in 1942. Under New York law the Superintendent of Banks does not have the power to accept a claim after the expiration of the time within which claims must be filed. Consequently, a claim by Pisal Panite Trading Company against the liquidator could not be allowed at this time. However, there is presently pending in the courts of the State of New York litigation involving the effectiveness of the bar date against creditors who were prevented from filing their claims or instituting suit on rejected claims within the statutory period because of failure of communications due to the outbreak of war. In the event that a decision favorable to the claimant is granted in this case, Pisal Panite Trading Company may then become eligible to file a claim against the New York Agency of Sumitomo Bank.

Under Public Law 671 of the 79th Congress, this Office is authorized to accept and process claims of creditors against the vested assets of their debtors. Under the provisions of this Public Law, however, eligible claimants are limited to United States citizens and persons who have resided in the United States since December 7, 1941. It would appear, therefore, that Pisal Panite Trading Company is ineligible to file a debt claim with this Office for the recovery of the credit balance in question. We are enclosing a copy of Public Law 671, 79th Congress, for transmittal to the Siamese Embassy.
