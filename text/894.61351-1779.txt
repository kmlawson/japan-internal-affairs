FROM: Shanghai
TO: Secretary of State
NO: 3945, September 20, 5 p.m.
FOR DAWSON FROM PEASLEE

Data for inclusion your sugar report. Though Shanghai cut off from major Chinese producing regions, Shanghai Chemical Supply Company, subsidiary of State Shanghai General Trading Company which took over Taiwan Sugar Company, stated TSC left about 38,000 tons sugar, estimated enough Shanghai for one year local consumption. This checks with TSC's early April report of 30,000. Present private stocks also believed large.

Prices Shanghai fairly stable past months, recently showing declines both in US and PN. Following mid-month PN prices per shih picul and exchange rate US dollar: June 22,500; 1,700 July 56,000; 1,900 August 60,000; 2,250 September 58,500; 3,200. Just before fall Shanghai 245 MM million gold yuan and exchange 45 MM million. Sugar subject commodity tax 25 percent ad valorem starting September 15. Last four months SCSC permitted unlimited sales but dealers and bulk users must register with company. Present sales out- port cities mainly through regional state-operated trading companies for trading other commodities. Present economic conditions, especially reduced purchasing power, absence speculative activities, general austerity tendency monthly sales inclusive to outports very small. Even during pre-changeover with intense military activity central China 3 to 4 thousand tons released monthly Shanghai market alone. Present volume believed about 1,000 tons monthly despite fact present usually heavy sales season with mid-autumn festival approach.

Manchurian

-2- #3945, September 20, 5 p.m., from Shanghai

Manchurian press reports indicate one beet sugar mill apparently Sino-Russian ownership operating. Another near Harbin under repair for beginning production January. 7,000 tons sugar reported produced 1948 from total beet acreage 100,000 shih now. 1949 acreage reported reduced to 80,000 due shortage seeds, in past usually from Europe or America. Consulate General Peiping reported August 17 sugar production expected be 5,000 tons. 1950 plan for sowing 150,000. Beet acreage all in Sungkiang province.

Following date Taiwan available Shanghai. Final production figure TSC 1948-9 placed 631,000 metric tons. Cane farmers generally dissatisfied prices paid their share, and complained depreciation currency due long delays in payment. Due lack foreign markets NRC decided reduce 49-50 to 90,000 hectares compared 120,302 during 43-9. TSC still encountering trouble from tenant farmers who refuse plant cane due low prices, besides 100,000 tons sold SCAP January additional 50,000 sold July. TSC also contracted sell 50,000 British South Seas 8,500 tons Syria, 18,000 Egypt. Kao Hsiung harbor office registered 158,000 tons left port from January through June 20.

NO: 687, November 10

![img-0.jpeg](img-0.jpeg)

Taiwan sugar report:

1948-49 sugar production 632,000 metric tons. Exports January 1 through end October about 271,000 tons including 150,000 to January, 50,800 to Britain, 25,500 to China mainland before May 18, 18,000 to Egypt, 11,000 to Burma, 8,500 to Syria, 7,300 to Hong Kong and Siam. Additional 100,000 tons contracted to Japan, 7,500 tons crude brown reported contracted to Spain. 26,000 tons destroyed in Kaohsiuv harbor explosion in August. Domestic consumption figures not available but even if considerably increased figure of 32,000 tons 1949 surplus appears about 200,000 metric tons. 1948 carry-over was 20,000 tons.

Agricultural Administration says planted area for 1949-50 crop 126,500 hectares, compared with 17,400 for 1948-49, and estimates 1950 sugar production 500,000 metric tons. Decreased yield attributed by other authoritative source to diversion of fertilizer (distributed to farmers by sugar corporation) to rice gbr irdl*, and to fact increased sugar planting area mostly dry field instead of higher yielding rotation planting in paddy fields. Cane varieties having shorter growing period and lower yield reportedly planted by farmers who are reluctant tie up fields for 18 months.

-2- #687, November 10, from Taipei

For 1950-51 crop 60,000 hectares of projected 103,000 planted by end of October. Believe total planting area will not exceed 85,000 hectares. Fertilizer distribution increased by Taiwan Sugar Corporation to 400 kilograms of ammonium chloride and 120 kilograms ammonium nitrate per hectare in effort to reach sugar production of 500,000 metric tons which necessary for efficient operation of sugar mills.

F.O.B. price expert sugar about US $110 per met 1 ton. Further price data will follow by airgram.

Sent Department; repeated A. CONGEN Shanghai 31. Pouched to FOLAD Tokyo.

MACDONALD
