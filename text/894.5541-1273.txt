No. 75
THE FOREIGN SERVICE
OF THE
UNITED STATES OF AMERICA
American Consulate General,
Harbin, Manchuria, June 19, 1941.

CONFIDENTIAL

Subject: Japanese Obstructive Policy in
Grooming Visas to British Subjects.

The Honorable
Clarence E. Gauss,
American Ambassador,
Peiping, China.

Sir:

I have the honor to report that the Japanese
Foreign Office, which now requires that requests for
visas from British subjects should be passed on in
Tokyo, is now following an "obstructive" policy in
retaliation for the British treatment of Japanese
wishing visas for various parts of the British Empire.

A frank statement to this effect was recently made
in Harbin by a Japanese consular officer in connection
with the delays being experienced by a prominent British
bank official, who has been transferred to Shanghai
after some years residence in Harbin. This British
subject requested only a mere transit visa to pass
through Dairen from the railway station to the steamer.
His wife was granted a visa on her American passport two
weeks ago.

Respectfully yours,

L. H. Courley
American Consul
