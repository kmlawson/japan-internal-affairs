Mr. Stanley K. Hornbeck, Chief,
Division of Far Eastern Affairs,
Department of State,
Washington, D.C.

May 7, 1937.

Dear Mr. Hornbeck:

We have just had a report from the representative of United Artists Corporation in Tokyo stating that the early part of April their manager there received from the post office in Tokyo an accumulation of mail from the United States that had been in that post office over a period of three weeks and all of the letters had been opened and read by the authorities. This man states that his mail seems to have been opened for some time in the past, both business and personal correspondence, and that such mail is often held for an extended time before it is delivered.

I do not know whether anything can be done about this or not but would appreciate it very much if the matter could be taken up with our Embassy in Japan to try to eliminate this type of hindrance in our business relations there.

With best personal wishes, I am

Most sincerely,

My dear Mr. Herron:

The receipt is acknowledged of your letter under date May 7, 1937, stating that your Tokyo representative has complained that his mail is being tampered with by the Japanese postal authorities and requesting that the matter be taken up with the American Embassy in Japan.

Your letter does not indicate whether your Tokyo representative has already made any representations locally in regard to the matter complained of. However, the Department is instructing the American Consul General in Tokyo to make an investigation of the facts and to take appropriate action.

Sincerely yours,

For the Secretary of State:

AMERICAN CONSULATE GENERAL
Tokyo, Japan, June 17, 1937.

SUBJECT: Tampering of Mail Matter of United Artists Corporation by Local Postal Authorities.

THE HONORABLE
THE SECRETARY OF STATE.
WASHINGTON.

SIR:

Letter to Frederick Herron July 23 1937

I have the honor to acknowledge receipt of the Department's instruction of May 11, 1937, enclosing copy of a letter from Mr. Frederick L. Herron, Foreign Manager, Motion Picture Producers and Distributors of America, Incorporated, addressed to the Chief of the Division of Far Eastern Affairs, regarding the alleged tampering by Japanese postal authorities of mail forwarded to the company's representative in Tokyo. Mr. A. A. Lowe is the manager in Japan of the United Artists Corporation and the person referred to by Mr. Herron. A precis of an interview with Mr. Lowe follows.

It appears that it was not the intention of Mr. Lowe to make complaint officially. Had he deemed that the circumstances warranted intervention, he would have appealed to the Consulate General directly. His statements to his home office were merely informative. He expressed regret that he had not amplified his remarks to his superiors. Mr. Lowe asks that no action be taken.

It is probable that Mr. Lowe is correct in his surmise that his mail has been subject to scrutiny and possible tampering by Japanese authorities. American firms and individuals from time to time voice the opinion that mail matter addressed to them has been unduly delayed and perhaps opened. Occasionally suspicion has been created that consular mail has received like treatment. This subject as it regards private and official matter has been discussed with the Embassy (Counselor Neville and Secretary Dickover). It was decided that in the absence of definite proof it would be useless to take the matter up with the Foreign Office or other competent authorities, since no serious consequences have ever ensued which might be charged to irregular practices on the part of postal authorities.

Mr. Lowe stated that his home office assembles an assortment of first class mail matter and forwards it in a large envelope. It is only such large covers which appear to be subject to tampering. Single letters are delivered timely and in order. Since motion picture films are subject to censorship it is possible that larger packages may foster suspicion that an attempt was being made to import films without censor scrutiny.

No further action will be taken by the Consulate General.

Respectfully yours,

Arthur Garrels,
American Consul General
