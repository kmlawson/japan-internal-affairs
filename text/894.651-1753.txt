TO : JK - Mr. Whitman
DATE: October 9, 1946
FROM : JK - Mr. Hodge
SUBJECT: Japanese Machine Tool Industry and Inventories.

The Japanese Ministry of Commerce and Industry estimated 1944 machine tool holdings at 788,700 units on the basis of inventories in 50% of civilian industry in that year, and after making allowance for an estimated 30,000 units destroyed by bombing. Bomb losses in 1945 are estimated at an additional 189,000 units. On this basis, present inventories should be about 600,000 units (neglecting production of about 8,800 units from the beginning of the 1945 fiscal year through June of this year). The estimate of current holdings must be considered only approximate; it is probably somewhat high in terms of units classifiable as machine tools in terms of accepted American definition.

Productive capacity of the Japanese machine tool industry is tentatively estimated at about 67,000 units annually on the basis of peak production at that level achieved in 1938. Under the Interim Program, the Japanese machine tool industry is to be reduced to an annual productive capacity of 27,000 units. It is therefore tentatively estimated that capacity for production of about 40,000 units annually should become available for claim under the Interim Program.

No direct provision is made in the Interim Program for reducing overall machine tool inventories, but a substantial number of machine tools should become available not only from units in the part of the machine tool industry designated as available for reparations, but also from aircraft plants, army and navy arsenals, and privately owned munitions plants, and from tools associated with other industrial facilities included in the interim reparations removals. It is very tentatively estimated that, aside from those units suitable only for armament manufacture and therefore not available for reparations, a total of about 200,000 units should become available for claim under the interim program.

Because the estimate of current holdings is believed to be somewhat high, the figure for units available for reparations must in consequence be considered as possibly inflated. In addition, allowance must be made for possibly poor condition of some of the units.

Production of new machine tools reached a low of about 200 units in October 1945, and was reported as 517 units completed in April 1946. No specific information is available as to the types now being made nor the purposes for which they are being manufactured. However, the machinery industry is concentrating actively on production of machinery for the mining, food processing, textile and other civilian industries, and it is safe to assume that activity in the machine tool industry is related to operations in the civilian machinery industry.

Subject: Information Regarding Manufacture of Lead Tetra-Ethyl in Japan.

The Acting Political Adviser has the honor to enclose copies of the following correspondence regarding manufacture of lead tetra-ethyl in Japan:

(1) Copy of memorandum dated June 20, 1949, sent by Australian Mission in Japan to General Headquarters, Supreme Commander for the Allied Powers, requesting, on behalf of the Associated Ethyl Co., Ltd., information regarding the possible resumption of manufacture in Japan of lead tetra-ethyl and its related and intermediate products;

(2) Copy of memorandum dated August 2, 1949, sent by Economic and Scientific Section, General Headquarters, SCAP, to Australian Mission, containing information regarding wartime production of lead tetra-ethyl and stating that no plants are now producing that item at present;

(3) Copy of memorandum dated July 6, 1949, referred to in (2) and sent by the Nippon Soda Co., Ltd., to Lt. Col. E. C. ALLAN, Patent Officer, Scientific and Technical Division, Economic and Scientific Section, General Headquarters, SCAP, regarding manufacturing process used by all Japanese wartime producers of lead tetra-ethyl.

The enclosed material is transmitted to the Department with the thought that information on lesser-known industries of Japan may prove of interest to various United States Government agencies.

Manufacture of Lead Tetra-Ethyl

An enquiry has been received from the Australian Government on behalf of the Far Eastern representative of the Associated Ethyl Co. Ltd., concerning the possible resumption of manufacture in Japan of lead tetra-ethyl and its related and intermediate products ethylene dichloride, metallic sodium, ethylene, ethyl chloride and bromine. It is understood that in pre-war times considerable quantities were produced in several plants, most of which were destroyed or damaged by bombing towards the end of the war.

It is believed that little heed was paid by Japanese manufacturers to international patent rights before the war. While it is not desired to pursue any detailed enquiries into past history in this regard, some assurance is sought that any attempt at re-establishment of the industry will be made with full recognition of Patents held by British and other Foreign owners.

Our enquirers would appreciate any information that may be available in this connection.

/s/ Patrick Shaw
(Patrick Shaw)

MEMORANDUM FOR: The Head of the Australian Mission in Japan

SUBJECT: Manufacture of Lead Tetra-Ethyl

1. Reference is memorandum from the Australian Mission in Japan to Supreme Commander for the Allied Powers, Attention C-2: Foreign Liaison, reference TI/37.2/667, dated 20 June 1949, subject: Manufacture of Lead Tetra-Ethyl.

2. Tetra-ethyl lead was produced in Japan by the following persons:

a. Nippon Soda Company, Limited (Nihongi Plant)
b. Hodagaya Chemical Company (Koriyama Plant)
c. Second Naval Fuel Depot at Yakaichi
d. Third Naval Fuel Depot at Tokoyama

3. Mitsui Chemical Company was about to commence production at the end of hostilities.

4. The Nippon Soda Company, Limited plant had an output of seventy (70) kiloliters per month at the end of hostilities. They have not produced any since then and, according to officials of the company, they have no present intention of resuming production. Their tetra-ethyl lead plant has been converted into a dyestuff plant. The special equipment for the tetra-ethyl lead production has been stored. They have a stock of twenty-two (22) kiloliters which they are anxious to dispose of.

5. The Hodagaya Chemical Company was producing 1,574 kiloliters of ethyl fluid in 1944. In 1945 they produced 300 kiloliters of fluid up to March 12, when the plant was almost completely destroyed

To: Colonel Allan

From: Nippon Soda Company, Limited

1. Manufacturing Process

A. Lead-soda alloy manufacturing

In the alloy vessel, lead soda and a trace of magnesium are placed and the air therein is displaced by carbonic acid gas. As the vessel temperature being gradually raised up to 400-440°C, the content is stirred up and after about one hour, is poured into the alloy receptacle to be pulverized.

B. Ethyl Chloride manufacturing

In the catalyzer vessel, ferric chloride solution is placed and the vaporized alcohol prepared beforehand is blown in. After a while, hydrochloric acid gas is also blown in. As the ethyl chloride is formed simultaneously with blowing in the gas, it is cooled down and condensed after being neutralized in the calcium neutralization tank and passed through the sulphuric acid washer and the calcium chloride tower. Then by blowing the air in, hydrogen is given out in the catalyzer vessel, to which chlorine is blown in to recover in oxidization of catalyzed ferric chloride into ferric chloride. The content is transferred to washing vessel, in which hydrochloric acid is passed through by displacing the lime content and is finally formed ethyl chloride.

C. Tetra Ethyl Lead synthesizing

In reaction pot with jacket, the ethyl chloride is poured and heated by hot water. Then to the pot, powdered lead-soda alloy is dropped in from alloy supplying tank. Meantime, the reaction is in process for a reasonable space of time, by being adjusted the reaction heat and pressure. At the completion of the reaction, a surplus part of ethyl chloride is recovered. When the recovery is finished, water is poured in the pot and heated. Then by the steam distillation, the tetra ethyl lead which is being distilled, is collected.

D. Lead Recovering process (Continuous)

After the steam distillation, cold water is placed in the pot and

E. Ethylene di-bromide synthesizing

Alcohol is heated to produce ethylene gas. The gas is passed up from the bottom of S bond tube, while bromine is being dropped from the upper entrance of the tube. Then the reaction is taking place inside and at this moment, the outside cooling is made so as to keep the temperature at 60°C to produce crude ethylene di-bromide.

In the reaction vessel, tetra ethyl lead is placed and in the following proportions of mixing the several materials, ethyl fluid is produced:
