AMERICAN CONSULATE GENERAL.
Batavia - Java.
February 13, 1934.

SUBJECT: 1-1251
CONFIDENTIAL.
Visit to Java of Major General Itagaki, of the Imperial Japanese Army.

THE HONORABLE
THE SECRETARY OF STATE,
WASHINGTON.

SIR:

I have the honor to refer to a confidential report to this Consulate general made by the Consul at Surabaya (copy of which was furnished the Department) dated January 15, 1934, in regard to the visit of Major General Seishiro Itagaki, of the Imperial Japanese Army, to the Surabaya consular district, and to state that I have endeavored discreetly to ascertain the purpose of the visit of Major General Itagaki to Java. For this purpose I approached Admiral Osten, Director of the Department of Marine of Netherland India, since the Department of War of Netherland India is at Bandoeng. Admiral Osten first informed me that he had no information concerning the visit of this Japanese general but that he would be glad to make the necessary inquiries and to inform me later of any significance which might be attached to the visit of this officer.

On February 9, 1934, at his suggestion, I called upon Admiral Osten, who gave me the following information.

Admiral Osten stated that Major General Itagaki is

is still in Java and that he is travelling entirely unofficially and apparently only for pleasure. Nevertheless, his every movement is being followed closely by the secret police. So far, he has delivered several lectures among his compatriots concerning Japanese action in Manchukuo and the Dutch authorities are inclined to believe that the real purpose of his visit is to explain, to the Japanese residents of Java, the foreign policy of Japan in regard to Manchuria. The Admiral further stated that the Netherland Indian military authorities are convinced that the Japanese would not utilize the services of such a well-known military officer for the purpose of military espionage in Netherland India where there is such a large number of inconspicuous Japanese subjects who can be, and probably are, employed for military intelligence work. Notwithstanding this, however, the actions of the General are being closely watched.

Respectfully yours,

K. S. Patton
American Consul General.
