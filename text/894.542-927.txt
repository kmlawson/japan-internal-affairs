MEMORANDUM REGARDING SITUATION IN JAPAN
August 9, 1978.
Singer sewing machines have been sold in Japan for many
years but up to the beginning of this century sales were few. With
a view to developing the business, the Singer system of selling
machines on hire, as well as for cash, through Singer shops and sales-
men working exclusively for the Comoany, was introduced in 1902. At
that time Japan was still a somewhat primitive country, the people
wore kimonos and practically all the sewing of germents, etc., was
done by hand and comparatively few of the Japanese then living had
ever seen a sewing machine.
Originally most of the machines were sold to tailors and
other industrial workers but through establishing sewing schools in
the larger cities and training Japanese young women to act as
instructresses at all Singer shops, we succeeded in gradually intro-
ducing machines into the homes of Japanese families. Subsequently
machine sewing was made a part of the curriculum in the Japanese
schools. This proved to be very popular as evidenced by a census that
was taken by our Japanese organization in 1936, which disclosed the
