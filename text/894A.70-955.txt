SUBJECT: Foreign communications laws, decrees, and regulations.

THE HONORABLE
THE SECRETARY OF STATE,
WASHINGTON.

SIR:

I have the honor to acknowledge the receipt of the Department's instruction to this Consulate of April 19, 1937, File No. 800.70/110, referring to two Imperial ordinances and one ordinance of the Taiwan Government General which promulgate regulations relating to the enforcement of communications laws in Taiwan, and requesting that I verify the correctness of the statement in my despatch No. 8 of February 16, 1937, to the effect that "no special local regulations are in effect in Taiwan but the same such laws, decrees, and regulations obtain as in Japan proper."

In compliance with the Department's instruction I have the honor to report that the information contained in my despatch under reference was obtained in February from the Chief of the Superintendent's Division, Bureau of Communications and Transport of the Taiwan Government General.

the two Imperial ordinances merely provide for the enforcement of the Japanese laws in Taiwan, while the Taiwan Government General Ordinance No. 80 of September 29, 1900, whose title has been amended to read "Regulations relating to the enforcement of the Postal Law, Postal Exchange Law, Railway and Shipping Mail Law, Telegraph Law, Wireless Telegraph Law, and of the Regulations Governing Construction of Telegraph and Telephone Lines," merely provides that these laws and regulations shall apply in Taiwan and that the regulations and notices of the Department of Communications in Tokyo shall govern the enforcement thereof except in cases where special regulations are prescribed. He further said that such minor regulations as do exist locally have the same substance and wording as those applying in Japan except for names of administrative offices, titles of local officials, and a few small points relating to local administrative procedure.

In accordance with the last paragraph of the Department's instruction the Consulate has communicated on the matter with the Consulate General in Tokyo, which has advised this office that it will not be necessary to make translations of local decrees merely providing for the application in Taiwan of the fundamental Japanese laws and regulations which are being translated by the Embassy.

Respectfully yours,

Alvin T. Rowe, Jr.
