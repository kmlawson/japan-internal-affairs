It is believed that the Department of State and the Navy Department might be interested in the following report concerning the activities of the Japanese in connection with their oil concessions on the eastern coast of Dutch Borneo.

It was reported recently from a reliable source
that

that several months ago a Japanese vessel of small draft was discovered by the Dutch authorities several miles up the Sankoelirang river near where the Japanese oil concessions are located. This vessel, manned by a Japanese crew, was said to be taking soundings for the purpose of navigating the river. It is understood that two Dutch cruisers proceeded immediately to Sankoelirang and placed all members of the Japanese vessel in detention. The Japanese claimed that they were sounding simply to ascertain a location for a landing stage in connection with the proposed shipment of oil. The Dutch accepted this explanation as bona fide.

This is concrete evidence of the great distrust of the Japanese which is felt by the Dutch authorities in Netherland India, particularly by the Navy. The fear that Japan may try to establish a naval base in Netherland India, or map some port for future use as a base, is one of the primary considerations of the Dutch navy. In this connection it might be mentioned that a motion in the Volksraad proposing that another cruiser be sent to Netherland India was passed. If this cruiser does come out it will be the total to three cruisers. In general it may be stated that Holland is extremely worried over Japanese penetration into the archipelago, both commercial and naval.

Enclosed is a map showing the approximate positions of the Japanese concessions in Borneo, located according to information furnished through the courtesy of the local representative of the Standard Oil Company of New Jersey (Koloniale), Mr. Lloyd Elliott. The Sankoelirang river is situated approximately Longitude

117° 30'

117° 30', Latitude 1° 5'. It was several miles up this river that the alleged Japanese mapping party was apprehended. It is about due east of Singapore.

Another source of annoyance to the Dutch authorities is the presence in territorial waters of Japanese fishing smacks throughout the archipelago. Crews from these vessels enter Dutch territorial waters to secure bait. When ashore they terrorize the natives and rob them of coconuts and other food stuffs. Since the Dutch cannot maintain a police force throughout the remote parts of the archipelago sufficient to oppose the Japanese fishermen, the so-called Japanese "raids" recur constantly. Every now and then a Dutch cruiser or a sea-plane patrols the islands but since the Japanese vanish at the first appearance of the Dutch patrol no headway is made, and after the Dutch leave, the Japanese come back again. The existence of this situation was confirmed by the Advisor of Japanese Affairs to the Governor General, Mr. P. A. van der Stadt, who expressed considerable annoyance with the Japanese in this respect and deplored the fact that the Dutch cannot maintain a force sufficient to keep them out of their territorial waters.

**Sources of Information.**

Conversations with visitors returning from Borneo and with the Advisor for Japanese Affairs.
