A-673, July 27, 1943.

Interest has been aroused in local shipping circles
by the arrival at China Jan Tow on July 19 of the Japanese
freighter Seibarei Maru. The excitement is the result of
the constant fear among Shanghai commercial and industrial
circles that a Japanese revival in any business direction
will adversely affect their uneconomical and inefficient
undertakings.

A news story in the Ta Fung Pao of July 22, read:

"Various quarters are paying close attention
to the infringement of Chinese navigation rights
by the Japanese freighter Seibarei Maru which
entered China Jan Tow on July 19. A report has
it that the freighter has already left China Jan Tow
for Japan because there was no Tailan coal for it
to carry to Japan.

"Shipping circles here hold a meeting yesterday
to discuss this matter. They were of the opinion
that although the Japanese freighter has left that
port yet it does not mean that the case has been
settled. With a view to preventing the occurrence
of similar incidents they decided to telegraphically
request the government authorities to negotiate immediately
with CAP to put a stop to such acts."

Cabot
