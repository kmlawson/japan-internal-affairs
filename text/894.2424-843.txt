November 24, 1933

SUBJECT: Purchases of Chemicals by Japan.

To: Assistant Chief of Staff, C-2,
War Department, Washington, D. C.

The following data given to a Military Intelligence Reserve Officer who is head of a large Wall Street firm is forwarded for your information:

"The writer has been informed that the Mitsui interests recently placed an order for an unlimited amount of phenol at any price for immediate delivery. The order was placed with a Mid-Western concern engaged in the creosoting of lumber. The firm's name is unknown to the writer, who believes, however, that it may be located in Indianapolis. The writer also has good reason to believe that the Monsanto Chemical Company has enough Japanese orders for phenol to keep them busy through August."

(Signed) KENYON A. JOYCE,
Colonel, G.S.C.,
Ass't Chief of Staff, C-2.

# United States Senate
## COMMITTEE ON THE DISTRICT OF COLUMBIA
August 2, 1935. August 8 1935

Dear Mr. Secretary:

Division of
FAR EASTERN AFFAIRS
AUG 9 - 1935
Department of State

I have an inquiry from one of my constituents relating to the sale of sulphur to Japan. It appears that the Japanese government, or some of the nationals of Japan, desire to purchase sulphur, and some negotiations are in progress with American citizens. There are some
supur deposits in the West and the owners are desirous of finding a market for the same, and, as stated, are negotiating to sell sulphur, the destination of which is Japan.

I have been asked to ascertain whether there are any laws or regulations forbidding the sale of sulphur to Japan. My constituent who wrote me states that he had been advised that sulphur might not be shipped to any country that was engaged in war-like activities.

I shall be glad to be advised whether there are any objections to the sale of the commodity referred to.

Sincerely yours,

The Honorable,
The Secretary of State,
Washington, D. C.

My dear Senator King:

I acknowledge the receipt of your letter of August 2, 1935, inquiring on behalf of one of your constituents in regard to the laws governing the exportation of sulphur to Japan.

In reply, I have to inform you that there are no treaties or statutes restricting the exportation of sulphur from the United States.

Sincerely yours,
Cordeli Huli

The Honorable
William H. King,
United States Senate.
