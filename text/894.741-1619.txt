The Acting Political Adviser has the honor to enclose four copies of a directive to the Japanese Government from this Headquarters (April 1830) dated December 6, 1947, concerning Japanese overseas radiotelephone service. There are set forth therein rates and procedures applicable to such service and authorization is granted for service between Japan and Hawaii. In addition it is stated that limitations on business or personal calls of a transactional nature are the same as those applicable to international telecommunications. In this connection reference is made to this Mission's Despatch No. 1325 of October 10, 1947, with which were transmitted copies of a directive issued by this Headquarters concerning international telecommunications.

a. To the United States as now authorized.

b. To Hawaii via the United States, effective 8 December 1947.

c. To other points beyond the continental limits of the United States, via the United States, effective at a date or dates to be determined and announced later by the Supreme Commander for the Allied Powers.

4. Collect calls to the United States only, from any person or organization in Japan, are authorized effective 5 January 1948.

5. Report Charges:

a. Any person or organization in Japan placing an overseas call, either prepaid or collect, will be required to pay a report charge for each call cancelled after a chargeable report has been obtained. A chargeable report is a report which indicates that:

(1) The called telephone has been reached but the called person is not available or is unwilling to talk.

(2) The called person is available, but the caller is not available, or is unwilling to talk, or the calling telephone does not answer.

(3) On a call filed for later completion, the called person has been notified to expect the call.

b. Payments collected for report charges will be accounted for separately from payments collected for completed telephone calls, in the following manner:

(1) All such payments for report charges, made by accredited persons or organizations in United States Military Payment Certificates or Foreign Trade Payment Certificates, and in yen by non-accredited persons or organizations, will be segregated in an account to be reconciled at the end of each settlement period.

(2) On each settlement date the American carrier will be paid its share of revenue from report charges from the payments made in United States Military Payment Certificates or Foreign Trade Payment Certificates.

(3) The Ministry of Communications' share of revenue from report charges will consist of payments collected in yen plus the balance of United States Military Payment Certificates and/or Foreign Trade Payment Certificates changed to yen at the military conversion rate, after the American carrier's share has been deducted.

MEMORANDUM:

SUBJECT: Information of General Application Pertaining to Directive Number (SCAPIN 1830), file AG 311.3 (6 Dec 47) CCS, this Headquarters, dated 6 Dec 47, subject: Japanese Overseas Radiotelephone Service

1. With reference to the subject memorandum, the following is published for the information of all concerned.

2. Radiotelephone service between Japan and Hawaii is being authorized effective 8 December 1947. Similar service to other points in the world, via the United States, is authorized on a general basis with such points to be specified by General Headquarters, Supreme Commander for the Allied Powers, as necessity arises and arrangements can be effected.

3. Collect calls from Japan to the United States are authorized for any person or organization in Japan effective 5 January 1943. This collect service is intended primarily to provide means whereby persons not authorized to possess and use acceptable currency or currencies may place calls over the radiotelephone circuit. Collect calls from Japan to Hawaii may not be authorized due to lack of collect arrangements between the companies operating the service in the United States and Hawaii.

4. The memorandum restricting calls to personal and non-transactional texts, for accredited persons or organizations only, is being rescinded and service of a limited transactional nature is being made available to anyone in Japan.

5. Reduced rates for Sunday radiotelephone calls between Japan and other points are being authorized. Rates for service between Japan and Hawaii, including reduced Sunday rates, are the same as the rates for service between Japan and the United States. These rates were suggested by the Mutual Telephone Company of Hawaii and agreed to by the American carrier and the Supreme Commander for the Allied Powers and are based on prewar rates already filed by the Mutual Telephone Company with the Federal Communications Commission in Washington.

6. Occupation Forces will extend the necessary surveillance to insure compliance by the Ministry of Communications with the provisions of reference memorandum.

M. F. NOYES
Lt Col AGD
Asst Adj Gen

MEMORANDUM FOR JAPANESE GOVERNMENT

SUBJECT: Japanese Overseas Radiotelephone Service

1. Reference is made to Memoranda for the Japanese Government as follows:

a. File AG 311.3 (24 Sep 46) CCS (SCAPIN 1228), dated 24 September 1946, subject: Revised Plan for Receiving Payments Due American Carriers for Messages and Radiotelephone Calls Filed by United States Military and Accredited Civilian Personnel for Transmission to or Through the United States, Its Territories and Possessions, Prepaid in Japan.

b. File AG 311.3 (6 Dec 47) CCS (SCAPIN 1830), dated 6 December 1947, subject: Japanese Overseas Radiotelephone Service.

2. Radiotelephone calls from Japan to the points listed in inclosure 1 attached hereto, via the United States, are authorized effective 15 February 1948, subject to the following terms and conditions:

a. Radiotelephone calls from Japan to the points listed in inclosure 1 may be placed by any person or organization authorized to possess and use United States Military Payment Certificates or Foreign Trade Payment Certificates.

b. All calls from Japan to the points listed in inclosure 1 must be prepaid in United States Military Payment Certificates or Foreign Trade Payment Certificates.

c. Rates and division of revenue for weekday and Sunday radiotelephone service from Japan to the points listed in inclosure 1 are shown in inclosure 1.

d. Report charges, payable by the calling person in cases of certain uncompleted calls from Japan will be handled in the manner prescribed in paragraph 5 of reference 1b, applicable to calls prepaid in Japan.

e. Settlement of accounts for revenue derived from prepaid-in-Japan service will continue to be accomplished as provided for in reference 1a.

3. Incoming radiotelephone calls to Japan from the United States, Hawaii, and the points listed in inclosure 1 of this memorandum, are authorized in accordance with the following terms and conditions:

a. Incoming calls to any person or organization in Japan, from the United States and Hawaii only, will be accepted to Tokyo only, effective 10 February 1948.

b. Incoming calls to any person or organization in Tokyo only, from the points listed in inclosure 1 (Canada, Cuba, and Mexico) are authorized effective 15 February 1948.

c. Incoming calls to any person or organization in Japan, from the United States, Hawaii, and the points listed in inclosure 1, will be accepted to Yokohama, Osaka, Kyoto, Nagoya, Sendai, Sapporo, Fukuoka, and Kokura, in addition to Tokyo, effective 20 February 1948.

d. Settlement of accounts for revenue derived from incoming calls to Japan will be accomplished as follows:

(1) All incoming calls must be prepaid at the points of origin. Rates and division of revenue therefor will be the same as for calls in the opposite direction.

(2) Revenue for incoming calls to Japan as herein authorized, prepaid at the points of origin, and for collect calls from Japan to the United States as authorized in reference lb, will be collected by the American Telephone and Telegraph Company. The Japanese share thereof will be deposited with the Treasurer of the United States, Washington, D. C., in a special account identified as follows: Trust Fund Receipt Account 218905.1, subject: Deposits, Proceeds of Remittance To and Export From Occupied Territories - Japan.

(3) The American Telephone and Telegraph Company will forward statements to the Ministry of Communications monthly through the Transfer Agent for Telephone and Telegraph Message Funds, showing the Japanese share of revenue derived from the service described in (2) above.

4. Routine operating practices, including rules and procedures governing placing, acceptance, and routing of calls incoming to Japan, will be as directed by the Commanding General, Eighth Army.

5. Direct communication between the Civil Communications Section or the General Accounting Section, General Headquarters, Supreme Commander for the Allied Powers, and the Ministry of Communications, regarding operating and accounting procedures within the scope of this memorandum, is authorized.

MEMORANDUM

SUBJECT: Information of General Application Pertaining to Directive Number SCAPIN 1830/1, file AG 311.3
(6 Feb 48) CCS, this Headquarters, dated 6 February 1948
subject: Japanese Overseas Radiotelephone Service

1. With reference to the subject memorandum the following is published for the information of all concerned.

2. Service from Japan by radiotelephone is being extended to include Canada, Cuba, and Mexico, via the United States, effective 15 February 1948. Incoming calls to Japan, from the United States and Hawaii effective 10 February 1948, and from Canada, Cuba, and Mexico effective 15 February 1948, are authorized to Tokyo only. Incoming service will be authorized to certain other places in Japan effective 20 February 1948.

3. Incoming calls to Japan for Occupation Force personnel will be routed initially to special overseas telephone booths located in the Japanese cities from which service is provided. A study is being made of the possibility of accepting such incoming calls direct to the called person's telephone in the near future.

4. Incoming calls to Japan for other than Occupation Force personnel will be routed to the called person's telephone in the cities to which service is provided, when the called person has a suitable telephone available, otherwise the Japanese overseas operator will summon the called person to a special telephone booth provided in the various cities for that purpose. Such summons will be accomplished through Ministry of Communications channels.

5. Command channel instructions will be issued to units throughout Japan for the purpose of having all Occupation Force personnel in Japan advise their correspondents outside Japan of the telephone number in Japan at which they may be reached.

6. All necessary operational and technical details for handling of incoming calls are being worked out through direct liaison between Occupation Forces and the Ministry of Communications.

7. Occupation Forces will extend the necessary surveillance to insure compliance by the Ministry of Communications with the provisions of reference memorandum.
