Since Taiwan is a colonial possession of Japan and closely affiliated with the mother country politically and commercially, Taiwan newspapers devote most of their space, not taken up by local news, to covering news item from Japan. However, a certain amount of foreign news items do appear in local newspapers.

Next to Chinese, American news items are estimated to be given more space than those of any other single foreign country. Below an attempt will be made to discuss this coverage of American news items by local newspapers during the past five years.

**Administration Policies**

Local newspaper interest in American administrative policies seem to be confined to those affecting or pertaining to the Far East and naval building policies. Naturally, exceptions may be cited as regards pronouncements of America's attitude or position in such cases as the Ethiops and Spanish troubles. But on the whole, little space is devoted to items respecting America's internal policies or America's relations with Latin-American countries.

Coverage of American policies regarding the Far East has decidedly decreased since the excitement over the Manchurean incident. On the other hand, interest in America's naval building program has accounted for increasingly more space being given this

this type of news during the past two or three years. Moreover, during the past three years there has been a certain amount of increase in coverage regarding such internal policies as the NRA and like legislation. However, of late such coverage has been on the decline. In summary, it is estimated that there has been little change in the amount of space devoted to American administration policies during the past five years.

**Commercial and Trade News**

Local newspapers give daily quotations of representative America stocks as indications of price trends in the United States. Cables from the Taiwan Trade Commissioner in New York regarding market conditions and prices for such Taiwan commodities as camphor and tea, especially the latter, are likewise given prominence. But it can not be said that such news items have increased during the past five years.

**Crime**

Little interest is shown by local newspapers in American crime cases. Exceptionally important cases like the kidnapping of the Lindbergh baby, etc., are covered briefly. It is estimated that coverage of crime items have definitely decreased during the past five years.

**General**

Taihoku's No. 12, January 21, 1938, entitled "Proposed Anthropological Studies of Paul King Benedict." This despatch tells of the efforts of one Benedict of Harvard to pursue anthropological research in Taihoku in connection with his Ph.D. thesis.

Benedict went to Shanghai in August, but as the war threatened to interfere with his research he came to Tamsui. At the insistence of a Japanese professor at Taihoku he sought the authorities' permission to do research work, but the official concerned said it would be unlikely that permission would be given. For one thing, investigation of the relationship between governing and governed would not be tolerated. This official pointed out that this question was bound to come up and had even arisen in the research work of the second Stewart Psycho-Ethnographic Expedition in 1937 into dream symbolism, and that the Expedition's purpose aroused no end of trouble to the Taiwan Government. Another thing that irritated the official was Benedict's purpose to make anthropological studies of Chinese, the Chinese of Formosa being regarded as Formosans and Japanese subjects, and not Chinese.

The result of the official's consideration of Benedict's request for permission
