## NOTE

FROM Japan ( ) DATED JUNE 7, 1937
TO NAME 1 1127 6PO

REGARDING: Subsidy calculations: Vice Schuler's March shipping report, April 22, 1937.

Department inquires whether ship prices and specifications are official. Under new law, are ship construction subsidies generally available or uniform for cargo, passenger and combination vessels? Are all mail subsidies included? What are budgetary provisions for various subsidy categories? Embassy is requested to forward translation of new law.

FRG.

156. June 12, 10 a.m.
Department's telegram No. 76/26a, June 7, 4 p.m.
Following from Kobe: "One. Ship prices and specifications given in April 22 shipping report are not (repeat not) official as official figures not available. The figures, however are taken from the Japan Shipping Exchange monthly publication which is the most reliable source available.

Two. Ship construction subsidies are available for cargo and combination vessels only. So-called first class ships are combination vessels while second class ships are cargo ships. Emphasis is laid on combination vessels which were allocated yen 44,693,567 out of a total of yen 50,693,567. Reference May 22 report.

Three. Total mail subsidies now appropriated for 1938 amount to yen 9,550,736 of which only yen 5,135,736 had been allotted on date of April 22 report.

Four. Text of new law and budgetary provisions to be forwarded by mail".

GREW
