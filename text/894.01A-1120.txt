A Sketch of Dr. Thomas Baty
By James Boswell

It is too well known that Dr. Thomas Baty is a savant and an undisputed authority of world-wide fame on jurisprudence, particularly in the sphere of International Law.

Dr. Baty was born at Stanwix, Cumberland, Great Britain, in 1869. He went to Oxford and Cambridge and studied at University and Trinity Colleges, from which he graduated as Stowell Fellow and Whewell Scholar. At a later date, the degrees of Doctor of Civil Law and of Doctor of Laws were conferred upon him by Oxford and Cambridge Universities respectively. He was also called to the bar at the Inner Temple. In 1916, he came to Japan at the invitation of the Japanese Government and was engaged by the Foreign Office as Legal Adviser. He has written numerous published works, amongst which may be mentioned,—Elements of Legal Procedure: International Law in South Africa: International Law: Britain and Sea Law: Private International Law: Vicarious Liability: Majority Rule (with A. M. Baty): War and its Legal Effects (with Prof. Morgan): The Canons of International Law, etc., etc., etc.

He has a legion of friends in Great Britain, Japan and other countries, and anyone who comes into contact with him is charmed by his elevated and refined character. He is loved and respected as if he were an affectionate parent by all, including the very pages in the Foreign Office. He gives us the impressions of a divine personality. Fully and thoroughly conversant with Japanese affairs, Dr. Baty is a conspicuous pro-Japanese. On any and every subject he holds original views. He writes without effort and his style is superb. He is prominent as a vegetarian, and his hobbies are heraldry, music, travelling, etc. He is a theist and well versed in Shintoism. He has been decorated with the Second Class of the Order of the Sacred Treasure in recognition of his many years' services in Japan. He is justly called a "national treasure" of ours.

I should like to express my sincere thanks to you and His Excellency Dr. Joseph Clark Grew for your exceedingly kind reply dated January 19th, 1939, with which you were so good as to send your message of congratulation concerning the Seventieth Birthday of Dr. Thomas Baty, Adviser to the Japanese Foreign Office.

On February 7th, steps were taken to lay your courteous message before Dr. Baty for his information; needless to say, I was deeply impressed by the high honour which you paid him by your message. On February 8th, his Birthday, Dr. Baty was the recipient of many magnificent presents showered on him from various quarters, notwithstanding the Sino-Japanese Incident now in progress. On the evening of February 10th, he was feted somewhat to his reluctance by the Foreign Minister and prominent Japanese jurists at the former's official residence. On the evening of February 17th, the Kohakubai Kwai (Red and White Plum Blossom Society), organised by Dr. Baty for the promotion of informal and friendly intercourse between foreigners and Japanese, made him a
