WE:
Mr. Green.

The attached letter to Messrs. Dayton, Price and Company has been drafted with several factors in mind. In the first place, the Japanese are enthusiastic hunters and it is believed that all of this shipment, or at least the principal part of it, will be used in the Kwantung Leased Territory. In the second place, the shipment consists of shotguns, a category of arms and munitions for which the assent of the Chinese Government would not be required even when the shipment was destined for use in China. In the third place, there is little likelihood, in our opinion, that commodities such as machine guns would be exported to Dairen. Were the Japanese to desire to purchase such equipment for military use, the likelihood is that the order would be placed through a large importing firm in Japan. In the fourth place, it seems advisable, in view of the existence of "Manchukuo", to proceed cautiously in formulating in detail the principles and regulations applicable to shipments of arms and munitions to Manchuria.

MMH/REK

DEPARTMENT OF STATE
DIVISION OF WESTERN EUROPEAN AFFAIRS
MEMORANDUM

July 6, 1935.

RE: JCD:
FE:

I have initialled the attached letter addressed to Messrs. Dayton, Price and Company, Limited, but I suggest that further consideration be given to it before it is forwarded to the addressee. The statements contained therein are technically correct, but if the policy indicated is adhered to, is there not danger that Dairen may become an open door for arms shipments to Manchukuo and to all Northern China? Should not Dairen be placed upon the same footing as Hong Kong and Macau?

J.C.G.

July 3, 1935.

Messrs. Dayton, Price and Company, Limited,
1 Park Avenue,
New York City.

Sirs:

The receipt is acknowledged of your application (your No. 35) of July 1, 1935, for a license to export to Dairen, Manchuria, a quantity of shotguns.

A license is not required for the exportation of shotguns from the United States to Dairen which is in the Kwantung Leased Territory and is administered by the Japanese Government under the terms of Article V of the Treaty of September 5, 1905, between Russia and Japan, and Article I of the Treaty of December 22, 1905, between Japan and China, relating to Manchuria. The texts of these treaties may be found in Volume I of MacMurray's TREATIES AND AGREEMENTS WITH AND CONCERNING CHINA, p. 522 et seq. and p. 549 et seq., respectively.

Very truly yours,

For the Secretary of State:

Maxwell M. Hamilton
Acting Chief,
Division of Far Eastern Affairs.
