(c) The Trials of Civilian, Military and Naval Assassins.

The public trials of the assassins of Finance Minister Inouye and Baron Dan opened in Tokyo on June 28; the court martials of the ten naval officers involved in the affair of May 15, 1932, which resulted in the slaying of Premier Inukai, commenced on July 24; and the public hearing of the eleven army cadets who were involved in the May 15th incident began on July 25. The trials of the civilians involved in the May 15th incident have not yet begun. These trials will be discussed more completely in a report which will be written after their termination. However, it should be stated now that they are attracting a great deal of public attention.

DEPARTMENT OF STATE
DIVISION OF FAR EASTERN AFFAIRS

December 12, 1935.

MHH:

A lawyers' law, to be enforced after April 1936, will debar foreign lawyers from practicing in Japan except on a reciprocal basis. As most States of the Union do not permit aliens to practice law, American lawyers in Japan (there are three) will be debarred from their practice.

The law is so framed as to inflict penalties on those giving legal advice for a monetary fee, regardless of whether or not such persons appear in court. Foreign lawyers will, therefore, presumably be debarred also from giving advice in a consultative capacity (See page 11 of enclosure).

The Embassy has been discussing the matter with the Foreign Office in an informal way, but in the Embassy's opinion there seems to be no ground for taking it up formally.
