Dear Mr. de Wolf:

This is in further reference to your letter addressed to Mr. Norfleet dated February 21, 1947, with which you enclosed a copy of a telegram from SCAP suggesting a change in the rate for telegraphic messages between Japan and the United States.

As you may know, the Commission on March 12, 1947, ordered that an investigation be instituted in the matter of charges for communications service between the United States and overseas and foreign points. Hearings in this proceeding are scheduled to commence on April 14, 1947. Accordingly, it would seem desirable to defer action on SCAP's request for the time being.

Very truly yours,

T. J. Slowie
Secretary

Dear Mr. Radius:

211.7493 YA 804.743

Receipt is acknowledged of your letter of November 17, 1947 transmitting a despatch dated October 17, 1947 from SCAP in Japan relating to the matter of telegraph rates from Japan, Korea and Okinawa. This despatch from SCAP appears to be responsive to discussions initiated by the Commission early in September looking toward the establishment of country-to-country rates based on the Bermuda Agreement pattern between the United States and Japan, Korea and Okinawa.

The Commission is considering the matter and will reply to your letter at an early date.

Very truly yours,

Paul A. Walker
Acting Chairman

Subject: Equalization of Radiotelegraph Rates.

The Acting Political Adviser has the honor to enclose five copies of a directive from this Headquarters to the Japanese Government (SCAPIN 1228/8 of February 6, 1948) giving notification of the revision of rates for radiotelegrams from Japan to the United States and Hawaii. This revision is designed to equalize the increase in rates for radiotelegrams from the United States and Hawaii to Japan. The revised rates for various types of messages are listed in an appended rate schedule.

Enclosure:
Five copies of SCAPIN 1228/8, February 6, 1948, concerning radiotelegraph rates.
