the Premotion of Japan," in whose interest he is making his present

Presidont of the Tohoku Imperial University, doctor of engineering,

The Lesture at the Nuuanu YMCA
1.
Teikichi Sato held a lecture meeting at the Nuuanu YMCA
gymmasium on March 8, 1935, from 7:30 to :30 p.m. About 250 at-
temded, mostly first generation Japanese. His topic was "The Mis-
sien of the Japenese Race." No admission was charged. However,
pemphlets written by himypore sold to those who wanted them at the
deer after the lecture for 10 cents a copy. There were 5 pamphlets,
the titles of which were: "Japan Spirit and Religion," "Glorious
Japen," "The Ray of the East," "Japan Spirit and The Way of The
Cross," "To God's Ray Rather Than Hardships."
Sato started out his speech by saying that the calamities
coming from the earth and heaven are the results of the will of God
and we must not think of them pessimistically, but must consider them
optimistieally. These disasters bring out more clearly some other
qualities, like in the showing of a moving picture, surroundings
are darkemed in order to show clearly the picture portrayed on the
sereen. These natural calamities that have occurred in Japan in
this manner have been beneficial to Japan.
He next talked of "critical Japan." He said that he be-
lieves the true meaning of "critical Japan" lies in the fact that
the peeple of Japan are changing mechanical civilisation, industries,
organizations, and ecenomics, which are guests ef Japan, into host,
and turning the Japan spirit that prevailed in Japen frem leng ago
inte guest. People are working for their own greed and profit while
preslaiming that they are working for the good of the country. For-
merly the people of Japan were glad to give their lives for their
country and never hesitated when ordered to do se, but at present
their attitude has changed and they are asking why they should die
fer the country. Herein, he said, lies the danger of Japan.

He said that well-wishers of Japan have come to realize
that danger and have begun thinking of methods of reestablishing
the real Japan spirit in the Japanese race. As a method for this
develepment, a religious recovery movement was started. He said
that the spirit of the Christ is similar to the spirit of Japen.
Christ died on the cross to save others; this is Christ's spirit.
The Japan spirit is to die for Japan and for the world. Hence, the
similarity between the two spirits. Seeing this similarity, an ef-
fort is being made to revive the Japan spirit through religious re-
covery movements.
Sato also said that, until a few years ago, radical move-
ments were serious in Japan. These Red activities were carried on
mostly by the middle class people. However, he said that the middle
olass and the intelligent class are now awakening to the racial
spirit and it is getting stronger than ever.
Sato pointed out that in the world's history there are
three civilizations, namely: the Mediterranean Era, Atlantic Era,
and the Pacific Era. He said that civilization originated in the
countries surrounding the Mediterranean Sea and went from Europe
over the Atlantic to the United States and it is now passing from
the United States over the Pacific to Japan. He said that many na-
tions have risen and fallen in the past. During the rise and fall
of the other nations, Japan remained obscure for about 2,600 years.
But now Japan is about to spring out from its long rest into the
world. He said that anything that grows fast dies fast and anything
that grows slowly dies slowly; that Japan was being built slowly for
the last 2,600 odd years, hence it will not readily fall. This he
said is the will of God. It is God's providence, he said, that Japan
complete the human civilization. He said that anyone who goes against
the will of God falls and since it is the will of God that Japan lead
the world from now on, Japan must do it and must not oppose His will.
He also said that the United States has seen material,
mechanical and philosophical civiliration, but has not seen a reli-
gious civilization. Japan is a religious country, he said. Buddhism
originated in India, but it remmins only in Japan, and Confucianism
started in China, but its rays are only in Japan, and that Christianity
began in Judea, but it is active now only in Japan. He said that
formerly people used to think that an apple fell, but was forced to
fall by gravity. In like manner a nation does not fall, but is forced
to fall by God's will. He added that similarly, it is the will of
God that Japan keep all the religions and lead others.
He also said that th spirit of Japanese people and Ameri-
cans differ totally. The Americans think of how to enjoy life, but
