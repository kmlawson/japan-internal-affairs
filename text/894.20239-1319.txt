Subject: Purchase by Japan of Dominican Salt.

810.00 Japanese

The Honorable
The Secretary of State,
Washington, D. C.

Sir:

I have the honor to refer to the Department's confidential instruction no. 8 (erroneously numbered) of October 8, 1940 (file no. 839.6371/2) enclosing a copy of a letter addressed to the Secretary of State by Mr. C. R. Thomas reporting that the salt which is being exported to Japan from the Government mines at Barahona is contaminated with gypsum. In reply the Department is informed that from inquiries which I have made here I am convinced that the information given by Mr. Thomas is entirely erroneous. It appears that the rock salt which is mined at Barahona has been analyzed on various occasions and is over 99% pure. I am informed that the Japanese have purchased this salt principally for the manufacture of caustic soda. The manager of the salt monopoly

monopoly here informs me that it is true that gypsum exists at Barahona, but in separate deposits from the salt. Furthermore, he states that a representative of the large Mitsui Company from Japan who is at present in Ciudad Trujillo has stated that his firm expects to make further purchases of this salt unless Japan becomes involved in war.

Although the purchase by Japan of salt in the Dominican Republic appeared at first to be rather unusual, the Legation is now convinced that the transaction referred to is a purely commercial proposition. I am informed that one reason for this transaction is that Japan previously purchased large quantities of salt from Ethiopia through Djibouti and this supply has now been cut off due to the war.

Respectfully yours,
