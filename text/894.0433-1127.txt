Sir:

Section 261 of the USCA, 36 Stat. 1139 provides as follows:

Aliens who are citizens or subjects of any Government which accords to citizens of the United States the right to prosecute claims against such Government in its courts, shall have the privilege of prosecuting claims against the United States in the Court of Claims, whereof such court, by reason of their subject matter and character, might take jurisdiction.

I am representing a citizen of Japan who is a claimant against the United States of America and contemplate filing suit on his behalf in the Court of Claims.

In order for the Court of Claims to have jurisdiction of the above claim it is necessary to allege that a citizen of the United States can sue the Government of Japan in its courts and it is, therefore, respectfully requested that the State Department ascertain if citizens of the United States can sue the Government of Japan in its courts. If the above information is obtained by telegraph, the writer will bear cost of same.

Respectfully,

Charles E. Foster, Jr.

DEPARTMENT OF STATE
THE LEGAL ADVISER
MEMORANDUM

September 10, 1938

Mr. Hackworth:

Mr. Charles W. Foster Jr. telephoned me that he had a client, a Japanese subject, who desires to bring suit against the United States in the Court of Claims. Mr. Foster referred to the provisions of Section 201 of Title 28 of the U. S. Code and asked whether the Department could advise the Court of Claims that American citizens were allowed to bring suit against Japan in the Japanese courts. I took up the matter with Mr. Fenstermacher of DCR and he advised that there was no correspondence on this subject.

This information was transmitted to Mr. Foster and he inquired whether the Department would request such information from the Japanese Government. I told him that such requests had been made in other cases and that I thought a similar inquiry would be made at his request. He stated that he desired the information as soon as possible and I said that if he was willing to pay the cost of telegrams that the request would be forwarded by telegraph with an instruction to the Embassy to transmit the reply received by telegraph. Mr. Foster said he would take up the matter with his client and, if he desired it,

September 29 1938.

With reference to your letter dated September 8, 1938 requesting the Department to ascertain whether citizens of the United States can sue the Government of Japan in its courts you are advised that a telegram dated September 28, 1938 has been received from the American Ambassador at Tokyo stating that in response to a communication addressed by the Embassy to the Japanese Foreign Office a note has now been received containing the following information:

The Japanese Government occupies a position before the courts in civil cases coordinate with Japanese subjects. Criminal proceedings may not be instituted against the Government of Japan. The courts of administrative law have jurisdiction over legal matters involving questions of public law.

EMBASSY OF THE
UNITED STATES OF AMERICA

No. 1046.

The American Embassy presents its compliments to the Japanese Foreign Office and has the honor to request from the Foreign Office an official statement responsive to the following questions:

(1) What classes of suits may be brought against the Japanese Government?

(2) Under what circumstances may such suits against the Japanese Government be brought?

(3) Are American citizens permitted to prosecute such suits against the Japanese Government on the same basis as Japanese subjects?

This information is desired in connection with the filing of a suit against the United States Government on behalf of a Japanese subject.

Inasmuch as the American Government has requested of the Embassy a telegraphic reply, it is hoped that the official statement of the Foreign Office on the above questions can be expedited.

Tokyo, September 16, 1938.

ADDENDUM

1. Although in civil cases the Japanese Government occupies
a coordinate position before legal courts with Japanese subjects,
criminal charges may not be made against the Imperial Government
itself.

However, except in special instances which should belong to
the jurisdiction of the ordinary courts, legal matters concerning
public law become questions for administrative litigation.

2. Regulations concerning the Government office which shall
represent the state in civil cases are set forth in the Civil
Procedure Code, article 4 and in Imperial Ordinance No. 3 of
1891 (Regulations concerning the representation of the state in
accordance with the Civil Procedure Code).

CODE OF CIVIL PROCEDURE

April 21, 1890
Law No. 29

Article 4. Original jurisdiction in civil cases involving a juridical person or any other association or foundation shall be determined by the location of its principal office or principal place of business, or if there is no office or place of business, by the domicile of the principal person in charge of its affairs.

Original jurisdiction in civil cases involving the State shall be determined by the location of the Government Office which represents the State in a particular litigation.

In regard to original jurisdiction in civil cases concerning foreign associations and foundations, the provisions of paragraph 1 shall apply to the offices, places of business or persons in charge of the affairs of such concerns in Japan.
