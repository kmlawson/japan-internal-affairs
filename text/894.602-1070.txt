Tokyo, November 28, 1934.

No. 1067.

Subject: Proposed Control of the Motor-Car Industry and Trade of Japan and "Manchukuo".

The Honorable
The Secretary of State,
Washington.

Sir:

I have the honor to refer to the recent correspondence between this Embassy and the Department on the subject of the Petroleum Industry Law in Japan and the Petroleum Komopoly Law in "Manchukuo" and to report that a somewhat similar system for the control of the automobile industry and trade in Japan and "Manchukuo" appears to be gradually developing.

Several months ago the War Department, the Department of Commerce and Industry and other interested branches of the

=== Page 4 === (confidence 0.98)
- 1 -

the Japanese Government organized a committee to investigate and report on the establishment of a national policy in regard to the motor-car industry. In August last this committee appointed a sub-committee to formulate a draft plan for the encouragement and assistance of the domestic manufacture of automobiles. The draft plan, according to the vernacular newspapers, consists of the licensing of the manufacturing or assembling of motor-cars, the development of a standard car to be called the "New Economic Car" which will be built by the existing Japanese manufacturers, the subsidizing of domestic manufacturers, the possible raising of import duties on automobile parts, and the encouragement of the use by all Government officials and public bodies of the "New Economic Car".

The Department's attention is also invited to Vice Consul Edson's report of July 15, 1904, from Mukden, entitled "Manufacture of Automobile Chassis in Mukden", in which it is stated that a company named the "Towa Jidosha Kogyo Kabushiki Kaisha" (in Chinese the "T'ang Ho" automobile industrial company) had been formed to assemble motor-cars from Japanese parts at the former Trench Mortar Arsenal at Mukden. Some 96 or 97 per cent of the capital stock of the company, which was formed in accordance with the provisions of an Imperial Ordinance of "Manchukue", is owned by Japanese interests, including the South Manchuria Railway and several Japanese automobile manufacturing concerns. In the latter part of the report referred to Mr. Edson states that rumors are current to the effect that

=== Page 5 === (confidence 0.95)
- 3 -

the "Manchukuo" government is considering the possibility of establishing an automobile sales monopoly in Manchuria.

An analysis of these two plans (the licensing system in Japan and the monopoly system in Manchuria) reveals a striking resemblance to the petroleum control systems which have recently been applied to the two countries. The general scheme, as in the case of petroleum, is currently in to develop a strong Japanese industry and to reserve the Manchurian market for this industry. This is to be accomplished, it would appear, by building down the American assembling plants in Japan (Ford and General Motors) by means of a licensing system, at the same time building up a Japanese automobile industry by designing a standard car which can compete with the Ford and Chevrolet, by means of subsidies and higher tariffs, and by controlling the use of the standard Japanese car by Japanese governmental organizations. The manufacture of trucks suited the Japanese conditions will be encouraged in the same way. The cars and trucks, in parts, will be shipped to Hokus, where they will be assembled by the Japanese-controlled Towa Jidosha Kogyo Kabushiki Kaisha. In order to prevent strong competition from American cars and trucks, a sales monopoly will be established, preference being given by the monopoly to the cars assembled in "Manchukuo" by the Japanese-controlled company, and the American cars being put on a quota basis. This is practically the general scheme adopted for the Japanese petroleum industry, except that the Manchuria Oil Company (corresponding to the Towa Jidosha Kogyo Kaisha) does not use Japanese materials but refines imported crude oils.

It cannot be stated positively that the scheme as outlined above is exactly the plan which will be worked out eventually, but there are various indications that the Japanese authorities have some such scheme under consideration.

Various steps, both positive and negative, have already been taken toward the fulfillment of the above or some similar plan. The positive steps consist principally of the organization at Mukden of the Town Jidosha Kogyo Kabushiki Kaisha, as described above, and the organization in Japan of the Nissan Jidosha Kabushiki Kaisha. Some months ago an attempt was made to obligate the six or seven existing Japanese automobile manufacturing companies into one strong company, able to compete with the American companies. The amalgamation did not materialize, but two of the largest of the manufacturers, the Ishikawajima Works and the Dat Automobile Company, amalgamated under the aegis of the Nippon Sangyo Kabushiki Kaisha (Kuhara interests) and formed the Nissan Jidosha Kabushiki Kaisha. This company has erected a large, modern automobile manufacturing plant near Yokohama, completely equipped with American machinery and under the management of American automotive engineers. It is at present manufacturing a small car, somewhat like the "Baby Austin", in fairly large quantities, and is prepared to manufacture parts in very large quantities. It is probable that this factory will be the basis of the forthcoming scheme for Japanese control of the automobile trade of Japan and Manchuria.

105, August 10, noon.

Cabinet approved August 9th for submission to the next session of the Diet a draft bill for the control of the automobile industry with the following pertinent provisions.

One. The imposition of a license system for assembling automobiles and the manufacture of parts.

Two. Licenses will be granted only to companies organized under Japanese law and whose majority of stock is held by Japanese subjects.

Three. Companies already established will not be affected to the extent of present production capacity but any extension or expansion in equipment or output facilities will be subject to control under the new law.

Further details contained in despatch No. 1431 today's pouch.

Please notify Department of Commerce.

The British Embassy to the Ministry of Foreign Affairs.

is Majesty's Embassy present their compliments to the Imperial Japanese Ministry of Foreign Affairs, and have the honour to invite their attention to reports from China that a Sino-Japanese concern has been formed, under the name "Central China Silk Mark Company", with a capital of 8 million yen, to operate silk reeling factories in Central China, principally in husiin, boochow and angchow. The reports state that the Japanese shareholders in the Company include the Hat Kura Silk Reeling Company, the Kanegafuchi Cotton Spinning and Waving Company, the Cauze Silk Reeling Company, and the Mitsui and Mitsubishi Companies. There is reason to believe that this concern is intended to have a monopoly of the establishment of silk filatures in Central China, and that it is proposed that only Japanese exporters shall be able to purchase raw silk in the districts named. It is further reported to be the intention of the new company to monopolise the cocoon crop in the areas in question.

2. The realisation of those plans would seriously interfere with important British interests in the Central China silk trade, in rendering it impossible for them to obtain raw silk, and in cutting off supplies of cocoons from filatures operated by them in Shanghai. The matter has been brought to the attention of the Japanese Acting Consul-General in Shanghai, who denied informally to the Secretary of the Consular body that any monopoly was intended.

NOTE VERBALE.

The Imperial Japanese Ministry of Foreign Affairs
have received the British Embassy's communication of the
3rd October regarding the establishment of the "Central
China Silk Yarn Company". According to reports from China,
the establishment of the "Central China Silk Yarn Company"
was sanctioned during the middle of August last by the
Renovation Government of China, as a Chinese juridical
person having its head office in Shanghai, and is a joint
Sino-Japanese concern capitalized at eight million yen.

The Ministry have the honour to inform the Embassy
in reply that although it appears that the above-mentioned
company, with the object of promoting the welfare of
sericulturalists in Central China, engages in the production
and distribution of silk-worm eggs, the management of
mechanical silk reeling operations, and the processing of
cocoons, et cetera, it does not monopolise these enterprises.
The Ministry are therefore convinced that the Company is not
designed to oppress and obstruct the vested interests of
nationals of third Powers which have carried on these enter-
prises in the past.

21st October, 1938.
