SUBJECT: Formal Designation of Airplane

The Honorable

Sir:

I have the honor to refer to this Consulate's
despatch No. 24 of March 22, 1935, File No. 800/824.8,
Subject: Tsingtao Japanese Community to Present Fund
to Japanese Government for Purchase of Airplane.

This morning, in the presence of 3,000 Japanese
residents of Tsingtao, the airplane which the Japanese
community of this city has sponsored was formally de-
signated the "Patriotic Service Airplane No. 83-TSING-
TAO". This airplane was brought to Tsingtao on the
Japanese cruiser KUMA, which was especially moored at
Pier No. 2, as was the IWATE, the flagship of the Japa-
nese Third Squadron under the command of Vice Admiral
Hyakutake, who in the name of the Minister of the Navy
formally accepted the plane and designated it with the
name given above, which in Chinese character reads:

報國第八十三號青島號飛行機滇華機

FILED SEP 13 1935

A dignified service in keeping with the Japanese spirit was held on the pier, on which the plane had been placed and where a Shinto shrine had been installed. Speeches were made by the Japanese Consul General in Tsingtao and by leading Japanese connected with the campaign to raise the 45,000 Yen. Invitations had been issued to contributors to attend the ceremony; a photograph of the machine, which is a hydroplane used for scouting purposes, together with a pamphlet were handed each person entering the pier. Copies of the photograph and the pamphlet are enclosed.

The hydroplane is described in the pamphlet as being a Type 90 scouting machine, two seat biplane; overall width of wings 12 meters; length of fuselage 9 meters; weight two tons fully loaded and equipped with a 450 H. P. engine.

The donations by the Japanese community totalled Yen 31,592,46. This is the first presentation by a Japanese community outside of the Japanese Empire with the exception of presentations by groups in the Mandated Islands.

Respectfully yours,

Samuel Sokobin,
American Consul.

RANGER ENGINEERING CORPORATION
Aircraft Engines
Farmingdale, L.I., N.Y.

30 Rockefeller Plaza

September 6, 1935

Navy Department
Bureau of Aeronautics
Washington, D.C.

Dear Sirs:

As a result of our advertising and distribution of literature both in the United States and foreign countries, we have today received a formal order from Okura and Company, 30 Church Street, New York, New York, their client being the Imperial Japanese Navy, for one Ranger Engine V-770 SG, supercharged, geared, air cooled, inverted 60° V type, rated at 420 h.p. at 2800 r.p.m. at sea-level.

We have accepted this order on the addition that we be permitted to ship this particular type of engine for export.

On June 8, 1932 this model was rated at this sea-level power for commercial purposes by the Department of Commerce under Approved Type Certificate Number 84. Some ten of these same engines are now on hand in a sub-assembled and semi-finished state, two being practically ready for the test stand.

At the present time we have a contract with the Navy Department, Bureau of Aeronautics, to build one experimental engine to specifications, to develop 450 h.p. at 9,000 feet altitude and 500 h.p. at sea-level for take-off. While some of the major parts of the engine referred to carrying Certificate No. 84 are common to the experimental engine we are building under contract, the new engine will differ widely in many details and has been kept by us on an entirely confidential basis.

The engine we propose to sell both domestically and abroad received its certificate and commercial rating more than two years before the Bureau of Aeronautics awarded to us the present order for an experimental model.

We would like to dispose of the engines which we now have in stock for export, as we have no domestic customers for these particular engines at the present time or in the near future.

For the above mentioned reasons, we are of the opinion that we come under the same status as other American engine manufacturers who have sold samples and manufacturing rights of their models to the Far East, Near East, and several European countries.

RANGER ENGINEERING CORPORATION

October 17, 1935.

To: Bureau of Aeronautics,
Navy Department,
Washington, D.C.

From: Ranger Engineering Corporation,
Farmingdale, New York.

Reference: Mr. S.M. Fairchild's letter of September 6, 1935.

Subject: Release of the Ranger V-770-SG 420 H.P. engine for export.

Gentlemen:

This company has secured orders for four (4) V-770-SG geared and
supercharged engines rated under the Department of Commerce certificate
#84 at 420 H.P. at 2800 R.P.M. sea level. One of these engines is for
delivery to the Japanese Imperial Navy and the other three engines are for
delivery to the Kawasaki Dockyard Company, Inc. The three engines involved
in the sales to the Kawasaki Dockyard Company are for the purpose of this
company securing the manufacturing license for this particular power plant
in Japan. The total amount of the sales for these engines at present on the
above orders is in the neighborhood of $23,000.00 with the possibility of
licensing this company for the manufacture of these engines at a fair value.
It is the desire of this company to secure this license as well as to sell
these engines.

The type of engine under discussion is somewhat on the same principal
but varies considerably in the detailed requirements and parts, from the
engines now being constructed by this company under contract #41730, bureau
#298. The engine which we wish to sell has no altitude rating and is purely
a typical sea level engine whereas the engine which we are building under
contract for the Navy is an altitude engine and is of a different rotation.
The engine being built for the Navy has a different supercharger, a different
arrangement in detail of reduction gear, has the opposite hand rotation
and is equipped with high compression ratio pistons and has a much higher
specific output than the engine which we wish to export.

The differences in the two engines may be seen from a list which is
herewith attached showing the parts used in the Navy contract under the
contract #41730 and parts used in the same group on the standard commercial
V-770-SG engine. It will be known from this list that almost the entire
supercharger is different, the rear crank cases of the engine being different,
crankshafts, oil sludge plugs, supercharger impellers, reduction gear
cages, oil nozzles and various other important features of the engine have
been considerably changed.

It is therefore thought that it would be impossible from the commercial
engine which we have in mind for export to develop an engine similar to the
engine on the Navy contract 41730, and we would be very pleased to have this
commercial engine released for export or any other sales proposition if it
meets with the approval of the Navy Department.

We remain

Yours very respectfully,
RANGER ENGINEERING CORP.
/s/ W.F. Davis,
Chief Engineer.

Tsingtao, China, April 22, 1940

Subject: Contribution by Tsingtao Japanese Resident for Purchase of Japanese Naval Seaplane.

The Honorable
Nelson Trusler Johnson,
American Ambassador,
Peiping, China.

COPIES SENT TO
O.N.I. AND M.I.D.

Sir:

I have the honor to refer to this Consulate's despatches numbers 24 and 40 of March 22, 1935 and July 30, 1935, file no. 800/834.8, on the subject of the presentation of an airplane by the Tsingtao Japanese community to the Japanese Navy.

Yesterday a ceremony was held in Tsingtao commemorating the addition to the Japanese Navy of a scouting seaplane, toward the purchase of which a local Japanese resident had contributed ¥40,000. This resident is a business man who has a dry goods store in the Japanese section of Tsingtao. At the same time a motor boat was also presented to the Japanese Navy by local Japanese residents.

Aside from speeches on the so-called Pagoda Pier in Tsingtao, the event was celebrated by an exhibition flight over Tsingtao and the bay in the course of which

which a short anti-aircraft practice was held, while at least two non-explosive projectiles were dropped near the cruiser Iwate and Japanese destroyer No. 21. No small alarm was caused to numerous Chinese who happened to be on one of Tsingtao's principal streets leading to the public park where the usual spring flower festival was in progress. It is not possible for the Consulate at this time to transmit a description of the hydroplane, but an attempt will be made to obtain a description thereof. A photograph which appeared in the local Japanese newspaper today shows the following characters and numerals on the rudder:

Respectfully yours,

## Hydroplane Presented to Japanese Navy

The Japanese community celebrated the gift of a hydroplane by the local Japanese community to the Japanese Navy.* A very realistic ceremony marked the formal acceptance by the Japanese Navy when the anti-aircraft guns on the cruiser Iwate were fired and planes dropped into Kiaoshow Bay objects which simulated bombs.

Several Japanese destroyers were in port during April.

The absence of any notable bodies of Japanese troops from Tsingtao continued. It is possible that the conspicuous absence of such troops may be connected with the Japanese campaign in western Honan and northern Hupen preparations for which were observed by foreigners.**
