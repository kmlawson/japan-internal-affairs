I have the honor to enclose a confirmation copy of my telegram to the Consulate General in Tokyo reporting the circumstances in connection with the disappearance of the Japan Air Transport Company's passenger plane Envoy (DAOL) and stating that no Americans were aboard the missing aircraft. The plane is said to be a Mitsubishi product, Hinaizuru type. The Consulate General was notified in case inquiries were received inasmuch as Americans occasionally make the East Coast trip by plane, and it might have been reported in the press in Japan Proper that there was a foreigner on board without stating his nationality.

The whereabouts of the plane is reported to be difficult to ascertain in the prevailing bad weather which renders search from the air ineffective. Some 6000 men connected with the police force and Young Men's Societies have therefore been engaged in

According to reports the missing craft took off from Giran Airport at 3:32 PM, on March 7, 1940 passed over Shokei at 3:34 PM at an altitude of 500 meters, headed northwest and the sound of its engines was heard over Koheirin and Koshiryo. At 3:45 PM it was again seen over Shokei, circled and flew off northwest again, appeared at 3:48 PM over Kobo and after circling for about 20 minutes flew northwest again disappearing into the clouds, and was reportedly not seen or heard again. The plane carried fuel sufficient for three hours of flight. It had no radio/equipment. It carried a pilot, mechanic, Police Officer Imai of Taito and his sick daughter, 3 years of age, two Japanese ladies from Karenko and a three year old Japanese girl with them, and a German mechanical engineer by the name of Enno Topp, as nearly as can be ascertained. He arrived in Taiwan on February 22, en route from Shanghai to Canton, but with a delay in shipping connections decided to make a trip around Taiwan. In the absence of German consular representation in Taiwan the Taiwan Government General has reportedly requested the Japanese Consulate General in Shanghai to notify his family. Documents which he was said to be carrying from the German Consulate in Shanghai are reportedly being held by the office of the Taihoku Railway Hotel, where he stopped while in Taihoku.

As a result of the accident the local aviation authorities

authorities are said to be considering the installation of radio sending equipment on planes on the Taiwan circuit, an increase in weather report facilities, exclusive telephone service between airports, and a change in the course of planes from Giran to Taihoku to the circuitous sea route which requires 40 minutes against the more dangerous mountain route which takes but 20 minutes.

As reported by despatch No. 24 of March 19, 1938, the pilot and mechanic of an air mail plane were killed when the plane fell into the sea near the Pescadores on March 17, 1938.

As reported in despatch No. 38 of June 25, 1938, there was a fatal accident at Giran airport on June 24, 1938 in which the pilot and 4 passengers of an Envoy (DOA) plane were killed. The present accident, if fatal, will have been the first fatal aeroplane accident occurring within this district since that time, although there have been several recent accidents in or near the Loochow's on the Taihoku-Fukuoka run, and minor accidents have occurred from time to time on the Taiwan circuit. Flights are generally canceled in case of adverse weather.

Respectfully yours,

Gerald Warner

[stamp: CONSULAR SERVICE] Aeroplane Accident between Giran and Taihoku

THE HONORABLE
THE SECRETARY OF STATE
WASHINGTON

SIR:

I have the honor to refer to my despatch No. 97 894a.7965/2 dated March 9, 1940, and to report that the Japan Air Transport Company's aeroplane ENVOY (D.A.O.L.) which disappeared after leaving Giran on the afternoon of March 7 for the short hop to Taihoku, has been found, completely demolished and with all aboard killed by the impact of the crash on the northeastern side one half mile from the top of Shichiseizan, a 3,660 foot mountain in the Daiton range in north Taiwan, as shown on the accompanying map. The plane, which disappeared into clouds shortly after leaving Giran and apparently at first endeavored to cross the mountains, may have eventually tried to take the sea route to skirt the northern end of Taiwan in its flight to Taihoku rather than to risk crossing the mountains which were weathered in a sea of fog and clouds. The plane was discovered from the air yesterday morning, the first clear day since March 7, and search parties were soon directed to it. While the plane did not burn, pieces of fuselage and wings were found scattered over a considerable area. It was determined that the eight persons in the plane must have been killed instantly by the impact of the crash.

The disposition of the body and effects of the German passenger is being cared for by the Taiwan Government General. The remains of the Japanese passengers and crew are being attended to by their families, the police and the Japan Air Transport Company which will probably pay consolation money to the families for their loss.
