Office Memorandum • UNITED STATES GOVERNMENT

TO : A-H - JK Secretariat
FROM : JK - Mr. Whitman
DATE: November 21, 1946
SUBJECT: Export of explosives from Japan.

SCAP has requested our opinion with regard to export of explosives to adjacent countries. There are two factors involved:

1. The FEC has now under consideration a policy which would limit capacity to that required for Japanese domestic needs. Over the long run this would prevent export of industrial explosives sufficient to meet the needs of adjacent Pacific areas. SCAP should be informed of this possibility.

2. A strict interpretation of the controlling policy referred to by SCAP would also prevent using explosives as a major export to nearby areas in Asia. Relevant policy is as follows:

"Neither the need for imports or exports (including exports that might be made on reparations account) shall be deemed a reason for requiring or permitting any branch of Japanese industry to be restored or developed to an extent that might significantly contribute to Japan's war-making potential, or promote dependence by other countries on Japan for strategic products."

In view of the above it seems proper to authorize SCAP to export quantities available as normal manufacturing surplus but to prevent exportation of substantial amounts which might create dependence on Japan for such supply.

Accordingly it is suggested that the War Department be requested to send the following cable to SCAP:

**DRAFT CABLE**

REURAD 67250, 15 November 1946, proposal (A) to export quantity available as normal manufacturing surplus resulting from program limited to domestic needs approved.

FROM : Mr. Claxton
SUBJECT: Export of explosives from Japan.

The attached draft cable has been prepared by JK and concurred in by the War Department (Lt. Col. Nagle, Ext. 72252). It is submitted for approval.

SCAP stated, in CM IN 2694, 15 Nov. 1946, that requests had been made to SCAP for exports to adjacent countries of industrial explosives and accessories, and that a small surplus now exists available for export. SCAP also clarification of policy as to export of quantities available as normal manufacturing surplus resulting from a program limited to domestic needs and as to export of quantities sufficient to meet the needs of adjacent Pacific areas if not in excess of present annual capacity.

The FEC now has under consideration a policy which would limit capacity to that required for Japanese domestic needs. Over the long run this would prevent export of industrial explosives sufficient to meet the needs of adjacent Pacific areas. The attached draft cable informs SCAP of this.

A strict interpretation of the controlling policy referred to by SCAP would also prevent using explosives as a major export to nearby areas in Asia. This appears from the policy statement that, "Neither the need for imports or exports (including exports that might be made on reparations account) shall be deemed a reason for requiring or permitting any branch of Japanese industry to be restored or developed to an extent that might significantly contribute to Japan's war-making potential or promote dependence by other countries on Japan for strategic products."

In view of the above, the attached draft cable authorizes SCAP to export quantities available as normal manufacturing surplus, but not to export substantial amounts which might create dependence on Japan for such supply.
