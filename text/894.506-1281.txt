The underlying communications represent further substantial steps in the liquidation of Japanese assets in this country. In view of the fact that American assets in Japan are being subjected to increasingly extensive control, I recommend, apart from all other political and economic considerations, that this Government ought to exercise control over capital transfers from the United States to Japan.

I would recommend further that at the same time such a step should be taken, there should be immediate notification to Japan expressing our readiness to negotiate an agreement for the transfer of capital between the two countries.

May 16, 1941

Dear Mr. Feis:

You will remember that several months ago I talked with you about releasing some money to a Japanese and also to an Italian company. At that time you were not interested.

We expect shortly to release another million dollars to the Japanese company in view of the fact that it is not required to protect United States creditors. The Italian company has made a request for the immediate release of $100,000 and is making plans to turn over its business to a new company which would be organized in this country. It is probable that an attempt will be made to sell the stock to American interests which will mean that considerable money will go out of the country into Italian hands.

If you wish me to come to Washington and discuss the matter with you I shall, of course, be glad to do so. If I do not hear from you to the contrary, I will assume that we can permit the withdrawal of such funds as are not required for the protection of policyholders in both instances.

Yours very truly,

[Signature]

Superintendent of Insurance

My dear Mr. Pink:

I have received your letter of May 16, 1941 concerning the proposed withdrawal of $1,000,000 of the funds of a Japanese insurance company and $100,000 of the funds of an Italian insurance company. This Department appreciates your courtesy in requesting an expression of its views and in offering to come to Washington to discuss the matter with the Department.

The Department does not desire to comment concerning the specific transactions under reference. In case, however, similar requests for the release of funds of foreign-owned insurance companies or the sale of their stock to American interests arise in the future the Department would be glad to have an opportunity to express its views.

Sincerely yours,

Herbert Feis

July 3, 1941
June 30, 1941

Dear Mr. Feis:

We have received an application from Appleton &amp; Cox, Inc., attorneys for the Tokio Marine &amp; Fire Insurance Company, Ltd. for remittance of $1,000,000. from the free funds. There seems to be no reason why we should not permit it based on the financial situation of the company and the relation of assets to liabilities.

The Tokio has reinsured most of its business in American companies and continues only in the marine business. The liabilities of the Marine Department are a little over $1,000,000. The trusteed surplus is sufficient to cover this and there are free funds in excess of $3,000,000. We shall in good faith have to permit this withdrawal unless we receive word from you to the contrary.

I shall not give my consent for a few days until you have had a chance to look into this matter.

Yours very truly,

Superintendent of Insurance

Mr. Herbert Feis
Adviser on International Economic Affairs
