March 27, 1940.

Reference a report from Richard F. Boyce, Yokohama, February 23, 1940, entitled "Japanese Automobile Industry 1939" and a despatch from Mr. Boyce of the same date entitled "Temporary Shutdown of Ford Motor Company of Japan, Ltd., and Tentative Plans for Cooperation with Japanese".

The Nissan and the Toyoda automobile companies have, due to vigorous government (army) support, stepped up their production to about 15,000 trucks a year each and even now their total capacity has not been reached. The present total production of trucks in Japan (30,000) is sufficient to take care of all normal domestic needs. Production could be increased to take care of "Manchukuo's" normal needs of about 5,000 trucks a year.

The Toyoda truck is largely an assembly of parts copied from Ford and Chevrolet models while the Nissan truck is a 1934 Graham-Paige model. These trucks are clearly inferior to American trucks but the Japanese Government has apparently been determined to make itself independent of American trucks in case of emergency.

The parts from which Japanese trucks are made are in most cases as good as American-made parts so far as workmanship is concerned but they are made with inferior materials.

The Nissan and Toyoda companies have imported a large amount of equipment in order to

to increase their production. In the event of cessation of hostilities and decreased military needs much of the present equipment will be unnecessary. As the equipment which has been acquired is for the production of an already outdated product, its obsolescence is increasing rapidly in so far as it might be used for producing a modern automobile. Much of the expansion in the Nissan plant has been effected without regard to efficient systematized factory arrangement. Independence of the United States at all costs seems to have been the objective.

Both the Nissan and Toyoda companies are heavily in debt but it is not likely that they will be allowed to go into bankruptcy.

Passenger cars may not be manufactured in Japan at the present time nor may they be imported, on account of scarcity of foreign exchange resources and emphasis upon self sufficiency in trucks.

As a result of the success achieved by the Nissan and Toyoda companies and for the additional reasons that foreign exchange is scarce and hostilities in China have reached the point where further large-scale activities are not planned, the Japanese authorities have progressively reduced the issuance of import permits to the Ford and General Motors companies (which operate assembly plants in Japan) and the Ford company has just been advised by the

the Government that no further import permits will be issued. The Ford company was due to close down in February. According to the despatch it was indicated to the Ford company "that the anti-Japanese feeling in the United States prevented them from giving any special consideration to American organizations".

The Japanese authorities have been trying for several years to obtain a controlling interest in either the Ford or the General Motors companies and have exerted considerable pressure to that end. However, up to the present time the American companies have been in an advantageous position and have not been anxious to sell out. The Japanese authorities now appear to be in a much better position to force the American companies either to sell out on favorable terms or cease operations. Negotiations are now taking place between the Ford company and the Japanese authorities. There has been suggested a plan according to which a new company would be formed by the Ford, Nissan and Toyoda companies according to which the Ford company would own 40 percent of the stock and control the management of the company and the Nissan and Toyoda companies would each own 30 percent of the stock. A new plant would be built capable of producing at least 50,000 units a year. The new company would provide all of Japan's needs and presumably would enter into export operations also.

The foregoing information appears to have been submitted to Consul Boyce by Mr. Benjamin Kopf,

**Comment:**

It has been estimated that 90 percent of the automobiles operated by the Japanese army are of American make. It is also known that the Japanese army distrusts Japanese automobiles and does not use them in the field. If it is true that the Japanese Government really intends to dispense with American trucks it would appear that the Japanese feel that they can embark on a policy of replacing American trucks in the field (i.e., China) by Japanese trucks and this in turn would seem to indicate that the Japanese do not contemplate further important military activity in China. However, it would appear much more likely that important permits are being denied at the present time in order to exert pressure on the Ford company to sell out its interests.
