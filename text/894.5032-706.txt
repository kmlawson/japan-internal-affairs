REGARDING:
Loans to small merchants - Japan

Details of the attention given by the Government to the possibilities of assisting small merchants and industrialists who are hard hit by the bad times. Details of a plan which has been drawn up for this purpose. (report #42 - copy attached).

The Government also turned its attention during the month to the possibilities of assisting small merchants and industrialists who are hard hit by the bad times. The press reported that a plan had been drawn up whereby approximately fifty million yen

yen would be made available for this purpose, the probability being that the amount of loans would range from Yen 1,000 to Yen 3,000 to individuals. The loans would be intended not only for the readjustment of their debts but for productive purposes as well. The intention seemed to be that funds would be obtained from the Savings Deposits Bureau and that the loans would be made through industrial credit associations. At the close of the month it seemed doubtful if these discussions would lead to anything of importance. Not only was it felt that those helped would probably use most of the money obtained for settling debts, leaving little if any for productive purposes, but that the Savings Deposits Bureau would be unlikely to supply funds on what would seem to be very uncertain security.

Leaders of syndicate banks in Tokyo met during the latter part of the month to consider the proposed plan of establishment of an organ for joint investigations of credits so that organized accommodation might be rendered industrial concerns in effecting rationalization. Mr. Ikeda of the Mitsui Bank was reported to have said that the system for credit investigation by individual banks is inadequate and that it is desirable to have a well-organized machinery for cooperative investigation leading to joint assistance. There was discussion whether a joint stock company (with a minimum capitalization of Yen 5,000,000) should be formed or an association. The former plan was apparently abandoned because of difficulties that would attend its immediate materialization. Three bankers (of the Industrial Bank, Mitsui, and Mitsubishi) were
