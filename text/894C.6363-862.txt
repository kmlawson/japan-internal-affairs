Sir:
I have the honor to enclose a copy of a letter
addressed to me by the Export Petroleum Company of
California, together with the original of a letter
addressed by the Company to the Manchuria Oil Company
in care of this Oonsulate.
In view of the unusual situation now pertaining
in Manchuria with respect to the trade in petroleum
products, the developments which threaten Amerioan
business in this area, the part which the Manchuria
Oil Company will have therein, and the attitude of
our Government towards the problem, I am of the
opinion that the Consulate should not be the agency
through which the Export Petroleum Company's letter
should be delivered to the Manchuria Oil Company.
Ambassador Grew, who was visiting in Dairen on the
23rd and 24th of this month, confirmed me in this
opinion and advised that the letter should be sent
to the Embassy for suitable action. It was my in-
tention before talking with Ambassador Grew to &end
the letter directly to the Department with the sugges-
tion that it be returned to the Company accompanied by
an appropriate explanation of the situation now con-
fronting the American oil trade in Manchuria.
While it seems obvious that the Government is not
prepared to attempt to prevent sales of petroleum to
the Manchuria Oil Company for its refinery at Dairen,
I do not believe that an agency of the Government
should under present conditions appear as assisting
such trade.
Respectfully yours,
(s) John Carter Vincent,
American Conmul.
