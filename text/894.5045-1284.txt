THE HONORABLE
THE SECRETARY OF STATE
WASHINGTON

SIR:

I have the honor to report that the Japanese employees of the American President Lines, who had been out on strike since July 3 over the question of retirement allowances, returned to work on the morning of July 10 at the conclusion of negotiations between Acting General Agent S. F. McCaskey and the employees and the police authorities.

During the course of the strike the assistance of this office was offered in effecting a settlement, but Mr. McCaskey said that the situation was not serious and he could handle it himself. During the progress of the strike, business was carried on by Mr. McCaskey and Mr. J. M. Conway, the Passenger Agent.

On July 10 Mr. McCaskey reported that the employees had returned to work and that he had not yielded on one point of their demands.

On July 12 Mr. McCaskey called at this office to inform me about the situation. He related that on the morning of July 3 the Japanese employees had entered his office with a petition which had the following points:

1. Retirement allowances to be paid at the rate of two months' salary per year of service.
2. Two months' previous notice of dismissal, or two months' additional pay at the time of dismissal.
3. One month's pay per year of service for those resigning voluntarily.
4. Annual bonus of at least one month's salary.

The employees requested that the demands be met by noon on July 3 or they would not return to work. Mr. McCaskey said that he had no power to decide on such matters himself and would have to refer the petition to the company's executive department.

Mr. McCaskey pointed out to me that Japanese are employed locally by the American President Lines with the understanding that the only allowance to be granted is one month's salary if the company terminates their services and nothing if they resign voluntarily. One immediate cause of the trouble may have been the dismissal of an employee of several months' standing on June 30 without extra pay, as is the local custom.

On Monday, July 7, Mr. McCaskey stated that police authorities called upon him to ask what settlement he was making of the strike and he said that he could do nothing

nothing about it. They asked that he take the matter up with his head office, and he informed them that he had done so but that the company might close the office as the employees were making demands at this time when there is no business. The police said that they would interfere if the office tried to close without a satisfactory settlement being reached, which Mr. McCaskey understands to be a threat that he will not be permitted to leave Kobe unless demands are met. Mr. McCaskey said he could not say what decision the executive department might reach in the matter. However, he believes personally that it will delay any decision as long as possible.

Mr. McCaskey is of the opinion that the employees were urged to strike by the police, although the employees told him that they had the backing of an "Association" which they could not name. He also said that it appeared that various foreign firms are being taken in turn, similar trouble having recently been had by the American Trading Company, and the British firms of Strong &amp; Company, Pearce &amp; Company, Cooper Findlay &amp; Company; Priest, Marians &amp; Company, Ltd., and Helm Brothers, which is now operating under the name of Toyo Unso K. K.

This office also believes that the employees are nervous over the present uncertain situation and wish to make certain that they will be given some sort of retiring allowance if the firm closes.

Respectfully yours,

Gerald Warner
American Consul
