Subject: Removal of Certain Companies from the Schedule of Restricted Concerns.

The Honorable
The Secretary of State,
Washington.

Sir:

I have the honor to enclose five copies of a memorandum from General Headquarters, SCAP, to the Japanese Government (SCAPIN 1951), "Removal of Certain Companies from the Schedule of Restricted Concerns", dated December 27, 1948.

SCAPIN 1951 removes seventy-three companies from the Schedule of Restricted Concerns compiled under the provisions of Imperial Ordinance No. 657 of 1945. Cancellation of the designations indicates that the companies in question have completed stock disposals under Ordinance No. 567 of 1946, that they are free from restrictions arising under the Enterprise Reconstruction and Reorganization Law, and that there are no outstanding claims against them involving United Nations property.

The present directive leaves a total of 1026 companies still on the Schedule of Restricted Firms. Officials of the Anti-Trust and Cartels Section of General Headquarters report that a SCAPIN releasing an additional seventy firms will be transmitted to the Japanese Government shortly.

SCAPIN 1951 affects a wide variety of firms, including machinery works, iron works, marine products, synthetic chemicals, wool combing, dyeing, harbor transport, shipbuilding, land development, machine tools, foundries, rubber manufactures, rayon and woolen textiles, fuels, mines, industrial medicine, steel wire, and fire and marine insurance.

It is interesting to note that, although the seventy-three companies are being released from designation, opinion in the Anti-Trust and Cartels Section is that the firms are still bound by the

provisions

provisions of Ordinance 567 rather than by those of the Anti-Monopoly Law. This interpretation arises from the wording of Article 2 of the Ordinance, which apparently requires that a company, once designated, may never acquire stocks or debentures in another company. Since the Anti-Monopoly Law allows inter-company stockholdings and inter-locking directorates in certain cases, restricted companies are bound for an indefinite period by more stringent regulations than those which apply to non-restricted companies, even though the former have satisfied the stock-disposal requirements of Ordinance 567 and have been released from designation. In order to remedy this apparent injustice, a new SCAPIN is at present being prepared to provide that, after a certain as yet undetermined "cooling-off period" subsequent to release from designation, the concerns in question shall be freed from the provisions of Ordinance 567 and thereby transferred to the jurisdiction of the Fair Trade Commission for supervision under the Anti-Monopoly Law.

Respectfully yours,

W. J. Sebald

The Department of State has forwarded for my attention and reply your letter of August 10, 1949, relative to transactions and advertisements in securities of Japanese issue.
