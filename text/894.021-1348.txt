I have the honor to refer to a confidential despatch No. 2841 of January 18, 1940, from the American Consul General at Shanghai to the Department, in which there was enclosed a record of statements recently made by a Mr. K. Obata, stated to be a member of the American Bureau of the

Japanese Foreign Office, to Mr. Hallett Abend, representative in Shanghai of the New York Times.

The Mr. K. Obata mentioned in the despatch under reference is undoubtedly Mr. Kunryo Obata, a part-time employee (shokutaku) of the Information Bureau of the Foreign Office. Mr. Obata, formerly a newspaper man, is understood to have been in the service of the Foreign Office for the past ten years. The Embassy has ascertained from the Foreign Office that Mr. Obata's rank, were he a regular government official, would approximate that of an officer of Class VI in the American Foreign Service, and that his position at the present time is that of traveling secretary to Mr. Yakiichiro Suma, Director of the Information Bureau of the Foreign Office, an occupation which consists largely, it appears, in disseminating propaganda on behalf of, and ferreting out foreign reaction with respect to, Japanese policies in China.

From the information available to the Embassy, it would appear unlikely that Mr. Obata occupies the position of authority and influence in the Japanese Foreign Office which his statements to Mr. Abend seem to suggest.

Respectfully yours,

Joseph C. Grew

Secretary of State,

39, April 1, 2 p. m.

Japanese Consul General T. Hori yesterday handed over charge to Consul General Y. Hanawa who has been transferred to Nanking from Hankow. Mr. Hanawa was previously Japanese Consul General at Nanking from February to October 1938. Mr. Hori is proceeding to the Foreign Office where he expects to be assigned to the new bureau.

These personnel changes are connected with the establishment of the Japanese Embassy at Nanking. Mr. S. Matsumoto, a Counselor of Embassy, has been in Nanking for the past two weeks supervising arrangements for the opening of the new Embassy office which is to be located in the building heretofore occupied by the Consulate General; the latter has been forced to move elsewhere. The Consul General's residence is to be occupied by General Abe who is expected to arrive in Nanking on the 22nd or 23rd.

Repeated to Peiping Chungking and Shanghai. By mail to Tokyo.

RR
STANTON

It is further reported that the Foreign Minister will endeavor to make "just and fair appointments" but that he will pick men of talent and ability from all circles including civilians and public servants in other ministries. Young able diplomatic officers will be promoted without regard, it is reported, to seniority and solely on the basis of personal merit. The most able representatives are to be appointed to "countries friendly to Japan" and those holding similar principles as well as to the countries of the "South Seas areas and of South America". It is expected that these reforms will successfully "eliminate transitional diplomats" under the old school of diplomacy of Japan's economic dependence on Great Britain and the United States. Young officials in the foreign service and in the Foreign Office are to be given training in nationalistic principles.

Sent Department via air mail to Shanghai. Shanghai please repeat to Chungking, Peiping.
(END OF MESSAGE)
