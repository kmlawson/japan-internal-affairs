## Railway Training Institutes

The South Manchurian Railway operates a large training Institute at Dairen and smaller institutes have been opened at Mukden, Harbin, Kirin and Tsitsihar.

The subjects taught are in general as follows:

### Regular Course:
I. Traffic - Station duties. Freight and passenger train service.
II. Mechanical Engineering - Locomotives, passenger and freight cars, etc.
III. Civil Engineering - Truck maintenance, etc.

### Short Session:
I. Telegraph operation (1 year)
II. Railway Guards (3 months)

### Apprentice:
Mostly workshop apprentices to train skilled labor (3 years). Outsiders with preliminary middle school education or of equal education and employees already in the service are admitted after an examination. The outsiders will be employed ultimately by the railways after successful completion of the course.

Four pictures of the Mukden Railway Training Institute are attached.

The Russians are reported to have transferred many of the best locomotives and a large number of freight cars from the Chinese Eastern Railway long before they started negotiations for selling this line to Manchoukue. Rolling stock, therefore, was very short when the line was taken over.

Later on, in 1937 and 1938, after the war in North China, the Japanese there were forced to borrow equipment from the South Manchurian Railway, and that caused a shortage throughout Manchuria. Since that time, however, there has been a great increase in production facilities not only at the railway shops but also by semi-official companies established in Mukden and Dairen. The above estimates were made by a contact who has kept well informed on all factors affecting the supply of rolling stock. The same contact has also supplied the following data on types of rolling stock.

## Rolling Stock

The S. M. R. and the Manchoukue State Rays, follow American railway practice in regards to construction, equipment and operation of rolling stock, all of which is carried out on a plane of high standards in workmanship and efficiency.

All rolling stock includes air brakes and automatic couplers.

## Locomotives

The larger units include automatic stokers and feed water heaters; all locomotives constructed in Dairen or in Japan.

Data on tractive effort not available but it is believed to range up to 50,000 lbs. and better. Freight train lengths range up to 60 cars and more, i.e., trailing loads range to 4000 tons under optimum conditions.

## Freight Cars

Freight cars in general conform to American Standards and are built to 30, 40 and 50-ton capacities.

Many of the old freight cars taken over with the Chinese Eastern Railway were of 20-ton capacity, but since 1915 only large cars of 30 or 40-ton capacity were built or purchased.

The rolling stock of the S. W. R. and the Manchoukuo railways has a large proportion of coal cars and open freight cars for hauling beans and produce. Open cars are used extensively because there is comparatively little rain or snow in that territory.

Troop capacity of average 3rd class passenger cars - 80 to 100.

It is estimated that the freight traffic in Chosen has increased to about 16,000,000 tons per year and in all Manchuria to about 60,000,000 tons at the present time.

There has been a steady increase in the volume of traffic, although it is less marked than in Japan. Many of the new lines have been built in undeveloped sections of Manchuria for strategic reasons and it may be some time yet before there will be any worthwhile movement of produce from these districts.

In Spring and Summer the traffic is comparatively light. The busiest time for the railways is from October to March, when frozen ground facilities cart traffic to railway lines throughout the country.

During the season of light traffic, rolling stock is accumulated at the various stations with repair shop facilities for an annual overhauling. In the Winter season rolling stock accumulates at the ports, especially Dairen, and at Mukden and Harbin, the chief commercial centers.

SUBJECT: WORK STARTED ON NEW MAIN TRUNK HIGH SPEED RAILWAY LINE BETWEEN TOKYO AND SHIMONOSEKI

CONFIDENTIAL

The Honorable
The Secretary of State,
Washington

Sir:

I have the honor to report that the local Japanese newspapers of January 5, 1943, carried reports that construction has started on a new main trunk railway line for high speed train traffic between Tokyo and Shimonoseki. The line will reportedly be 989 kilometers in length, of standard width, and the run will be made in nine hours. The new line may have an underground terminal at Tokyo, leaving the city at Tamagawa, stations from that point on being planned for Odawara, Mishima, Shizuoka, Hamamatsu, Toyohashi, Nagoya, Kyoto, Osaka, Kobe, Himeji, Okayama, Onomichi, Hiroshima, Tokuyama, Kagawa and Shimonoseki. The line will reportedly not be completed for ten years, but our air force may desire to take note of construction when it next visits Japan.

Respectfully yours,
For the Ambassador

Richard Fors,
First Secretary of Embassy

WORK HAS COMMENCED ON A NEW 989 KILOMETER TRUNK LINE TO CONNECT TOKYO WITH SHIMONOSEKI IN A NINE-HOUR RUN

(Tokyo, January 4, Domei)

Plans laid in 1942 for the construction of a new high speed express line, a "bullet express", to connect Tokyo and Shimonoseki in less than nine hours on a new standard width trunk line 989 kilometers long, are now coming to realization and work has already commenced on three new tunnels. These tunnels are Shintanna (or Niitanna) (the present Tanna tunnel runs between Atami and Numadzu on the Tokaido Line); Nipponzaka (or Nihonsaka); and Higashiyama (at Kyoto).

Beginning with the coming fiscal year (starting April 1, 1943) main construction work on the line will begin. The Railway Ministry is planning to speed construction to implement the national transportation policy. The new trunk line will expand transportation strength, and will give added protection against wartime and earthquake disasters and other natural calamities. It will not alone concentrate on high speed long distance traffic, but will also shorten the present run of some 1100 km.

In general the layout of the line has been decided upon, but it has not yet been decided whether Nagoya and Kyoto shall be connected by a direct route by tunneling through Tanna Toge (Tanna pass) or whether this pass shall be skirted. Other places in which present main trunk lines will not be followed are between Toyohashi and Nagoya, Himeji and Okayama, etc. The site of stations in Odawara, Shizuoka, Toyohashi, Nagoya, Osaka, Himeji, Okayama and Tokuyama have already been decided upon. But those in Tokyo, Mishima, Hamamatsu, Kyoto, Kobe, Onomichi, Hiroshima, Kagawa, and Shimonoseki have not yet been settled. In connection with the Tokyo station, a well backed plan has been advanced locating the terminal underground in the center of the city with a tunnel leading in from Tamagawa.

Furthermore, it has not yet been decided whether the new trunk line will end at Shimonoseki, or be extended on to Kyushu through the newly opened Shimonoseki-Moji tunnel. Present stations which will be used by the new line include Odawara, Mishima, Shizuoka, Toyohashi, Nagoya, Kyoto and Kagawa. But stations at other stops will be separate from present stations. Also whether or not the "bullet express" will stop at all stations en route or not has not yet been decided. In addition to the three tunnels on which work has started, a tunnel may be made through Rokkosan (at Kobe) and at other places as well to straighten the line out as much as possible. Some of the tunnels may be about five times as long as present tunnels (the longest in Japan is now the Shimizu Tunnel, 9702 meters, on the Joetsu line between Tokyo and Niigata).

Average speed for the new "bullet express" will be about 100 km. per hour at first, but eventually it is planned to raise it to 200 km. per hour. A speed of 150 km. per hour is 42 meters per second, which is faster than a typhoon, and so great is the wind resistance that the whole train must be streamlined. JAPAN

Reports taken from Foreign Broadcast Intelligence Service, November 16, 1942

Kwanmon Undersea Tunnel is Opened. Tokyo (DOMEI) tells the world in English: "Culminating 6 years of labor an expenditure of 50,000,000 yen, and the sacrifice of lives, the first passenger train ran through the Kwanmon undersea tunnel over pointless rails connecting the mainland of Japan with Kyushu Island at 5:00 a.m. (Japan time) today (November 15) as the tunnel was formally opened to public service.

"Through service between Tokyo and Kagoshima, which took 40 hours prior to the construction of the tunnel, now takes only 31 hours 15 minutes by express.

"Another Moji-Shimonoseki undersea tunnel will be opened next year and still another for the projected wide gauge trunkline will be opened in the near future. The undersea highway is to be opened in 1948."

Indochina Told to Obay Petain. The Lyons Radio quotes in a home program an alleged proclamation by Admiral Jean Decoux, French High Commissioner for the Pacific, urging Frenchmen and natives of Indochina to answer with "absolute obedience to the Marshal's orders."

In a later broadcast the Paris radio asserts: "It is reported from Hanoi that Admiral Jean Decoux, French High Commissioner for the Pacific, declared in an interview that the most cordial relations existed between Indochina and Japan, and that the two countries were making the greatest efforts to fulfill their respective needs." The speaker adds: "As far as the political situation is concerned, the Admiral noted: 'All the peoples of Indochina, French and Annamites, are firmly united to defend if necessary the independence of the colony.'"

DeGaullist Views Alarm Indochina. Berlin's TRANSOCEAN says in English to the world: "Tokyo: Authorities in Indochina are on the watch to prevent attempts at deGaullist propaganda, so Japanese correspondents reported from Saigon. The newspaper DEPECHE was suppressed at Saigon for deGaullist tendencies. Some business houses were closed for the same reason."
