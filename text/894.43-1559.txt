His Excellency, the President
of the United States of America
Washington, D. C., U. S. A.

Excellency:

Since Japan's surrender, she has been given tremendous help in materials as well as spiritual from your Government and people. The Japanese people have, as a result of this American generosity, been able to extricate themselves from confusion and hunger that usually go with defeat. Having been inspired deeply by this fact, and knowing that I represent the feelings of the Japanese people, I wish to express my sincere gratitude for your kindness.

The American help extended to the Japanese people has been made possible by General Douglas MacArthur's Occupation Policy which is based on democracy. His enforcement of this policy, together with the rigorous discipline of the Occupation Forces and their fraternal attitude towards the Japanese people have wiped out disorders and confusions caused by defeat. I am very glad, therefore, that no untoward event of any kind had happened since the occupation of Japan by the Allied Powers.

The present condition of Japan is just the same as that of a baby who has lost its parents. The Japanese people are craving for warm love and at the same time they desire nourishment. They are living along the border of starvation and they know that they are not able to walk without your help. It is my firm conviction that if you continue to help us by sending to Japan raw materials and Christian guidance, the devastated Japan will be revived in time and will then be able to contribute to world peace.

I understand that the objective of your Excellency's foreign policy is to build a peaceful and prosperous world. I believe that Japan can contribute, with your help and leadership, to the building up of the foundation of a prosperous and peaceful world. Japan is now in the cradle of your democracy, so that freedom and peace are going to spread all over the country. But the Japanese economy is at present in a chaotic condition and, if this condition continues, Japan will eventually collapse.

I believe that the only way of getting Japan out of this chaotic economic condition is to rehabilitate its peace industries to enable it to support itself and develop its foreign trade as rapidly as possible. It can do this if it will be given raw ma-

=== Page 17 === (confidence 0.99)
-2-

terials so that it can produce sufficient commodities both for home consumption and export purposes. In other words, Japan should be made self-sufficient so that it will not continue to depend upon the Allied Powers for its existence. In this way, it can contribute to the building up of a sound foundation for world peace. Upon this belief, I have made up my mind to unite the able business men in the field of foreign trade and organized the International Club. This organization is housed in the Mitsukoshi Building on Ginza Street at the heart of Tokyo, the Japanese capital city.

The motto of the Club is "Promotion of peaceful foreign trade". This is the first and only club of its kind in the New Japan, organized on the basis of democracy for international business men. The members of the Club have the influence and means to lead the Japanese people to adopt your democratic principles and ideals, which are now being introduced by your Great General MacArthur into all our activities in this country. We want to make this Club, through its members, the faithful agent of American Democracy so that your ideals and ways of life may eventually be made to take root in the hearts of the seventy-eight million Japanese inhabitants.

We wish to assure the American people that we are ready at all times to serve them and welcome them to use the facilities of the International Club freely, especially when they find themselves in Japan either for business purposes or just sight-seeing.

As mentioned above the International Club has a very important mission to perform in the field of international economy. To develop its activities and to enable it to fulfill its mission, the members of the Club desire to study American ways. Therefore, I, as its President, am soliciting your guidance and suggestions. I would be very much obliged if I will be able to have the honor to receive your Excellency's encouraging words.

Yours truly,

SHUJIRO IWASAKI

CONFIDENTIAL

SUBJECT: Proposed Establishment of a Peace Construction Research Institute

The Counselor of Mission in Charge has the honor to inform the Department that Mr. K. Asakai of the Central Liaison Office, called at this Mission for the purpose of discussing a project for the establishment of a Peace Construction Research Institute. Copies of the proposed project are enclosed.

Mr. Asakai was informed that the matter had been given most careful consideration and that there could be little objection to the establishment of such a body, but that it is felt that it is premature to attempt a project of this nature. It was pointed out that it might be unwise to attempt such research until the peace treaty has been promulgated. Furthermore, delay in the formation of such an institute would have no effect upon its future efficiency as the historical factors would need more time before they could be viewed with entire objectivity.

THE PROSPECTUS

of the Proposed Foundational Juridical Person

"PEACE CONSTRUCTION RESEARCH INSTITUTE"

It is, we believe, a pressingly important matter of to-day to inquire into the means of guaranteeing eternal peace and establishing a new healthy State. For this purpose, the unfortunate causes of the various incidents or wars in which the Japanese people have been involved in the past, especially, the Manchuria Incident, the Sino-Japanese Affair, and the Pacific War, among the rest, must be investigated thoroughly, and from all possible angles, political, diplomatic, financial, economic, educational, ideological, cultural, scientific, and technical, and the results of such investigations should be recorded and handed down to posterity, so that no succeeding generation may ever repeat the blunder of attempting such incidents or hostilities again. In order to guarantee permanent peace, it is also indispensable to make future generations understand clearly enough the terrible disasters of war by leaving for them the detail records of what the present nation had to suffer in and after the wars, the tremendous loss of human life and property, severe privations of living, enormous material ravages, etc.

With the object of investigating the means of building up a new healthy State along the lines referred to above, the proposed foundational juridical person is to be established.

The Articles of Incorporation

of

the Peace Construction Research Institute

a Foundational Juridical Person.

Chapter I. Name.

Article 1. The Institute shall be called The Peace Construction Research Institute, a Foundational Juridical Person.

Article 2. The office of the Institute shall be established at 45 Shimo-Takanawa-cho, Shiba-ku, Tokyo Metropolis.

Chapter II. Object and Business

Article 3. The object of the Institute shall be to inquire into the means of guaranteeing eternal peace and establishing a new healthy State.

Article 4. For the attainment of the object as mentioned in the preceding article, the Institute shall undertake the following business:-

(1) Thorough

(1) Thorough research and investigation concerning the unfortunate causes leading to the outbreak of the Manchurian Incident, the Sino-Japanese Affair, and the Pacific War from all possible angles, political, diplomatic, financial, economic, educational, ideological, cultural, scientific, and technical.
(2) Inquiry into full details concerning what the Japanese people suffered in and after the Pacific War, the loss of human life and property, privations of living, material damages, etc.
(3) Recording of the results of the researches and investigations as mentioned in Items 1 and 2 of this article, and the compilation and publication, when necessary, of such records.

Chapter III. Membership and Assets.

Article 5. Persons who support the undertaking of the Institute and have contributed money or material thereto shall be called its members.

Article 6. The assets of the Institute, as of the date of its establishment, shall be fifty thousand yen in cash.

Article 7. The expenditure of the Institute shall be defrayed out of contributions and other revenue.

Article 8. The budget of the Institute and the settlement of its accounts for each fiscal year shall be submitted to the Board of Trustees for approval.

Article 9. The fiscal year of the Institute shall commence on the first of April each year and close on the thirty-first of March of the following year.

Chapter IV. President and Advisors.

Article 10. The Institute shall have its President. The President of the Institute shall be appointed in accordance with the resolution adopted by the Board of Directors. The President of the Institute as of the date of its establishment shall be appointed in accordance with the recommendation of its organizers.

Article 11. The Institute may have its Advisors to reply to questions asked by the President.

Chapter V. Officers.

Article 12. The Institute shall have its Directors not more than ten in number, and its Superintendents, not more than five in number. The Directors and Superintendents of the Institute shall be appointed by the President in accordance with the recommendation of the Board of Trustees. Interim Directorate, up to the assumption of office by the Directors appointed as per provision in the preceding paragraph, shall be held by the Organizers of the Institute.
