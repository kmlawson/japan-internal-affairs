Cultural Relations

(1) Dr. Theodore Dykstra requests following
communication be transmitted Dr. F. Stevenson,
Potato Project, Bureau of Plant Industry Station,
Beltsville, Maryland:

"Make arrangements to obtain 100 pounds of
foundation seed of each of the following varieties:
Red Warba, Sebago, Chippewa and Juma. Also send 6
tubers of rural New Yorker, zero 50, Triumph, Sequoia,
W-T-6, 46422, Russet Burbank, Bartlett, Earlaine,
Pontiac, Warba, President, Mesaba, Spaulding Rose,
95-96, 46923, 47205. For shipment to China. Letter
follows".

of disease control and seed selection potato yield
in China can be doubled or trebled and that potatoes

He states that potatoes must be sent by plane as the long period required for shipment by sea would in the absence of refrigeration cause rot to develop and render potatoes useless. He feels that if potato program is to be effective these American varieties must be made available not later than January 1. He suggests that appropriate Department of Agriculture officials be consulted regarding arrangements for shipping.

(2) The Embassy has informed Dr. Dykstra that while we are aware of the very beneficial implications of the potato program and the desirability of obtaining these seed potatoes, we are not in a position to make a definitive recommendation that such shipments be made by plane in view of the present urgency of shipments of military supplies.
