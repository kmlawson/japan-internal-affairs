Enclosure No. 1 with despatch No. 564 of Jan. 22, 1936.

San Salvador, enero 10 de 1936.

Señor Ministro de los Estados
Unidos de America.

Presente.

Excelentísimo Señor:

A ampliando nuestros últimos informes, nos permitimos poner en su conocimiento ciertos detalles que se están desarrollando a través de la política tanto interior como exterior que inspira al gobierno salvadoreño; manejos que no solo perjudican al país en lo que concierne a sus tendencias netamente democráticas y a la paz interior de los pueblos vecinos, sino que tratan de comprometer la integridad territorial de Central América, poniendo en grave aprieto al pueblo norteamericano, al pretender darle facilidades a otra potencia, para que establezca en las inmediaciones del Canal de Panamá, bases navales y de aprovisionamiento.

El gobierno del general Martínez acaba de mandar al Japón en calidad de Enviado Confidencial, al Coronel Carlos Mejía Osorio, quien lleva entre otras instrucciones, la de comprar numerosos elementos bélicos que habrán de ser transportados para mayor seguridad, en unidades de la armada japonesa hasta costas salvadoreñas.

Acaso el señor Ministro ignore que el gobierno intervino de manera directa, en una de las últimas revoluciones ocurridas en Honduras hace dos años, proporcionándole armas, tropas y dinero, sin consentimiento alguno del pueblo y con grave peligro para la paz interior de estos países.

También hemos sabido por boca de un diplomático centroamericano, de manera confidencial, desde luego, que el gobierno del general Martínez está ejerciendo presión sobre el gobierno hondureño y por medio de su Ministro en Managa, Doctor César Virgilio Miranda, acreditado ante el gobierno del Dr. Sacasa, a efecto que, de común acuerdo, le ceden al gobierno del Japón por unos cuantos millones de yens, los derechos que cada uno de estos países tiene adquiridos en el Golfo de Fonseca y que han de aprovechar para futuras actividades de la escuadra japonesa.

Al señor Ministro no se escapará la trascendencia que entra la actitud de desafío que ha adoptado el gobierno de fuerza que impera en El Salvador, ni las consecuencias que pueden derivarse de una política personal que está creando dificultades internacionales a una Nación amiga, con quien nos ligan lazos de amistad y de buen vecino, que ha puesto de manifiesto su buena voluntad hacia un acercamiento basado no solo en el respeto mutuo sino en consideración hacia pueblos que no han llegado todavía a su completo desarrollo.

Esperamos que al señor Ministro le sean de alguna utilidad estos, datos y tenemos fe que tarde o temprano nos habrá de prestar su valioso apoyo moral y sus buenos oficios, para restablecer en nuestro país la normalidad constitucional y el imperio de la justicia.

Enclosure No. 2 with despatch No. 564 of January 22, 1936.

San Salvador, January 10, 1936.

Minister of the United States of America.

Excellency:

Adding to our last reports, we are permitting ourselves to bring to your knowledge certain details of what is going on with regard to the policy, internal as well as external, which is inspiring the Salvadoran Government; intrigues which not only injure the country as concerns its purely democratic tendencies, and the internal peace of neighboring peoples, but which tend to compromise the territorial integrity of Central America, placing the North American nation in grave danger, by an attempt to give facilities to another power for the establishment of naval bases and provisioning bases in the vicinity of the Panama Canal.

The Government of General Martínez has just sent to Japan as a Confidential Envoy, Colonel Carlos Mejía Osorio, who bears, among other instructions, orders to buy quantities of war materials which will have to be transported, for greater security, in units of the Japanese fleet to the Salvadoran coast.

Possibly the Minister does not know that the Government intervened in a direct manner, in one of last revolutions occurring in Honduras two years ago, furnishing arms, troops and money, without any consent from the people and with grave danger to the internal peace of these countries.

We have also learned from the mouth of a Central American diplomat, confidentially, that the Government of General Martínez is exercising pressure on the Honduran Government, and through its Minister in Managua, Doctor César Virgilio Miranda, accredited to the Government of Doctor Sacasa, to arrange that by common agreement they cede to the Government of Japan for some millions of yen the rights which each of them owns in the Gulf of Fonseca and which are to be taken advantage of for the future activities of the Japanese squadron.

The importance of the challenging attitude adopted by the Government by force ruling in El Salvador will not escape the Minister, nor the consequences which may result from a personal policy which is creating international difficulties with a friendly nation, to which we are bound by ties of friendship and as good neighbors and which has shown its good will for a rapprochement based not only on mutual respect but on consideration for peoples who have not yet reached their complete development.

We hope that this data will be of some use to the Minister and we are confident that sooner or later he will have to lend us his valuable moral support and his good offices, to re-establish in our country constitutional normality and the reign of justice.

TWO STRANGE AND SILENT JAPANESE
SAID TO BE TOURISTS

Impossible for Us to Make Them Issue Statement on Object of Their Trip Through Central America

In room No. 23 of the Hotel Nuevo Mundo are stopping two Japanese gentlemen, whom we went to interview yesterday by mere accident. The fact was that we were informed that the Chargé d'Affaires of Japan was in the hotel, and we communicated with the hotel by telephone in order to find the diplomat. But we were put in communication with the Messrs. Yomada and Bamba, young Japanese recently arrived in the country who have been in the capital eight days.

We proceeded to the room they occupy, and found them busily working at a table full of papers and documents.

After the usual salutations, our reporter tried to obtain an interview with them, all of his efforts meeting with failure. They explained that they are tourists on a pleasure trip through Central America, without any desire whatever to state where they came from or where they are going. In spite of our insistence on a statement regarding their impressions of the country, since they say they are tourists, all was useless.
