December 23, 1938

Reference a report from the American Consul in Taihoku, Taiwan, November 5, 1938, "Activities of Taiwan Development Company and Its Subsidiaries".

The Taiwan Development Company whose paid up capital is at present only 18,750,000 yen has announced that it will shortly call in authorized but unissued shares and it is reported that the company will interest its capital to 150,000,000 yen (the company is controlled by the Taiwan Government General) for the purpose of developing south China and areas in the South Seas. Prior to this year the Taiwan Development Company restricted its activities to developing enterprises in Taiwan and during the year it expanded its interests in Taiwan as follows: (1) South Japan Industrial Salt Company Limited; (2) The Far Eastern Refining Company Limited - whose purpose is to acquire and develop mineral resources and to erect a nickel refining plant at Narihko; (3) Livestock Industrial Company Limited; (4) Kokusan Motor Company Limited - whose object is to handle the sale of Japanese-made trucks; (5) Taiwan Raw Cotton Company Limited - this company has already built two ginning mills and it is reported that it contemplates using the plants to gin cotton grown abroad. Its overseas activities are known to include investigations of cotton cultivation in Siam and the Philippines.

# ACTIVITIES OF TAIWAN DEVELOPMENT COMPANY AND ITS SUBSIDIARIES.

From: American Consul Gerald Warner
Taihoku, Taiwan, Japan.

Date of Completion: November 5, 1938.
Date of Mailing: November 5, 1938.

The Taiwan Development Company is reported to be laying various plans for the economic development of South China and elsewhere in the South Seas after the close of the China Incident. A large increase in its capital is also rumored. However, the plans are not being announced in view of their military importance and delicate international bearings.

If Chiang Kai-shek's influence declines now that Hankow and Canton have fallen, it is ardently hoped by the Japanese that there will be a change from anti-Japanese sentiment in South China and a reliance on Britain, to friendship with Japan. The question of economic operations by Japan will naturally arise, and at present it is said to be the policy of the Tokyo Government to have either the Taiwan Development Company or the Fukudai Koshi see to the economic development of South China without the establishment of special companies as in the case of North and Central China. In this connection the policy of the Taiwan Development Company itself will be to attend to the economic development of South China while it is under military occupation, and to restrict the activities of the Fukudai Koshi to Fukien Province. The Fukudai Koshi was primarily established to foster economic and industrial cooperation between Taiwan and Fukien Province

Taiwan Development Company

The present capital of the Taiwan Development Company is Yen 30,000,000, of which the Taiwan Government General's share of Yen 15,000,000 (represented by lands) has been paid up. Of the public's share of Yen 15,000,000, only Yen 3,750,000 has been paid in. The total paid in capital is thus still Yen 18,750,000 in spite of attempts to raise capital at various times during this year.

The second general meeting of the shareholders of the Taiwan Development Company was held in Taihoku on June 29th, 1938, when accounts for the year ended March 31st, 1938, were approved.

Net profits for the year amounted to ¥463,753; a dividend of 6% on publicly held shares was declared, absorbing ¥225,000, and after sums had been allotted to reserve and bonus funds, ¥149,896 was carried forward. The company now again announces that it will shortly call in a payment of unpaid shares owned publicly and that the capital of the company will be increased. To finance a ten year plan for activities in China the company had been planning to increase its capital to Yen 100,000,000. However, with the change in the situation it is announced that the capitalization will be increased to more than Yen

Yen 150,000,000.

In furtherance of national policy the company has reportedly been carrying on various enterprises in Taiwan, and recent new plans for enterprises are as follows:

1. Establishment of a water power company to supply power to various chemical industries on the East Coast of Taiwan (at Karenko).

2. Establishment of a cement company capitalized at Yen 5,000,000 to produce 100,000 tons of cement annually.

3. Completion of factories at Kagi for the yearly production of 143,000 gallons of butanol (?) and acetone from sweet potatoes, to be used as a solvent for paint.

4. Joint establishment with the Japan Marine Products Company of the Taiwan Chikusan Kaisha with a capital of Yen 5,000,000 (1/4 paid in) for stock-farming and leather manufacturing in Taiwan.

5. Joint establishment with the Japan Marine Company of the East Taiwan Fishing Company with a capital of Yen 1,000,000 (all paid in), for coastal fishing on the East Coast of Taiwan.

6. Establishment before the year end of the Colonial Sea Fishing Company with a capital of Yen 2,000,000, one-half paid in.

7. In addition, work on the general opening up and cultivation of undeveloped parts of Taiwan is reportedly being continued and intensified. The program during this fiscal year includes the reclamation of some 3,700 acres of marsh land near Rokko in Taiwan Province; the opening and development of about 16,600 acres of mountain and forest land; planting of some 430 acres of ramie and 500 acres of castor oil plants; the manufacture of vegetable oils from cotton seed and peanuts, and experiments in the chemical products of sweet potatoes. Researches are also being made concerning the manufacture of banana fibre.

**Subsidiary companies.** Now companies formed during the year in which the Taiwan Development Company has important interests are:-

**Minami Nippon Engyo Kaisha** (South Japan Industrial Salt Co., Ltd.). This company, (formerly tentatively called the Taiwan Industrial Salt Co) which has been organized to control an intensive expansion of salt production, now has a capital of ¥10,000,000 (40% paid up) of which the Development Company has subscribed ¥3,000,000. A "five-year plan" calls for the increase of salt production from about 160,000 metric tons in 1937 to 250,000 metric tons in 1941 and 450,000 tons in 1945. (For further details see voluntary report of March 10, 1938 entitled "Industrial Salt Production to be Increased in Taiwan)

**Toho Kinzoku Seiren Kabushiki Kaisha** (The Far Eastern Refining Co., Ltd.) The purposes of this company are stated to be the acquisition and development of mineral resources and the erection of a nickel refining plant at Karenko. The Development Company is reported to be a large shareholder. (For further details see voluntary nickel reports of May 10, June 7 and August 27, 1938)

**Overseas Activities of Development Company.**

It is thought that the overseas activities of the Taiwan Development Company are at least as important as its activities within the Island; potentially far more so. The economic penetration of the vaguely defined South Seas Area is finding in this company one of its keenest spearheads. Unfortunately, information as to these activities is most difficult to obtain - at least in Taiwan itself - but the following outline will give some idea of their scope.

The Fukudai Koshi (Fukudai Co., Ltd.), which was established in November, 1927 by a joint investment of the Taiwan Development Company, the Kochu Koshi, and several Taiwan sugar companies, has announced that it will shortly call in a payment of unpaid capital,

The Fukudai Koshi is now capitalized at Yen 3,000,000, of which the Taiwan Development Company owns Yen 500,000, and it is expected that the capitalization will be increased five times. Whether the field of activity of this company will be limited to Fukien Province or not will be decided according to future war developments. A quarter of this company's capital is at present paid in. 10,000 shares owned by the Kochu Koshi were taken over by the Taiwan Development Company upon the recent dissolution of the Kochu Koshi, which amalgamated with the North China Development Company. The object of the company is to foster the economic cooperation of Taiwan and principally Fukien Province, and at present only Amoy, on an import-export basis. The consolidation of Japanese control in Fukien province and the restoration of peaceful trading conditions in South China ports would give this company great opportunities for development. The president of the company, Mr. Takato, is managing director of the China and Southern Bank and it is understood that this bank is interested in the possibility of giving financial backing

The French Indo-China Industrial Company has been formed for the purpose of acquiring mineral rights in French Indo-China and export of products to Japan. Some French capital is invested in it. It is understood that some properties of the company are already functioning but the extent of activities is not known.

The Nanko Koshi, Ltd., was organized this summer to handle exports of solely monopoly goods from Taiwan to South China. This firm is now actively engaged in shipping cigarettes and liquors to Amoy. At present it states that freight and other charges plus high import duties place monopoly goods in a disadvantageous position for competition elsewhere with foreign goods. To best meet competition with foreign goods this company plans the erection of tobacco and liquor factories in Amoy, and is now making investigations for this purpose. If it is finally decided to erect factories in Amoy, raw materials for tobacco and liquor manufactures will be obtained from the Monopoly Bureau in Taiwan. In order to carry out the above plans the present capital of Yen 480,000 of which 1230,000 is held by the Taiwan Development Company is regarded as too small. If it is finally decided to push plans then capital will reportedly be increased to Yen 10,000,000, and preparations for the capital increase are reported to be under investigation. With

**Takuyo Suisan Kabushiki Kaisha** (Colonial Sea Fishing Co., Ltd.), is in process of formation and little has so far been published about it, but it is being formed to develop the fishing resources of the South China Seas. **The Iizuka Iron Ore Mining Company** in Johore is, according to the press, now mainly capitalized by the Taiwan Development Company. It is stated that improvements and enlargements are in progress and that imports of ore from the property during 1938 will exceed 200,000 metric tons.

**Trengganu Mines.** The Development Company is reported to have an interest in mining properties of the Nippon Kogyo Company in Trengganu, F.M.S. The extent of these properties and their activities is not known here, but the British authorities have raised no objection to the recent immigration of 60 Formosans and their families to work the mines.

The Taiwan Kaiun Kabushiki Kaisha (Taiwan Marine Transport Co., Ltd.) is a company organized in 1937 to provide shipping services between Takao and Appari, imports Philippine lumber and exports miscellaneous dry goods. The north of Luzon is regarded as a potentially valuable outlet for Japanese goods. In connexion with this company a subsidiary concern, the Nampo Sangyo Kaisha (South Industrial Co.) has been formed to operate mills working Philippine lumber, distribution of products being to South China and the South Seas. The Development Company has some interest in both of these companies.

The Shipping Division of the Taiwan Development Company is located in the company's Tokyo office, and thus far it has handled only charter negotiations necessary for transporting its cargo from foreign countries. **Comments.**

It will be seen from the foregoing that the Taiwan Development Company is still in the initial stage of organization and expansion and that, through the numerous subsidiary companies which it has created or invested in, it proposes to cover a very wide field of commercial activities both at home and abroad. So long as the policy of economic expansion in the "South Seas" continues to prosper, the overseas activities of the company will tend to become more prominent. As a semi-official organization, all of its activities reflect the economic policy of the Government which controls it. And, moreover, it seems clear that it has the support of the military as well as of the civil authorities. With regard to possible overlapping and conflict with the older developers of Taiwan, such as the Firm of
