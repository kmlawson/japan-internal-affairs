Mr. Morishima then said that he wished to discuss the
case informally with me with a view to ascertaining whether
this Government might not reconsider the matter. I told
Mr. Morishima that the attitude and the decision expressed
in the Department's two conmunications to the Japanese
Ambassador were in line with a development of a good many
years in the direction of consolidating our official repre-
sentation abroad and in the direction of curtailing expansion
in this country of foreign governmental offices other than
regular diplomatic and consular establishments; that this
Government's decision had been arrived at after very
careful consideration; and that I did not think that the
decision was subject to reconsideration. Mr. Morishima
said that the reason for assigning personnel to Los Angeles
was to facilitate purchase of oil. I said that we had
taken this factor into consideration; that the Japanese
Government had for a good many years made substantial
purohases of oil on the West Coast without having stationed
there official personnel of the Japanese Naval Purohasing

we would object to officers from the office in New York
making trips to the West Coast for the purpose of facilitating

Japanese Government of oil from the Los Angeles region;
that the amount of oil purchased by Japan from this re-
gion amounted in value to some $10,000,000 per year;
and that during the first three months of this year
some 32 ships had sailed from Los Angeles for Japan
with cargoes of oil. Mr. Morishima continued that the
practice of the Japanese Naval Inspector's office at
New York City had been to send personnel from New York
to Los Angeles to take care of matters incidental to
the purchase of oil; that the personnel sent from New
York City had remained at Los Angeles for periods of a
good many weeks at a time; that the Japanese Navy De-
partment had felt that it would be more practicable to
have personnel regularly assigned to Los Angeles than
to continue the past practice of sending personnel to
Los Angeles from New York for extended periods of time;
and that the Japanese Navy Department had therefore in-
cluded in last year's budget an item to provide for the
definite assignment of personnel to Los Angeles. Mr.
