SUBJECT: NA position on C4-277/5 and /8, "Principles for Japanese
Farmers' Organizations".

After study of the subject papers, and in light of
Mr. Kroll's memorandum of July 1, NA's position on these
papers is as follows:

1. It is believed that the UK's first proposed
amendment in C4-277/8 may be accepted but that the other
two should not.

2. It is our understanding that SCAP never expressed
any views on the SWNCC paper on this subject (SWNCC 356/2),
although it was sent to him for comment. It is also
understood that the Army Department has not been asked to
approve the amendments to this paper made in the FEC.
Although it may perhaps be assumed that silence means
consent in both cases, it is believed that specific Army
approval of C4-277/5 with the first UK amendment should
be obtained without delay, and that the Army's view
should be sought as to whether SCAP's approval of the
paper should not also be obtained before final action on
it by the US Government. The circumstances surrounding
the introduction and progress to this point of the paper
should of course be explained at the time to SCAP.

3. If Army clearance and, should the Army think
necessary, SCAP clearance of the paper is obtained there
would seem to be no need for the U.S. Delegation to the
FEC to seek to delay action on it, and it is believed
that the U.S. may appropriately approve the paper in its
present form with the first UK amendment.

SUBJECT: U.S. Position on Ch-277 Series (Principles for Japanese Farmers' Organizations)

I. In connection with Ch-277/3 ("Principles for Japanese Farmers' Organizations: United Kingdom Amendments"), I saw on June 17th Dr. Ward W. Fetrow, Cooperative Research Division, U.S. Farm Credit Administration Department of Agriculture. Dr. Fetrow was an economic consultant to the Natural Resources Section, Agricultural Division, GHQ, SCAP from April 26 to June 30, 1947, and is familiar at first hand with the cooperative movement in Japan.

Dr. Fetrow was favorably disposed toward the U.K. proposal that the principles contained in Ch-277/5 be put into effect gradually to avoid dislocation in the collection and distribution of food. He thinks that the U.K. amendment #1, suggesting the inclusion of words to that effect in paragraph 3 of the General Principles in Ch-277/5 would enhance the practicability of the paper. His reason is that a sudden freeing of cooperatives from long standing government control would expose the movement to certain dangers of aimlessness and rashness which might hinder its progress.

For the same reason Dr. Fetrow felt that Amendment #2 suggesting revision of paragraph 7 of Ch-277/5 was acceptable only insofar as it called for progressive reduction of the Japanese Government's control over the internal management and operation of farmers' cooperatives. However, Dr. Fetrow did not view favorably that part of U.K. amendment #2 calling for Cooperatives to remain liable to submit reports to the Government and to remain subject to inspection by the Government upon complaint, with the latter retaining power to order cooperatives to rectify illegalities and correct abuses. He felt that the ultimate goal for farmers' cooperatives should be the principles as contained in Ch-277/5 and that to allow the government to retain such powers was exposing Japanese cooperatives to eventual danger of repression as in the past.

a. Reference question 1: Food delivery quotas are assigned by the National Government to the prefectures, and are sub-allocated by prefectures to guns (counties) and subsequently to villages and individual farmers. Assignment of such quotas is the responsibility of the administrative head of the government at each echelon, who is advised by an elected food committee. At the village level the food committee consists of about 15 members elected by and from among the local farmers. Gun committees are elected by representatives from the village committees. Prefectural and national committees are formed in a similar manner. At the village level the mayor, with the advice of the committee, assigns each farmer his portion of the delivery quota, taking into consideration the planted acreage, estimated yield, and retention allowance of the farmer. Agricultural cooperatives play no official role in the allocation of delivery quotas or in food collection administration. Agricultural cooperatives may, and usually do, act as buyers for the Central Government in the food collection program. They have responsibility only for the physical handling and storing of food in the village and are employed by the government on a commission basis.

b. Reference question 2: The Agricultural Cooperative Association Law (Law 132, 1947) imposes no restriction upon the number of agricultural cooperatives or the business areas of such enterprises. Any group of 15 or more farmers may organize an agricultural cooperative association to engage in one or more of the activities specified in Art. 10 of the above Law. Cooperatives have been organized by farmers in the rural communities in accordance with the varying needs of each individual locality involved. This practice is in full accord with GHQ, SCAP, policies which permit farmers to decide within legal limits, matters relating to the form and structure of the cooperative organizations.

c. Reference question 3: The meaning of "enforcement associations" as used in the questionnaire is not clear. They could mean practice associations, associations such as an irrigation association, or organizations commonly called control associations. Although authorized by separate legislation, practice associations actually served as a substructure of the agricultural association system (nogyokai). Remarks made below in relation to nogyokai should therefore be interpreted as applying to the practice associations. Associations concerned with administering the affairs of an irrigation or reclamation district have existed in Japan for many years. In general, these associations are constituted to perform activities similar to those carried on by such associations in the United States. These associations continue to operate, and are not to be confused with the control associations about which the questionnaire is probably concerned. Control associations, including agricultural associations, have either been or are now in process of being dissolved. Here these activities were considered vital to the economy, Indians, (Government Control Agencies) have been organized to perform such functions. The Agricultural Cooperative Association Law, Law 132, 1947, has served as the basis for the organization of approximately 30,000 agricultural and agricultural cooperative associations which now perform necessary economic activities formerly carried on by the dissolved control associations and agricultural associations.

d. Reference question 4: The organization of democratic agricultural cooperative enterprises, under Law 132, 1947, and the liquidation of the agricultural associations and agricultural and agricultural practice associations under Law 133, 1947, constitutes one part of the rural land reform program envisaged by SCAPIN 411, 9, 49. Aside from the fact that each deals with the democratization and economic stabilisation of agriculture, there is no direct connection between the treatment of land tenure problems under land reform and the agricultural cooperative program.

e. Reference question 5: Under the Agricultural Cooperative Association Law only farmers may be voting members. A farmer within the meaning of the law is an individual person who is personally engaged in cultivation, stock production or sericulture. Persons other than farmers who reside within the main areas of a cooperative may be admitted as associate members without voting privileges. The present reported farmer membership of 7,356,095 is accounted for by (1) farmers joining more than one cooperative, and (2) the above definition which permits any qualifying person in the same farm household to become a voting member. Officials of the Ministry of Agriculture and Forestry estimate that less than 1 per cent of Japanese farmers are not members of any agricultural cooperative.
