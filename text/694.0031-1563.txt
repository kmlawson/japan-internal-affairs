Excellency:

I have the honor to refer to your note of December 29, 1945, in which you have presented certain preliminary views concerning the proposals of the United States Government for the disposition of exportable stocks of rubber, tin, and antimony located in Japan.

As you have remarked, the Subcommittee on Trade of the Far Eastern Commission discussed on December 6, 1945, the possibility of exporting 50% of the total quantity of each of the three materials to the United States for sale in this country. This proposal was advanced by the United States Government not only because of the urgent need to make at least some part of these scarce materials quickly available, but also because of the necessity of obtaining funds to pay for essential imports into Japan. The figure of 50% has a further explanation in the fact that the

Supreme Commander has stated that some portion, as yet undetermined, of each material will require to be retained in Japan for essential uses. Therefore, pending the receipt of replies from the several Allied governments to the Department's note of December 5, 1945, and pending a more exact determination of quantities required for retention in Japan, the United States Government proposed that 50% of the aforementioned stocks be immediately shipped to the United States.

I observe that the French Government expresses an interest in ascertaining the legal status of these raw materials inventories. This Government holds the belief that the subject of restitution, particularly as it applies to raw material stocks, calls for the most thorough study, but in the meantime there is no objection to ascertaining whether any part of the stockpiles was looted. The views of the French Government concerning the types of information which it desires to obtain concerning various raw material stocks are invited.

At the next meeting of the Subcommittee on Trade of the Far Eastern

Accept, Excellency, the renewed assurances of my highest consideration.
