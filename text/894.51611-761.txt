My dear Mr. Stimson,

I represent Edward J. Murtaugh, appointed guardian June 22, 1931, by the Court of Common Pleas of Philadelphia, of Samuel Mack, also known as Samuel Mackin and Shoraku Maki, a person of incompetent mind. Maki by reason of his unfortunate affliction is now confined in the Philadelphia Hospital for Mental Diseases, and has been continuously confined there since March 19, 1931. At the time of his commitment to the hospital, Maki, a Japanese subject, had certain certificates of deposit, amounting to ¥4,407.93 (which at the rate of 49.25, amounted to $2,170.83) in The Yokohama Specie Bank, Limited, 120 Broadway, New York.

The guardian went to the bank in August and November, 1931, and presented the certificates of deposit for payment, but payment was refused. After persistent effort I received a letter from The Yokohama Specie Bank, under date of January 20, 1932, advising me as follows:

"We wish to advise you that as we are not permitted by the Banking Law of New York State to accept deposit accounts, therefore the above named person has no account with us and we cannot comply with your request to send you a statement."

After more persistent effort I finally received a letter from the said bank, dated July 26, 1932, as follows:

"Your letter of July 23rd just to hand. The deposit in question will be paid at any time, as stated in our letter, thru the National City Bank of New York, provided the Court evidence is produced.

As to the rate of exchange, we presume you misunderstand the position of this New York office. As we explained in our previous correspondence, these deposits are made in our Head Office in Yokohama payable there in Yen. The New York Office serves as an intermediary between the depositor, his guardian, and our Head Office, therefore, we are not obligated to pay in Dollars, to say nothing of paying at the rate of last August as you are insisting upon.

the position it will pay nothing, and the certificates will have to be presented in Japan.

I shall be glad if you will advise me just what the status of The Yokohama Specie Bank is - offices in the Equitable Building, 120 Broadway, New York City. Is the bank of a foreign government permitted to function, receive money and issue certificates of deposit of the Japanese government, and then when payment is demanded, tells, first, it has no accounts; second, it is prepared to pay (what it wishes to give you) and third, when you again demand the amount due, it declines to pay, stating the certificates must be presented in Japan. If the statement in the bank's letter of January 20, 1932, is true, that it is not permitted by the Banking Law of New York State to accept deposit accounts, then, whose duty is it to prevent the Yokohama Specie Bank from accepting deposit accounts, or accepting money and issuing certificates of deposit? I shall be glad if you will also advise me what Act of Congress, or treaty, if any, permits The Yokohama Specie Bank to function as a bank in the United States, and in the event it refuses to make payment of an amount it is legally bound to make, is it not the duty of the State Department to make inquiry into the bank's activities in America, especially in view of the Japanese government's attitude towards the United States?

I shall appreciate a prompt reply.

Very truly yours,

Daniel R. Rothman

The Department acknowledges the receipt of your letter dated November 21, 1932, in which you state that one Samuel Mack, also known as Samuel Mackin and Shoraku Maki, a Japanese subject, had certificates of deposit in the amount of yen 4,407.93 in the Yokohama Specie Bank, Limited, 120 Broadway, New York; that the certificates of deposit were presented to the Bank for payment in August and November, 1931, and payment was refused; that you received a letter from the Bank dated July 26, 1932, expressing willingness to refund the deposits at the current rate of exchange; and that subsequently the Bank refused to make any payment, holding that the certificates would have to be presented for payment in Japan.

In conclusion you request an expression of the Department's views with regard to the status and certain operations of the Bank and inquire whether it is not the duty of this Department to make inquiries into the bank's activities in the United States.

The Department has no authority over the operation of banking institutions and it is therefore not in a position to express any views respecting the reported actions of the bank to which you refer. It is inferred from the Bank's letter of January 20, 1932, quoted in your letter under acknowledgment, that the Bank was established in New York pursuant to the laws of the State of New York and it is suggested that you may care to communicate with the banking authorities of that State.

The only provision of any treaty between the United States and Japan which appears to have any application to the operation of branch Japanese banks in the United States is Article VII of the Treaty of Commerce and Navigation concluded between the two countries on February 21, 1911. The Article reads as follows:

"Limited-liability and other companies and associations, commercial, industrial, and financial, already or hereafter to be organized in accordance with the laws of either High Contracting Party and domiciled in the territories of such Party, are authorized, in the
