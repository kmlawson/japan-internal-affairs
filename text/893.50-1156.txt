ECONOMIC CONDITIONS IN SOUTH MANCHURIA
NOVEMBER 1939.

TRADE

Detailed figures of the trade of Manchuria in November have not yet been published. Figures for the period January 1 to November 30 of this year, however, put the gross trade at Yen 727,761,000 for exports and Yen 1,345,096,000 for imports, as compared with Yen 671,591,000 for exports and Yen 1,170,373,000 for imports in the first eleven months of 1938.

Exports to Europe were at a virtual standstill in the month under review. To America, Japan and China they were also reported to have declined. An indication of the great drop in exports to third countries, viz., countries excluding Japan and China, is given in their figure for the second ten days of November, Yen 944,000 as compared with Yen 1,513,000 in the corresponding period of November 1938. Despite stopping of imports of gunny ones from India and of new cotton from China, total imports in November rose over those of the preceding month and of November 1938, the rise being accounted for largely by heavy imports of consumers goods from Japan and construction equipment from the United States.

Export monopolies - The law for the control of Staple Produce, requiring all beans shipped by rail or stored in railway warehouses or shipped by means other than rail to be sold to the Manchuria Staple Produce Control Company (see October 1939 report) went into effect on November 1. As farmers can no longer sell directly to any third parties and must cash in on their crops, and as exporters cannot obtain stock except from the monopoly, it is expected that the inevitable cooperation under the circumstances will allow the monopoly to function effectively.

On November 2 the law for the control of important cereals was passed. Like the triple produce control law mentioned above and the Rice Control law of 1937 this law requires all designated cereals to be sold by producers to a state company, in this case the Moncurie Cereals Company, which alone may export or resell to wholesalers and retailers for domestic consumption. This law goes further than the other two in empowering the government to stop speculative buying, to force sale of speculatively held stocks at fixed prices, and even to fix retail prices if necessary. The purpose of the law is to prevent a rise in prices of staple cereals, both to aid exports and protect consumers. As the grains coming under the scope of the law are haoliang, corn and millet, which are only exported to Japan (their exports to China and other countries are restricted

**Control of Daily Necessities** - Since the occupation of
Manchuria, Japan has come to supply about 60% of the consumption
goods imported into the country. With shortages of raw
materials developing in Japan, corresponding yen bloc export restrictions
necessary to protect home needs and exports to gold bloc
countries of finished products have been put into effect in that
country. These restrictions culminated in the Japanese Law
of September 29, 1939, to adjust exports to Kwantung Leased
Territory, China and Manchukuo, limiting exports of certain items
to these countries. In order to cooperate with Japan in enforcing
the measure, "Manchukuo" at the close of November was
revising its Trade Control Law to apply the import license
method to the items controlled by the Japanese law. At the
same time the official news agency reported that the Daily
Necessities Company, a state company, would be vested with the
sole right to import consumer goods into Manchuria, giving
rise to much alarm in the host of small Japanese traders in
the country.

**Control of Wheat Flour and Wheat Milling Industries** -
The drafting of laws to control wheat flour and wheat milling
industries was completed during the month and the drafts were
approved at the meeting of the State Council on November 25.
The official press reported that the laws would be promulgated
early in December, and that under the first law wheat flour
would be a direct government monopoly.

### Control of Livestock

On November 16 the Law for the Adjustment of Livestock was promulgated. This law has as its purposes the "conservation of livestock resources and the adjustment of livestock supplies", and empowers the competent minister or his deputies to live orders and make dispositions in connection with the removal, distribution, export, import and slaughter of livestock. Livestock under the law refers to horses, cattle, mules, donkeys, camels and sheep.

**FINANCE**

In order to interest the people of Manchuria and Japan in investment in Government securities, the Government took steps to dispose of Ten 25,000,000 (750,000 shares) of Manchuria Telegraph and Telephone Company stock to the public. This stock, owned by the Governments of Japan and "Manchukue", is among the gilt-edged securities of Manchuria.

In order to obtain revenue and reduce lavish living, the Government enacted a law putting taxes on geisha and sing-song girl charges and on drinking and dance hall bills ranging from 8% to 60%, and authorized local Governments to collect sur-tance up to 25% on these taxes.

**INDUSTRY**

**Patents** - The Patent Bureau announced that 22,059 trade-marks, 11,928 patents and 1,669 designs were registered by it in the first 10 months of 1939. But one of the inventions originated with a native, dealing with a method of house con-struction. One invention by a native fortune-teller, prescribing a diagram telling the future of every human being, is said to have been rejected by the Bureau.

**Bean Stalk Pulp** - The Ten 10,000,000 bean stalk rayon pulp mill at Kaiyuan, begun in 1937, was completed during the month under review and will begin operating as soon as its power plant is finished.

**Mining** - The Mitsui Company acquired mining rights to 40 mines in Eastern Manchuria and formed a 9500,000 subsidiary company (Mitsuo Mining Company) to work them.

**Bailrands** - The Government on November 29 announced that it had completed and turned over to the South Manchuria Railway Company for regular traffic the following lines as of December 1:

**Panhsihu** - Tianshihfu - 85 kilometers - June from Panhsihu on the Yutung - Yukon Railway east through mountainous country supposed to be rich in mineral and timber resources. Is intended to carry iron ores and coal to the Panhsihu smelters.

**Haisui** - Yungning - 91.1 kilometers. June from Haisui, a new station between Peilinho and Suiyang on the eastern section of the former Chinese Eastern Railway, and Yungning on the Soviet border. (See May 1939 Report.)

GOVERNMENT LIFE INSURANCE

The Manchuria Life Insurance Company, the State small policy life insurance monopoly established in 1937, reported that it had 47,113 active policies, valued at ¥61,348,604, on its books at the end of October.
