TELEGRAM RECEIVED

JR
This telegram must be closely paraphrased before being communicated to anyone. (br)

FROM Tsingtao via N. R.
Dated March 16, 1939
Rec'd 7:50 a.m.

Secretary of State,
Washington.

55, March 16, 9 a.m.

One. Referring to building of Japanese military establishment, reportedly hospital, in Tsingtao, reported in my telegram of January 23, 3 p.m., number 17, and pages eight and nine of political reports for January and February respectively, I was amazed on a visit yesterday to the vicinity of site by the enormous extent of the construction now in progress. It can hardly be doubted that more than a hospital is being constructed; and in view of the Japanese War Minister's statement of January 30 in the Diet on the subject of stationing of troops in China "for a considerably long time" and that "construction of the necessary barracks is under way" it would appear that the Tsingtao establishment will be a complete and regular army post where thousands of men if not more than a division will be quartered.

Two. Usual telegraph and mail distribution.

SOKOBIN

WWC

# PARAPHRASE

A telegram (no. 55) of March 16, 1939, from the American Consul at Tsingtao reads substantially as follows:

On visiting, on March 15, the vicinity of the site where the Japanese are building a military establishment said to be a hospital, the Consul was very much surprised to note how extensive was the construction work now going on there. There can be little doubt that more than a hospital is being built. In the light of the statement made in the Diet on January 30 by the Japanese Minister of War in regard to stationing troops for a considerably long time in China and to the effect that the necessary barracks are now being constructed, it seems that the project at Tsingtao will be a regular and complete army post where it will be possible to lodge thousands of soldiers, if not more than one division. (In connection with the above see telegram no. 17 of January 23 from Tsingtao.)

E.J.C.
