Dear Sir:

A Colorado constituent desires
authoritative answers to the following
questions:

1. Did the Japanese permit
land ownership by private
American citizens prior to
Pearl Harbor?

2. Do the Japanese permit land
ownership by private American
citizens today?

3. Were religious institutions
permitted to own property in
Japan prior to Pearl Harbor?

I shall appreciate it very much if
I may have the views of your Department on
these questions at your early convenience.

March 30, 1944
In reply refer to
JA 894.5211/24

My dear Senator Millikin:

I have received your letter dated March 25, 1944 in which you make some inquiries concerning Japanese land ownership laws.

Ever since 1925 alien individuals and corporations, including religious institutions, have been permitted to own land in Japan, with certain minor exceptions. In this connection it should be noted that as many American missionary societies were not incorporated their properties in Japan were held either in the names of individual missionaries or by juridical persons created under Japanese law.

Although we have no definite information on the subject, it is believed that the Japanese Government has taken no step to invalidate the titles on property held by American citizens in Japan prior to the war. Of course, such property is under the control of an official similar to our Alien Property Custodian and presumably no enemy alien would be permitted to purchase land.

Sincerely yours,

Cordell Hull
