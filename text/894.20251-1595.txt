STRATEGIC SERVICES UNIT, WAR DEPARTMENT
WASHINGTON, D.C.

COUNTRY: France
SUBJECT: Kikue KATO
DATE OF INFORMATION: 20 August 1946
EVALUATION: See below
REFERENCE: SSU DB-1083 of 17 September 1946

The following information on Subject has been received from a source who has wide and usually reliable contacts with French officials. It is supplementary to that contained in Reference report, though conflicting in some instances, with certain statements included therein.

1. Miss Kikue KATO came to France approximately ten years ago to study the French language and French culture. While engaged in her studies Miss KATO was connected with the office of the South Manchurian Railway, which engaged extensively in propaganda activities, and at least to some extent in political and economic espionage. Miss KATO was later employed at the office of the Japanese Military attache, a position she continued to hold until the evacuation of Paris. During the occupation of France, Subject acted as liaison between the Japanese colonies in Paris and Switzerland - presumably in connection with intelligence matters.

2. Subject at present resides in Paris at 114 Rue de Courcelles, 17 Arrondissement. She recently applied for, and was granted, a French exit-visa, issued at the Prefecture of Police. She has since applied to the Chinese Embassy for a transit visa. Inasmuch as this is the first time since the outbreak of the Sino-Japanese War, that a Japanese national has requested a transit visa, the Chinese officials are not certain of the prevailing regulations. However, they may take the position that there are no grounds for refusing the request in view of the favorable action already taken by the French. It is not known, however, whether the Chinese have actually granted Miss KATO's request.

3. According to an informant whose reliability we are unable to assess, Miss KATO is married to a Russian known only as "Monsieur PIERRE." It is believed more likely that Miss KATO is his mistress.

4. The files of this office contain reference to a "Monsieur Pierre" who is described as a Russian emigre. He is reported to concern himself with "political negotiation" in connection with the Union des Patriotes Sovietiques, although he holds no official position in that organization. He maintains close contact with members of the Communist Ligne Interieure and it is believed likely that he works closely with the

CENTRAL INTELLIGENCE GROUP
NEW WAR DEPARTMENT BUILDING
WASHINGTON 25, D. C.

COUNTRY: France/Japan
SUBJECT: Kikue KATO
DATE OF INFORMATION: 15 January 1947; 22 January 1947 (Paragraph 3)
EVALUATION: Documentary
REFERENCES: a) SSU DB-1083 of 17 September 1946
b) SSU DB-1982 of 14 November 1946

The following information supplements the two previous reference reports in the case of Subject, a suspect Japanese national residing in Paris, France, now seeking repatriation.

1. An official source has informed our Paris station that Miss KATO through the efforts of the French Foreign Office has been able to overcome certain preliminary difficulties connected with her trip to Japan. The Allied Control authorities in the Pacific have no objection to her going home provided she can manage transportation. To this end, she intends to apply shortly for a U.S. transit visa.

2. It is understood that Miss KATO will be employed by the French Embassy, Tokio, if and when she arrives there.

3. An investigation conducted in Paris has shown that the "Monsieur PIERRE" referred to in paragraph 3 and 4 of reference b) as either the husband or lover of Miss KATO is one Pierre TCHELEBI. It is believed extremely unlikely that he is identical with the "Monsieur PIERRE" described in paragraph 4 of the same memorandum as concerning himself with "political negotiations" in connection with the Union des Patriotes Sovietiques and as maintaining close contact with members of the Communist Ligne Interieure.

SUBJECT: Repatriation of Miss Kikuo KATO. 20251/12 1346

FC CA SA WE EUR/x

The United States Political Adviser has the honor to refer to the Department's secret airgram A-173, December 13, 1946, and to this Mission's secret airgram A-15, January 24, 1947, regarding the repatriation from France of Kikuo KATO.

Under date April 1, 1947, this Mission received a communication from the Japanese Government in which it was stated that Kikuo KATO was expected to embark at Marseilles for Saigon, French Indochina, on or about March 26, 1947 and requesting on behalf of her father, her early repatriation from Saigon. This Headquarters was requested to make arrangements with the French authorities for this purpose.

As the subject individual is a Japanese national, no objection was perceived in advising the local French Mission of the request of the Japanese Government, and a copy of the latter's communication was transmitted to the French Mission without comment or recommendation.

There is enclosed copy of the reply of the French Mission, June 21, 1947, from which it appears that the subject individual has arrived in Saigon and that she will board the first available ship bound for Japan.
