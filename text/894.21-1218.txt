# MILITARY INTELLIGENCE DIVISION
## WAR DEPARTMENT GENERAL STAFF
### MILITARY ATTACHE REPORT

JAPAN
Country Reported On

Subject Major General Raishiro Sumita
I.G. No. 5990

Source and Degree of Reliability:
Source: Press. Official Publications.
Reliability: Good.

Summarization of Report
A short outline of the career of Major General Raishiro Sumita.

1. 1890 Born in Guma Prefecture.
1912 Graduated from the Military Academy with highest honor (24th graduating class)
1912 2nd Lieut., Artillery.
1912 Attached to the Army Heavy Artillery School.
1914 Graduated from the Army Artillery and Engineers School with highest honor.
1915 1st Lieut., Artillery.
1920 Captain, Artillery.
1921 Graduated from the Army Staff College with highest honor.
1923 Staff member of the Military Affairs Bureau, War Department.
1927 Major, Military.
1927 Military instructor at the Army Staff College.
1928 Military instructor at the Army Staff College and at the same time staff member of the General Staff Headquarters.
1929 In addition to his two duties above mentioned, appointed a staff officer of the Naval General Staff Headquarters.
1931 Lieut. Colonel, Artillery.
1932 Was appointed a staff officer of the Tokyo Garrison Headquarters in addition to duties above mentioned.
1933 Military Attache to the Japanese Embassy in France, concurrently Military Attache to the Japanese Embassy in Belgium, and at the same time acted as a residence officer of the Army Air Corps Headquarters in France as well as a residence officer of the Army Technical Headquarters in France.

### MILITARY ATTACHE REPORT
#### JAPAN
Country Reported On

Subject: Lieut. General Kenji Doihara
I.G. No. 5990

Source and Degree of Reliability:
Source: Press and Official Publications.
Reliability: Good.

Summarization of Report
When Replied
A short outline of the career of Lieut. General Kenji Doihara.

DECLASSIFIED
E.O. 11652, Sec. 3(E) and 5(D) or (E)
OSD letter, May 3, 1972
By _______________ NARS Date 5/12/73

1. 1883 Born in Okayama.
1904 Graduated from Military Academy (16th graduating class)
1904 2nd Lieut., Infantry.
1907 1st Lieut., Infantry.
1912 Graduated from Army Staff College.
1912 Attached to General Staff Headquarters.
1913 Captain, Infantry.
1919 Major, Infantry.
1922-23 In Manchuria as "special agent" under General Shigeru Honjo.
1923 Lieut. Colonel, Infantry.
1924 Adviser to Chinese Government.
1927 Attached to Headquarters of the 1st Division.
1928 Adviser to Marshal Chang Tso-lin and later to his son, Marshal Chang Hsuch-liang.
1929 Commander of 30th Infantry Regiment.
1931 Attached to the Kwangtung Army Headquarters and acted as the head of the Kwantung Army's Special Service Office at Mukden. Is credited with having played a leading role in the situation following the Mukden outbreak on September 18, 1931, and acted as Mayor of Mukden for one month soon after the incident.
1932 Head of Special Service Office in Harbin.
1932 Major-General.
1932 Commander 9th Infantry Brigade.
1933 Attached to the Kwantung Army Headquarters as the head of special organ stationed in Mukden.
1936 Highest adviser to the North China Autonomous Government.
1936 Lieut. General.

took part in engagements at Chochow, Paoting,

2. Lieut. General Boihara is one of the better or more
widely known officers of his grade both for his reputation
as a soldier as well as through his long association with
the Special Service organization in various places. He is
regarded as one of the best informed officers in the Japanese
Army on the subject of China and its problems, and his appointment
to the Supreme Military Council is popularly believed to
have taken place in order that that body may have this specialized
knowledge at its disposal.

Source and Degree of Reliability:

Summarization of Report
When Received
An outline of the career of General Gen
Sugiyama, recently appointed Chief of General Staff.

DEC 11 1932
E.O. 11652, Sec. 2(C) and 5(D) or (E)
In Comments on Current Events Enter Date Deedings Date
OSD letter, May 3, 1932
Miljaythmian, MAR 3, 1940

1. 1880 Born in Kokura City.
1901 Graduated from the Military Academy (12th
graduating class).
1901 2nd Lieut., Infantry.
1903 1st Lieut., Infantry.
1904 Participated in the Russo-Japanese War.
1905 Captain, Infantry.
1910 Graduated from the Army Staff College with
high honors.
1913 Major, Infantry.
1917 Lieut. Colonel, Infantry.
1918 Residence officer in India.
1921 Colonel, Infantry.
1922 Chief, the Aviation Section, War Department.
1923 Chief, the Military Affairs Section, War Dept.
1925 Major-General.
1926 Director, the Supplies Department of the Army
Air Corps.
1927 Military Attaché to the Japanese Embassy in
Paris, and member of the Japanese delegation
to the League of Nations.
1928 Director, the Military Affairs Bureau, War
Department, and concurrently chief secretary
of the Supreme Military Council.
1930 Lieut. General.
1930 Vice-Minister of War and concurrently Chairman
of the Army Technical Commission and Chair-
man of the Aeronautical Research Council.
1932 Commander 12th Division.
1933 Chief, the Army Air Corps.
1934 Vice Chief of the Army General Staff and con-
currently Commandant of Army Staff College.

1936 As a gesture of responsibility for February 26, 1936 Incident, resigned position of Vice Chief of Staff, and continued on General Staff in attached status.

1936 Director General of Military Training and concurrently a member of the Supreme Military Council.

1936 General.

1937 Minister of War in the first Konoe Cabinet which was formed just before the outbreak of the China incident and retained the post until the Cabinet changed at the end of June, 1938. Present Minister of War, Lieut. General Hideki Tojo was then Vice-Minister of War.

1938 Supreme Military Councilor.

1938 On December 9 made the Commander-in-Chief of Japanese Expeditionary Forces in North China succeeding General Count Hisaichi Terauchi.

1939 Member of Supreme Military Council.

1940 Chief of General Staff.

Court rank: Senior 3rd.
1st Order of Merit.

Highest Japanese decorations:
1st Order of Rising Sun.
5th Class Order of Golden Kite.

Foreign decorations: One each of British, French, Chinese and Polish.

1. a. General Sugiyama has had a long and distinguished career in the Army. He has held a wide variety of important positions, and enjoyed a breadth of experience greater than that which comes to most officers.

b. General Sugiyama is liked and respected in the Japanese Army. He has a strong character, is regarded as a good disciplinarian, and has an easy and pleasant manner in dealing both with his own people and with foreigners.

c. His selection for the position of Chief of General Staff at this important period in the history of Japan is universally approved of and regarded as a highly logical and satisfactory one.

Harry I. T. Creswell,
Lieut. Colonel, Acting General Staff,
Military Attache.
