Clyde, Ohio, 1 - 9 - 33.

Hon. Simeon D. Fess,
Washington, D. C.

Dear Sir:

I have written a little on the unemployment &amp; am in somewhat of a dispute on machinery and have heard several times that Japan has laws prohibiting the introduction of machinery that will throw so many out of work. I am trying to find out what the truth is about that &amp; so wrote, about 3 wks. ago, to the Japanese Ambassador here asking him about the facts. I thought I wrote in such manner that I would get a reply, but I suppose he is such a busy man that he could not bother with answering such a letter.

I am determined to find out &amp; am coming to you as being in a position to get this information from him, or direct me to some other source. Thanking you in advance for a little help I am,

Respt.,
H. Burkholder.

FE - Mr. Salisbury:

On reconsideration of the attached letter, I would be inclined to suggest an answer somewhat as follows:

The Department does not maintain complete collections of the laws of foreign countries and it regrets that it is not in a position to make an authoritative reply to your inquiry. It is possible, however, that you might obtain the desired information by communicating with the International Labor Office, 20 Jackson Place, N. W., Washington, D. C.

I would also be inclined to omit the last paragraph of the draft letter to Senator Fess.

Mr. H. Burkholder,

Sir:

Your letter of January 9, 1933, to Senator Simeon D. Fess, inquiring whether there are laws in Japan prohibiting the introduction of machinery which would create unemployment, has been forwarded to this Department for reply.

As the Department does not maintain complete collections of the laws of foreign countries, it is regretted that it is not in a position to make an authoritative reply to your inquiry. It is possible, however, that you might obtain the desired information by communicating with the International Labor Office, 20 Jackson Place, N. W., Washington, D. C.

Very truly yours,

For the Secretary of State:

Stanley K. Hornbeck,
Chief,
Division of Far Eastern Affairs.

January 20, 1933.

My dear Senator Fess:

I acknowledge the receipt of your letter of January 11, 1933, enclosing a letter from Mr. H. Burkholder of Clyde, Ohio, with regard to the existence in Japan of laws prohibiting the introduction of machinery which might create unemployment.

I have consulted with Japanese experts of the Department in respect to this inquiry and have been informed that they doubt the existence of any such laws in Japan. In view of their experience, which has been wide, it seems probable that, were there any such laws, they would have knowledge of them. Without, however, a thorough search of the laws of Japan a definitive answer could not be given, and I find, upon investigation, that the Department unfortunately does not maintain complete collections of the laws of foreign countries. It is believed, however, that Mr. Burkholder might obtain the information which he desires

The Honorable
Simeon D. Fess,
United States Senate.
