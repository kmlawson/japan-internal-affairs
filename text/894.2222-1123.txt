Alarm Caused by Rumors of Japanese Conscription

Much alarm was aroused among the Chinese population by rumors that the Japanese would conscript young men aged 16 to 25 years. A large exodus of Chinese took place. The rumor that conscription would take place was denied by the Japanese adviser to the Police Bureau who stated that the activities of the police in visiting business establishments and households was connected with educational courses which would explain to the Chinese younger generation the significance of the reconstruction of a new China. Small establishments such as tailoring shops employing Shanghai and Ningpo men were particularly affected by the sudden departure of their help, as were some larger industrial establishments.

While the possibility of Japanese exploitation of China's great man-power for military purposes has been a favorite subject for speculation among foreigners and returned students, that possibility, even with the degree of pacification achieved in Tsingtao, still appears remote.
