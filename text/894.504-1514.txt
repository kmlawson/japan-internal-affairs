# DEPARTMENT OF STATE

## Memorandum of Conversation

DATE: 3/28/46

SUBJECT: Repatriation of Japanese industrial labor from China and Manchuria

PARTICIPANTS: JK - Mr. MacDonald
ORI - Lt. Trezise
ED - Mr. Magill

COPIES TO: Mr. Lockhart and Mr. Sumner

Mr. MacDonald pointed out that apparently the Chinese Government is moving very slowly, if at all, to arrange for the repatriation of Japanese industrially skilled labor, engineering and management personnel. Assuming that it is U.S. policy to encourage such repatriation, he raised the question as to whether there is any positive action which this Government might take to elicit active steps in this direction by the Chinese Government. I suggested that a possible move might be to indicate our willingness to finance the importation of American technical personnel to replace repatriated Japanese and to assist China in labor training programs. (Since the Eximbank is considering the possible allocation of part of a major credit for this purpose, this possibility might be appropriately mentioned in the course of negotiations for this credit, or by General Marshall on his return to Chungking.) Mr. MacDonald reports that Mr. Ringwalt is actively concerned with this question.

Lt. Trezise subsequently told me that he has just received a request from Mr. Penfield in FE to do a rush study of immediate Chinese requirements for skilled labor, engineering and management personnel. Presumably, this reflects an interest in the same problem with which Mr. Ringwalt is concerned. While the elimination of Japanese influences from China is appropriately of concern to the political offices, it seems to me that initiative and responsibility for planning economic implementation of this policy should lie with ED.
