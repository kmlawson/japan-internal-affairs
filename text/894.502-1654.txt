My dear Mrs. Harloe:

The receipt is acknowledged of your letter of October 5, 1946 concerning the possibility of your receiving a monthly rental for your home in Japan and the transfer to this country of the funds realized.

It is suggested that you furnish the Department with a sworn statement in quadruplicate drawn along the lines indicated in the enclosed memorandum. You should also furnish evidence of your American citizenship. If you are a native born citizen of this country, a certified copy of your birth or baptismal certificate will be sufficient. If you are a naturalized citizen, you should request the Immigration and Naturalization Service of the Department of Justice, Franklin Trust Building, Philadelphia, Pennsylvania, to forward to this Department confirmation of your naturalization.

Upon receipt of the above requested data, the Department will be in a position to give further consideration to this matter with a view toward requesting the United States Political Adviser at Tokyo to make an investigation to determine the present status of the property and to take any other action which might be appropriate under the circumstances.

Dear Sirs:-

About one month ago I wrote you a letter requesting certain information regarding the possibility of my obtaining rental on my home in Japan. To date, I have received no reply.

As stated in my previous letter, I own a home located near Kobe, in a suburb called Shioya. The home is registered in the name of my late husband Mr. Robert Young, former owner of the Japan Chronicle. I understand that my home is at present being occupied by the American Forces.

All of my funds are tied up in Japan. I have already submitted a statement of my property in Japan to you. This was done about three years ago on the form prescribed at that time. So far, I have received no information as to what has been done regarding same.

I am seventy four years old. My funds on hand are getting low and for this reason am most anxious to obtain a monthly rental for my Shioya's home to meet my current living expenses. Please reply.

Yours very truly,
Annie Harllee

My dear Mrs. Harloe:

The receipt is acknowledged of your letter dated November 6, 1946 concerning the possibility of obtaining rental on your home near Kobe, which you understand to be occupied by American forces in Japan.

Rental payments in the case of property requisitioned for the use of United States forces is a responsibility resting primarily with the War Department. Even in the event that the property is being so used, and even were there funds appropriated for payment to the owners, there is at the present time no provision for the transfer of funds from Japan for any purpose. Until existing regulations change, therefore, it will not be possible to have such rentals as might be due from the property sent to you.

Sincerely yours,

For the Secretary of State:

Benedict M. English
Assistant to the

SUBJECT: Accommodations in Japan.

Public interest in reopening Japan to limited private trade requires full information as to details be available here. SCAP made available to the Trade Mission his tentative plans for housing, accommodations, transportation, etc., as outlined in SCAP's memorandum to the Trade Mission 10 May 1947, entitled "Plan for Opening Controlled International Commerce with Japan by Private Interests." Part III of that memorandum contains a series of plans in process. The following cable is designed to ascertain whether SCAP has finalized such plans. The pertinent part of the reference memorandum is as follows:

III. Logistical Support.

5. Quarters and Meals.

a. Hotel accommodations to be provided in Tokyo-Yokohama, Kyoto, Osaka and Nagoya.

b. The number of sales representatives that can be admitted will be determined by the availability of facilities in these various areas. The time that the first increment can be permitted entrance into Japan will be determined by the date on which the available facilities can be made ready. Plans are being developed by G-4.

c. It is planned to make the accommodations as impressive and attractive as possible.

d. A standard "American Plan" tourist rate will be charged for the basic services rendered. In brief, these services will provide for transportation, housing and meals at the four control points indicated in "a" above.

e. A special payment plan for business trips from the control points is being prepared. This includes payments for taxicab service, individual billeting where necessary and other requirements related to a specific business negotiation at other than the control points.

6. Transportation.

a. Rail, On "Occupation coaches" of Japanese railways under procedures developed by the SCAP Transportation Section.

b. Taxicabs, Special taxi transportation under Japanese operation will be provided. These cabs will be reserved for trade representative travel.

c. Steamship. Private company agents will be available to book passage as required.

d. Airplane. Same as in "c."

7. Payment arrangements.

a. A basic charge per day will be set for lodging and meals payable in dollars upon clearance for departure.

b. For business expenses other than those provided at the main control points such as special trips mentioned in "III 5 e" credit will be extended by the business office at the control point subject to settlement prior to departure from Japan.

c. For special accommodations or services that may be required on a nonpredictable basis such as taxicab service, American Club privileges, U.S. Occupation motion picture attendance, etc., special or regular coupon books will be made available at the control center business office.

d. Indigenous Japanese currency for use in purchasing non-restricted commodities such as souvenirs will be made available at the military exchange rate either at the authorized banking agencies or at the control centers.

e. Representatives will be requested to deposit against receipt their U.S. currency notes, Traveler's Checks or other recognized free currency in a bank upon arrival and advised that the possession or use of such currency or checks or of Military Payment Certificates is not authorized. Technically Military Payment Certificates may be made available if developments indicate this to be a desirable expedient. FEC Circular #3, 10 January 1947, provides that such certificates may be used "by personnel, who, in the opinion of the theater commander can best perform their missions by having access to U.S. Army facilities, when specifically authorized by the theater commander." Subject to restrictions issued by SCAP.

f. Prior to departure from his own country each representative will be advised to provide himself with a Traveler's Letter of Credit available for negotiation at the Tokyo branches of such banking firms as may be authorized to do business. These letters of credit must be expressed in terms of United States dollars and Sterling. All currency requirements of the representatives while in Japan will be provided by withdrawals from the Traveler's Credits.

8. Provision of Facilities, Services and Supplies.

a. Housing facilities will be provided by the Japanese. G-4 is charged with the responsibility for the procurement to assure noninterference with Occupation requirements.

b. A properly organized Japanese agency will handle the arrangements in a manner similar to a standard tourist agency.

c. Procurement of Occidental foods, gasoline, equipment, etc., will be programmed on an import basis and paid for from the SCAP account.

d. It is contemplated that minimum demands will be made upon the Occupation military forces for logistical support. Any such temporary support provided in the interest of expediting the entry of private trade representatives will be replaced in kind from imports or paid for at rates established by SCAP.

9. Amusements and Entertainment.

a. If practicable the visiting trade representatives should have access to U.S. motion pictures under restrictions to be announced by Special Services.

b. The same general policy to be inaugurated with respect to recreational facilities.

c. With approval of the Board of Directors, the American Club of Tokyo to be made available for food, liquor and social entertainment.

d. No restrictions will be imposed upon personal entertainment of trade representatives by either Occupation or Japanese civilian personnel.

Subject: Directive Instructing the Japanese Government to Arrange for Offices and Housing for Commercial Representatives and Licensees.

The Acting Political Adviser has the honor to enclose five copies of a directive issued by this Headquarters (SCAPIN 1839) dated December 31, 1947, instructing the Japanese Government to arrange for offices and housing for commercial representatives and licensees authorized to remain in Japan on a semi-permanent basis. Payment for such accommodations may be made in acceptable foreign exchange, subject to approval by the Foreign Trade Division of the Economic and Scientific Section of this Headquarters. Facilities returned to the Japanese Government when no longer needed by the Occupation Forces may be rented to commercial representatives and licensees; payment for such facilities is to be made in acceptable foreign exchange.
