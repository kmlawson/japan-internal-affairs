# P A R A P H R A S E

A confidential telegram of April 20, 1939, from the American Consul General at Singapore reads substantially as follows:

There are at the present time in Singapore Japanese agents buying any large capacity excavating and road building machinery which is available--either used or new. However, they are interested only in such machinery as can be delivered within thirty days. The machinery is to be shipped to a place 80 degrees 25 minutes north and 100 degrees east in the southern part of Siam. Since stocks in Singapore are scattered and small, two important British firms are acting as intermediaries in getting this material together and one of the firms has available $200,000 (Straits Settlements currency) for this purpose. The American Trade Commissioner obtained the above information from one of the intermediaries. An English officer who surmises air field construction has received from the other intermediary confirmation of the information.

AMERICAN CONSULATE GENERAL

SUBJECT: Japanese Purchases of Machinery in Malaya for Construction Work in Siam.

STRICTLY CONFIDENTIAL.
THE HONORABLE
THE SECRETARY OF STATE,
WASHINGTON.

SIR:

I have the honor to refer to my communication dated April 20, 11 A.M. and to submit the following information:

On April 18, 1939, the American Trade Commissioner reported to me information which had been confided to him by a member of William Jacks and Company, an important local British firm handling heavy machinery. This information was that Japanese agents were in Singapore purchasing heavy capacity roadbuilding and excavating machinery to be delivered in southern Siam; that Mitsui Bussan Kaisha was financing such purchases; that since the stocks of such equipment in this territory are small both second hand and new machinery was being sought through the intermediary of William Jacks and Company; that price was not an important factor; and that the Japanese agents were not interested in anything which could not be delivered within thirty days.

The following day the British Assistant Security Defense Officer confidentially told me that the Japanese agents had also approached another British firm, United Engineers, to which a sum of some S$200,000 (US$110,000) had

had been made available for the purchase of large capacity roadbuilding and excavating machinery to be delivered at a point in southern Siam, the exact location being 8 degrees 25 minutes North and 100 degrees East. My British informant, who was inclined to suspect the equipment to be intended for aviation landing fields, telegraphed to the British Legation at Bangkok and has received a reply that a Japanese concern has obtained a contract for constructing a 28 kilometer (17.5 miles) highway which will skirt a small bay, at the latitude and longitude indicated above, and connect with the railroad in that area; and that the total amount of the contract for such construction is S$480,000 (US$261,000). The Assistant Security Defense Officer further stated that despite the information received from the British Legation in Bangkok in regard to the purpose for which the machinery is sought, he is still inclined to suspect the utilization of such equipment for military purposes which may be inimical to the defense of Malaya. He bases his suspicions on:

(1) The indication that delivery be made within thirty days which is indicative of urgency incompatible with normal civil needs;

(2) The high cost of construction which, according to information received from the Public Works Department of Malaya, is excessive unless extraordinary construction difficulties (bridges, drainage, tunneling etc) are encountered;

(3) The location of the road which would not seem to be needed for commerce or other peaceful purposes.

ROM:

Tientsin's despatch No. 417 under date October 13, 1933, encloses a copy of a despatch to the Legation stating that on October 1 the local vernacular press reported that the Japanese military had acquired about one thousand mou of land at Nankai for the construction of an airdrome. The Japanese authorities have assured the municipal government that no airdrome is planned and the matter appears to be settled.
