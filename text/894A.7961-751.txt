Secretary of State,

285, December 29, 5 p.m.
Department's telegram No. 45, March 30, 1 p.m.

Japanese Foreign Office states that there were no objections to the flight of Stevens and Halliburton over Taiwan if route and regulations which it specifies are followed: provided, the American authorities will permit the landing at Manila of super-universal type aeroplane equipped with a 420 horsepower Jupiter motor, which would make a flight from Japan to Australia some time before next April under the auspices of SHINAICHI SIGASHA, a newspaper of Nagoya. If the landing of the Japanese plane at Manila is authorized please instruct this Embassy where it should forward the Japanese regulations for the Halliburton and Stevens flight.

FORBES

AMERICAN CONSULATE,
AMoy, China, May 17, 1932.

CONFIDENTIAL

Subject: Proposed Flight to Taiwan.

The Honorable
Nelson Trusler Johnson,
American Minister,
Peiping, China.

Sir:

I have the honor to refer to the Legation's instructions dated February 15, 1932, regarding the proposed flight of Messrs. Stevens and Halliburton over Taiwan, and to enclose a copy of a map which the Japanese Consul of Amoy gave me for the use of the above persons. He informs me that this map is of the military drill ground which has been prepared as a temporary landing field on the island of Taiwan especially for these fliers.

A few days ago, the Japanese Consul told me that he had received information to the effect that these persons had advised that this proposed flight would not take place.

A copy of this map was given by me to the Commander of the U.S.S. STEWART, who I understand forwarded it to the Commander-in-Chief of the U. S. Asiatic Fleet.

Respectfully yours,

Lynn W. Franklin,
American Consul.
