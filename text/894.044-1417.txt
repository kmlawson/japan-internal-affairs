1. The system of jury trial in Japan dates from 1923 at which time a law was passed providing for the employment of juries in criminal cases.

2. The cases which may be tried by jury are those involving capital punishment, penal servitude for life, or imprisonment. Upon demand by the accused a jury may be called in cases involving penal servitude or imprisonment for more than three years and which come under the jurisdiction of a local court. Such demand by an accused must be made prior to the first day of public trial. On the other hand, an accused may, prior to the statement of the case by the procurator, withdraw his request for trial by jury or decline to be passed upon by a jury.

3. The following cases may not be tried by jury:

a. Crimes coming under the special jurisdiction of the Court of Cassation.

b. Violations of the Military Secrets Law, the Military Criminal Code, the Naval Criminal Code, or other offenses relating to military secrets.

c. Crimes relating to public elections required by laws and ordinances.

d. Violation of the Peace Maintenance Law.

e. Offenses against the Imperial Household, causing internal disturbances, aiding a public enemy, disturbing international relations, and sedition.

4. Jurymen shall possess all and each of the following qualifications:

a. They shall be male subjects of the Empire and thirty or more years of age.

b. They shall have resided two or more years consecutively in the same city, town, or arrondissement.

c. They shall have paid a national tax of three yen or more annually for two or more years consecutively.

d. They shall be able to read and write.

5. The following persons are not eligible to act as jurymen:

a. Incompetents or quasi-incompetents.

b. Bankrupts who cannot rehabilitate themselves.

c. Those who are deaf, dumb, or blind.

d. Those sentenced to penal servitude, six years or more imprisonment, convicted of a felony under the old Criminal Code, or sentenced to major imprisonment.

6. The following may not be called upon to serve as jurymen:

a. Ministers of State.
b. Judges, procurators, law officers of the Army or Navy in the active service.
c. President or councillors of the Administrative Court in active service.
d. Officials of the Imperial Household in active service.
e. Members of the Army or Navy in active service.
f. Principal administrative officers of prefectures.
g. Police officials in active service.
h. Prison officials in active service.
i. Clerks of courts of law in active service.
j. Taxation and customs officials and officials of the Imperial Monopoly Bureau in active service.
k. Mariners and those engaged in the service of mails, telegraph, telephone and railways.
l. Heads of municipalities, townships and arrondissements.
m. Barristers and solicitors.
n. Notaries and public scribes.
o. Instructors in elementary schools in active service.
p. Shinto and Budhist priests and other ministers of religion.
q. Physicians, dentists and pharmacists.
r. Students.

7. Jurymen may be excused from service under the following circumstances:

a. When the juryman has incurred damage or loss.
b. When he is concerned in a civil suit.
c. When he is or has been a relation of the accused, victim, or of anyone concerned in a civil suit.
d. When he is head or member of the household of the accused, victim, or anyone concerned in a civil suit.

From: M/A, Tokyo. Report No. 10,158 July 26, 1940.

Moscow newspapers for March 3 published a Tokyo despatch headed "extraordinary measures of the Japanese Government" reading in translation as follows:

"According to Domei the Japanese Government at its meeting of February 29 passed a resolution regarding four extraordinary wartime measures based on the necessity of 'regulating the government of the country and also the life of the state in connection with the sharpening of the military situation'. These four measures include according to the agency: first, the temporary closing of first class recreational establishments; second, abolition of Sunday holidays for government institutions; third, limitation on business trips of government personnel; fourth, destruction of all documents which are not considered essential and expedient utilization everywhere of superfluous office equipment.

"The Japanese newspaper 'NIPPON TIMES' has published a list of extraordinary measures, approved at the session of the Japanese Government.

Buildings of educational institutions must be turned over to war factories or transformed into military warehouses, hospitals or shelters. The extension of the law regarding obligatory labor service to women is planned.

Mr. Blakeslee

The meeting of the territorial subcommittee on July 16 will be devoted to the problem of the disposition of the remaining outlying territories of Japan: Southern Sakhalin, Kuriles, Luichius, Bonins and the Spratly Islands. Dr. Bowman believed that the entire session would be taken up by a consideration of these outlying territories.

The meeting of the subcommittee on July 30 would be devoted to the problems of Japan proper, including (1) conditions of surrender, (2) political and security issues, and (3) the problem of an economical viable Japan.

In September the meetings would be devoted to Southeastern Asia, including the issues of Indo-China, Malaya, Thailand, Burma and the Netherlands Indies.

After that would come the problem of an international organization for the Far East and the Pacific. Finally there would be the many topics relating to China.

Dr. Bowman suggested that in September it might well be advisable to have a meeting of the territorial subcommittee each week.
