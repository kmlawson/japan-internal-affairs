ASSISTANT SECRETARY OF STATE

I have the honor to report that I have just received an official request from the Governor of the Kwantung Leased Territory for assistance from the appropriate authorities of our Government in connection with the future development of the city of Dairen. Although, as the Department is aware Dairen, following the Russian designs, was an early example of a planned city, recently because of its rapid rate of growth (it now has a population of approximately half a million) and of the fact that it is shortly to become the seat of government of the Kwantung Leased Territory, the problem of further planning and controlling the future growth of the city has become pressing. As indicated in the Consulate's report of August 29, 1935, entitled "Construction Projects in Dairen" not only is a civic center planned for Dairen to

to accomodate the new government offices and a building program drawn up to house the families of the employees of the Territorial Government, but a new railroad station and approaches thereto are under construction, which it is claimed will be the finest in Manchuria when completed.

Inasmuch as Governor Takeshita has expressed especial interest in the three related problems of town planning, zoning, and "satellite" town planning, I should be grateful if the Department could see its way to enlisting the assistance of the Department of Commerce, as it lid in the matter of the smoke nuisance problem (see despatch No. 5 of March 1, 1935, file No. 812.4) regarding which the Governor has expressed sincere appreciation, to obtain copies of such reports as the Advisory Committee on Zoning of that Department has available, as well as a copy of the report of the findings of the Senate Park Commission for the improvement of Washington (1902) and of such subsequent reports as may have been written concerning city planning in Washington. I should also appreciate it if it were feasible for the Department of Commerce to obtain copies of the zoning regulations and of the reports on regional planning undertaken by the cities of New York, Philadelphia, and Los Angeles and particularly, in connection with the first city mentioned, of the conclusions reached by the group of architects and landscape architects who surveyed the problem of regional planning of New York City and published a report thereon in May, 1929, as well as of the recommendations of the Westchester Park Commission.

Dr. McAllister also states that the National Resources Committee has issued other more recent material on planning and zoning.

I assume that should this additional material be desired, your Department will take up the matter directly with the Superintendent of Documents and the National Resources Committee in the Interior Department Building.

Very truly yours,

Lacey C. Zapf,

My dear Mr. Secretary:

Reference is made to a report to you from Stuart E. Grummon, American Consul at Dairen, Manchuria under date of April 20, 1937 on "Request of Kwantung Government for Assistance of United States Authorities in Connection with City Planning in Dairen" which was with further reference to the Consulate's dispatch No. 48 of September 10, 1935. A copy of Consular Grummon's report was sent to this Committee.

For the information and use of the Government of the Kwantung Leased Territory in connection with the planning of "Greater" Dairen, enclosed you will please find the following publications which were extra copies in our files and which may be forwarded to Consular Grummon:

"The City Plan of Memphis, Tennessee", published by the Memphis City Planning and Zoning Commission, 1924.
"Building Zone Ordinance of the City of Nashville, Tennessee", published by the Nashville City Planning and Zoning Commission, 1936.

The latter publication contains a building zone map at a scale of 1" = 1,000' but this map is not an actual engineer's map at a large scale such as is desired by the Kwantung authorities. The maps in the printed copies of zoning ordinances of American cities which are distributed free or at a nominal cost are usually very small. Some cities put out zoning atlases which are sold at prices of $3.00 to $15.00.

The San Francisco Zoning Map (address City Planning Commission, City Hall, San Francisco) is printed at a scale of 1" = 800', and Akron, Ohio (address L. B. Hievel, Planning Engineer, City Planning Commission, 701 City Hall, Akron, Ohio), prints such an atlas at a scale of 1" = 600'. Also, the published zoning ordinance and maps of Dayton, Ohio (address Harold E. Winey, Secretary-Engineer, City Plan Board, 401 U.B. Annex Building, Dayton, Ohio) may be of interest and value. Prices for these two are unknown. The Building Zone Ordinance for Nashville (1936), a copy of which has been enclosed, is considered by some authorities in the field to be one of the best of the new ones.

Other city planning reports which might be of use to the authorities in Dairen are as follows:

"Report of the Regional Plan of New York and Its Environs", published in several volumes by the New York Regional Plan Association, Inc., Madison Avenue, New York City.
