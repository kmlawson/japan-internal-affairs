The Yokohama Specie Bank has adopted a new policy
with regard to the issuance of drafts on its branches
in the United States to travelers leaving Japan on
business or pleasure. Such travelers are by Japanese
law permitted to carry with them not more than five
hundred yen in Japanese currency or the foreign currency
equivalent of the maximum amount of five hundred yen.
Up until recently the Yokohama Specie Bank issued to
travelers to the United States on production of passports
and steamship tickets drafts in United States currency
up to the maximum amount permissible, i.e. $117.18
U.S. currency, the equivalent of Yen 500 at the bank's
selling rate of 23 7/16.
Possibly as a result of the recent political events,
specifically, the signing of the Japanese-German-Italian
pact on September 27, 1940, the Yokohama Specie Bank in
Kobe has since then ceased to issue drafts in United
States dollars to travelers to America, and will now
issue only a draft in yen on a branch in the United
States which is payable in United States currency at the
bank's buying rate for such sight drafts on the day
presented.
The change in policy is of interest if not of
special significance in the near term outlook for the
value of the yen in foreign exchange.

"Mr. Gerli, you know what the general situation is in Japan.

completely done away with all considerations except the one of the relations
between our two countries. Frankly, the position is one where not only
for business but for our diplomacy, we are on what you might call a
day-to-day basis, with no plans except as they may develop because of certain
happenings.
