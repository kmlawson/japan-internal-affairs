AMERICAN CONSULATE GENERAL
Hong Kong, May 10th, 1938
SUBJECT: Domei News Agency Opens Office in Hong Kong.
THE HONORABLE
THE SECRETARY OF STATE
WASHINGTON.
SIR:

I have the honor to report that the Consulate General has been informed that the Japanese Domei news agency opened a regular office in Hong Kong as of May 1st, 1938. The Domei agency has, of course, had correspondents in this Colony but according to our information this office is its first one regularly established here for the sale of its news service.

The Domei office is a small one located in Holland House, an office building on the corner of Ice House Street and Queen's Road, Hong Kong. It is in charge of Mr. Robert Y. Horiguchi who, according to our information, was at the School of Journalism of the University of Missouri in 1930-32, and was later employed in Chicago by the Hearst International News Service. Mr. Horiguchi is understood to have been chosen for the job in Hong Kong because of his good knowledge of English and because, as the offspring of a Japanese father and a Belgian mother, he is somewhat European in appearance, manner and temperament. We understand that his father was a

Japanese

Japanese diplomat who married the mother when he was stationed in Belgium more than thirty years ago. Mr. Horiguchi's associates in Hong Kong speak highly of his ability as a newspaper man and as a propagandist.

Our inquiries indicate that so far the local branch of the Domei agency has only three clients in Hong Kong. They are the HONG KONG PRESS and the HONG KONG TELEGRAPH, and a little Japanese afternoon daily published by a group of Koreans in the Wanchai district of Hong Kong. The two English dailies are said to pay HK$100.00 per month each for the Domei service. The Domei agency has circularized the twenty-odd Chinese newspapers in Hong Kong with a view to selling its news but, according to our information, not one has subscribed. Close control is exercised by the Hong Kong Government over the incoming radio news messages for Domei.

The main reason for the establishment of this Agency, which cannot possibly be commercially profitable in these times, is undoubtedly one of official expediency requiring the dissemination of Japanese-colored news to combat the situation complained of by ASAHI and other Japanese newspapers "that the Hong Kong newspapers are flooded with false news of the war - if not of deliberate distortion of the facts".

There is rumor locally that the HONG KONG PRESS has finally accepted persistently offered subsidies from both the German and Japanese Governments to publish
