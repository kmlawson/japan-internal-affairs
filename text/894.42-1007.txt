The increase of the State subsidy for compulsory education to relieve the financial burdens of the local governments is one of the present Government's most important policies. The present draft budget, which was approved by the Cabinet for submission to the approaching Diet on March 25th, includes an appropriation of Yen 10,000,000 for this purpose. According to the press the total cost of compulsory education amounts to Yen 271,598,000 of which Yen 164,140,000 is teachers' salaries. During the month under review the press reported considerable opposition among the members of the House of Peers to this appropriation. The arguments of opponents were given, in brief, as follows: the grant would aid too greatly wealthy towns and villages and too little poor towns and villages; local authorities would administer the subsidy unwisely; local authorities as a result of this financial aid would be tempted into extravagances. The argument has also been advanced that the peculiar feeling which the people of a community have toward teachers as a result of their participation in the payment of salaries would be altered with the result.

the result that the respect of the community, including the pupils, toward the teachers would lessen.

Toward the beginning of the month the Parliamentary Vice Minister of Education was reported to have informed certain peers that the Government's aim was to pay eventually the salaries of all teachers engaged in compulsory education but that financial conditions made possible a beginning of only Yen 10,000,000. Later in the month a Government official was reported to have said that this appropriation is not a part of the main plan but has been included in the draft budget so that local taxes might be decreased. This latter statement was regarded as an effort on the part of the Government to soften the alleged opposition existing in the House of Peers.

In order to keep pace with the advanced ideas of education and the change in the international outlook, it is reported that the Ministry of Education is planning a revision in the textbooks in use throughout Japan. The authorities plan to tone down the warlike stories in these books, giving more emphasis to social and political aspects of the history of Japan rather than the military. Most of the textbooks now in use have not been revised since 1914, when the Sino-Japanese and Russo-Japanese wars were still relatively fresh in the minds of the people and the World War just looming to give impetus to the Government's policy of arousing the martial and

Sir:

I have the honor to state that a few days ago I was talking with two members of the National Motion Picture Censorship Committee. The subject of nationalistic propaganda having come up for discussion, I remarked that I had been reliably informed that Chinese textbooks contained material intended to arouse hostility to foreign nations, including the United States.

The two officers with whom I was conversing, who are also officers of the Ministry of Education, denied this assertion and since there was not available to me any evidence supporting the charge against Chinese textbooks, it was necessary to accept the denial.

It is of interest to note, however, that the two officers with whom I was talking told me that researches of the Chinese Government showed that the Japanese Government was deliberately utilizing Japanese textbooks to promote a policy of Japanese territorial expansion in Manchuria, as well as to arouse "anti-foreign" sentiments among Japanese children.

YOUNG PEOPLE'S SCHOOL.

1. The Young Men's Military Training Centers (Seinen
Kunren Sho) and the Industrial Continuation Schools have
been combined to form the Young People's School effective
April 1, 1935.

Object: While the ostensible reason given was a
vague statement to the effect that the union would greatly
improve the health of the young men and young women, the
real reason is that the amalgamation will increase the
numbers of young men eligible to take pre-scriptional train-
ing.

Formation: Any prefecture, city, town, village, school
association, or other public bodies in either Japan proper
or the Hokkaido may form a school center. It is expected
that the facilities of the former Young Men's Military
Training Centers will be enlarged to take care of the com-
bine or the two merged activities will meet as formerly in
the same buildings (usually schools).

Funds: The government appropriation for Young Men's
Military Training Centers and Industrial Continuation Schools
is usually about one tenth of the amount of funds provided by
prefectures, cities and towns. It is estimated that the total
cost of the Young People's School will be about Yen 15,000,000.

Attendance: The following table shows the numbers of
students, instructors, units and graduates for the years 1926-
1932 for the Young Men's Military Training Centers and for
the year 1932 for the Commercial Schools.

Subject: Schools and Colleges.
University Autonomy.

1. The extent of autonomy in Imperial Universities became an issue during the past year. The autonomy referred to is the practice, in effect for some twenty years, under which deans and presidents of the Imperial Universities have been chosen by faculty elections, with appointment by the Education Minister following as a routine formality. The practice was without basis in law, which vests the appointive power in the Education Minister, but it had come to be recognized in the internal administrative regulations of several of the universities, and they were very reluctant to surrender the privilege.

2. The issue was first raised early in 1938 by Marquis Kido, then Education Minister; a Professor Ouchi of the Tokyo Imperial University was then under investigation for complicity in a Communist conspiracy, and it was conceived as possible that Communist elements in the university faculties might extend and perpetuate their influence by means of the faculty elections. The issue was brought to a head by General Araki as Education Minister at the end of July, and there ensued three months of negotiations between the Ministry and the faculties before the latter finally yielded the point. Under the present arrangement, the faculty elections are still held, but only in the sense of a recommendation to the Education Minister; and written public ballots are required instead of the former secret ballots, to prevent covert support of undesirable candidates. The Tokyo Imperial University was the last hold-out, and after agreement was finally reached, its president resigned and was replaced by a retired naval officer, Vice-Admiral Hiraga.

3. Meanwhile the Tokyo Imperial faculty continued to be vexed and divided by the aftermath of the affair which had precipitated the abolition of autonomy. Professor Ouchi had been tried and imprisoned, and one of his supporters in the economics department Professor Kawai, was in turn under police pressure on allegation of subversive passages in some of his writings. When it became evident that Professor Kawai would be indicted, he was dismissed at the same time Professor Hijikata, leader of the nationalist faction in the economics faculty which had agitated against Kawai, was dismissed as a disturbing influence, precipitating wholesale resignations which left the department unstaffed on the eve of year-end examinations. The dismissals were eventually confirmed, a new dean of economics was appointed, and the new term has been started with the aid of lecturers drawn from practical economic circles pending the appointment of permanent professors.
