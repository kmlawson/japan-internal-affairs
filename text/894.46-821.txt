The Honorable
The Secretary of State
Washington, D.C.

My dear Mr. Secretary:

On Wednesday, July 4, the Japanese Broadcasting
Company has arranged a special program between 4:15 and
4:30 P.M., E.S.T., which will include a brief message
by Foreign Minister Hirohito and Ambassador Grew, with
songs by American school children.

From 4:00 to 4:15 P.M., E.S.T., we have arranged
a musical program which will be presented by the Chicago
Symphony Orchestra and we should like to have someone
from the State Department give a two or three minute
talk appropriate to the occasion, if this is possible.

Very truly yours,

K. H. Berkeley
Manager

June 27, 1934

PC
Mr. Southgate:

The attached letter of June 21 will explain itself. I have discussed the matter with FE and Mr. Phillips.

I have also asked Mr. Berkeley to ascertain definitely whether the Minister for Foreign Affairs, Mr. Hirota, is to give his message personally over the radio or whether it is to be given as from him and be read or delivered by someone else. Mr. Berkeley has promised to let me know, and I have asked Mr. Morlock to see that you get his message when it arrives. If Mr. Hirota is to give his message personally, it would seem appropriate for the Secretary to give a message likewise personally, if it is convenient for him to do so on the Fourth of July. It does not appear to be of sufficient importance to have him make any change in his plans for this purpose.

If a message from the Secretary is to be read over the radio by someone else, it would be preferable that such a message make no allusion to the so-called Far Eastern situation or for that matter to the Far East at all. The message might better be drawn up along the lines of historical reference in connection with the establishment of the American Nation.

As for the official of the State Department who could give the message, FE suggests Judge Moore, Mr. Hewes or Mr. Perkins, Acting Historical Adviser. Judge Moore, unfortunately, is otherwise engaged on that day. You might, therefore, explore the possibility of having either Mr. Hewes or Mr. Perkins deliver the message.

As for the preparation of the message, I shall leave that to you in consultation with whoever reads the message and Mr. Phillips, who should be consulted also with regard to its content. It might be as well to have Doctor Hornbeck see that the message is appropriate for the occasion and meets with his approval from the point of view of our relation with the Far East.

I am explaining this to you, as I may not be here when it comes time to arrange the details for delivering the message.

SA JCD.GAM

# DEPARTMENT OF STATE
## THE SECRETARY

June 28, 1934

894.46

Mr. Berkeley of the National Broadcasting Company telephoned this morning to give me information in response to my inquiry as to whether Mr. Hirota, Minister of Foreign Affairs of Japan, was definitely to speak himself over the radio on July 4.

Mr. Berkeley said that he had cabled to Tokyo yesterday and had just received a reply to the effect that, as Mr. Hirota did not speak very much English, Prince Tokugawa had been substituted for him on the program and that, in view of Prince Tokugawa's unofficial position, the National Broadcasting Company was endeavoring to have Mr. Taft, President of the Japan Society of New York, or perhaps Mr. Castle or someone not having an official position but having some connection with Japanese affairs, to speak from this country in connection with this Fourth of July program. He asked if that would be satisfactory to the State Department and I said I considered that such an arrangement would be entirely appropriate, as it appeared from his information that no official of the Japanese Government was entering into the program.

SA JCD.GAM
