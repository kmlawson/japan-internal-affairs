SUBJECT: Manila Sample Fair of Taiwan Products.

THE HONORABLE
THE SECRETARY OF STATE
WASHINGTON

SIR:

I have the honor to report that the Taiwan Chamber of Commerce in Taihoku sponsored a trip by Taiwan business men to hold a sample fair to exhibit Taiwan products in Manila during the latter part of April and first part of May. Originally the Chamber planned to send about twelve prominent Taiwan business men on this mission in March, 1940, but apparently exchange difficulties with the Finance Ministry necessitated a modification of original plans. In the end George Makoto Ohyi, an American born Japanese in the Export Department of Kikumoto's Department Store (the largest in Taihoku), Clerk M. Mori of Matsumoto Piece Goods Store, Clerk Y. Goto of the Takasago Chemical Industry Company and Attache G. Ueda of the Commercial Division of the Bureau of Productive Industries of the Taiwan Government General, made the trip. They took with them

them typical Taiwan products such as coral, tea, camphor, canned pineapple and other canned fruits, vegetables and fish products, monopoly wines and liquors, wall board, bamboo poles and baskets, woven products such as hats, toys, horn articles, flavoring extracts, perfumes, and wearing apparel, valued in all at some Pesos 2,000.

Upon returning to Taiwan Mr. Ohyi told me that in general they had counted the trip as being successful, that it was the first time such a fair had ever been held, that they had given away most of the sample goods they had taken with them, and not much direct business had resulted, but they felt the exhibit was a step in the right direction of stimulating trade. He said that their entry into Manila was facilitated by immigration and customs, although they had barely enough money to pay customs duties levied, and that their stay in Manila was pleasant. He was particularly impressed by the remark of a Filipino whom Ohyi described as being the Trade Minister, namely that: "I am very much interested in seeing this exhibit of Taiwan products, for the climate in Taiwan and the Philippines is much the same, and if these products can be made successfully in Taiwan, then we can make them here too."

Respectfully yours,

Gerald Warner
American Consul.

DEPARTMENT OF STATE
DIVISION OF FAR EASTERN AFFAIRS
June 7, 1940

Shanghai's 3128, May 9, 1940, transmitting memorandum from Owen L. Dawson, Agricultural Attaché, on general conditions encountered on official trip to Taiwan.

The Agricultural Attaché reports that he experienced some difficulty in obtaining information and statistics and attributes this to a ruling by military authorities that no figures for the last two years and in many cases basic data for former years shall be given out without express permission. In many cases this permission is not forthcoming.

He suggests that future developments with respect to restrictions on agricultural information may indicate the advisability of a request to Japanese authorities to grant freer access to information in reciprocity for information which has been made available to them in the United States and the Philippines.

The Agricultural Attaché reports that, in spite of difficulties encountered in obtaining information, the trip to Taiwan was of value.
