EMBASSY OF THE

No. 1336.
The American Embassy presents its compliments to
the Ministry of Foreign Affairs and has the honor to
state that the American Consul General at Osaka reports
that he has been informed by Mr. Ernest L. Vest, Manager
of the Kobe Central Agency of the Singer Sewing Machine
Company of New York, that on March 28, 1939, Mr. Vest
was obliged to pay to the Japanese Consul General at
San Francisco a fee of $10.00 for the visa of his own
passport and also a fee of $10.00 for the visa of his
wife's passport. It appears from the passport records
of the Department of State that Mr. Vest has been resid-
ing in Japan since 1922 as a representative of the above-
mentioned company and, according to the affidavit that
he has made out concerning the fees which he has now
been required to pay, this is the first time that fees
have ever been collected from him for a Japanese visa.
It will be recalled that under the terms of the
Visa Waiver Agreement between the United States and Japan
which has been effective since l926, fees for visas should
not be collected from American citizens of certain cate-
gories who are proceeding to Japan. These categories
include among others those who are visiting Japan tem-
porarily as tourists or temporarily for business or
pleasure, and those who are entitled to enter solely
to carry on trade under and in pursuance of the present
existing

=== Page 39 === (confidence 0.99)
-2-
existing Treaty of Commerce and Navigation between the
United States and Japan which was signed on February 21,
1911.
As Mr. Vest's residence in Japan is for business
purposes in connection with the carrying on of trade
between the United States and Japan, it is believed that
his case and that of his wife might properly be regarded
as coming within the above-mentioned category. This
view seems to be strengthened by the fact that, except
on the present occasion, fees for visas have not been
collected from them. Consequently, it is felt that the
Japanese Consul General in San Francisco may have been
under a misapprehension as to their status.
The American Embassy would greatly appreciate it
if this matter could be inquired into. If the fees have
been charged in error, it is requested that refunds of
the fees be sent to the Embassy for transmission to Mr.
and Mrs. Vest.
Tokyo, July 25, 1939.
