You may wish to bring to the attention of the Supreme
Commander for the Allied Powers the following comments upon
the instruction given by SCAP to the Japanese Government
on the subject of travel of Japanese technicians outside
of Japan, SCAPIN 2035 dated August 1, 1949.
SCAP's olicles governing the travel abroad of Japan-
ese technicians are of major significance in relation to
this Government's "Point Four" Program. The Economio Com-
mision for Asia and the Far Est has also attached con-
siderable importance to this subject as it affects the
United Nations program of technical assistance for economio
development.
In general, the controls and prescriptions set forth
1n SCAPIN 2075 eppear to involve the participat1on by
SCAP to an unnecessary degree in a program which should
be governed by the ordinary relations between friendly
states. They appear to treat travel of technicians abroad
as of excentional nature, to be perm tted only in special
circumstances, rather than as normal and desirable acts
in intern&tional relations. It is recognized that it
is appropriate at this time for SCAP to examine travel
requests in order to enforce exchange control measures
and to screen the subjects politically. The Department
strongly doubts the desirability, however, of a SCAP model
contr ct containing extremely detailed provisions.
On the following four points SCAPIN 2035 appear 1n-
consistent with the general commercial polioy of the United
States and, in partioular, with this Government's policies
