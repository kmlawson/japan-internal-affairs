The company's capital is fixed at 73 million yen,
with its main office in Tokyo and 16 branch offices in
East Asia (including Tientsin, Tsingtao and Shanghai),
and the announced purpose is the establishment and main-
tenance of shipping lines between Japan and China, on
the China coast, and between China and foreign countries.
The company will also engage in wharfage and warehousing
and will also manage and invest in other allied enter-
prises. The firm will operate an initial fleet of 59
vessels contributed by 9 Japanese shipping companies,
the Nippon Yusen Kaisha, the Osaka Shosen Kaisha, the
Nisshin Kisen Kaisha, the Mitsui, the Kawawaki, the
Okawaki, the Awa Kyodo, the Harada, and the Yamashita
companies. The following consolidated services are sche-
duled for operation in October of this year: Japan-
Tientsin, Japan-Tsingtao, Japan-Shanghai, Japan-South
China, Formosa-Shanghai, Formosa-South China, Dairen-
North China, and Tientsin-Dairen-South China.
The following communique is reported to have been
issued by the Japanese Ministry of Coamunications on
August 11, 1939:
"The Government for many years has been
paying attention to the strengthening and
unifying of the Japanese shipping business
in East Asia and has been engaged in its
guidance. Therefore it is a matter for
sincere congratulation that all our shipping
firms operating in the region have cordially
agreed to pool together their interests and
resources and to establish the Toa Kaiun
Kaisha. The marine traffic in the nearby
seas is assured hereafter of a systematic,
economic and highly efficient service.
Needless to say it is of urgent neces-
sity that we strive for the improvement and
expansion of our network of communications
in embarking upon the construction of a new
East

East Asian order. In order to make Sino-
Japanese collaboration really fruitful and
further enhance the relationship of mutual
assistance between the two nations in poli-

The communique concludes with the statement that the
Government's policy is to give proper guidance and to
exercise appropriate control over the new company so as
to enable it to achieve its task in East Asia.
The question naturally arises whether the new com-
pany will attempt to exclude, by one means or another,
foreign shipping interests from the routes mentioned.
In this connection, the denial of adequate port facili-
ties (as at Tsingtao, for example), the creation of labor
unions or guilds (as at various ports), and the curtail-
ment of facilities for trading with the hinterland (as
the closing of the Yangtze and the Pearl rivers, for
example) may be considered of evidential importance.
American shipping interests along the routes mentioned
are extremely limited.
While it is much too early to attempt a prediction
as to the future course of operation of the new company,
it may be remarked that the Japanese continue to hew to
the line of economic domination in China, particularly in
North China. The consolidation of shipping services,
which will probably adversely affect competition from
foreign shipping interests, is but a further manifesta-
tion of this purpose, previously witnessed in the forma-
tion of the various development companies. It may also
be remarked, in a broader aspect, that the regimentation

despite the aim of making Sino-Japanese collaboration
