AMERICAN CONSULAR SERVICE
American Consulate General
Nanking, China, December 2, 1933.

Subject: Rescue of Japanese "Liberty Party" by U.S.S. SACRAMENTO Motor Sampan.

The Honorable
The Secretary of State,
Washington.

Sir:

I have the honor to report that on November 27, 1933, a motor sampan from the U.S.S. SACRAMENTO saved three Japanese sailors from drowning.

A "liberty party" of ten Japanese sailors from the Japanese gunboat SUNIDA was thrown into the river when its boat capsized. The American motor sampan immediately proceeded to the scene of the accident and was successful in rescuing three of the unfortunate victims. Four others were picked up by Chinese sampans and the remaining three were drowned.

The Japanese Consul General and the Japanese Naval Attache at Nanking graciously expressed their appreciation to this office, and to Captain Braisted of the U.S.S. SACRAMENTO, for the assistance which had been rendered.

Respectfully yours,

Willys R. Peck,
American Consul General.

In triplicate to the Department
Duplicate copies to the Legation
