SIR:

I have the honor to report that the Japanese cruiser "KUMA" arrived from the naval base at Formosa on March 2, 1934, at the port of Cheribon, Java, on a courtesy visit, and sailed for Semarang and Surabaya, Java, on March 4, 1934. The cruiser is under the command of Captain S. Horiushi. Upon its arrival the officers were met by officers of the Japanese Consulate General, after which the usual courtesies were exchanged between the commander and the local authorities. Japanese residents in Bandoeng and other points in Java were present to welcome the officers of the cruiser.

Other ports to be visited in Netherland India are Macassar and Menado.

SIR:

I have the honor to report the visit of the Japanese cruiser "Kuma", under the command of Captain Horiuchi. This vessel arrived here early on the morning of March 8, 1934 and sailed at noon on March 11, 1934 for Macassar.

The visit to this portion of Java was apparently a routine call, and no special significance is attached to it. The usual formal calls and functions as well as informal entertainments were made for the officers and men.

None of the local newspapers made any comments upon the call other than to publish the program of entertainment. As far as I could observe the visit resulted in no increase in goodwill among European residents. However, in contrast to the visits of other national war-ships, a large crowd of natives were at the pier to see this ship. It is believed that Japanese owners of small stores catering to the native trade encouraged them to go to see the ship. Although originally curiosity was the motive to visit the vessel, the result was very likely a closer feeling and respect for the Japanese.

I have the honor to report that on June 30, 1954, the Japanese cruisers "Iwate" and "Asama" visited this port. The two vessels were moored to buoys in the roadstead of Tandjong Priok, the port of Batavia.

On Sunday, July 1, a reception was given on board the "Iwate" by her commander, Vice Admiral Hajime Matsushita, which was attended by local officials, members of the consular corps, and resident Japanese.

The vessels sailed on the morning of July 4, their destination being given as "probably one of the Japanese mandated islands in the Pacific".

There appears to have been no political significance in the visit of these Japanese naval units. Their appearance during the Netherlands-Japanese trade negotiations in Batavia was, however, criticized in unofficial circles as an example of bad taste.
