996, July 14, 4 p.m.

New regulations effective July 12 permit the use of the Japanese language only in telephone communications to points within the Japanese Empire, as for instance between Tokyo and Yokohama or Faruizawa. Telephone calls within the city may still be made in foreign languages.

In telephone conversations between Japan and European countries only German and Japanese may be used; between Japan and other countries except China and Manchukuo only English and Japanese; between Japan and Japanese, China and Manchukuo only those three languages.

Telegrams sent abroad may be only in English, German or Japanese. Code telegrams must have translation in one of these three languages attached when filed for transmission. This does not apply to official government telegrams. A member of my staff was informed by the Foreign Office that it is intervening to render it possible for chiefs of mission absent at summer resorts to communicate

413
Your 996, July 14, 4 p.m., new telephone regulations.

If you consider that the prohibition against the
use of the English language in telephone communications
within the Japanese Empire will prevent or seriously
interfere with the normal functioning of the Embassy or
consular offices, you are authorized in your discretion
to protest against the prohibition on the ground that
the use of the English language in communication between
the Embassy and consulates or between those establishments
and American citizens resident in Japan is essential to
the normal functioning of the Embassy and consulates and,
therefore, that the denial of the right to use English
impairs the effectiveness of the official representation
of the interests of this Government in Japan and is re-
pugnant to the reciprocal permission accorded by the
United States and Japan for the maintenance and functioning
of their respective consular and diplomatic establishments.

DEPARTMENT OF STATE
PUBLICATION OF FAR EASTERN AFFAIRS
July 16, 1941.

Eu states that the only case known to it of restriction on the use by our missions or consulates in Europe of foreign languages in telephone communication was in Spain, during a critical period in the Revolution, when conversation in any language but Spanish was forbidden and would be cut off by the operator regardless of the parties. We made no representations in view of the battle or siege conditions existing. The prohibition was removed with the end of these conditions. In Germany no restrictions on the use of English by our representatives in their telephone communication existed up to the time these representatives left, even in communication with occupied countries. During the Warsaw campaign telephone communication in English would be cut off if begun. No notification was given to our offices and no representations made. When the campaign was over English was again permitted to be used, even with Warsaw.

In the British Isles our consuls at present may not use codes in their telegrams, but must them send them en clair.

EU knows of no precedent in Europe of acquiescence on the part of our representatives to restrictions on the use by them of their own language in telephone communication which would clash with the proposed reply to Tokyo.

WRL
Langdon

1060, July 23, 7 p.m.
Department's number 413, July 19, 6 p.m., new telephone regulations.

The prohibition against the use of English in telephone communications constitutes a serious interference with the normal functioning of the Embassy and the several consular offices in Japan especially as it relates to communications between Tokyo and Yokohama and Kobe and Osaka. Prior to receipt of (?) we approached the Foreign Office on our own initiative and brought the foregoing to their attention laying special emphasis upon the question of reciprocal treatment. The Foreign Office regretted the "inconvenience" to which we would undoubtedly be put as a result of the new restrictions but pointed out that similar hardships would not (?) if such restrictions should be enforced in the United States owing to the fact that the great majority of Japanese there speak English.

We therefore feel that further protest or representations

On his own initiative the Foreign Minister today referred to our representations concerning the Japanese regulations against the use of English by American officials on the telephone in Japan and said he regretted the delay in giving us satisfaction in this matter but that the regulations were issued as a result of similar regulations in effect in Great Britain. I emphatically pointed out the illogical reasoning of penalizing Americans in Japan for measures in force in Great Britain. The Minister merely indicated that it is a complicated problem and said that he proposes to discuss the matter in the near future with the British Ambassador with a view to reaching a satisfactory solution.

Sent to the Department via Shanghai.

AMERICAN EMBASSY,
LONDON (ENGLAND)

The Japanese Foreign Minister in a conversation with the Ambassador at Tokyo referred to representations made by the Ambassador in regard to regulations prohibiting American officials from using the English language in telephone conversations in Japan and stated that those regulations were issued because of similar British regulations in Great Britain.

The Department desires to be informed whether there are in effect in Great Britain regulations prohibiting Japanese officials from using the Japanese language in telephone conversations. If such regulations are now in effect, please report the date enforcement began and give a brief outline of the nature of the restrictions.

EMBASSY OF THE
UNITED STATES OF AMERICA
Tokyo, August 5, 1941

No. 1870

Excellency:

With reference to the recent regulations prohibiting the use of the English language in telephone communications within the Japanese Empire which prevent or seriously interfere with the normal functioning of the American Embassy and consular offices, and acting under instructions from my Government, I have the honor to protest against such prohibition on the ground that the use of the English language in communication between the Embassy and Consulates or between those establishments and citizens residing in Japan is essential to the normal functioning of the Embassy and Consulates and, therefore, that the denial of the right to use English impairs the effectiveness of the official representation of the interests of my Government in Japan and is repugnant to the reciprocal permission accorded by the United States and Japan for the maintenance and functioning of their respective consular and diplomatic establishments.

I am further instructed to request a prompt formal reply to the foregoing protest for the consideration of my Government.

I avail myself of this opportunity to renew to Your Excellency the assurances of my highest consideration.

JOSEPH C. GREW
