# SIXTY THOUSAND GIRLS SOLD INTO SLAVERY BY STARVING PARENTS IN JAP FAMINE AREA

(Copyright, 1934, International News Service)

Ohio, Nov. 23.—(I. N. S.)—Nearly $1,000 girls, many of them not more than 10 years old, have been sold into slavery by their starving parents, a survey of the famine-stricken six northern prefectures showed Friday.

Seven million peasants face death this winter, due to an unprecedented succession of rice crop failures. Relief agencies are bringing pressure to bear on the government to aid the institute, primitive farmers to save them from the successful practice of bartering their daughters to unchecked “Yoshitwara” brokers.

Sixty yen—$18—is said to be the common price with which agents tempt the penniless peasants to sell their children.

Every day hundreds of farm girls, depressed, hungry, spiritless, are sold, to on away and thrown into the barren rooms of government licensed resorts.

Welfare workers are up against an almost impossible task in trying to force the slave traders to give up the girls, while their parents and brothers are subsisting on grass, tree bark, locusts and other insects.

Specific figures furnished from reliable philanthropic sources showed that 59,173 girls have been sold in the last nine months. They have been consigned to the “Yoshitwara” service, sent as maids to questionable cafes or forced into drudgery in factories where they hardly get a living wage.

Government officials profess inability to help the stricken peasants yet indignant welfare workers pointed out that the cabinet Friday approved a record army-navy budget of a billion yen.

In the meantime, with the peasants facing an early winter when snow will cut off even their supply of food, grass, yellow slave agents were literally scouring the countryside.
