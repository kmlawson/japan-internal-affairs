On June 22nd occurred the first accident of any magnitude in the commercial aviation of this country with the death of three persons, one a passenger, in an accident to a plane of the Japan Air Transport Company in Kyushu. The cause of the accident is not known but it is believed that the plane caught fire while crossing the mountains in Kyushu and plunged to the earth in flames. The Japan Air Transport Company took over the Tokyo-Osaka service on April 1, 1929, and neither before nor since that date had any lives been lost until the crash in Kyushu. Later the company extended its service to Fukuoka, Kyushu, and still later it began operating all the way to Dairen via Chosen. Thousands of passengers have been carried over the line during the two years that it has been operating.

* Despatch No. 7, of January 27, 1930.

SIR:

I have the honor to report that on March 17, 1938 a Japanese air mail land plane which had left the city of Tainan, Taiwan at 11:30 A.M. on its way to Mako in the Pescadores developed engine trouble en route and fell into the ocean three miles before reaching the Pescadores off the small eastern coast village of Ryobunko. A Pescadores mail boat and a destroyer from Mako immediately went to the rescue, but by the time they had reached the still floating plane, the pilot Mr. Sonehara and the mechanic Mr. Horiuchi had both disappeared. The search instituted for them by sea and air has proven fruitless and it is believed that they were drowned in the high seas then running. The plane was towed to Mako with its mail intact.
