THE HONORABLE
THE SECRETARY OF STATE
WASHINGTON.
SIR:
I have the honor to refer to my code telegram dated January 29th, 1931 informing the Department of the resignation of the Governor General of Taiwan, of the possibility that other executive heads would resign, and expressing the opinion that this change of executive authorities would not affect the Lake Candidius Hydro-Electric Project.
Subsequent to the press report that Governor General Ishidzuka had resigned as a consequence to the Mushaf fair, the information was received that the resignation of Mr. Jiro Hitomi, Director General of Administration of the Government General, Mr. T. Ishii, Chief of the Federal Police of the Government General, and of Mr. K. Midzukoshi, Governor of the Province of Taichu, had been presented and accepted.
Inasmuch as each new Governor General has the customary privilege of appointing the heads of executive Departments, it is possible that a number of further resignations may be presented and accepted. The Bureau chiefs who are considered as Taiwan career men will no doubt be exempted from this procedure. Other Department heads, such as the Chief of Finance and others, who are considered as having an expert knowledge of their departments and hence not easily replaced, may also retain their positions.

In view of the fact that the resignation of the Governor General was occasioned by an incident not affecting a change in the Tokyo Central Government, the resignation being presented under the avowed purpose of saving the Government from an embarrassing political situation in the Imperial Diet, it is quite possible that an understanding was or will be reached that a wholesale exodus of Bureau chiefs be not necessary and that only those directly connected with the Musha affair and personally with the outgoing Governor General should be expected to resign from their present positions.

According to the press, Mr. Masahiro Ohta, Governor General of Kwantung Leased Territory, has been appointed Governor General of Taiwan. Mr. Ohta has had a long career in the Government service, chiefly in the police branch, having held the posts of Commissioner of Miye Prefecture, Chief of Police of the Miyazaki, Shimane, and Ehime Governments, Chief Secretary of the Metropolitan Police Board, Chief of the Police Bureau in the Department of Home Affairs, Governor of the Prefectures of Fukushima, Ishikawa, Kumamoto, Niigata, and Aichi, Chief of the Metropolitan Police Board, etc.

Although many strong candidates for the position of Director General were named in the press, notably Mr. Kinoshita the Governor of Nagasaki Prefecture and formerly an official in the Taiwan Government General, it was announced in to-day's press that Mr. Morio Takahashi, the present Governor of Hyogo Prefecture, had been appointed to this position. However, the first intimation this office had of the appointment was a telegram from Mr. Takahashi announcing his appointment and asking the assistance and cooperation of this office in every way possible. This action, of informing foreign consuls,

consuls, is unprecedented and may presage closer and more advantageous relations than in the past. In reply this office telegraphed assurances of cordial cooperation.

As a consequence to these changes, in the internal organization of the Taiwan Government General there will be numerous changes and transfers, all of which will entail some new effort on the part of this office to build up friendly contacts akin to those established with the now out-going executive heads and which have served in facilitating the commercial investigation and research of this consulate.

In the code telegram mention was made of the Lake Candidius Project. As far as can be ascertained from the press, from informal interviews with officials and private persons, there seems to be no likelihood that this change will affect the resumption of the hydro-electric project at that site. There has been no change in the Tokyo Government and hence no far-reaching change in policy, such as might be expected if the Minseito party had gone out of power. It is natural that successive Governor Generals should advocate different policies but under the circumstances it is not to be expected that any obstacle will be cast in the way of resumption of construction, or, in its preliminary phase, the negotiation of a foreign loan.

Politically, the significance of these resignations was pointed out in Despatch No. 58 dated December 9, 1930 (File No. 800) from this office, wherein considerable stress was placed upon the possible political repercussions of the uprising of savages at Musha. It was freely rumored that the Shiyukai party planned an attack on the Minseito government in the impending session of the Imperial Diet. The resignations

Department of State

SIR:

I have the honor to report the appointment of Mr. Kenzo Nakagawa as Governor General of Taiwan on May 27, 1932.

Mr. Hiroshi Minami, former Governor General who served but three months in that capacity, and who happened to be in Tokyo at the time of the assassination of the late Premier Tsuyoshi Inukai, tendered his resignation as Governor General, on May 26, 1932 and accepted an appointment as Minister of Communications in the Saito cabinet.

A short biography of the new Governor General follows:

Mr. Nakagawa was born in Niigata-ken on July 16, 1875, the son of a Mr. Yamomoto, and was adopted in 1893 by a Mrs. Nakagawa, and succeeded to the head of that family in 1904. He graduated from the French law department of the Imperial University at Tokyo in 1902, and immediately entered the civil service. He served as administrative secretary in the Hokkaido government in 1903, and was later appointed successively, Councillor in the Legislative Department, inspector in the Pension bureau, Councillor in the Colonial bureau, chief of the Sendai Communication bureau.

In 1919 he was made a director of the South Manchurian Railway, and was Governor of Kanagawa-ken in 1923, Governor of

of Kumamoto-ken in 1924, Governor of Hokkaido in 1925, resigned in 1927. In July 1929 he was appointed Governor of Tokyo by the Hamaguchi cabinet and was later Vice Minister of Education, resigning in December 1931 at the time of the collapse of the Wakatsuki cabinet.

The new Governor General is not expected to arrive in Taiwan before July of this year, and is believed to have Minseito leanings.

Director General Hiratsuka, who ranks second to the Governor General in the civilian administration of Taiwan, left for Tokyo on May 28. He is a class-mate of Mr. Nakagawa at the Imperial University, and will discuss conditions in Taiwan with the new Governor General. It is problematical whether Mr. Hiratsuka will also resign, although it is understood that the new Governor General has urged him to remain in office.

During the past three years there have been five Governors General of Taiwan, every political change in the Imperial Government has resulted in a like change in Taiwan.

It has been the custom for each Governor General to make his own appointments in the Taiwan Government, and this system has resulted in wholesale changes of department heads and bureau chiefs, reaching in some instances to even minor clerkships, and with resulting uncertainty and inefficiency.

Heretofore the Taiwan public has remained more or less silent on the subject of wholesale changes in the Government General, but in the present instance the people have begun to express their dissatisfaction with the practice which has prevailed in the past.

The Taihoku Business Men's Association was the first of

=== Page 9 === (confidence 1.00)
-3-

of various organizations in Taiwan to protest against the prevailing spoils system. In a meeting held on May 31, it was advocated that the tenure of office of the Governor General be fixed for a certain number of years, either that or a military governor be appointed, or a Governor General appointed from among the princes of the blood.

The Taihoku Provincial Assembly, a body of leading business and professional men, appointed by the Governor of Taihoku province, which acts in an advisory but entirely unofficial capacity also resolved to protest against wholesale changes in the Taiwan administration. Telegrams urging that there be no changes in the Taiwan Government were despatched to Premier Saito and to all state ministers in Tokyo.

The Taihoku Commercial and Industrial Association sent similar telegrams to Tokyo.

The various organizations of Taiwan are unanimous in stating that the too frequent changes in Taiwan are a hindrance to the sound development of Taiwan in commerce and industries, that such changes cause public uneasiness, and that nothing can be accomplished by a Governor General who remains in office for a short time.

A mass meeting of Taihoku citizens is to held June 1, at the Taiwan Railway Hotel, at which a number of lawyers and leading business men will deliver speeches on the subject of political changes in Taiwan.

The protest movement has not been confined to Taihoku; similar meetings are being held in the other cities of the island, and telegrams expressing the opinion of the people, Japanese and leading Formosan Chinese, have been sent to Tokyo.

Taiwan military authorities are informally quoted as stating that they consider too frequent changes of officials as undesirable from the standpoint of Taiwan's progress and general administration; that in Kwantung people wanted a military governor General at the time of anti-Japanese feeling after the assassination of Chang tso-lin; that in Korea a military Governor General is ruling successfully, and although the military authorities desire to reserve comment, such frequent changes are against the best interests of Taiwan.

The inference is plain that it would suit the military to have one of their own men as a military Governor General, and apparently this would also please a great number of Taiwan citizens, who are at last beginning to publicly express themselves at the inefficiency resulting from too frequent changes in the civil administration.

DEPARTMENT OF STATE

## SUBJECT: Proposed trip of Governor General to South Seas area.

## THE HONORABLE
THE SECRETARY OF STATE.
WASHINGTON.

SIR:

I have the honor to report that articles have recently appeared in the local press which infer that Admiral Seizo Kobayashi, Governor General of Taiwan, is contemplating an extensive "good will" tour of the South Seas region, including the Philippine Islands, the Netherlands East Indies, the Straits Settlements, Siam, French Indo-China, and Hong Kong.

The Governor General is reticent concerning his plans at present but has expressed his desire to make such a trip if possible. He states that he can make no definite plans until certain affairs pertaining to the administration of the Island have been settled after the general election in Japan on April 30, 1937, when he intends to visit Tokyo for a short while.

If he makes the tour it is possible that a Japanese naval vessel may be used and that a group of prominent business men of Taiwan may accompany him,
