SUBJECT: Frozen Account of Siamese Firm in Sumitomo Bank, Ltd. in New York.

The charge d'affaires has the honor to report that a letter has been received from the Ministry of Foreign Affaires in Bangkok concerning the dollar account of a local firm which is frozen in New York and requesting information regarding the possibility of having this released.

The local firm is the Fisal Panite Trading Company of Bangkok and it is stated that during 1941 before the outbreak of war in the Pacific they had an account with the branch office of the Sumitomo Bank, Ltd., New York with a credit balance of $6,455 which sum had been remitted from the Banca Commerciale Italiana, Torino, Italy, by order of the Bank of Asia for Industry and Commerce, Bangkok, Siam.

Judging by the name the New York bank presumably was Japanese and no further information regarding details of the account have been furnished but the Legation will appreciate a report on the status of the matter which can be furnished to the Ministry of Foreign Affairs on behalf of the local firm.

January 21, 1947

Mr. Walter S. Surrey, Chief
Division of Economic Security Controls
Department of State
Washington 25, D. C.

Re: The Yokohama Specie Bank, Ltd. (New York)
Vesting Order Number 915

Dear Mr. Surrey:

It will be appreciated if a request can be made to the appropriate diplomatic or consular officials for an investigation in China to obtain information needed in connection with the liquidation of the above-named bank. This liquidation is being conducted under the supervision of the Office of Alien Property by the Superintendent of Banks of the State of New York.

The information is desired to assist the Superintendent of Banks in meeting a claim for the recovery of $60,387.73 from the New York agency of The Yokohama Specie Bank, Ltd. This action is referred to as Neil C. Potter v. The Yokohama Specie Bank, Ltd.

The Yokohama Specie Bank, Ltd., prior to the war, was licensed by the Superintendent of Banks to maintain an agency in the City of New York for the purpose of doing a limited banking business. Upon the outbreak of the war on December 8, 1941, the Superintendent of Banks took possession of the business and property in New York of The Yokohama Specie Bank, Ltd. for the purpose of liquidation under the provisions of the Banking Law of the State of New York. Under Section 606(4) of the Banking Law, only certain creditors of Yokohama Specie Bank, Ltd. are permitted to share in this liquidation. On February 26, 1943, the Alien Property Custodian, by Vesting Order No. 915, vested the excess proceeds which may remain after liquidation.

In the event that the plaintiff in this action is not entitled to prove his claim and the action is dismissed, whatever surplus the Alien Property Custodian vested on behalf of the Government would be correspondingly increased. It is therefore in the interest of the Federal Government, as well as the Superintendent of Banks as liquidator, that the desired information be obtained from China.

The following is a summary of the circumstances which gave rise to the action by Neil C. Potter referred to above:

1. On July 18, 1941, a cable was received by the New York Agency of Yokohama Specie Bank from the Shanghai office of Yokohama Specie Bank advising that Hua Hsing Commercial Bank of Shanghai had opened a confirmed and irrevocable letter of Credit B#14, in favor of Neil C. Potter, the seller, for the account of a concern known as Maruzen Sekiyu Kabushiki Kaisha, the purchaser, of #16 The Bund, Shanghai. This letter of credit was to the extent of $221,000.00, covering 10,000 barrels of lubricating oil to be sent from New Orleans to Shanghai, and the letter of credit expired on August 15, 1941. Upon receipt of the cable, the New York Agency confirmed the letter of credit to Neil C. Potter. Under the confirmation of the letter of credit, Potter presented on July 25, 1941 Bills of Lading and received payment of $15,996.10. On the same date, the New York Agency, in connection with that payment, sent a letter to Yokohama Specie Bank, Ltd., Shanghai, a copy of which is enclosed herewith. Thereafter, the State Department refused to permit shipment of oil from New Orleans to Shanghai consigned to the purchaser.

2. Potter claims that by cable communication with the purchaser in Shanghai, he arranged for modification of the original agreement. As to this alleged modification and transactions thereunder, the Superintendent has not been able to obtain any information whatsoever. Under this modification, Potter claims he agreed with the purchaser as follows:

(a) The letter of credit should be extended to November 15, 1941.

(b) Potter was permitted to ship the oil to the purchaser at Manila instead of Shanghai.

(c) Potter would be entitled to payment upon forwarding the bills of lading directly to the purchaser in Shanghai instead of surrendering the bills of lading to the New York Agency of Yokohama Specie Bank.

3. Potter claims that under the alleged modification, the purchaser received shipment of 2,376 barrels of oil subsequent to September 26, 1941 at Manila, from the S/S Zoella Lykes, but that he has never received payment for these 2,376 barrels totalling $60,387.73 either from the purchaser in Japan or from any other source.

=== Page 6 === (confidence 1.00)
- 3 -

In order that the Superintendent of Banks may be properly prepared to meet this claim, it is important that the following facts be obtained:

(a) What was the exact transaction between Hua Hsing Commercial Bank and Yokohama Specie Bank, Ltd. in Shanghai, under which Hua Hsing requested Yokohama Specie Bank to confirm and make irrevocable in New York the letter of credit which Hua Hsing issued in favor of Potter? In this connection, did the purchaser, Maruzen Sekiyu Kabushiki Kaisha, deposit any security or "cover" with Hua Hsing for the issuance of the letter of credit? If so, what was the nature of the security or "cover" and what disposition has been made of it? Likewise, did Hua Hsing deposit any security or "cover" with the Shanghai office of Yokohama Specie Bank for the confirmation of the letter of credit by Yokohama Specie Bank in New York? If so, what was the nature of the security or "cover" with Yokohama Specie Bank and what disposition was made of it?

(b) What were the exact terms of the modification which Potter claims he effected with the purchaser as set forth in the paragraph numbered 2 above, under which, instead of shipping the oil to Shanghai, it was shipped to Manila and instead of presenting the bills of lading to the New York Agency of Yokohama Specie Bank, he forwarded the bills of lading directly to the purchaser? In connection with this alleged modification, what arrangements did Potter make with the purchaser for payment of $60,387.73 when the purchaser received the bills of lading and the oil at Manila? It does not seem likely that Potter would voluntarily surrender the bills of lading to the purchaser at Shanghai without in some way arranging for payment to himself. It is quite conceivable that Potter may have already been paid for this $60,387.73 through some credit set up in his favor either in Shanghai or Manila.

(c) Did the Hua Hsing Commercial Bank or did Yokohama Specie Bank, Ltd., Shanghai, participate in any respect in making the alleged modification and if so, what was the actual extent of their participation?

On October 2, 1946 the Office of the Superintendent of Banks addressed inquiries on this matter to Hua Hsing Commercial Bank in Shanghai and the representative of The Yokohama Specie Bank, Ltd. in Shanghai, but to date has received no reply. A request for information was also made to the representative of The Yokohama Specie Bank, Ltd. in Manila, the response being that the bulk of the records of that bank have been destroyed.

This Office will greatly appreciate the assistance of the State Department in obtaining the desired information.

Very truly yours,

The Secretary of State requests the assistance of the Office of the Political Adviser in determining whether the records of the Sumitomo Bank, Osaka, Japan indicate credits in the amount of $6,455.00 in favor of a Siamese firm named Pisal Panite Trading Company. The Royal Siamese Embassy at Washington, which has solicited the aid of the Department in tracing the account mentioned, states that the credits originated with the Banca Commerciale Italiana, Torino, Italy, sometime before the United States entered the war against Japan. These credits were reported to have been transferred to the New York branch of the Sumitomo Bank instead of direct to Japan because of foreign exchange restrictions. However, there appears to be considerable doubt about the latter transfer, since the Liquidation Bureau, Foreign Department, State of New York Banking Department has been cited by agents of the Pisal Panite Trading Company as stating that,

"...the record disclosed that the sum in question represented the unutilized portion of a credit established on behalf of Pisal Panite Trading Company which was transmitted on February 27, 1941 by the Torino Office of the Banca Commerciale Italiana to the Osaka Office of the Sumitomo Bank via the New York office of the respective banks; that the amount in question was actually received by the Sumitomo Bank, New York, from the Banca Commerciale Italiana, New York, and credited by it to the Osaka Office of the Sumitomo Bank; that thereafter Sumitomo Bank, Osaka, requested Sumitomo Bank, New York, to repay the amount to the Torino office of the Banca Commerciale Italiana but Torino refused to accept payment; that as matter stood the records of Sumitomo Bank, New York, did not reflect any amount due and owing to Pisal Panite Trading Company; and that the fund "received from Banca Commerciale Italiana had been transferred by cable to Osaka and so it would appear that Pisal Panite Trading Company has a claim against Sumitomo Bank, Osaka".

My dear Mr. Moffat,

A matter has been brought to my attention on which, I am afraid, I have to solicit your kind assistance for clarification of certain points. It concerns the claim of a Siamese firm, named Royal Panite Trading Co., against the Sumitomo Bank, Ltd., New York, for the sum of $6,455.00. The facts of the case appear to be as follows:

The above-mentioned Siamese firm had at one time opened credits with the Banca Commerciale Italiana, Torino, Italy. In the year 1941 a credit balance of $6,455 was left, which amount the firm instructed the Banca Commerciale Italiana to transfer to the Sumitomo Bank in Japan. Owing possibly to war time restrictions, instead of transferring the amount mentioned direct to the Sumitomo Bank in Japan, the Banca Commerciale Italiana transmitted it to the New York branch of the Sumitomo Bank. Though at that time the war in Asia had not yet started, it happened that your Government had amended the regulation relating to transaction in foreign exchange and foreign owned property...

The Ministry of Foreign Affairs presents its compliments to the American Embassy and has the honor to refer to the Embassy's third person note of March 24, 1947, in which it was requested that the appropriate authorities be asked in turn to investigate the circumstances under which the Hua Hsing Commercial Bank at Shanghai, opened a letter of credit which was confirmed and made irrevocable by the Yokohama Specie Bank for the Maruzen Sekiyu Kabushiki, Shanghai, for a shipment of lubricating oil from Mr. Neil C. Potter of the United States between July and September of 1941.

The Embassy is informed that the matter was referred to the Ministry of Finance for investigation and reply and that a response has been received as follows:

Upon instructions, the Shanghai Office of the Bank of China reported that no entry with respect to this case appears on the table showing guaranteed liabilities upon the taking over by that Bank of the Shanghai office of the Yokohama Specie Bank. Further, according to a reply received from the Central Bank of China's Office for the Liquidation of the Hua Hsing Bank, the old account books of the Hua Hsing Bank have not yet been checked and handed over, and hence the case in question cannot be verified. However, at the time of the taking over (of the bank), the office in question inquired of the staff members of this puppet bank concerning its affairs in regard to international remittances. It was stated that, with the exception of Japan, all the international remittances and letters of credit of the Hua Hsing Bank were handled through the Yokohama Specie Bank, and that the puppet bank had no direct dealings with its foreign (banking) colleagues. Again, there is no record of this letter of credit in the books of the said puppet bank at the time of its closing, which is sufficient proof that the payment may long since have been computed and settled.

If the American citizen in question wishes to pursue his claim, based on this letter of credit, it would seem that he might negotiate direct with the Yokohama Specie Bank for the settlement thereof.

(Stamped) MINISTRY OF FOREIGN AFFAIRS
OF THE REPUBLIC OF CHINA
