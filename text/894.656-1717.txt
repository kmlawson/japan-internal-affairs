SUBJECT: Report on Wood Pulp and Paper Production in Japan during February and March 1947.

The United States Political Adviser has the honor to refer to this Mission's despatch No. 971, dated April 14, 1947, subject: "Reports on Wood Pulp and Paper, Requested by the Office of International Trade, United States Department of Commerce".

Of the information concerning wood pulp and paper production in Japan during the first quarter of 1947 requested by the Office of International Trade of the Department of Commerce, only a part is contained in the despatch under reference. An additional report has now been received, together with a table of statistics, from the Natural Resources Section of General Headquarters, Supreme Commander for the Allied Powers. The report, in the form of a memorandum for the record, dated April 18, 1947, subject: "Japan's Pulp and Paper Production, February and March 1947", and the table of statistics are given below:

"1. Paper production in March made a new high since the occupation. The increase was 16 percent above the February production. Chief improvement was in newsprint, printing, and bag papers, the three categories which the Pulp and Paper Branch, Natural Resources Section, has been emphasizing as the most essential to the Japanese economy.

"2. Pulp production also showed a marked increase over February figures by increasing 35.5 percent. Here again the categories stressed by the Pulp and Paper Branch, Natural Resources Section, showed remarkable percentage increases. Rayon B pulp, which is for viscose rayon fibre production, shows almost a ten fold rise. Unbleached kraft, which is for critical cement, food, and fertilizer bag production, almost tripled the February figure. Groundwood and unbleached sulphite, which are the basis of newsprint and printing papers, also showed creditable gains.

"3. These improvements will be maintained and improved. Without the cooperation of the Japanese Pulp and Paper groups, these benefits could not have been realized. The policy of cooperation and mutual understanding between the Japanese and Pulp and Paper Branch, Natural Resources Section, and Economic and Scientific Section, will be continued. Duress is not the answer for industrial recovery."

GENERAL HEADQUARTERS
SUPREME COMMANDER FOR THE ALLIED POWERS
Natural Resources Section

17 May 1947

MEMORANDUM FOR: Record

SUBJECT: Japan's Pulp and Paper Production, March and April, 1947

1. Adjustment in the reporting of all pulp and paper mill production to the 20th of each month was ordered by the Economic Stabilization Board. Because Oji Paper Manufacturing Co, who produce over 80 percent of the Japan tonnage, had been reporting as of the first of the month, the production figures for April appear lower in the following tables. Newsprint and printing papers, as well as groundwood and unbleached sulfite, are essentially of Oji Paper Manufacturing Co. origin. Consequently they show a substantial reduction in apparent production, although the drop is not actual.

2. The indications appear plausible that newsprint production was reduced in order to meet the serious shortage of other printing papers. Assuming that the 26 day figure of newsprint, 4,899 tons, is projected to a 26 day actual production then a production of 5,330 tons of newsprint is probable and compares with 8,878 tons of newsprint in March. Printing papers on the other hand show by the same correction a tonnage of 6,110 compared to 4,722 in March.

3. Increased production of rayon pulp from 2,125 to 3,132 in April is as projected. Also substantial increase of unbleached kraft from 737 to 1,271 is important. These two items are not affected by the change of reporting dates as these producers have always closed their books on the 26th of the month. Oji Paper Manufacturing Co. does not produce either rayon or kraft pulps.

/s/ Harold R. Murdock

HAROLD R. MURDOCK
Head, Pulp &amp; Paper Branch

21 June 1947

MEMORANDUM FOR: Record

SUBJECT: Japan's Pulp and Paper Production, April and May 1947

1. Production of pulp and paper for May is shown in the accompanying table in comparison to April.

2. A serious drop in rayon pulp tonnage occurred in May. The Japanese state that shortages of coal, bleaching powder and electric power seriously hindered operation. However, the definite increase in unbleached sulfite production does not completely support the argument of coal and power shortages. Printing paper increased tonnage suggests an effort on the part of the Japanese industry to increase printing paper at the expense of rayon pulp production.

UNITED STATES POLITICAL ADVISER FOR JAPAN

UNCLASSIFIED Tokyo, February 27, 1948.

Subject: Survey of Newsprint Production, Exports and Imports.

The Acting Political Adviser has the honor to refer to the Department's circular instruction of January 20, 1948 regarding the newsprint industry in Japan and to enclose copy of chart entitled "World Distribution of Newsprint Paper--Calendar Year 1947" upon which the statistical information requested concerning Japan's newsprint industry for the calendar year 1947 has been entered.

This Mission has been informed by the Economic and Scientific Section of this Headquarters that the capacity figure of 175,000 short tons represents effective capacity assuming adequate raw material and fuel supplies. Furthermore, it is estimated that if needed operating repairs are made and some out-of-commission mills rehabilitated installed capacity might reach 217,728 short tons. In this connection, it is anticipated that such a rehabilitation program would require at least one year for completion.

In view of current allocations of fuel and wood for pulp, the above-named Section estimates that the newsprint production for the first quarter of 1948 will be about 75 per cent of effective capacity.
