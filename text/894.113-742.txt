The last paragraph of Article V of the Treaty of Com-

UNITED STATES' EXPORTS OF WAR MATERIALS TO JAPAN AND CHINA
Official figures compiled from customs statistics of the Department
of Commerce show that exports from the United States to Japan of firearms,
ammunition, aircraft and parts, and other war materials totaled $147,212
for the year 1951 and $58,756 in the first two months of 1932, compared
with similar materials to the value of $1,115,797 exported to China in
1951 and $65,643 in the first two months of 1932. Of these totals, aircraft
engines and parts to Japan accounted for $135,111 and $57,066 respectively; and
to China $913,173 and $24,055. respectively. The remaining items making up
the total include revolvers, pistols, shotguns, and shot shells, used chiefly
for sporting purposes; so that the total shipments to either or both countries
of materials suitable for war purposes was extremely insignificant.
The United States shipped to Japan in 1931, 912,253,718 pounds of Upland
raw cotton valued at $79,586,661, and 365,655,262 pounds valued at $27,133,879
in the first two months of 1932, the season of heaviest shipments. Exports
of raw cotton to China totaled 452,151,698 pounds, valued at $37,703,127 in
1951, and 104,874,506 pounds valued at $7,593,195 in the first two months of
1952. This cotton is used in the cotton textile industries of Japan and
China, and the unusually large amounts purchased may be ascribed to the extra-
ordinarily low price of American cotton.
Exports from the United States to Japan of cotton linters, which may
be used in the manufacture of explosives, totaled 3,084,336 pounds, valued
at $102,986 for the year 1931, and 1,015,720 pounds valued at $26,604 in the
first two months of 1932.
