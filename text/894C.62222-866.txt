SUBJECT: Affort to Increase Wool Yield of Manchuria by Importation of American Sheep for Cross-breeding.

TO: HONORABLE
THE SECRETARY OF STATE,
WASHINGTON.

RECEIVED
DEPARTMENT OF STATE
893 (illegible) 11 1935
DIVISION OF COMMUNICATIONS AND RECORDS

As of interest to the Department of Agriculture, I have the honor to report that as a result of experiments conducted by the South Manchuria Railway Agricultural Experimental Station at Kungchuling, and of the recent visit to the United States of two officials of the "Manchukuo" Mongolian Affairs Department, a flock of 775 Merino sheep from Utah, Wyoming, and Nevada was unloaded at Dairen yesterday. Of the above number, which includes 723 rams, 550 were consigned to the Department of Industry of "Manchukuo".

The importation of the sheep in question was undertaken in order to improve the yield of wool in Manchuria. According to a recent press estimate, there are approximately four million head of native sheep in Manchuria, which are kept principally for their meat and consequently give wool of an indifferent quality. The yield of wool per head is stated to be about 2 1/2 pounds, giving a total annual wool production in Manchuria of nine million pounds. Since the South Manchuria Railway experiments have produced cross

November 6, 1934.

TOKYO'S despatch No. 1014, dated October 18, 1934:

The Export Petroleum Company of California, Ltd., has addressed a letter to the Manchuria Oil Company, care of American Consul at Dairen, containing a sales proposition covering crude oil for refining purposes. In view of the unusual oil trade situation developing in Manchuria the Consul did not see fit to forward the letter to the addressee, a judgment in which he was confirmed by Ambassador Grew while visiting Dairen. The letter was sent to the Embassy at Tokyo, and is enclosed with the attached despatch from the Embassy.

The Embassy states that as no intimation has been received from the Department in regard to the desirability of selling American crude oil to the Manchuria Oil Company for refining purposes, it is not in position to determine whether the American company's letter should be forwarded.

It is apparently the Consul's idea that the letter might properly be returned to the addressor with an explanation of the situation which
