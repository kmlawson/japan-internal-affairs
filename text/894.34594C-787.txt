I have the honor to refer the Embassy to the recent establishment of a Japanese naval station at Port Arthur and to enclose herewith a translation from the Kwantung Government Official Gazette No. 890, dated April 28, 1933, of Navy Department Ordinance No. 2 of April 20, 1933, giving the revised port regulations for Port Arthur. PORT ARTHUR REGULATIONS

Naval Department Ordinance No. 2, April 20, 1933

The Port Arthur Regulations are revised as follows:

PORT ARTHUR REGULATIONS

Art. 1. The water area of the port of Port Arthur is divided into three areas. In the accompanying map, that portion of the water area comprised within the limits of the single dotted line is the First Area, that portion outside the First Area and comprised within the limits of the double dotted line is the Second Area, and the entire remaining portion which is not included in the First and Second Areas is the Third Area. The Western Harbor is situated within the limits of the Third Area.

Art. 2. War and commercial vessels, both Japanese and foreign, have free access to the Western Harbor (Third Area).

Within the limits of the Third Area outside the Western Harbor, all vessels are allowed to lie at anchor so long as they do not obstruct the fairway. But with respect to vessels carrying explosives or any other highly inflammable materials, the Director of the Port Office shall give special direction for their anchorage.

Art. 3. Unless belonging to the Imperial Japanese Navy, no vessels are allowed to enter the First and Second Areas without special permission from the Commander-in-Chief of the Port Arthur Naval Station; but vessels coming out of the Third Area and passing through the Second, and then again entering the Third Area are excepted.

Art. 9. Art. 20. The use of wireless telegraph or wireless telephone is not allowed within the limits of Port Arthur without permission from the Commander-in-Chief of the Port Arthur Naval Station. But communication by vessels under weigh, signal of distress, and military communication are excepted.

Art. 21. The preceding prohibition is not applicable to the undertaking of these practices by the military authorities outside the land and water area reserved for the use of the Navy.

Art. 22. In case the Commander-in-Chief of the Port Arthur Naval Station suspects any one of making an observation of the state of military defence or of studying the topography of any place within the limits of Port Arthur, he has power to cause such person to leave the area of Port Arthur.

Art. 23. Matters relative to sanitation within the limits of Port Arthur will be carried out in conference between the Director of the Port Office and the Governor of Kwantung.

Art. 24. When he deems it necessary for the maintenance of order on a public road adjacent to the naval reserve land, the Commander-in-Chief of the Port Arthur Naval Station, in consultation with the Governor of Kwantung has the power to prohibit the use of any such thoroughfare by the public.

The Commander-in-Chief of the Port Arthur Naval Station has power to admit the public to any thoroughfare in such portion of the naval reserve land as will not cause inconvenience to the control of the area.

Art. 25. It is prohibited to destroy or alter the location of stones, poles, plates, etc., indicating the area or demarcations of Port Arthur. The destruction or altering of the location of buoys or the like, set up within the limits of the water area of the port is prohibited.

Art. 26. The detailed regulations pertaining to the maintenance of order in Port Arthur are provided by the Commander-in-Chief of the Port Arthur Naval Station in consultation with the Governor of Kwantung.

The present regulations become effective from the date of issue.

# PORT ARTHUR'S REVIVAL

Several purposes underlie the restoration of the Port Arthur naval base by the Japanese Government. Defence of Manchoukue, more efficient patrol of North China waters, greater efficiency in repairs, readior concentration, protection of fishery rights in the north are some of them.

After the Russo-Japanese war the former Russian naval base was developed by Japan into an adequately fortified haven and outfitting point for war shipping. As dependence upon the base dwindled, its admiralty status was reduced and Port Arthur became an ordinary naval base in 1914. Further reduction in rank followed in 1922, and in 1925 Port Arthur was written off the list of bases.

Now there is to be a modest revival to naval base status in view of the more extensive services to be rendered to Manchoukue. It will be recalled that on March 29 the Tokyo Government announced that a Navy office would be opened in Hsinking. One of the functions was the defence of territorial waters of Manchoukue. Obviously an office alone could not do much in the way of defence. The provision of facilities followed, and the revival of Port Arthur was a logical development. Naturally Port Arthur had been maintained to a high standard of readiness for such work, even while holding no naval rank.

The revived Depot includes four departments as follows: Harbor, Wireless, Hospital and Works. The Works Department is to be established some time in the future.

The service system of the Depot is the same as others in Japan. The Wireless Department takes over the existing naval wireless station with added equipment for a wider range of communications. The hospital attached to the Hospital Department will be enlarged to include increased accommodation.

The Works Department will recover the existing docks rented to the Manchuria Dock Co. and amalgamated by the D. K. K. to undertake repairs and the building of warcraft.

Port Arthur Depot is different from depots in Japan in the absence of marine patrols.

The Second China Squadron under Rear-Admiral Shimuye Tsuda, comprising the Flagship Hirate, Tokiwa, Shin-i, the 15th and 16th destroyers' flotilla, is to be attached to the Depot. Accordingly, the Squadron will be dissolved.

Although no announcement is to be about the personnel for the Depot, Admiral Tsuda will be appointed Commander of the Depot with Captain Haharu Kubota, Naval Resident Officer at Port Arthur, a Chief of Staff.

Right after the outbreak of the Manchurian Issue, the Headquarters of the Kwantung Air was removed to Mukden and then to Halking. The revival of the Depot is expected to restore the town to its former liveliness.

An official evaluation of the Port Arthur revival is given by a short man of the Navy Office as follows:

"The duty of protecting Japanese residents along the coast of Manchuria and that of North China, and also that of protecting the commercial and fishing rights of Japan through the Submarines Squadron. With the expansion and increased in the past few years, the number of Japanese warships in of various sizes. The authorities at a result have faced the problems of speeding up repairs to those ships and economizing in the supply of material for them, and they have come to the conclusion that the best solution is establishment of a naval base in that area. A nearby base for these warships is indispensable if they are to perform their allotted task with facility.

"With the rise of Manchoukuo, the importance of Port Arthur and Dairen as the door to Manchuria, has increased enormously, especially when trade between Japan and Manchuria is taken into consideration. In view of this it is a matter for congratulation that the new base has been established at Port Arthur for the purpose of safeguarding peace and order.

"The task of guarding the coast of the Kwantung Leased Territory, heretofore undertaken by the 2nd Overseas Squadron, will now be taken over by the Port Arthur Naval Base. This means a step forward insuring peace and order in the Far East."

The rehabilitation move naturally has received the attention of the Foreign Powers, and news reports tell of a move by Britain to direct the attention of the signatories of the Nine Power Treaty to the move. There is no offensive character to the Port Arthur development. It can constitute no threat that did not exist a month ago. For Port Arthur always was available if needed. Now it is being used in a small way for local defense.
