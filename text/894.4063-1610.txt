January 6, 1948.
THE Honorable George C. Marshall
Secretary of State
Washington, D. C.
Dear Sir:
An inquiry has come to the National Y.M.C.A. Physical Education Committee, from a member of the Military Government in Japan who is related to Youth Programs, relative to participation of Japanese athletes in the 1948 Olympic Games scheduled for London. This inquiry pointed out the desirability of Japanese participation as one means of helping to bring about better international understanding on the part of the young people of Japan and the young people of other nations.
We have discussed this matter at a meeting of our Executive group and, while recognizing that the United States Olympic Committee would be the most directly related to this problem, there is also the matter of the attitude and policy of the United States State Department that should be known in considerations of this kind.
It is quite possible that if this matter is brought to the attention of our Committee, the members would be inclined to favor such participation if it could be properly worked out but before our Committee could examine this problem they would like to have some word from your Department apprising us of the conditions relating to such participation on the part of the Japanese. Any endorsement from our Committee would of necessity be influenced by a statement from your Department and, of course, would be handled through the regular Olympic channels.
A meeting of our full committee is scheduled for the middle of this month, January 16th. If a reply from you could reach us before that date we would appreciate it.
Very truly yours,
H. T. Friermood, Senior Secretary,
