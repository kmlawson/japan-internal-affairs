TREATY DIVISION
DEPARTMENT OF STATE MAY 12 1943
SPECIAL DIVISION DEPARTMENT OF STATE
April 28, 1943

Reference is made to the underlying communication to the State Land Board, Salem, Oregon, in which it is stated in the penultimate paragraph that "The Department is not in a position to inform you at this time in regard to specific Japanese laws governing the rights of aliens to own real property in Japan."

As the Swiss Legation in Tokyo reported in Bern's 3204 of July 9 that the juridical position of American citizens as regards the inheritance of real and personal property has undergone no modification since the declaration of war, it would appear that the Japanese alien land law of 1926 is still in force. As the files of the Department will undoubtedly reveal, Imperial Ordinance no. 334 of November 3, 1926 provided in part that all aliens in Japan could enjoy ownership of land except in certain designated areas necessary for national defense. This law was, it is was believed, forwarded to the Department as an enclosure to the Embassy's Despatch no. 320 of November 4, 1926. (See attached)

In the circumstances if there is any question as to whether the alien land law of 1926 is still in force, it might be advisable to request the Legation at Bern to have the Swiss Legation at Tokyo confirm that foreigners may enjoy the ownership of real property in Japan except in certain areas designated by the Japanese as "fortified zones."
