SECTION K - BANK LOANS

At the end of 1930, the last year for which Government statistics are available, the Commercial Attache reports a total of 9,458,000,000 yen in bank loans outstanding. These are long term and not commercial loans. This total has been fairly constant since 1927, when it stood at 9,600,000,000 yen indicating very little liquidation.

Land mortgage loans amount to 2,870,000,000. Conditions in agricultural regions are bad and urban (?) real estate projects are not profitable. Many competent observers are of the opinion that the present worth of the security for these loans is not more than one-third of the value at the time these loans were made. If liquidation were attempted now many banks would fail.

Industrial loans amount to 527,000,000 yen and show a steady increase over several years. Well informed opinion infers a large part of this money cannot be liquidated and will have to be written off. Commodity

Commodity loans stood at 218,000,000 yen indicating a considerable reduction since 1927. Commercial Attache feels that the reduction shown is more apparent than real as the silk loans for example have a government guarantee of over 29,000,000. This amount and possibly other loans, may have been taken over by Bank of Japan, or security other than commodities put up as collateral.

Security loans are given as 2,268,000,000 yen, a decrease of over 400,000,000 since 1927. Security prices have fallen and it is hard to estimate the value of these loans.

Loans other than those listed above amounted 3,575,000,000 yen, an increase of nearly 200,000,000 since 1927. The type of security for these loans is not given, but it includes probably promissory notes, debentures and frozen assets of different kinds.

Besides loans listed above, Bank of Japan is carrying some 575,000,000 yen advanced to banks in 1927. Indications are this will not be liquidated for many years.

GOVERNMENT FINANCE:

The government revenue is running behind. It is estimated that a bond issue of between four and five hundred million yen will be put out this spring. The extra cost of military activities in China is estimated as about 137,000,000 up to May. It is anticipated that no effort will be made to borrow money abroad.

HPD - WSB
NEVILLE
