April 12, 1934.

Mr. Green and Mr. Yanka, representing the Westinghouse Electric and Manufacturing Company. Possible purchase by Japanese Government of beacon transmitters.

Mr. Yanka called by appointment at my office this morning. He had already discussed the business which brought him to the Department with Mr. Dooman of FE who had arranged to have him see me.

Mr. Yanka stated that the Westinghouse Company was informed that sometime ago a Japanese Naval Mission had applied to the Department of Commerce for permission to inspect the beacon transmitter at the landing field in Elizabeth, New Jersey, with a view to the possible purchase of similar beacon transmitters for the Japanese Government, and that Mr. Jackson of the Airways Division of the Department of Commerce had referred them to the Department. Mr. Yanka wished to know whether the Japanese Naval Mission had approached the Department in regard to this matter and, if so, what reply had been given.

I stated that I had never heard of the matter and could not understand how permission to visit a landing field

=== Page 4 === (confidence 0.99)
-2-

field in Elizabeth, New Jersey, could be considered the proper business of this Department. I called up Mr. Murphy of A-C/C, who told me that he had never heard of the matter. I then called up Mr. Jackson of the Airways Division, but he was not in his office at that time.

Mr. Yanka asked whether, if the Japanese Naval Mission were permitted to inspect the beacon transmitter and should desire to purchase similar beacon transmitters, this Department would have any objection to the sale. I replied that there were no treaties or statutes which restricted the sale of such articles to Japan, provided that no military secrets in the custody of the War or Navy Departments were involved. He assured me that there was no possible question of a military secret, as the beacon transmitter had been fully described in a radio magazine, and as the Bureau of Standards had released the specifications in an official publication.

After Mr. Yanka's departure, Mr. Jackson of the Airways Division called me by telephone. I asked him what he knew about the case, and specifically whether he had seen members of the Japanese Naval Mission and whether he had referred them to this Department in connection with their desire to inspect the beacon transmitter at

at Elizabeth. He remembered the request of the Japanese Naval Mission to be permitted to inspect the beacon transmitter, but his recollection was hazy and entirely lacking in detail. He said that the case was actually handled by someone else in his Department, whom he had advised to have the request transferred to this Department.

Later in the afternoon, Mr. Yanka called me by telephone to state that he had seen Mr. Jackson and had now ascertained that the Japanese Naval Mission had already inspected the beacon transmitter at Elizabeth. He repeated his question as to whether this Department would have any objection to the sale of this instrument to the Japanese Government and I repeated the reply set forth above.

Joseph C. Green.

JAPAN (Military).
Subject: Japanese Admiral Inspecting Kurile Islands.
Division of FAR EASTERN AFFAIRS SEP 29 1934 Department of State
794.348

The following appears in the "Daily Herald" of September 5, 1933: -

"Admiral Shiozawa, head of the Japanese naval air arm, is visiting the Kurile Islands, in the North Pacific, with the object of inspecting possible sites for air bases, according to sections of the Press in Tokyo.

"The object is to counteract the American plans for similar bases in the Aleutian Islands of Alaska (says the Exchange).

"The "Daily Herald" revealed exclusively in July that the Japanese Government had decided to spend $2,000,000 on a so-called "fishing base" in the Kurile Islands.

"This base is to be equipped with radio station, hospital and other facilities which may well serve in the event of war."

From: M.A. London. Report No. 36306. Date: September 6, 1934.
