SIR:

I have the honor to report that in a personal interview with the Chief of the local Criminal Investigation Department, I was informed that there was a considerable espionage activity in British East Africa, it being conducted chiefly by the Japanese, the Italians and the Germans.

Japanese. About three years ago, about twenty Japanese, holding themselves out as deep-sea fishermen, came to Mombasa, purchased a number of downs (local deep-sea sailing vessels) and apparently carried on the usual activities of deep-sea fishermen. Actually, they were, according to the Criminal Investigation Department, Japanese naval officers making soundings, preparing maps and plans and familiarizing themselves generally with the British East African coast. As soon as the present Sino-Japanese difficulty broke out, they all

all returned to Japan.

A few weeks ago, a group of twelve young Japanese, apparently students off on a tour, visited Mombasa, Dar-es-Salaam, Nairobi and other centers, and made an extensive tour through Kenya, Uganda and Tanganyika. The Chief of the Criminal Investigation Department stated that he was convinced that the object of their trip was primarily espionage. He based his opinion on the number and subjects of the photographs taken and the places and buildings that interested them. Many photographs of railway stations and junctions were made.

**Italian.** The Italian Government has just assigned a new Consul General to Nairobi, Commendatore Italo Zappoli. Although a Consul General, Mr. Zappoli has only been in the consular service for seven years, but I understand that he ranks very high in Fascist circles at home. I know Mr. Zappoli very well, play tennis with him occasionally, and am one of his few acquaintances, since he speaks almost no English. We speak only Italian together. Naturally, he does not discuss his activities here with me, but I heard that he had circularized all the Italians in the district, criticizing the British Government, its colonial policy and its local officials. The Chief of the Criminal Investigation Department, however, told me that, as far as he knew, the only circular sent out by Zappoli was a schedule of the Italian propaganda radio broadcasts.

Mr. Zappoli's predecessor, according to my informant

formant, financed at least one Italian engaged in espionage work on the Northern Frontier, but this gentleman's work was so barefaced that he was asked to leave the country. **German.** Although I have yet to visit Tanganyika, those with whom I have talked, tell me that it is now a German Colony in every way, except for the British Administration. Many British planters have, during the last few years, sold their plantations to Germans and cleared out. This probably rejects a lack of confidence in the future British status of the Territory.

In discussing the question of the return of Tanganyika to Germany recently with a local official, he gave it as his personal and unofficial opinion that part of the territory would be returned to Germany, but that Britain would retain a corridor through it connecting Kenya on the north with Northern Rhodesia and Nyasaland on the south. Thus would the "Cape to Cairo" British Territory be maintained.

It is generally recognized that the German community has perfected plans to take over Tanganyika at a moment's notice. Some of the details of the plan, as rumored, challenge the imagination. For instance, I was told that each German in Tanganyika had

had been assigned particular British subjects to murder upon the outbreak of hostilities! There is no question, however, that a well planned skeleton organization has been prepared to run the colony when and if "der Tag" comes again.

Furthermore, local officials make no pretence as to the ability of their military forces to hold either Kenya or Tanganyika, if a serious attempt were made to take either. The local force of the Royal Air Force is only eight old bombers. The King's African Rifles is only a border and native reserve patrol and there is no other defence. I am preparing a fuller report on this phase of the subject now.

Respectfully yours,
