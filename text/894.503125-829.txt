Mr. Hamilton:

I see no objection to furnishing the Uruguayan Chargé in Tokyo any information Commerce may have for publication and which would be furnished to anyone asking for it. If there is some specific information of a confidential nature which the Chargé wants, I think the request should come from his Government through our Legation in Montevideo, or through the Uruguayan Legation in Washington. I do not think, however, that I would have the Embassy in Tokyo intimate to the Chargé that there would be information of a confidential nature available, if any.

J. F. McGurk.

DEPARTMENT OF STATE
DIVISION OF FAR EASTERN AFFAIRS
September 10, 1934.

MMH:
SKH:

Tokyo's despatch No. 910 of August 2, 1934, reports that according to the Uruguayan Chargé d'Affaires in Tokyo a group of Japanese manufacturers presented to him a proposal that permission be granted for the establishment of factories in a free port zone recently inaugurated in Uruguay. These factories would engage in the manufacture of porcelains; toys; and cotton, wool, and silk textiles. The Chargé referred the proposal to his Government with the recommendation that the proposal be rejected.

The Chargé d'Affaires requested that our Embassy supply him with information in regard to the growth of Japanese exports to the United States in order that the information might be used in a further report by the Chargé d'Affaires to his Government in support of his recommendation that the proposal be rejected. The Embassy suggested to the Chargé d'Affaires that "it would be better if the matter were referred to the Uruguayan Legation in Washington for study". The Chargé d'Affaires argued, however, that the information would be given by his Government more weight if it came from him than from the Uruguayan Legation in Washington. The Embassy requests that if the Department thinks that the Embassy can, without impropriety, furnish the Chargé with the information

I suggest that this despatch be referred to A-S for study. It is my tentative opinion that there will be no objection to furnishing the Uruguayan Government, whether through its Legation at Washington or through the Legation at Tokyo, such information as is made available to the public. The statements attributed to the Chargé d'Affaires at Tokyo do not inspire confidence that any information supplied to him by the Embassy would be used in an ethical manner; and it seems to me that the appropriate manner in which to handle this matter would be to instruct the Embassy at Tokyo to inform the Chargé d'Affaires that the Department would be glad to receive a request from the Uruguayan Legation in Washington.
