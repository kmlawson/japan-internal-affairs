CONFIDENTIAL

My dear Mr. Ennis:

The Department has recently learned from a confidential source that the Swedish Vice Consul at Honolulu in charge of Japanese interests in the Territory of Hawaii has reported to the Swedish Legation at Washington that he is unable to issue or extend Japanese passports in as much as martial law continues to be enforced in Hawaii.

I should appreciate receiving your comments regarding the accuracy of the Swedish Vice Consul's statement. It is felt that any report of this nature coming to the attention of the Japanese Government would be likely to result in a protest from that Government and, as well, might lead to some form of retaliation against United States nationals held in Japanese-controlled areas.

TRAVEL DOCUMENTATION OF JAPANESE NATIONALS
To
American Diplomatic and Consular Officers.
The Secretary of State encloses for the information of the Officers in Charge a memorandum received from the Civil Affairs Division of the Department of the Army concerning the procedure to be followed in extending the validity of passports of Japanese nationals permitted to travel abroad. The procedure outlined imposes no direct burden on American Foreign Service Officers. However, because of the role played by the United States in the occupation of Japan, questions regarding the travel documentation of Japanese nationals may be raised at any post and the information may accordingly be useful.
Further to the subject the Office of the United States Political Adviser for Japan has recently informed the Department that:
1. Japanese passports will be issued to Japanese nationals in Japan only upon specific instructions from the Supreme Commander for the Allied Powers in cases involving the travel of Japanese nationals outside Japan in connection with current programs for Japanese travel abroad (export-import, cultural exchange, etcetera).
2. In cases where a given country is willing to issue to a Japanese national in Japan an entry visa for the purpose of establishing permanent residence in the given country, it is the policy of the Supreme Commander's Headquarters that the country issuing the entry visa also issue a certificate of identity or similar travel document which will serve in lieu of passport; Japanese passports will not be issued for this type of travel.
3. The

STANDING OPERATING PROCEDURE FOR EXTENDING
VALIDITY OF PASSPORTS OF JAPANESE
NATIONALS WHILE TEMPORARILY ABROAD

1. Should it be deemed desirable to extend the period of validity of a passport issued by the Japanese Government upon direction of the Supreme Commander for the Allied Powers to a Japanese national subsequent to the individual's departure from Japan, the standing operating procedure outlined below, approved by the Department of the Army, The Department of State and The Supreme Commander for the Allied Powers will be employed.

2. The Sponsor (old or new) of a Japanese national may initiate action to extend the period of validity of the latter's passport by requesting SCAP to authorize such extension. The Sponsor must state his willingness to cover the extended period with appropriate guarantees, give the name of the passport holder, the number of the passport, the reason for such extension, designation of additional countries (if any) to be visited by the Japanese national, and the current address of the Japanese national. Where subject Japanese nationals are in the United States, Sponsor should submit request direct to the Supreme Commander for the Allied Powers. However, where they are in countries other than the United States, the request should be submitted to the Supreme Commander for the Allied Powers through the government of the country of temporary residence. This government should be requested to present the application to the Supreme Commander for the Allied Powers either through its mission in Japan or through the mission of its protecting power in Japan, or where neither such missions exist, through the Department of

=== Page 21 === (confidence 0.99)
-2-

State of the United States Government. The fact that the government of the country of temporary residence thus presents this application to the Supreme Commander for the Allied Powers will be accepted as evidence that the government concerned concurs in the requested extension. If the request for extension includes travel to or through other countries, it will be the responsibility of the Sponsor to secure in writing the approval of these other governments for such travel.

3. The Supreme Commander for the Allied Powers will address the message containing his decision on each request as follows:

a. (If the Japanese national is in the United States of its territories):

Chief, Civil Affairs Division
Department of the Army

b. (If the Japanese national is in a country other than the United States or Japan):

Chief, Civil Affairs Division
Department of the Army
Washington 25, D.C.

of the following: Name of Japanese national, name and address of his Sponsor, his passport number, period of extension and any additional countries to be inscribed.

Should the message from SCAP authorize an extension of validity of a passport, or inscription of additional countries to be visited, or both, the addressee will proceed as follows:

a. Notify the Japanese national concerned to present his passport.

b. Inscribe in passport in paragraph indicating period of validity on page 3 "see page 6", and write on page 6 "validity extended to (new date)", quoting authority (cable number from SCAP) and sign.

c. If applicable, inscribe on page 6 the additional countries to be visited.

d. Inform SCAP of completed action.

5. The message from SCAP will contain the following information:

a. Request that addressee upon receipt of the message, notify the Japanese national of the content of the message.

b. Authorization of extension of validity and inscription of additional countries to be visited, or both.

c. A statement authorizing the addressee to act as the agent of SCAP in amending the original entries in the passport in accordance with the content of the message.

d. Sufficient data to identify the person to whom the passport is issued and the document itself, in order that any government will be assured of the exact intent of SCAP relative to amendment of the passport.
