A-143 March 9, 3 pm

The following is reported for what it may be worth, from the same source as Secret Airgram No. A-101 of January 8, 4 p.m., 1945.

Japanese Minister SUMA has allegedly informed source that 10,000 homes in Tokio have been destroyed by recent bombings, and that the business section between the Imperial Hotel and the Imperial Palace is untouched, as well as the Ginsa Section. Source deduced, therefore, that the bombing has been concentrated on the 40-kilometer highway, completely industrialized, beyond the Palace, extending to Yokohama. SUMA declared that Japan had taken appropriate steps to move factories and to install others underground, and source points out similarity to German provisions in 1943 in this regard.

As to hostilities approaching through the islands closer to Japan, SUMA regarded such hostilities as still far distant. The fleet still exists, the Americans are becoming more and more imprudent, believing that nothing lies ahead, but when they reach the point which the Japs wish them to reach, the Japanese fleet will attack.

MAR 24 1945
SUMA

SUMA continued that American losses are high in both men and ships, that much more will be lost with incommensurate return, that although they will approach the Asiatic continent and a debarkation may occur some day, there is no guarantee of success; Japan's suicide (illegible words) Americans have taken few (illegible words) to refund to (handwritten)

As to production, in the face of great American production, SUMA said Japan has all it requires, that it is transforming its aviation, does not lack war materials nor men, and further is activated by the desire of every Japanese for total sacrifice — Japan's defeat would not be easy.

Politically, he said that the threat of Russia attacking Japan is Utopian. He was very confident that the non-aggression pact would be renewed, and said conversations were proceeding at Moscow of the utmost importance, but that the conclusions were not yet ready politically.

SUMA said the Japanese Ambassador at Moscow is engaged in important operations. SATO is a very able politician and is aided by thirty carefully selected men, hard at work, who would bring results.

Source gained the implication of the possibility of Russian desires for territory in North China, with the 400,000 in the communist army aided by Russia, and also felt that if Russia should realize its plans in Europe, Japan would obtain more in the Far East.

The following is reported for what it may be worth, and was obtained by the same source as the material in Secret airgram No. A-143, March 9, 3 p.m.:
