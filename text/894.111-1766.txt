October 3, 1949

Adrian S. Fisher, Esq.
Legal Adviser
Department of State
Washington, D.C.

Dear Mr. Fisher:

I have been requested to assist A.L. Wirin, Esq. in a matter which I believe is appropriate for your consideration.

Mr. Wirin is counsel for a number of individuals born in the United States of Japanese parentage who are alleged to have sacrificed their United States citizenship by their conduct in Japan before or during the last war. All of these individuals are, of course, now in Japan. Mr. Wirin has brought on behalf of these individuals cases in the District Court of the United States for and in the Southern District of California to determine their citizenship. The Court on August 2nd, 1949, upon motion entered an order that the depositions of the plaintiff and of a certain extra witness be taken in Japan at such time after August 16, 1949, as would be convenient. Mr. Wirin thereupon applied for a passport to proceed to Japan in order to take these depositions. The passport was denied.

Mr. Wirin thereupon wrote a letter to the Secretary of State, a copy of which I enclose, attaching a certified copy of the Order of the Circuit Court of the Southern District of California and requesting his intervention. That letter is I understand now in the possession of Mr. Brown in

September 26, 1949

Honorable Dean G. Acheson
Secretary of State
Washington, D. C.

Dear Mr. Acheson:

I have come to Washington from California in connection with my application for a passport to go to Japan. You will recall my writing to you about the matter some months ago; and your generous good wishes if I got to Japan. I haven't been there; instead, I have come to Washington to find out, if I can, why. I had not intended to bother you personally with the problem; except that Roger Baldwin and other friends advised me that I really do need your personal intercession in the matter.

Hence my writing to you; and hence my wanting to see you for a few moments, if I may.

Specifically, the situation is this:

Some months ago, I received a formal communication from the Passport Division, reciting that my application for a passport had been denied because my presence in Japan was not desired.

I realize fully that the Passport Division acted solely because of the position of General MacArthur's Headquarters. But the legal responsibility for the decision in rejecting my application was nonetheless that of the State Department; for my application for a passport was directed to the State Department and was acted upon adversely by the State Department.

I have been furnished with no reason as to why my presence in Japan is undesired; nor have I been offered an opportunity to adduce any evidence in my behalf in support of the application. As a younger constitutional lawyer to an older one, have I not been denied "due process"?--and this in connection with a legal project, which has had the sponsorship of Roger Baldwin and the American Civil Liberties Union, as well as Mike Masaoka and the Japanese American Citizens League. Perhaps you know that but recently Roger Baldwin himself, although having gone both to Japan and to Germany, upon invitation of the War Department, was publicly designated by the Army as "unemployable"; but has been cleared. I am anxious to secure similar clearance.

When I last applied for a passport, it was in connection with my general representation of a number of American-born Japanese who were claimed by the State Department to have lost their U.S. citizenships, and who challenged this claim in the United States courts. In connection with their cases now pending in the United States District Court at Los Angeles, the United States District Court has made an order, a certified copy of which I enclose, directing that depositions be taken in Japan. I desire to go to Japan at this time in order to be present at the taking of those depositions, and in order personally to represent my clients at the taking of those depositions.

Accordingly, this letter of mine is a renewed application, or a new application if the latter is necessary, for a passport to go to Japan for the purpose of attending to depositions above set forth.

May I, accordingly, bespeak your personal interest in the matter, first, to the extent of an inquiry as to the precise reasons for the rejection of my former application for a passport, and, secondly, your consideration of this present, now renewed application in this letter for a passport to be issued at this time.

Sincerely yours,

A. L. Wirin

ORDER
DIRECTING TAKING OF DEPOSITIONS

The above cases having come on for further hearing upon the motion of the plaintiffs to take the depositions of each plaintiff and of one Ko Ishii in Japan, before the United States Consul at Yokohama, Japan, the plaintiffs appearing by their counsel A. L. Wirin, and the defendant as Secretary of State, appearing by the United States Attorney by Robert J. Kelleher, Assistant United States Attorney, and good cause appearing therefor, and the Court being fully advised in the premises, the plaintiffs having withdrawn said motion with respect to the plaintiffs Toshiie Nishida and Yukio Nakanishi,

IT IS HEREBY ORDERED that the depositions of the following named plaintiffs:

Michiko Takigawa
Naru Yanamoto
Toshio Watanabe
Yutaka Tokuda
Katsumi Mayemura
Lily Yamaguchi
Minoru Furukawa;

as well as of Ko Ishii, be taken in Japan at the office of the United States Consul at Yokohama, Japan, on August 16, 1949, or such other date thereafter as counsel for the plaintiffs and for the defendant may mutually agree upon.

PAUL J. McCORMICK
United States District Judge

Dated Aug. 2nd, 1949.

My dear Mr. Horsky:

Reference is made to your letter of October 3, 1949 and subsequent telephone conversations, concerning the application of Mr. A. L. Wirin for a passport to visit Japan in connection with certain depositions to be taken in behalf of his clients.

This matter has been given very serious consideration in the Department as a result of correspondence directed to another division concerned with the issuance of passports. There is enclosed for your information a copy of a letter to Mr. Wirin from the Secretary, dated November 1, 1949, concerning the determinations made in this respect. You will note in the final paragraph of this letter that further consideration will be given Mr. Wirin's application.

Sincerely yours,

Adrian S. Fisher
The Legal Adviser
