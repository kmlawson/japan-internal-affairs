JAPANESE COASTAL PATROL (SC-305/8)
A POLICY PROPOSAL SUBMITTED BY THE NEW ZEALAND REPRESENTATIVE
VIEWS OF THE U. S. GOVERNMENT, APRIL 27, 1948
1. The establishment of Maritime Safety Authorities,
including provision for a coastal patrol or coastguard
service, 1s a natural and proper action to be taken by
the Japanese Government.
2. A legislative act authorizing the establishment
of these Maritime Safety Authorities is a proper procedure
for the Japanese Diet.
3. The Act passed by the Diet on April 15th, according
to the draft of the bill and the telegraphic explanations
of the Supreme Commander, contains no provisions, in the
judgment of the U.S. Government, which are in conflict with
any policy decision of the Far Eastern Commission.
4. Further, the Supreme Commander, who is "charged
with the implementation of the directives which express
the policy decisions of the Commission", has a responsi-
bility to prevent any action by the Japanese Government
which would be in contravention of any of these policy
decisions.
5. Under these circumstances it would appear inadvisable
for the Far Eastern Commission, even if it possessed the
requisite authority, to postpone the enforcement of an act
passed by the Japanese Diet dealing with a subject which
is within the competence of the Diet and which the Supreme
Commander has virtually certified is consistent with the
policy decisions of the Far Eastern Commission.
6. However, in our opinion, the Far Eastern Commission
does not have the requisite authority to pass this kind
of decision. The draft decision presented by the New
Zealand representative to the Commission does not appear
to be policy in the normal meaning of the word as used
in the Terms of Reference. It does not provide that the
Japanese may have a coastal patrol, or may not have one,
or may have one under detailed provisions and restrictions.
It deals with administration, and would provide merely
that an act of the Diet shall not go into effect until
some indefinite time in the future. It appears, moreover,
