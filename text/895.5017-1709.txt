My dear Mr. Terrill:

I have received for reply your letter of April 1, 1949 addressed to the Office of Far Eastern Affairs requesting information concerning the cost of living in Korea at the present time as compared with 1937 and also the rate of exchange in Korea for dollars is 1937 and in 1948.

The cost of living index for December 1948 in Korea, based on 1937 as 100, was 0.00000. This is equivalent to the wholesale price index in Seoul for that period as computed by the Bank of Korea. The wholesale price index is stated to be a "single geometric mean for 80 commodities." There would, of course, be considerable variation between this index based on the Korean consumption pattern and an index which might be computed for the consumption pattern of foreign missionaries in Korea.

The exchange rate for dollars averaged 3.47 to 1 in 1937. In October 1948, an official military exchange rate was established at 450 to 1, but no rate has yet been announced for other transactions. The Korean Foreign Exchange Bank is the licensing authority and is operating on an ad hoc basis until an official exchange rate shall have been established.

An

Mr. Lee A. Terrill,
International Committee of Young Men's Christian Associations,
347 Madison Avenue,
New York 17, New York.

An indication of the present cost of living for Americans in Korea who do not have regular access to the Government commissary is the estimate that commodity prices in the open market in Seoul on the basis of the conversion rate of 450 to 1 are between 25 and 50 percent higher than commissary prices or roughly from 15 to 40 percent higher than prices in New York or Washington.

Since no changes have been reported in trade regulations or customs duties under the new Korean Government, they are believed to be the same as under the American Military Government. This indicates that "reasonable quantities of personal and household effects" may be exempt from duty and either exempt from licensing or subject only to routine licensing procedure. The customs duty on goods not so exempt is, at present, 10 percent ad valorem.
