Dairen, Manchuria, November 10, 1932.

Alien Employment Restrictions in Foreign Countries.

THE HONORABLE
THE SECRETARY OF STATE
WASHINGTON

I have the honor to refer to the Department's instruction of September 26, 1932, file FA 800.504/36, quoting a letter of September 19, 1932 from the Secretary of Labor, and directing the preparation of reports relative to restrictions placed on the employment of aliens in the Kwantung Leased Territory.

Investigation discloses that there are no restrictions placed on the employment of aliens in the Kwantung Leased Territory. Dairen (and the Leased Territory) is not a place where American and European aliens would ordinarily seek employment and Chinese are allowed freely to enter and seek employment, subject only to police examination to determine the nature of their business.

Respectfully yours,

John Carter Vincent
American Consul.

Dairen, Manchuria, November 28, 1934.

Special Immigration Reports

THE HONORABLE
THE SECRETARY OF STATE
WASHINGTON.

I have the honor to refer to the Department's mimeographed instruction dated September 14, 1934, (File No. 800.55/184), concerning the admission and residence of aliens seeking certain types of employment, viz:

(1) Conditions of temporary admission and residence of instrumental musicians, other musicians, actors, artists, and choristers.

(2) Conditions of temporary admission and residence of aliens for employment other than specified in (1).

In reply I have the honor to advise the Department that an officer of this Consulate recently conferred with the interpreter of the Kwantung Government Marine Police, who is in charge of the examination of aliens entering Dairen by sea, regarding this matter. The interpreter stated that there were no special regulation governing the admission or residence of aliens in the first category as distinguished from the second. He pointed out that there were three conditions, however, which are applicable to the entry of aliens of both class (1) and (2) into the Leased Territory:

first

first, that the aliens be not destitute; second, that the aliens will not seek to perform 'coolie' labor; and third, that the aliens seeking admission will not engage in political activities or communistic agitation. He stated confidentially that Russians are submitted to a closer examination than other nationalities.

In general, aliens of both categories usually find little difficulty in entering Dairen if they can present concrete evidence that they will be employed by a concern of known standing.

Statistical information of the nature requested in the Department's instruction is impossible to obtain.

There is enclosed as an appendix to this despatch a translation of the Kwantung Government Ordinance No. 15, dated March 20, 1918, which sets forth regulations that are still valid as to the admission of all aliens into the Kwantung Leased Territory.

Art. 5. The Civil Administrator, the Branch Civil Administrator or the Chief of Police having jurisdiction over the districts may prohibit entry of persons into the Kwantung Leased Territory or the South Manchuria Railway Zone or may order them to depart outside the districts if they obtain or intend to obtain permission to enter by means of false statements.

Art. 6. The provisions of the present regulations do not apply to an ambassador, minister, member of embassy or legation, consular officers and their families of the high contracting powers and a person who is on an official mission of a government of the high contracting power.

This is also applied to the crews of the vessels of the high contracting powers, calling at ports in the Kwantung Leased Territory.

**Supplementary Rule**

Art. 7. The present regulations come into force on the date of promulgation.

Art. 8. The provision of Art. 1, paragraph 1, Clause 1 of the present regulations may not be applied to those foreigners who are on the way to the Kwantung Leased Territory or the South Manchuria Railway Zone and who have valid reasons for not being able to obtain a passport or certificate of nationality mentioned in Art. 1, at the time of issuance of the present regulations.
