DEPARTMENT OF STATE
DIVISION OF FAR EASTERN AFFAIRS

July 28, 1931.

MME:

As I understand the situation the matter of granting licenses to practice any branch of medical science in Japan is regulated on a reciprocal basis, namely, licenses to practice granted in a foreign country are recognized in Japan provided licenses granted in Japan to practice are likewise recognized in the foreign country.

Licenses to practice medicine in the United States are granted by the various State authorities; hence, there is no possibility of making a reciprocal arrangement with Japan for the recognition of medical licenses. I understand that licenses may be, and usually are, obtained by American practitioners in Japan after examination.

I assume that this information was given to Mr. Ishimaru by the Tokyo Embassy when he went to Japan this spring to be married, and it is quite possible that he did not choose to take the examination and is now looking for some short cut to attain his end. I see no way, on the information which we have, of rendering him any assistance.

I am routing the attached letter through Le by way of precaution.

RSM

Sir:

The receipt is acknowledged, by reference from the Department of the Interior, of your letter of July 15, 1931, relating to your desire to receive the assistance of this Government in obtaining a license to practice optometry in Japan.

In reply you are informed that the matter of granting licenses to practice any branch of medical science in Japan is one which rests entirely with the Japanese Government authorities and one in which this Government does not feel warranted in interfering so long as the regulations relating thereto are framed and administered without discrimination against American citizens.

From the information now available to this Department there appears to be nothing it can do in compliance with your request.

Very truly yours,

Monsieur l'Ambassadeur:

I have the honor to refer to your note of October 27, 1932, in regard to the examination of foreigners for the practice of medicine and dental surgery in Japan.

Your inquiry was at once referred to the Department of Home Affairs. At that time the Department of Home Affairs had no definite policy in regard to languages to be used in examinations. Quite recently it has been decided to give the examinations in either English or German in addition to Japanese, for the time being.

There is hereby appended a memorandum setting forth, for your information, an outline of the procedure to be followed in taking these examinations.

I avail myself of this opportunity to renew to Your Excellency the assurances of my highest consideration.

MEMORANDUM.

(1). Anyone who has completed a course of more than four years in a foreign medical school may undergo examination for medical practice in Japan using, for the time being, either the English language or the German language in addition to the Japanese language. However, anyone who comes under the purview of one of the following items may not undergo examination.

(a). One who has been subjected to an undetermined penal servitude or imprisonment of more than six years.

(b). A deaf, mute or blind person.

Anyone who has been subjected to penal servitude or imprisonment of less than six years, or anyone who has been sentenced to fine in connection with the practice of medicine may not undergo examination.

(2). Those who desire to undergo examination shall submit to the Minister of Home Affairs during the month of December every year a written application (in accordance with Form No. 1) together with the documents mentioned below:

(a) A curriculum vitae (in accordance with Form 2).

(b). Identification card issued by the authorities of his or her country. However, in special circumstances, an identification card issued by the consular authorities of his or her country in Japan may be accepted.

(c). Certificate of graduation by the President of a medical school, from which the applicant has been graduated.

(d). Photograph (carte-de-visite size, 4 inches by 2-1/2 inches) taken without hat within a period of less than six months before making application, on the back of which shall be inscribed the date on which the photograph was taken and the name of the applicant.

(3). The applicant shall affix on the written application a revenue stamp of Y.15 (an applicant who desires to undergo examination for first section and examination for second section separately shall affix revenue stamps of Y.10 each), as fee, and then submit the application. The fees thus paid shall not be refunded.

(4). Examination for medical practice shall be conducted at Tokyo once every year. The date for examination shall be notified in the OFFICIAL GAZETTE by the Minister of Finance.

(5). Subjects for examination are divided into two sections, as follows:

First Section.
(Theories)
- Anatomy (including histology).
- Physiology (including biological chemistry).
- Pathology (including pathological anatomy and medical jurisprudence).
- Pharmacology.
- Sanitary Science and Bacteriology.

## Second Section.
(Practice).

- Surgery (including Otorhinolaryngology, dermatology and syphilology).
- Internal diseases (including paediatrics and psychiatry).
- Ophthalmology.
- Obstetrics and gynaecology.

## Clinic Examination:

Clinic Examination shall be conducted in surgery, internal diseases, obstetrics and ophthalmology. However, as regards obstetrics and ophthalmology, the applicant shall draw lots and undergo examination in either of the two.

It is possible for the applicant to undergo examination for the first section and second section separately. But unless the applicant has passed the first examination successfully, he cannot undergo the second section examination.
