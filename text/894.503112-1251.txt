MAR 24 1941
Mr. Acheson:

In Tokyo's 383, March 10, 5 p.m., Ambassador Grew reports that American-based corporations engaged in manufacturing in Japan are extremely apprehensive over their investments in Japan. On the one hand there has been recently enacted in Japan legislation extending rigid government control over all industrial production, prescribing the license system for designated machines and machine tools and for raw materials, providing for expropriation of lands and materials, labor, plants and equipment, and for inspection and supervision of business offices, factories, et cetera. It is anticipated that under the broad provisions of the revised general mobilization law and foreign exchange control laws the enforcing regulations will stipulate a Japanese majority of shareholders with complete Japanese control of management. On the other hand American firms are prevented by Japanese laws from selling their property and transferring their funds to the United States. A number of American industrial firms already have large frozen cash balances and there have been many indications recently that the Japanese have been tightening up on

The question of freezing Japanese funds in this country and controlling their liquidation on a reciprocal basis with Japan seems still to be a part of a larger question affecting many other countries. The situation as it exists at the present time does seem to be inequitable in that American funds are frozen in Japan, whereas the Japanese are free to withdraw their funds from this country, and are doing so. The action suggested by the Ambassador seems to be justifiable from a commercial point of view. However, as has been indicated above, this is only part of a world problem and FE perceives no reason for singling out Japan for action.

Should it be decided to undertake freezing operations with respect to all countries and to exercise exchange control with respect to future transactions with all countries, FE would present no objection as far as Japan is concerned. However, this of course involves policy which is not the primary concern of FE. There is offered for consideration the suggestion that Mr. Acheson may wish to call a meeting in his office to discuss this matter from a comprehensive point of view.
