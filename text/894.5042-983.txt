IN REPLY REFER TO 20-ILL-ER

Dear Mr. Carr:

Reference is made to despatch No. 5002, dated September 5, 1930, from Charles L. De Vault, Consul at Tokyo, on page 46 of which a statement is made that the Japanese Government is directing serious attention to the question of introducing an unemployment insurance system, and the Insurance and Labor Laws Section of the Division of Commercial Laws would appreciate it if it were kept informed of developments along this line. The statistics on life insurance business in 1929 on page 47 are important and are to be included in a summary on this subject to be forwarded the Association of Life Insurance Presidents, New York, to be used later in an annual report.

The Division of Commercial Laws is grateful for the Consul's interest and cooperation in incorporating in his report information relating to foreign insurance and labor laws and will appreciate his continued attention to this subject.

Very truly yours,

Robert J. Phillips,

To the American Consul General,

No. 2277. Tokyo, February 25, 1937.
SUBJECT: SOCIAL INSURANCE.

The Honorable
The Secretary of State,
Washington.

Sir:

With reference to the Department's instruction of
December 9, 1936, Diplomatic Serial No. 2733, (File No.
800.5074/1), concerning the application of foreign social
insurance systems to foreign nationals in American diplo-
matic and consular offices, I have the honor to report
that there are no provisions in Japanese law which require
foreign nationals in our mission and consulates in Japan
to contribute to social insurance of any kind.

Law No. 42 of June 2, 1936, reported in the Embassy's
despatch No. 2024 of September 3, 1936, providing for the
establishment of retirement reserves and retirement al-
lowances by employers for their employees, is applicable
only to enterprises employing fifty or more workmen and
which

which are subject to the provisions of either the Factory law or the Mining law. The so-called white collar employee in offices and enterprises not covered by the provisions of the above mentioned laws is generally taken care of by contributions from his salary, usually made compulsory by the employer as a condition of employment, to social insurance features set up within the organization employing him.

Respectfully yours,

Joseph C. Crew
