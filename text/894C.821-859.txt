AMERICAN CONSULAR SERVICE
American Consulate, Dairen, Manchuria, April 23, 1935.

SUBJECT: direction of New Air Beacons and Recent Air Maneuvers.

The Honorable
Joseph C. Grew,
American Ambassador,
Tokyo, Japan.

Sir:

As of possible interest I have the honor to report that the press of April 20th last reports that a fourthousand candle power radio beacon is to be built soon on a hill-top near Choushuitzu, the Dairen airport. The rays are to be strong enough to be visible at a distance of 50 miles. The construction of the beacon in question is reported to be the first step in the program for the construction of 13 such beacons throughout Manchuria to assist airplanes in night flying. The program is sponsored by the employees Association of the South Manchuria Railway Company. The second beacon is to be constructed near Wafangtien, some 30 kilometers north of the boundary of the Kwantung Leased Territory.

In this connection it is of interest to report that air maneuvers were held over Chinchow, Dairen, and Port Arthur in the Kwantung Leased Territory during the three days from April 17 to 19. Although it was reported at

first

first that 200 military planes would engage in the maneuvers, actually no more that 30 took part, most of which were sent from the Heijo field in Korea. The maneuvers included the actual bombing of a "wooden target" 20 meters in diameter at Port Arthur. Planes of the squadron that maneuvered over Dairen engaged in extremely skillful stunt flying at a dangerously low altitude over the city.

Respectfully yours,

Stuart E. Grummon,
American Consul.
