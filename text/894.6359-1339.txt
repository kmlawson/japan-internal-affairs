That they make no specifications as to grades or impurities, other than the basis of Contract "B".

"Needless to say that Minerales y Metales are not interested in negotiation, but it is likewise obvious that others are and can obtain higher prices than in the States. This information is given after a conversation between the two parties named on September 16, 1940."

COPY/ Copy of a Memorandum Compiled by M.E.W. in March 1942.
JAPANESE BAUXITE RESOURCES.

During the years 1938-41 Japan is estimated to have imported between 200,000 and 350,000 tons of bauxite per annum from various sources, of which the Netherlands East Indies was the most important, supplying about 60-70% of total imports. Even assuming that the entire output of aluminium in these years, estimated at about 125,000 tons in all, was based on bauxite, which is known not to have been the case, this would represent a consumption of only 500,000 tons of ore out of a total import of about 1,100,000 tons. Consumption of bauxite in other uses would not exceed 100-150,000 tons and was probably much less, so that Japan would have about 500,000 tons in stock at the beginning of 1942, sufficient for two years output of aluminium at the industry's present estimated rate of production.

Japan's pre-war sources of bauxite were the Netherlands East Indies which supplied 230,668 tons in 1939, Malaya (85,736), Carolinas (39,000), Greece (23,959) and India (2,179). Other sources of supply open to her are Indo-China, Thailand and Hainan Island, but the material found in all these territories is low-grade and the reserves are small. Deposits of aluminous shale in Korea, Manchuria and North China, as well as in Japan Proper, have been developed to some extent, but costs of production have proved so high that aluminous shale is steadily being abandoned in favour of bauxite. Probably about 75% of present production - representing the old-established and technically more efficient portion of the industry - is based on bauxite. Aluminous shale is used by the newer government-subsidised companies but certain of these, including the important Japan Light Metals Co., have adopted bauxite for at least a part of their production.

At present Japan controls her three most important sources of supply, namely N.E.I., Malaya and the Carolinas, and has ample stocks of ore in hand to tide her over the brief period necessary to set the quarries working again.

There should be no difficulty in raising output from the Bintan mines in N.E.I. to 400,000 tons p.a. The maximum annual output hitherto achieved by the Malayan mines of 86,000 tons could probably be doubled within twelve months, while at least 60,000 tons could be obtained from the Carolinas. From the point of view of bauxite supplies, therefore, and providing transport and technical problems can be successfully overcome, the Japanese industry should shortly be in a position to operate at its present reported capacity of 100,000 tons without further drawing on stocks.

# NETHERLANDS EAST INDIES.

Production comes entirely from the island of Bintan opposite Singapore, and from the neighbouring islands of Angkoet and Kojang where deposits estimated at 10-20 million tons have been developed by the Nederlandsche Indische Bauxiet Exploitatie Maatschappij. The ore is of good quality, containing about 53.7% alumina, 2.5-2.8% silica and 13.3-13.9% ferric oxide. The principal workings are at Soengi Kolak, on the Kidjang Strait. The ore from these deposits is washed and some of it calcined on the spot. It is then stored at the wharf nearby, where storage space for 22,000 tons is available. From the storage depots it can be loaded direct on to ocean freighters. Ore mined on the islands of Angkoet and Kojang on the opposite side of the Strait is transported across to the landing stage by cable ropeway.

# MALAYA

The principal bauxite deposits in Malay are in South and West Johore. Deposits have been developed at Bukit Pasir and Sri Medan, near Batu Pahat, by the Japanese Ishihare Sangyo K.K. and at Perigi Achih, 12 miles east of Johore Bahru, by Chinese enterprise.

The ore has an alumina content of 52-61% with 5-15% ferric oxide and 1.6-6.52% silica.

There are washing plants at the mines but the ore is exported in the crude state. The ports of shipment are Batu Pahar and Penggerand.

## CAROLINE ISLANDS.

Deposits estimated at 10 million tons in the Japanese-mandated Caroline Islands (Talao, Ponape, Yap, Marakaro and Carasumao) have been developed by the Jango Aluminium Industry Co., a subsidiary of the Japanese South Seas Development Co.

The ore has an average alumina content of 55.4% with 1.1% silica and 11.5% ferric oxide.

Production began in 1938 and is estimated at 20,000 tons in that year, all of which was shipped to Japan. Japanese imports from this source in 1939 and 1940 are estimated at 39,000 and 43,000 metric tons.
