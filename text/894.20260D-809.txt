LEGATION OF THE
UNITED STATES OF AMERICA
Helsingfors, February 14, 1934.

No. 109.

![img-0.jpeg](img-0.jpeg)

Sir:

I have the honor to report that during the past two
months reports about strange airplanes observed in Northern
Finland and Scandinavia have appeared almost daily in the
local as well as in the Scandinavian press. It is claimed
that the mystery planes have only a few times been sighted
in daylight, but that the drone of an airplane motor has
been heard and the contours and running lights of a large
monoplane have been seen by a great number of persons in
the

the dark hours of the night.

While the mystery planes have certainly in many instances proved to be merely figments of a vivid imagination, in several cases reports have been confirmed by local military and civil authorities. Thus, for instance, the following reports of relatively recent date are taken to have established the existence of the mystery planes: Major General Reutersward, Commander of the Swedish military forces in the Norrbotten district, has confirmed the report of a strange airplane observed on January 22nd flying over the Boden fortification district. During the following night fifteen persons heard and saw distinctly a large airplane carrying green and red running lights against the clear, starlit sky at Kemi, Finland. On February 5th a frontier guard patrol at Suomussalmi, Finland, reported a strange airplane flying over the frontier from Finland into Russia. The next day a Norwegian lieutenant accompanied by two soldiers observed an airplane which apparently had made a forced landing on a field near Tromsø in northern Norway. The plane was, however, not located by patrols sent out the following day. The observations have generally been made along the line Kuolajärvi - Kemijärvi - Rovaniemi - Kemi (in Finland) - Boden - Gallivare - Kiruna (in Sweden) - Tromsø (in Norway).

On January 27th Admiral Lindman, Swedish conservative leader, directed in the Swedish parliament an interrogation to the Prime Minister, inquiring what measures the Government intended to take to prevent strange airplanes from flying over Swedish territory. In his reply the Prime Minister, on

on February 2nd, stated that investigations conducted in northern Sweden had failed to throw any light on the mystery airplanes. The commandant of the fortifications at Boden has, however, taken necessary precautions to prevent the recurrence of the incident of January 22nd. Three military airplanes have been stationed at Boden. The Norwegian Government is also carrying on investigation in the northern provinces of that country.

According to Major M. Saurio of the Finnish Ministry of Defense, two pursuit planes from the Military Aviation School at Kauhava have been despatched to Kemi under the command of Captain G. Appelgren of Air Station No. 2 (Helsingfors) to conduct investigations regarding the operations of the mystery airplanes in Finland. The military forces, civic guards and police authorities in northern Finland have been placed under the orders of Colonel T. Ilmoniemi, Commander of the North Ostrobothnia Military Province, who is in charge of the investigations. For the present no official communication has been issued regarding the outcome of the investigations and during the past few days no new reports with reference to the phantom flyers have appeared.

The following theories have been presented in the press regarding the origin and object of the mystery airplanes:

1) Japanese airplanes from an aircraft carrier operating off the Norwegian coast, mapping out a route to attack Soviet Russia in the event of war;

2) Russian airplanes from a base in Soviet Karelia on reconnaissance flights or carrying arms for Swedish and Norwegian communists;

3) Airplanes employed by a smuggling ring to convey narcotics

narcotics into Soviet Russia.

These theories would seem, however, to be refuted by the fact that the mysterious flyers certainly do not hesitate to advertise their presence. Conscientiously complying with the international rules of the air, they illuminate their planes with green and red running lights, visible for several miles on a clear night, sweep the ground with powerful searchlights and frighten the inhabitants of the northern latitudes by the roar of their motors flying at a low altitude over sleeping villages and towns.

Only one mystery airplane has been observed at a time, but since observations have almost at the same hour been made at places far apart, the newspapers have drawn the conclusion that there exist at least three mystery planes.

On January 26th the HELSINGIN SANOMAT (Helsingfors), leading liberal newspaper, reported that the Honorable Boris Stein, local Soviet Minister, had on the previous day been questioned by a representative of the newspaper as to whether he thought it possible that the mystery planes come from Russia. The Minister replied that he knew nothing of the said airplanes except what he had learned from the local press, and that in his opinion the "spook" flyers would remain "spooks." Commander Captain V. Miettinen, Chief of the Finnish Coast Guard, in a recent conversation with a member of this Legation, compared the mystery airplanes of northern Finland and Scandinavia with the Loch Ness monster.

Any further developments in the activities of these mysterious aviators will be reported promptly to the Department.
