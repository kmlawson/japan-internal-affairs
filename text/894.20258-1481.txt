JA - Mr. Dickover

SUBJECT: Despatch No. 4079 from Stockholm.

I agree that the attached document (despatch No. 4079 from Stockholm) should be classified CONFIDENTIAL and certainly no higher than SECRET to protect the source of the information.

The indoctrination of all Foreign Service officers in use of security classifications will be a slow process. In the meantime, the classifications of incoming communications may be changed by responsible officers of the Department and, when appropriate, the attention of officers in charge of Foreign Service posts may be called to extreme cases of incorrect classification.

In this case, I recommend that the classification be changed in accordance with rules given in paragraph 9 of Administrative Instruction, Security Control 3 of August 14, 1944.

L. W. Parke

Attachment

November 24, 1944

A-S
Commander Parke:

As you no doubt desire that documents with the security classification of "Top Secret" be kept at as small a number as possible, in order to avoid the necessity of providing excessive security facilities for these documents and in order to preserve the significance of the classification term "Top Secret", it is believed that you will be interested in the attached document (despatch no. 4079 from the Legation at Stockholm). FE finds nothing in the document which would warrant the security classification of "Top Secret", and, in fact, believes that the document would deserve the much lower classification of "Confidential" only should such classification be necessary to protect the supplier of the information.

Possibly the "Top Secret" classification was given to the enclosure to the despatch by OSS, and the Legation felt that it had no option but to continue that classification in its transmitting despatch.

The Honorable
The Secretary of State,
Washington.

Sir:

With reference to the Legation's telegram No. 3568
of September 11, 11 p.m. concerning possible transfer
of Japanese intelligence activity from Lisbon to Stockholm,
I have the honor to forward herewith enclosed a
copy of a report submitted to the Office of Strategic
Services by its representatives in Stockholm concerning
the Japanese who have arrived in Sweden or departed
therefrom by air since October 1943.

It is to be noted that the report points out that
the Office of Strategic Services cannot ascertain exactly
which persons are arriving in Sweden for the first
time. The Legation has been informed that the persons
listed in the enclosed report are all suspected of being
engaged in intelligence activity.

Respectfully yours,

Herschel V. Johnson.

TOP SECRET

Date: 5 September 1944

JAPANESE IN SWEDEN

1. Attached is a summary of the air traffic in Japanese since October, 1943. It is to be noted that this represents air traffic only and others may have been arriving or leaving by boat or rail.

2. It is also to be observed that we have no means of positively identifying those who are arriving for the first time - in other words, some persons on the list may have been already resident in Stockholm and just returning from a trip to Berlin or Helsinki.

3. The starred names represent persons who are in our files, but we have no record of their having travelled.
