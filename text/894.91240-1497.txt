November 1, 1945

CONFIDENTIAL

To: The Honorable
J. Edgar Hoover, Director,
Federal Bureau of Investigation.

From: Frederick B. Lyon,
Chief, Division of
Foreign Activity Correlation.

The Department has been advised by the American Legation,
Bern, Switzerland, that a Russian born Austrian national by the
name of NIKOL BASSECHES, or with a similar first name, desires
to become the European representative of the Tokyo ASAHI SHIMBUN.
It is understood that Basseches is a well-known journalist who
has worked for the weekly WELTWOCHE ZURICH.

It would be appreciated if you would furnish any information
you may have available regarding this person in order that the
Department will be in a position to decide whether or not this
proposed appointment would be agreeable.

November 9, 1945

NICHOLAS BASSECHES

One Nicholas Basseches, who has been described as the son of a former Austrian Consul in St. Petersburg, Russia, was known to have visited the house of an individual in Washington, D. C. who was being surveilled by one of the Government agencies because of suspicious activities. The address of the registered owner of an automobile carrying another visitor to this house was found to be c/o the Japanese Embassy in Washington, D. C.

The New York Times for June 17, 1943, related that Nicholas Basseches had written a book entitled "The Unknown Army", the topic of which was the Russian Army and its activities in the conflict just passed.

A weekly magazine "Nosotros", said to be the organ of Casa De La Cultura y Asistencia Social (House of Culture and Social Assistance) reputedly the center of Communist activities in Havana, Cuba, carried an article written by Nicholas Basseches entitled "El Hombre de Rusia" (The Man of Russia) which praised Stalin.
