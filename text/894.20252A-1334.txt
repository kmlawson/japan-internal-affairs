AMERICAN CONSULATE
Las Palmas de Gran Canaria, Canary Islands, February 19, 1942.

SUBJECT: Films of Japanese Propaganda Shown by Spanish Jesuit Missionary

THE HONORABLE
THE SECRETARY OF STATE
WASHINGTON, 1942

SIR:

I have the honor to enclose a copy and a translation of an advertisement in the newspaper "Palmage", of February 19, 1942, of a film entitled "Modern Japan", shown in a Las Palmas theater, indicating that a lecture on the subject is to be given by a Spanish Jesuit Priest.

In the same newspaper an article was published reporting that a film "Japanese Blood" was shown at the theater on February 18th with comments by Father Domenzain, who is the priest of the parish in Japan that was founded by Saint Francis Xavier in the 16th Century, and that Father Domenzain, of Navarra, was given 50 such films by the Japanese Foreign Ministry and he has been showing them throughout Spain, during the present year, before he returns to his parish.

The film "Japanese Blood" appears to concern the 26 martyrs of Nagasaki, of the 16th Century and, from the description, seems to be made up mostly of beautiful landscapes.

From the newspaper account, the purpose of Father Domenzain in showing the pictures and delivering the lectures, is to collect money for further missionary work.

The priest's purpose is obviously religious, but the occasion allows for other propaganda such as Saint Francis Xavier's praise of the Japanese, which is translated from the newspaper as follows:
