It has been decided to submit this information in the form of the following supplemental report, which is accompanied by a revised index and source of information sheet, the idea being that the corresponding pages in the section of the report already submitted may be removed and these inserted in their stead, thereby forming one combined report.

THE KAIJI KYODOKWAI (MARINE UNION)

The following extract was translated and is quoted from a pamphlet published in June, 1930, by Kaiji Kyodokwai, an organization which has its head office at 8 Kyo-machi, Kobe, Japan:

History: Under the date of September 8, 1926, the authorities concerned in the Department of Communications handed instructions to the Japan Shipowners' Association, the Japan Seamens' Guild, and the Mariners' Association, directing their amalgamation into a union. In compliance therewith, each of these associations or guilds, through its own resolutions, adopted, formulated, and organized the present Kaiji Kyodokwai on December 24, 1926. It took about six years from the time of the Genoa Conference in 1920 for Japan, one of the parties of the conference and of certain treaties for the betterment of seamen, etc., to organize the union.

Object: To throw light on the subject of capital versus labor in shipping and navigation.

Organization: A joint body or union of Associations of Shipowners and of seamen.

Undertakings:
(1) To adjust the demand and supply of labor.
(2) To promote harmony between owners or operators and the crew, and to bring about better mutual understanding.
(3) To assist in seamens employment bureau work.
(4) To prevent disputes and to act as arbitrators in matters relating to the treatment of crews of vessels.

|  Position | Minimum Actual Sea Service Years | Grs. Tons, Vessels | Grs. Tons, Vessels over 3,500 | Grs. Tons, Vessels over 3,500  |

A sailor detailed as a storekeeper, and a fireman as assistant engineer (on watch) shall have an allowance (extra) of Yen 3.00 per month; such a sailor or a fireman shall not exceed one each to a vessel.

Term of apprenticeship in each department shall be one year from the date of the shipment on board the vessel, during which term a monthly allowance of Yen 15.00 shall be paid; persons serving the apprentice term of one year, and serving in excess of this period for a term of less than six months, shall be paid Yen 27.00 per month, and those serving in excess of the six months and up to the completion of the second year shall be paid Yen 32.00 per month.

# JAPANESE LAW CONCERNING SHIP'S OFFICERS

Japanese law specifies certain requirements pertaining to ship's officers; the following data is translated and quoted from Chapter II of Section II of the Genko Kaiji Horei Kuisho (Collection of Marine Laws and Regulations Now in Force), tenth edition (the latest available), published June 23, 1930, by the Kairiku Unyu Jihosha (Sea and Land Transportation Times' Offices), 118 Ishigatsuji Tennoji, Osaka, Japan:

## Law Relating to Ship's Officers

Article 1. Japanese vessels, except in cases designated to the contrary by ordinance or by Government instructions, shall be manned by officers in accordance with the present law. The term "Ship's officers" shall be construed to mean the master, first mate, second mate, third mate, chief engineer, first assistant engineer, second assistant engineer, and third assistant engineer.

Article 2. Only holders of licenses may be qualified to serve as officers.

Article 3. Licenses shall be of the following twelve classes:

- Master: Class A, B, or C.
- First Mate: Class A, B, or C.
- Second Mate: Class A, B.
- Chief Engineer.
- First Engineer.
- Third Engineer.

Article 4. The regular staff of officers to be detailed to an ocean-going vessel, and the classes of licenses such officers shall hold, shall be as prescribed in Table No. 1:

Table No. 1, Part 1. Masters and Mates: For passenger ships exceeding 3,000 gross tons, or for non-passenger ships exceeding 5,000 gross tons:

|  Officer | Class of License | Number Officers  |
| --- | --- | --- |
|  Master | A | 1  |
|  First Mate | A | 1  |
|  Second Mate | A | 1  |
|  Third Mate | A | 1  |
