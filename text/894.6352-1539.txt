Office Memorandum • UNITED STATES GOVERNMENT

TO: JK - Mr. Whitman
DATE: December 17, 1946
FROM: JK - McCormick.
SUBJECT: Copper Stocks in Japan.

Apparently these large stocks are actually held, the main reason
being that they were taken over from Jap Army and Navy holdings.

The figures quoted in the attached cable are from SCAP cable Z-17356,
10 Sept. 1946, which adds that monthly consumption is about 2,500 tons,
and that there will be a surplus in Japan until rehabilitation demands
increase. However, the June Export Program lists only 5,000 tons as
available, and the July 2 export Program fails to mention copper at all.
This I take to be an oversight, and the September cable is in all probability
authoritative.

USCC, in cable WAR 81381 23 Sept, said they could take as much copper
as Tokyo could spare, ignoring the brass and bronze. They further stated
they preferred electrolytic in view of short supply here. IR informs us
that copper is no longer under allocation, but that unless the specifications
are standard, the tonnage should be sent here for auction. Electrolytic
copper's specifications are usually standard and the UK Mission could buy
it out there; USCC feels that they may as well do the dickering there. The
supplies are not ready for shipment yet, and if it were auctioned here a
currency problem would develop. Probably it is better to await Tokyo
developments.

Dear Arthur:

Our Embassy in London advises that the UK Liaison Mission in Tokyo is being instructed to "stake a claim" for a share of whatever copper and copper scrap is made available for export from Japan.

It is our view that in disposing of copper, a commodity no longer subject to international allocation, SCAP should be guided by the principle that his primary objective is to obtain as large a return as possible. In other words we do not favor at this time the use of any allocating formula to divide available Japanese copper among prospective purchases and would be prepared to see the entire quantity sold in the market which would not SCAP the greatest return.

In view of the above we believe you should keep SCAP currently advised of copper prices in the U.S. in order that SCAP will know what action to take on any offers received from other countries. It is expected that the USCC will represent in Tokyo the U.S. demand for copper.

Sincerely yours,

cc: SCAP-FOLAD, Tokyo
Lt. Col. Hartman (CAD)
