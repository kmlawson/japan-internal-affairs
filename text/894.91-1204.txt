Following is text of Embassy's letter published in this morning's JAPAN TIMES AND ADVERTISER: "To the Editor:

While it is not the usual practice of the American Embassy in Tokyo to take public cognizance of inaccuracies appearing in the press with regard to the policies and actions of the United States, the Embassy is constrained under present circumstances to depart from that practice in respect of certain specific charge put forward in the editorial of the October 31 issue of JAPAN TIMES AND ADVERTISER part it was alleged therein that the United States had presented at Chungking certain demands which, if accepted, would create a mortgage on China, these demands, specifically, begin: First, exclusive rights to maintain and utilize four unnamed naval bases in China

April 16, 1940.

Tokyo's 4585, March 13, 1940, "Information with regard to the English language press in Tokyo".

Since Mr. Toshi Go became Managing Director and Editor of the Japan Times on January 10, 1940 there has been apparent an effort by that paper to appeal to the American point of view in presenting the case for Japanese policy in the Far East. An American newspaper man from Salt Lake City has been employed and a Chicago newspaperman is reported also to have been engaged. Opening salaries have been doubled to attract American talent and now stand at the equivalent of $100 United States currency a month.

An American informant states that the Japanese Foreign Office subsidizes the Japan Times to the extent of at least 7,000 yen a month.

The Embassy has received information (probably from the Fleishers who own the Japan Advertiser) that the Japanese Foreign Office has not made (direct) attempts to buy the Japan Advertiser but that the Asahi made overtures looking to the purchase of the paper; that a German offer (not from the
