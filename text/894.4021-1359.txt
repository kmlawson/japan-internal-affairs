DEPARTMENT OF STATE
DIVISION OF FAR EASTERN AFFAIRS
June 25, 1942

Reference the underlying letter from Mr. Hoover of the Federal Bureau of Investigation with which there was transmitted a chronological table of Japanese literature.

FE believes that this book in the Japanese language would be of more value to the Library of Congress than to the Department. It is suggested, therefore, that unless there should be some possible objection owing to the fact that the book was apparently left by a Japanese who was detained at the Green Brier Hotel at White Sulphur Springs, West Virginia, the book be forwarded to the Library of Congress.

SD may perceive some reason that this book should be handed over to a custodian of enemy property or be given to the representative of Japanese interests in this country.

There is attached a draft letter to the Library of Congress.

June 26, 1942

To: [redacted] - Mr. Hamilton
From: SD - Mr. Green

SD perceives no objection to sending the attached book in the Japanese language to the Library of Congress provided that the accompanying letter to Mr. MacLeish indicates that the Japanese book in question is on indefinite loan from the Department to the Library of Congress. Such a proviso would make it possible for the book to be returned without embarrassment to its owner, Mr. Tamon Mayeda, in the event that he should request the return of his property after the war.

Joseph C. Green

DEPARTMENT OF STATE

JUL 8 1942

Honorable Adolf A. Berle, Jr.
Assistant Secretary of State
Department of State
Washington, D.C.

My dear Mr. Berle:

I am transmitting herewith a chronological table of Japanese literature, which was left at the Greenbrier Hotel, White Sulphur Springs, West Virginia, by Mr. Tamon Mayeda, former Director of the Japanese Institute in New York City, who was detained at the Greenbrier Hotel with other Japanese nationals and diplomats awaiting repatriation to Japan.

This book sets out information concerning Japanese authors, the time in which they lived, the works they produced and principal events that were happening in the world at the time they lived. It is indexed according to dates, authors and books produced. Japanese translators have said that it appears to be quite a scholarly piece of research work in its field.

It is suggested that, if this book is of no value to the State Department, it be transmitted to the Library of Congress as an addition to the Japanese Department there.

Sincerely yours,

Enclosure
