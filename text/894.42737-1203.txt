visit to Japan. Professor Massip, who holds degrees from Habana, Columbia, and Paris Universities; has also studied in Berlin, and was formerly Cuban Minister to Mexico, has expressed his object in coming to Japan in the following language: "I have come here not only as a scholar (of geography) who wishes to come into touch with Japanese scholars of the same subject, and not only as a student who wishes to study the geographical conditions of Japan, but I have come to try to establish some permanent cultural intercourse between Japan and Cuba. So far the two countries have had no connection whatsoever, and if I could establish some system of exchanging professors or students, it would be my greatest happiness."

Professor Massip stated that the inspiration for his trip to Japan had come from two Japanese, Mr. Naokata Yamazaki of the Tokyo Imperial University and Mr. Kaoru Tanaka of the Kobe University of Commerce, whom he had known intimately in New York and Habana respectively, as well as through an interest in the geographical situation of Japan which, he asserted, is similar to that of Cuba.

Respectfully yours,

Joseph C. Grew

The book was written by Salvador Masui, of the Miami Mining Co. and is on the "Scholar" structure of the Japanese archipelago.

When Professor Masui regarding his object in coming to Japan he said:

"I have some here not only as a scholar who wishes to come into touch with Japanese scholars of the same subject and not only as a student who wishes to study the geographical conditions of Japan but I have come to try to establish some permanent cultural intercourse between Japan and Cuba. So far the two countries have had no connection whatsoever, and if I could establish some system of exchanging professors or students it would be my greatest happiness."

Being asked how Mr. Masui had taken such an interest in Japan, he said:

## JAPAN AND CUBA ALIMA

"There are many reasons. First of all the geographical and geological position of Japan and Cuba are so similar, that in the course of my studies I found myself extremely attracted to this far away country. The next reason which practically motivated my coming to Japan was the friendship which was born between myself and Mr. Nackata Yagomaki of the Tokyo Imperial University in New York at the Professor's Congress at Cambridge University. Also, Mr. Kaoru Tanaka of

Professor Salvador Masui of the University of Hokkaido who is in Tokyo to promote cultural relations between Japan and Cuba.

the Kobe University of Commerce, who I came to view in Hokkaido and Mr. Nobuyuki Inouye of the Tokyo Higher Women's Normal School School I met in Amsterdam, where great inspirations to me. Through them I came to take an interest in the culture of spirit of the people, and furthermore, the astonishing development of Japan since the Meiji Restoration. This was something which I could not understand until I had put my feet upon the soil of Japan. I now understand it is the spiritual forces which are beneath the Japanese people.

"If we only could have more people like Mr. Sakae Nanjyo Japanese charge d'affaires in Cuba, continued Mr. Masui, "more interest in Japan could be promoted. Mr. Nanjyo is not only a political enemy, but he is also a cultural enemy."

Mr. Masui has been commissioned by the University of Hokkaido to prevent diplomas to four Japanese members of the Geographical Institute of Hokkaido. The reviewers are Mr. Takeo Kato of the Tokyo Imperial University, Mr. Sadakatsu Tokuda of Mitsu Company, and Mr. Goritsu Hosokawa, the President of the Geographical Institute of Japan. Professor Masui concluded by saying, "Through the kindness of Count Kuroda of the K.B.S., Mr. S. Yoshizawa, head of the American Section of the Foreign Office, and Mr. Eiichi Shibunawa, I am learning and accomplishing much, and when I return to Cuba I shall devote a great portion of my time writing and lecturing on Japan."

Mr. and Mrs. Masui intend to stay in Japan for about five months, and will visit the historical spots of Kansai.
