Dear Mr. Linville:

In the early '20s West Coast millers used to ship flour to Japan and Korea. Since VJ Day, I have tried unsuccessfully to find out something about the flour milling capacity in Japan and what the prospects are of American mills entering that market.

An agent representing a West Coast mill has cabled for quotations and, I am given to understand, anticipated that some barter arrangement could be effected.

I would greatly appreciate it if you could advise me how such a transaction could be implemented, or cause this inquiry to be placed in the proper channels to the end that I can tell the miller how to proceed.

Very truly yours,

Martin F. Smith

Dear Martin:

Reference is made to your letter of September 8, 1947 inquiring as to flour milling capacity in Japan and the prospects of export of flour to Japan and Korea by American mills.

From the fragmentary information available here, it appears Japanese flour milling capacity may be as great as 200,000 metric tons per month. In 1940 production averaged about 100,000 metric tons per month according to some reports.

All grain and flour exported to Japan and Korea is procured by the Production and Marketing Administration, United States Department of Agriculture. The West Coast mill to which you refer will be able to secure information on procurement procedure from the Agriculture Department.

I hope the above information will be helpful to you.

Sincerely yours,

Francis A. Linville
Assistant Chief
International Resources Division

Dear Martin:

Reference is made to your letter dated September 30, 1947 asking for information as to how a West Coast miller might enter into a barter arrangement with an agent in Tokyo to the end that he might ship flour to Japan and take Japanese products in return.

While the proposed trading arrangement is unusual, it might possibly be worked out. Since, however, all such arrangements must be made through the Supreme Commander for the Allied Powers, we suggest that you discuss your proposal with Colonel O. J. Baldwin, Civil Affairs Division of the Department of the Army. If Colonel Baldwin thinks that some arrangement might be feasible he will communicate with the Supreme Commander for the Allied Powers in an effort to develop the possibilities.

I hope this information will be useful to you.

Sincerely yours,

Francis A. Linville
Assistant Chief
International Resources Division

Mr. Martin F. Smith,

SUBJECT: Suggested change in letter.

It appears to us that the attached draft is perhaps somewhat more negative than it needs to be. We would like to suggest that a new draft be drawn incorporating your first and third paragraphs as they stand and a second paragraph that would read as follows:

While it is unusual, it might possibly be washed out.

Once, however, all such arrangements must be made through the American Commander for the Allied Powers, we suggest that you discuss your proposal with Col. C. J. Baldwin, Civil Affairs Division of the Department of the Army. If Col.
