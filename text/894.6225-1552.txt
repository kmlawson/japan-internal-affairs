SUBJECT: Sales of Japanese Silk

PARTICIPANTS: USCC Board of Directors

COPIES TO: Mr. Wilcox - ITP
Mr. Kennedy - IR

Mr. Whitman - JK

OFFICE OF INTERNATIONAL TRADE POLICY
JUL 18 1946
DEPARTMENT OF STATE

The type of auction to be arranged for the next sale was the principal subject of the meeting. The first sale had been the offering of 5,350 bales to be sold on the basis of sealed bids and on the basis of grading by the United States Testing Company. The present plan is to offer 8,000 bales within the near future with much of this next lot being offered on Japanese grading because the United States Testing Company has not been able to keep pace with the quantity of silk received.

The silk trade has expressed considerable opposition to the sealed bid method of distribution. USCC desires to institute open auctions with upset prices but opinion differed as to the advisability of such change in the next sale.

Some favored immediate change to the open auction method on the grounds that this would satisfy trade objections and would accomplish the change without the danger of appearing later to have been compelled by pressure to do so and upon the further grounds that this would be a direct step toward more normal marketing procedures.

Other

=== Page 7 === (confidence 0.99)
-2-

Other directors emphasized that offerings upon Japanese grading would be a new and uncertain factor in the forthcoming sale and that such uncertainty ought not to be combined with a basic change in the type of auction for fear of reducing prices to an unwarranted extent. It was argued that more information regarding the volume and type of demand is needed in establishing sound upset prices and that such information might well be obtained from another use of the sealed bid scheme. It was thought that upset prices established entirely upon the basis of price information yielded by the first sale might not be a very sound basis for an open auction so soon. The consensus of opinion was that the overall realization in the first sale had been quite satisfactory and that an immediate change to the open auction method would involve some danger of reducing the realization. Proponents of the open auction method conceded that the sealed bid plan possibly maximized the immediate realization but felt that this plan probably fails to encourage manufacturers to undertake sizable operating schedules requiring continuous supplies and therefore may not be in the best interests of volume usage or highest prices over a period of time.

The decision was to arrange the next sale as another sealed bid sale with minor modifications of procedure on the basis of experience with the first offering. Announcements will be issued within a few days. Two weeks will be allowed for submission of bids. After the second sale the change to an open auction plan will again be considered.

Dear Mr. Whitman,

**Subject: Silk from Japan**

Will you kindly refer to your letter dated 11th September, 1946, to Mr. Farookhi, regarding imports of raw silk from Japan? I understand that at the informal meeting held on Monday last, it was agreed that there is no objection to raising India's quota to the extent necessary to enable her to meet her full two million pounds requirements. We are grateful for this.

2. Our immediate requirements, however, are approximately 700,000 pounds in 20 x 22 deniers and 300,000 pounds in 13 x 15 deniers, both preferably of International Grade C, D and E. In case superior grades are not available we are prepared to accept grades F and G.

3. Incidentally, I may mention that silk prices in India are now going down and I shall be glad if you can give us an idea of the present trend of prices (F.A.S. Japanese port) of raw silk in which India is interested.

4. We have no objection to a separate quota of 900 bales being allotted to New Zealand but feel that this should be debited to Australia's share of available supplies.

Sincerely yours,

P.A. Menon

In your letter of September 29 you inquire regarding prices. We are recommending that silk sold to India be priced on the basis of the average prices obtained by the U.S. Commercial Company for the same grades and dealers in the New York market at sales nearest to the dates of sales to India, adjusted downward to allow for the ocean freight and insurance costs included in these prices. For your information the freight rate on raw silk, Japan to New York, in the recent past has been $8.50 per hundred pounds or 8.5 cents per pound; and the marine and war risk insurance rate has been 70 cents per $100. of value or .7 percent. A U.S. Commercial Company press release of September 20 gave the prices obtained for silk of various grades and dealers at the auction sale conducted on September 18. We do not have an extra copy of this press release but are informed that a copy was sent to the Agent General for India by the USCC. Of course this information is also made available to GHQ, SCAP. The second auction sale will be held in New York in a few days.

My dear Mr. Culbertson:

This is in reply to your letter of December 9, 1948
to Mr. McCornick of this Division concerning your client's
report that there will be a 10% increase in the price of
Japanese silk on January 1, 1949, and his belief that the
French have been receiving undue quantities of Japanese
silk.

The Department of State has not been informed that
there is an impending rise in the price of Japanese silk.
It is known, of course, that the guaranteed period expires
on January 1, 1949, and it is consequently expected that
SCAP will make an announcement in the next few days.

I find it difficult to understand the statement of
your client regarding French silk purchases in Japan
which make it difficult for other buyers to enter the
market. The Department accordingly has requested information
in this regard from SCAP officials. When it is
received I will communicate with you again.

If the Department can be of further service to you,
please do not hesitate to write.

We have your letter of December 13, in which you indicate that the Board of Trade has asked you to take up with the Department of State the charges to be deducted from U.S.A. auction prices to arrive at prices f.o.b. Japan for raw silk.

We are of the opinion that this is a proper matter to be decided in Tokyo. Although we have not yet had a reply to this telegram we assume that SCAP has made definite proposals to the UK trade representative.
