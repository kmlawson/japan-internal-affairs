Filed Station Place Guam, M.I. Date 31 March, 1939
Intelligence Officer Cdr. G. W. JOHNSON, USN Name of C. O. Capt. J. T. ALEXANDER, USN
C. O. George Scharff, Fish Warden Island of Guam
(Name) (City) (Index title) Movement of vessel

Between 1300 and 1400 on 29 March, 1939, George Scharff, Fish Warden, Island of Guam, out fishing in his boat off Rittidian Point came around the point to westward and sighted a vessel which he believed to be diesel driven, about 125 tons burden, equipped with radio antenna and direction finder, two masts, one stack. Ship was painted white with yellow stack, two rings (white). No sails on her. Boat was slung in davits, not secured. The weather was squally and he approached to within about 200 yards of the ship. The ship was lying to and got underway rapidly, apparently as soon as Mr. Scharff was sighted. The ship stood off in a northeasterly direction. About fifteen (15) Japanese were board. They were not fishing. When sighted the ship was about three (3) miles west of Uruno Point. She was in sight for about one-half (1/2) hour before getting underway.

This report was not made until two (2) days later. Mr. Scharff has been instructed to report immediately in the future if he sights other vessels in order that ship may be sent to investigate, but in this case it is doubtful if the vessel could have been overtaken as Scharff states she made off at about ten (10) or eleven (11) knots.

The Chief of Police, Island of Guam, has been directed to make a thorough search of the re-around Uruno Point. Dense jungle growth, however, exists in that area. Report will be made if anything is discovered.

Note: This information is considered to be very reliable, insofar as source is concerned.

Submitted by G. W. JOHNSON, Comdr.
