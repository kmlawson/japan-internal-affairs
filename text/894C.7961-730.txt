AMERICAN CONSULATE
Dairen, Manchuria, March 16, 1931.

SUBJECT: British Airplane Violation of Fortified Zone,
Kwantung Leased Territory
SOLICITOR'S OFFICE
APR 14 1931
SAMPLING OF STATE

THE SECRETARY OF STATE,
WASHINGTON.

SIR:

I have the honor to report the outcome of the recent case of a British airplane which flew over fortified area in the Kwantung Leased Territory.

On February 9, 1931, an airplane appeared from the north, flew over the city of Dairen, turned back on its course, and landed at the aviation field outside the city. The plane was discovered to be one owned by the Far East Aviation Company, a British commercial organization with headquarters reported to be at Hongkong. It was in the charge of a civilian pilot and a civilian mechanic who had been entrusted with flying the machine from Mukden to Tientsin. The two men stated that they had been following a railway which they thought was the railway from Mukden to Tientsin, and had met a blizzard, making accurate observation impossible. They stated that their first knowledge of being in the Kwantung Leased Territory came with discovering the end of the railway at Dairen.

The Acting British Consul immediately took up the matter with the authorities, submitted an apology, and asked for the release of the man. They were not re-

leased,

leased, but were left in the Acting British Consul's care until the case might be decided.

There was considerable delay during which interval the Acting British Consul, according to my information, repeated insistent requests for the immediate release of the men and the recognition of the accidental nature of their arrival here. The military authorities appeared willing to drop the matter but the local civil authorities evidently insisted that the violation be brought to trial. The outcome was a summary court (ryakushiki saiban) procedure. The pilot was found guilty of infringing the regulations against flying over the fortified area and was fined two hundred yen or forty days hard labor. The defendant was not present and was neither asked to be present nor to offer evidence to the court, either in person or through attorney. From the decision of such a summary court it is possible to appeal to the ordinary courts, where the usual means of defense are available. However, much as the Acting British Consul was annoyed by the decision, no appeal was made because of the delay which would inevitably follow. The fliers immediately afterward sailed to Shanghai, shipping their plane with them.

The Acting British Consul informs me that he has passed the entire case on to the British Embassy in Tokyo for negotiation through the Foreign Office.

Respectfully yours,

Cabot Coville
American Consul.

Enclosure to unnumbered despatch to Legation, dated July 25th, 1921, on the subject "Reported Flight of American Planes Over Japanese Fortified Zone Near Bairam."

American Consulate, Chafee, China, July 25th, 1921.

William R. Langdon, Esquire,
American Consul,
Bairam.

Sir:

Permit me to inform you, for such action as you may care to take in the matter, that the Japanese Consul at Chafee has today verbally advised me that, in compliance with instructions from his Government, it is desired that the American Consul at Bairam express his regrets on behalf of the Captain of the U. S. Aircraft Tender "JASON" stationed at Chafee to the Governor General of Nantong Island Territory at Fort Arthur for the appearance on July 14th, last, about 8:30 a.m., of three planes over the Japanese fortified zone near Bairam.

Upon investigation of this matter, I find that three planes belonging to the U. S. Aircraft Tender "JASON" flew at an altitude of approximately 2,300 feet westward of Round Island (Latitude 35°40'30" North, Longitude 122°8'40" East) and three miles to the north of Enaccenter Rock (Latitude 35°04' North, Longitude 121°20' East) on July 14th at 8:30 a.m. in a navigation flight to these points and return to Chafee. The navigators of these three hydroplanes appear to have been ignorant of having flown over any Japanese territory or any fortified zones.

I am of the opinion that the above is a true statement of the facts in the case. I have been informed that orders have been given by the Commander of the U. S. Aircraft Tender "JASON" for his planes not to appear in that vicinity. No objections to previous flights of a similar nature have reached me. Further, I am informed that, so far as is known, none of the planes from the U. S. Aircraft Tender "JASON", during its present stay at Chafee, have flown over any known fortified zone.

Very respectfully yours,

Lynn W. Franklin,
American Consul.
