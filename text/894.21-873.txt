# Military Power and Autumn Mancouvres:

A further indication of the power of the Military clique in opposition of the Government appears in the controversy regarding the attendance of the Emperor at the autumn Mancouvres in Kyushu. The Government contends that it will be a more impressive demonstration to the world of its determination in its attitude toward the situation in Manchuria if the Emperor feels it of sufficient importance to cancel his trip to Kyushu in order to be present in Tokyo when the League meets on November 16th. The Military claim that it will be more impressive if the meeting of the League is not considered of sufficient importance to cause the Emperor to change his plans. As usual the army has won and the Emperor will not cancel his plans to attend the Mancouvres.

V.

* Monthly Report for November, 1930.

AMERICAN CONSULATE GENERAL
HARBIN CHINA, June 26, 1933.

SUBJECT: JAPANESE AIR MANOEUVRES.

The Honorable
Nelson Trusler Johnson,
American Minister,
Peiping, China.

Sir:

I have the honor to quote below, as of possible interest to the Military Attache, an extract from a letter dated June 19th received from Seoul, Korea:

"The last three days here the Japanese have been having air manoeuvres. Search light, machine guns, and anti-aircraft guns have been stationed all over the city. At night no light can be left on unless it is covered with black cloth. All the autos and street cars have been going around with queer blindors over the headlights and there are no street lights at all. When a plane comes over head all the guns in the district let go and you can't hear yourself thinking, it's so loud. The planes never try to dive out of a search light beam but fly contentedly as the nothing could ever happen to them even if it was the real thing."

My informant stated that similar practices had taken place at Pyengyang, Korea, and in Mukden.

Respectfully yours,

G. C. Hanson
G. C. Hanson
American Consul General.

(e) Army and Navy Maneuvers.

During the month of August both the Japanese Army and the Navy staged maneuvers which were obviously of a propaganda nature. On the nights of August 8th, 9th, and 10th the Army staged air raids over the Kanto district, which included both Tokyo and Yokohama. Elaborate regulations were issued regarding control of lights and civilian groups were organized to help enforce them. 162, Aug. 5, 11am.
Department's 116, August 1, 1pm.

One. It would be advisable for the Secretary of War to visit Japan. Failure to do so if other countries are to be visited would be interpreted as a slight. It would be impossible to make the visit unofficial and an official visit would mean a stay of say five days. The visit could be made either on the way to or from Manila.

Two. The Embassy should be notified at least a month in advance of (a) the date of arrival (b) the name of the ship and (c) the duration of stay. The date of the visit must not conflict with the army grand maneuvers (November 5 to 16, inclusive) as the Emperor and high officials will then be absent from Tokyo.

NEVILLE
