SYNOPSIS: Subject ascertained to have moved to Laguna Beach, California, to allegedly go into business at that city with an unidentified individual. No information uncovered as to Subject's activities in San Francisco.

REFERENCE: Report of Special Agent F. S. SLATER, San Francisco, California, dated October 26, 1940.

DETAILS: AT SAN FRANCISCO, CALIFORNIA:

The title of this case is being changed to show Subject's full name as SHIGEO ITO. This is not a positive identification, but is believed correct, based on information that SHIGEO ITO was registered at the Yamato Hotel, San Francisco at the time that Mr. ITO was alleged to be staying there; and because the description contained in this file of the Subject conforms with that of SHIGEO ITO.

At the Yamato Hotel, 717 California Street, the desk clerk whose name was not asked in order not to arouse suspicion showed the writer a postcard from the Subject which had been

mailed from Laguna Beach, California, on September 8, 1941 and which stated that he had decided to locate at that point and that his address would be in care of General Delivery.

The desk clerk advised that the Subject had checked out of the Yamato Hotel on August 28, 1941, and upon leaving had informed him that he was planning to go into business with another Japanese whose name he did not know.

It was also learned from this desk clerk that the Subject had stayed at the Yamato at various times since early 1938, but that none of his sojourns had been long.

Confidential Informant S. F. 9 advised that they had no recent information on the Subject, that he was of no particular interest to them and that they did not know the Subject's present whereabouts.

Confidential Informant S. F. 496 advised that he had never heard of the Subject or of any under-cover passport activities in this area.

For the benefit of the Los Angeles Field Division, which has no previous information on this case, the following summary is presented:

A letter from the Bureau to the San Francisco Field Division, dated February 20, 1940, and captioned: Mr. ITO -- PASSPORT MATTER, advised that LLOYD LAWSON PATTERSON, the Subject in another matter, had stated that a group in San Francisco, California was allegedly dealing in passports and that it was directed by a Mr. ITO, who resided at the Yamato Hotel in the above-mentioned city. PATTERSON described the Subject as being 50 years old, 5'5", and weighing 130 pounds.

Police and credit records at San Francisco, California, failed to reveal any information concerning the Subject.

The files of Confidential Informant S. F. 9 as checked on June 21, 1940, by Special Agent N. J. ALAGA, at San Francisco, reflect that in July of 1939 on e DONALD F.

C., above the rear license plate; Mr. BERINGER states that ITO paid them in American Express Company Traveler's Checks of $20.00 denomination. Mr. BERINGER states that when the ITOS first rented this place the wife came in and made all the arrangements. The wife is a white woman. Mr. BERINGER states that he was surprised to find that her husband was a Japanese, so he asked Mrs. ITO if they were just married and she replied that they had been married seventeen years. Mrs. ITO also told him that they were from San Francisco. During the ITOS' stay there Mrs. BERINGER was talking to SHIGE: ITO and he told her that he was a lecturer and intended to speak at various churches on his return to San Francisco. Mrs. BERINGER states that she asked ITO what he lectured on, and he replied, 'Always peace'. Mrs. BERINGER then asked ITO what church they belonged to and ITO replied that he belonged to no specific church but lectured at any church he was asked to. ITO also told Mr. and Mrs. BERINGER that he sold dishes. Mr. BERINGER recalled that when ITO was talking to him about returning to San Francisco he stated he was going to San Diego first to lecture, then to Long Beach and on up to San Francisco, and when he left he gave the Yamamoto Hotel in San Francisco as a forwarding address. The BERINGER states that SHIGE: ITO seemed to have plenty of money and appeared to be a very intellectual Japanese. Mr. ITO, during his stay, gave Mrs. BERINGER a sample of Japanese tea and also cooked up a Japanese dish, of which he brought the BERINGER's sample; and the BERINGER stated they liked this so well they asked ITO for the recipe, so ITO, in the presence of Mrs. BERINGER and in the BERINGER front room, wrote out this recipe and gave it to Mrs. BERINGER.

"The BERINGER" state that the ITOS had no visitors while they stayed there and seemed to stay pretty close to their apartment all the time, and only appeared to have two or three pieces of luggage. They seemed to be very pleasant to talk to. Mr. BERINGER states that he recalls asking Mrs. ITO how her husband would feel if the United States and Japan went to war and Mrs. ITO replied that her husband was a very strong American. Mrs. ITO also stated at one time they lived in Japan for several months; and then Mr. ITO later on told Mr. BERINGER that during the first world war he did a lot of work in Washington, D.C. and was often in the White House. Mr. BERINGER states that ITO didn't explain what kind of work he did in Washington, D. C. Mrs. BERINGER states that the conversation then turned to the Jews, and SHIGE: ITO stated that there wasn't a business big or small that the Jews didn't have control of. Mrs. BERINGER states that she agrees with Mr. ITO on this issue. SHIGE: ITO then told the BERINGER that he had a very close friend who is a dentist and recommended that the BERINGER's visit him in case they need any dental work. The BERINGER cannot recall this dentist's name, but do recall that his office was on the second floor in a building located at First and San Pedro Streets, Los Angeles. Mr. BERINGER also doesn't recall any mail coming for the ITOS while they were there. Mr. BERINGER states that the ITOS didn't have a radio. Mrs. BERINGER states

that the ITOS appeared to be ultra-religious, and on one occasion when Mrs. BERINGER took some linens over to their apartment she observed an open Bible on the table. Mrs. BERINGER states that it appeared strange to her that Mrs. ITO did a lot of writing in long hand, and Mr. ITO explained that this wife was a great help to him in his work. The BERINGERS states that they recall no remarks being made relative to Germany, or England or China by the ITOS. Mrs. BERINGER states that the ITOS seemed to have lots of groceries with them. The BERINGERS do not remember of over hearing Mrs. ITO's first name. Mrs. ITO called Mr. ITO just 'Ito'. Mr. ITO told Mrs. BERINGER that his wife was born in England. Then Mr. BERINGER told Mr. ITO that he had read in a newspaper about a man with a similar name (ITO) being affiliated with the Japanese government in Japan, and ITO replied, that, yes, he knew the man.

"The BERINGER describe ITOS' ITO as approximately fifty years old, 5'2", 175 pounds, dark eyes, extremely neat dresser; wore gray suit and hat and speaks very good English with very little oriental accent; smooth shaven, and they don't recall whether he wore glasses or not; he drove a late model Dodge, pearl gray, sedan. The BERINGERS describe Mrs. ITO as approximately thirty-seven years of age, 5'4", 140 pounds, medium brown hair, do not recall whether she wore glasses or not; pale complexion, no cosmetics; usually wore blue afternoon dress; speaks very good English."
