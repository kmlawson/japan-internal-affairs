Office Memorandum • UNITED STATES GOVERNMENT

TO: Mr. Abbot L. Moffat
DATE: Sept. 19, 1944
FROM: Mr. Walter Kahn
SUBJECT: Adequate Staff to Examine Enemy Files.

We have been carefully formulating a policy at the Interdivisional Area Committee with respect to Enemy Consular and Diplomatic files which presumably would be of substantial value and available to us upon the unconditional surrender of Japan, except those files which are under the protection of a neutral country which could not be induced to surrender them.

There would be a staggering volume of material to be examined and analyzed and it seemed to me, particularly with regard to the Japanese files, that the volume of work to be done is likely to be beyond the capacity of any available staff unless recruiting and training is undertaken well in advance. I advanced this thought at the meeting and was encouraged to see how the matter stood. Mrs. Correll suggested getting in touch with the Office of Foreign Service. I got hold of Mr. Bechhoefer up there who said that while they recognized the problem, preparations for Europe have been absorbing all their efforts and that they are not far along in their preparations for the Far East.

I suppose that OSS would be a good place to inquire about this matter but I have not yet made any connections over there and so will leave the matter until you return.

Chief, Division on Cultural Relations
Department of State
Washington, D.C.
U. S. A.

Dear Mr. Cherrington:

The Kokusai Bunka Shinkokai takes great pleasure in announcing a world-wide prize essay contest to be held under its auspices and direction this year to commemorate the 2,600th anniversary of the founding of the Empire of Japan. Being an auspicious year of great national significance and, to be sure, of no little international interest, it is our fond hope that our project will realize a hearty response and active participation in all parts of the world. The contest will be conducted according to rules and regulations, a copy of which has been enclosed.

May we take this liberty to invite your support and prevail upon your kindness to interest your friends and potential contestants in this project. It is our sincere hope to have this undertaking serve as a means of contributing to a greater understanding of Japanese culture abroad and to the furtherance of closer international cultural relations.

Respectfully yours,

M. Nagai
Matsuzo Nagai,
Chairman.
