THE HONORABLE
THE SECRETARY OF STATE,
WASHINGTON.

SIR:

I have the honor to report the following important changes in the Taiwan Government General as a result of the accession to power of the Seiyukai cabinet on December 13, 1931.

Former Governor General Masahiro Ohta tendered his resignation to Premier Imai on February 18, 1932, but the resignation was not accepted until March 2, 1932.

Mr. Hiroshi Minami, a member of the House of Peers, was appointed Governor General on the latter date, but is not expected to arrive in Taiwan until March 29. Mr. Minami was born in Toyama-ken, Japan in October, 1869, the second son of Mr. K. Iwama, and he was adopted by the Minami family. He graduated from the law department of Tokyo Imperial University in 1896. He became a judge in the Administrative Court, was chief secretary in the Saionji cabinet, governor of Fukuoka-ken, vice-minister of education. In 1912 he was made a member of the House of Peers, and holds the court rank of fourth senior grade and second order of merit. He is auditor of the Ujigawa Electric Company. Mr. Minami was long connected

connected with Prince Saionji, the only elder statesman now living, as private secretary, and it is believed that his friendship with the Prince was responsible for his appointment.

His family consists of his wife, son and daughter-in-law. Mr. Minami visited Taiwan once before in 1928 with a party of members of the House of Peers.

Other changes in the Government General have also taken place. Former Director General Kinoshita was ordered to retire on January 13, 1932 and was succeeded by Mr. Hiroyoshi Hiratsuka. Mr. Hiratsuka was born in Yamagata in 1875 of a Samurai family. He graduated in 1902 from the political science department of the Imperial University at Tokyo and entered the civil service the same year. He was councillor of the Fukui government in 1905; administrative secretary in Miyagi and Nagasaki; chief of police of Miye, Niigata and Kanagawa; vice governor of Ehime, Niigata and Hyogo; governor of Tochiki, Nagasaki and Hyogo; and governor of Tokyo from 1925 to 1929.

The new Director General, who ranks second to the Governor General in the civil administration of Taiwan, arrived on February 23, 1932. In a statement to the press on his arrival, he referred to the importance of Taiwan to the Japanese Empire, and to its particularly close economic relations with South China and the South Seas due to its geographic proximity (The South Seas to Japan mean the Philippines and Malaysia). Taiwan's favorable geographic position must be utilized to the fullest extent in developing trade and commerce with neighboring lands.

Another important change was the promotion on January

January 9, 1932 of General Masaki, formerly in command of the Taiwan forces, to be assistant chief of the general staff in Tokyo. This is tantamount to being the actual chief of staff of the Japanese army, as a Prince is always the nominal chief of staff, the prince being a figurehead.

The new commander-in-chief, General Abe, who was commander-in-chief of the Osaka division, arrived in Taiwan on January 28.

Inasmuch as the military in Taiwan exercise a rather rigid censorship on information given out by the civilian government, this change is of importance.

On the arrival of the new Governor General it is expected that there will be wholesale changes of the various bureau chiefs of the Government General, on recommendations made by him.

Respectfully yours,

John B. Ketcham
American Consul.
