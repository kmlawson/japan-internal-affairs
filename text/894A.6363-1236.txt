Gentlemen:

The National Geophysical Company has received an inquiry from the Nippon Mining Company of Tokyo concerning our services in supervising a program of geophysical exploration in Formosa, looking forward after several years to the location of oil deposits and the production of oil.

This letter is written to you to obtain information concerning the regulations and policy of the United States Government toward such work in Formosa. We would like to accept this business; but do not wish to do so, unless it corresponds to the letter and spirit of our duty to our country.

Very truly yours,

NATIONAL GEOPHYSICAL COMPANY

Vice President

January 8, 1941.

Reference letter of December 26, 1940,
from the National Geophysical Company.

The matter of a possible oil survey of
Taiwan by the National Geophysical Company on
behalf of a Japanese principal has been in-
formally discussed with ONI and MID, both of
which offices agree that, from the point of
view of advantage to the United States by
reason of information about Taiwan, it is de-
sirable that an American company make the oil
survey of Taiwan under discussion. It is be-
lieved that if the project is not undertaken
by some American company it will probably be
performed by German engineers.

Neither ONI, MID nor the Department has
information with regard to the National Geo-
physical Company. The Department of Commerce
has no information about the company but ex-
pects to obtain a report from one of its dis-
trict offices.

A representative of one of the large oil
companies (e.g. Mr. Walden) might perhaps be
able to supply information concerning the
National Geophysical Company.
