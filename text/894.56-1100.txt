The Couto Amendment.

The Couto Amendment Not Effective in 1934.
The same Constitution of Brazil which placed into

THE FUTURE OF JAPANESE EMIGRATION.
Now that the last gateway to Japanese emigration
in America has been all but barred, there remain two
alternatives. The first is to seek other lands to
which Japanese settlers may go and the second is to
cease to rely upon emigration as a means of relieving
the pressure of population.
In the opinion of the writer the second alternative
will be the one eventually adopted, not by conscious
direction of policy so much as by admission of facts
as they are. There is no country in America save
possibly Paraguay or Peru where large contingents of
Japanese might settle. Europe is out of the question,
Australasia is barred, other Asia is full of teeming
millions. Manchuria offers area but not climate. In
spite of the efforts of the Japanese General Staff to
oncourage mass migration to "Manchukuo" the results,
to be disoussed below, are not encouraging. If there
ever is a Japanese migration to Manchuria it will be
an ordered migration for strategical purposes; the
establishment of garrisons under the guise of settlers.
It seems probable that, in the face of the dis-
apointing results which have everywhere attended the
polioy of the Japanese Government in encouraging its
nationals to live abroad, the Empire will at length
give up the present polioy of subsidizing emigrants
and rely wholly on accommodating the inorease of
population by industrialization. It is still easier
to introduce Japanese exports to the nations of the
world than to export Japanese.
