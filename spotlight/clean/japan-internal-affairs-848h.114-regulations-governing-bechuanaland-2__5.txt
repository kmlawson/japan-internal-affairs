1. History and Outline of Opium System
in Formosa

At the time Formosa came under the rule of the Japanese Empire, one of the pressing questions that evoked much public attention at home and abroad was what measures should be taken for the control of the habit of opium-smoking prevalent among the islanders. Opinion was divided over the question, one party advocating the immediate suppression of the habit, and another being in favour of a non-interference policy.

Meanwhile, the absolute prohibition of opium-smoking being the long established policy of the Japanese Government, and a law prohibiting opium-smoking and regulations providing for punishing those violating its provisions having been long in effect, the Government considered it as a matter of course that opium-smoking should ultimately be suppressed in Formosa. Upon in vestigation of the condition of things obtaining in the island in those early days, however it was found that a great many islanders had already become hopelessly addicted to the evil habit, so that it was thought next to impossible to put an end to it all at once.

Accordingly with a humanitarian view to letting those confirmed smokers enjoy their remaining days, the Japanese Government decided on adopting a system of gradual suppression by recogniting them as addicts and leaving them alone.

In accord with this decision, the Government General of Formosa issued a declaration in February, 1896, to the effect that non but the Government would be allowed to import opium and the use of opium would be permitted for medical reasons only to habitual smokers under certain limitations.

Subsequently in January, 1897, the Government promulgated the Opium Ordinance for Formosa providing for the total prohibi-

tion of opium-smoking and at the same time granting special licenses to those chronic smokers recognized as such by authorized physicians. It was provided that the licensed smokers would be supplied with prescribed quantities of prepared opium manufactured by the Opium Monopoly Bureau and that the opening of opium smoking rooms and the manufacture and sale of implements used in smoking should be treated as business requiring special government license.

In April of the same year, the Ordinance was put into force, and a statement setting forth its essential points was issued to the general public. At the same time, the Government took up investigation of chronic opium smokers throughout the island, which work was completed in September 1900.

The total number of licensed smokers registered then reached as many as 169,064. It was then decided that no more license should be granted except to such chronic smokers as had been prevented, owing to unavoidable circumstances, from being registered at the time of general registration. On the other hand, the Government authorities in cooperation with school teachers, police officials and social welfare workers made every endeavour to bring home to the public mind the evil of opium-smoking and to prevent the diffusion of the habit among younger people.

As might be expected, however, the harmful habit that had gained ground during ages past could not be broken down at a stroke, and in course of time it was discovered that many people had contracted it in secret. But in consideration of the fact that it would entail intolerable trouble upon the police and judiciary authorities, if punishment had to be meted out to the large host of offenders, licenses were specially granted to such smokers, with the result that recognition was given in 1904—1905 to 30,543 and in 1908 to 15,849 people. This policy consistently pursued by the Government of Formosa has turned out remarkably successful and the object originally aimed at may be said to have been practically accomplished, as clearly borne out by the figures.

In view, however, of the fact that there were still found from time to time some secret habitual smokers, and inconsideration of the enforcement of the Geneva Opium Convention of 1925, the Government of Formosa found it necessary to frame a new policy, so that stricter control could be exercised over opium smoking and various relevant matters as well as nothing might be left undone for making the Convention really effective. By so doing it was desired more especially to accomplish through prevention and correction of habitual smoking and to complete absolute suppression of the vice.

Accordingly, in January, 1929, the Government revised the Opium Ordinance for Formosa by making good whatever defects found in the old system.

In the revised Ordinance, it was made quite clear that licenses would be specially issued only to those persons who had acquired opium habit before the new Ordinance came into effect and that all others should be strictly forbidden to continue the evil practice.

Now according to the old Ordinance, any person who was found opium-smoking without a license was liable to penal servitude or to a fine. But in the new Ordinance monetary penalty was abrogated and punishment by imprisonment only was reserved. This amendment was introduced for no other reason but for curing and preventing the bad habit with better and quicker effect. Further, it was

On the other hand, it was considered that it would never do to leave the matter of prohibiting and correcting the opium habit entirely in the hands of the police force and rely on the effect of punishment only for attaining the object in view, for such step was not only at variance with the cardinal principle of humanity but was also not in conformity with the object of the present Ordinance, not to say it was in fact a matter of impossibility.

One might anticipate that by the time the present Ordinance achieved its object, when the present licenses would pass out of existence, secret opium smokers would also have disappeared through the effect of punishment, corrective measures, death and from other causes. However, it was not thought to adopt any such policy of letting things take their course, for it implied the existence of dark spots in the body of our opium system and could in no sense be taken as furthering its object, namely the absolute suppression of the vice.

In view of this situation, simultaneously with the enforcement of the new Ordinance, the Government decided to grant licenses to those persons only, whose case was pronounced to be too far advanced for remedy, but in the case of relatively young smokers and of those whose habit had not reached an advanced stage, corrective and remedial measures should to be taken.

2. Outline of Control of Opium.

As stated above, after the policy of suppression of opium smoking was instituted, the people in general in the island have been strictly forbidden to smoke opium, with the exception of those who have been found on medical examination to be incurably addicted to the vice. Dealing in opium was made a monopoly business of the Government, the production, manufacture and consumption thereof being placed under the control of the Government. The control of opium is provided for in the Opium Ordinance and in the Regulations for the Enforcement of the Ordinance, and the police authorities take responsibility for seeing to it that the Ordinace and the Regulations are put into execution. Prepared opium is delivered to licensed smokers only through channels under Government con-

As central organ for the control of opium, a sanitary section is established in the Government-General of Formosa, charged with superintendence of the business throughout the island. As local organs each provincial, district, and municipal office has in its service officials in charge of sanitary affairs whose duty it is to control opium in their localities, while police officials stationed in the country districts stand, so to speak, on the front in the battle against opium, are directly concerned in the work of control.

Thus though the measures taken for the control of illicit production and for the manufacture of opium by the Government leave nothing to be desired, the Government authorities are always beset of difficulties in exercising control over the smuggling of the goods from China. Ways and means resorted to by smugglers are, as told later on, exceedingly ingenious and scarcely be completely brustrated. Such being the case, the number of opium addicts who had contracted the harmful habit through secret smoking has never been on the wane. In view of this, as already said, the Government authorities promulgated the revised Opium Ordinance for Formosa in January, 1928, inaugurating a new policy for checking the spread of the evil habit by controlling the addicts themselves. 2. Manufacture, Sale, and Consumption of Prepared Opium.

Prepared Opium is manufactured at the Government Monopoly Factory, and is delivered to licensed smokers through two monopoly organs, viz., wholesale dealers in prepared opium appointed by local Governors and retail dealer in prepared opium who have obtained licenses therefor from local Governors. In regard to the sale, purchase and delivery of prepared opium that are carried on amongst the monopoly organs themselves, or between them and licenses smokers, and also in regard to the consumption of opium by licensed smokers, the following restrictions are provided with a view to preventing unlawful transactions and illicit consumption.

a) A licensed smoker of prepared opium shall not buy or possess any quantity of prepared opium exceeding his portion for three days. If he is in special circumstances, he may be allowed to buy or possess such amount as is permitted by the police.

c) A licensed smoker of prepared opium shall not be allowed to consume any amount of prepared opium exceeding the prescribed amount for daily use, nor to smoke in company of nonlicensed persons except his own family member.

4. Control of Smuggling of Opium.

Attempts at Smuggling opium into the island are made for the purpose of supplying it to secret smokers. The smugglers are

( 19 )

[Note: In the table, figures in columns 4, 6, and 7 are faint in several rows (especially 1913–1920 and 1926–1928), but are legible and verified by column and row totals. The table header reads "bnsiness" as printed.]

=== Page 50 ===
chiefly Chinese sailors and merchants who secretly bring in the goods through Keelung, Takao, Anping, Rokko and some other ports on the west coast by means of Chinese junks.

A few examples of the ingenious ways and means resorted to by opium smugglers are given below.

1. As to the method of carrying opium on board a vessel, it is concealed in some part of its sides or some other place, which is lined double and is not easily noticeable. Often opium properly packed is suspended from the rudder or the full below the water-line and is sunk deep by means of a weight attached thereto, so that it does not float up. Such clever devices make the ordinary procedure of inspection of a vessel carrying the prohibited goods quite ineffective.

2 As to the method of landing, after official inspection has been made the smugglers mix up opium with other goods, or land it by means of sampan under cover of darkness. Oftentimes, a vessel carrying opium casts anchor far away in the offing, and the smugglers calling a small boat along-side it by signalling with lights or other means deliver it into the hands of their accomplices by pre-arranged methods.

3. After landing, the smugglers conceal opium for the time being by burying it deep in the sand along the beach or put the goods in trust in the hands of those who are likely to escape the eye of the police. Later on, when circumstances warrant no fear of being detected, they transport or sell the contraband goods.

Persons engaged in the traffic of smuggled opium seem to have and observe a code of honour of their own and to be pledged to

( 20 )

=== Page 51 ===
absolute secrecy, so that the rounding of smugglers is, in most cases, attendant upon cases of secret sale and purchase being discovered and taken up.

Purchasers never tell the truth about the name or address of the sellers, invariably declaring that they have bought the goods from Chinese of whose name or address they know nothing. In these circumstances, detection of the offence of smuggling is far more difficult that of other crimes.

But as the control of smuggling is the precursor of the control of secret smokers, the police officials in collaboration with the Customs authorities are taking special pains to eradicate it. For doing so, all in-coming vessels are inspected as a matter of course and a careful watch is kept on the doings and movements of suspicious ships and crews. When a smuggling case is found, the officials in charge at once report it to the Government, giving in detail the name, address and age of the smuggler, imformation concering the vessel carrying the contraband goods, the method of smuggling and other noteworthy paticulars. Meanwhile the case is communicated to local Governments so that the central and local police force may act in coordination against smuggling. In this respect the Police Affairs Department of the Central Government is constantly at work for collection and arrangement of information from all sources regarding smuggling for giving assistance and instruction to the local authorities in regard to the control of the offence. ANNEXED LAWS AND REGULATIONS

Opium Ordinance of Formosa.
(Revised in 1929 in accordance with the provisions of the International Convention concluded in Geneva in 1925).

ARTICLE 1.—In application of the present Ordinance the term "Opium" means raw opium, prepared opium and medicinal opium.

ARTICLE 2.—No opium shall be smoked except when an opium addict who had contracted the habit previous to the coming into force of the Ordinance and who obtained the special permit from the Governor-General, smokes opium which is sold by the Government-General.

ARTICLE 3.—The manufacture of opium and sale of prepared and medicinal opium is carried on by the Government-General.
No instrument for smoking opium shall be manufactured unless it is permitted by the Government-General.

ARTICLE 4.—No opium or the instruments for smoking opium shall be imported or exported except when it is done in accordance with any one of the following provisions :
1. When the Government-General imports opium.
2. When medicinal opium is exported in accordance with the provision of the Government Order.
3. When raw opium or medicinal opium is transhipped in Formosa or in transit through Formosa with a destination outside of Formosa with the certificate issued by the

( 24 )

=== Page 55 ===
Government of the importing country and is recognised that it is not directed for the purpose of illicit use.

ARTICLE 5.--No opium or instrument for smoking opium shall be, without permit issued by the Governor-General, sold or bought, given or taken, or possessed or held except when it comes under any one of the following provisions :

1. When the wholesale agent of prepared opium or the retailer of prepared opium sells or buys, gives or takes or possesses or holds prepared opium.

2. When the manufacturer of the instrument of smoking opium or its retailer sells or buys, gives of takes, or possesses or holds the instruments for smoking opium.

3. When the licensed smoker of opium receives, possesses or holds prepared opium or instrument for smoking opium.

4. When a physician, a dentist, a veterinary surgeon, a druggist, or a manufacturer of drugs, sells or buys, gives or takes, possesses or holds medicinal opium.

5. When any person receives, possesses, or holds medicinal opium on the strength of the prescription gives by a physicians, a dentist, or a veterinary surgeon.

6. When, in accordance with the provision of any one of the previous clauses, a person who possessed or held prepared opium, medicinal opium, or instruments for smoking opium, is no longer being capable to possess or hold them, or in case no person possesses or holds them, the person, or the heir or the administrator of the property, hands them over or possesses or holds them.

ARTICLE 6.—The appointed wholesale agent or retailor of the prepared opium shall not work up or mix with other substances the prepared opium.
