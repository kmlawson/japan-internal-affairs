SIR:

I have the honor to report concerning the recent enforced dissolution of the Taiwan Minshuto Party (Taiwan Democratic Party) by action of local police acting under direction of the authorities of the Government General.

This political party held a general meeting on the 18th to discuss formulation and provisions of a platform to be adopted by the party. On the evening of that day, the Chief of Kita Police (municipal division of Taihoku) visited the meeting and ordered the disbanding of the party under Article 8 Clause 2 of the Law Governing Preservation of Peace and Order. Sixteen of the most prominent persons in the party were arrested and taken to the Kita Police Station, some still being confined in jail, others being released after domiciliary searches disclosed no affiliation with communistic activities in Japan Proper and other countries.

The real causes, however, for the action of the authorities in disbanding the Minshuto Party are believed to lie in the opium question and the Musha Affair. The Department will recall that in the report "A Survey of the Opium Situation in Taiwan" was set forth a summary of the stand of the Minshuto Party on this question, a translation of the petition of this party to the League of Nations being appended. There is, therefore, no doubt that the continued opposition of the Minshuto to the opium monopoly and policy in Taiwan has evoked this dissolution as a measure of governmental retaliation. As concerns the Musha Affair, it is stated that the Minshuto Party took the opportunity to spread rumors contrary to the public peace and welfare, a [handwritten above next line: 894A.4016/3] number of which were noted in Despatch No. 58 from this office, "The Uprising of Savages in Taiwan", dated December 9, 1930. In connection with the Musha Affair this office hopes to be able to submit for the consideration of the Department a translation of some of the material prepared by the Minshuto on that occasion.

Leaders of the party are quoted as saying that the Minshuto will be reorganized to continue political activities in another form.

There is some basis for doubting the bona fides of the statement of the Chief of Federal Police that the existance of the party obstructs the amity between Formosans and Japanese, inasmuch as actions by the Japanese themselves do not foster any deep impression of desire for amity but rather of attaining and maintaining absolute and despotic supremacy, in commerce and otherwise. Many instances in trade practises might be quoted about this point. It may also be mentioned in passing that there is a strong current of opposition to intermarriage between Formosans and Japanese.

On the whole, the dissolution of the Minshuto because of present radical tendencies is justified. However, the political and social problems are not settled by the disbanding of this party. These will continue to crop up from time to time and at some later date will have to be answered.
