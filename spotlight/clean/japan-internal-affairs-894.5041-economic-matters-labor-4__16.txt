The above-quoted wages are for Formosan Chinese laborers. Japanese laborers in these industries are negligible. The working hours are 10 hours a day, 7 days a week. However, most employees get off two days a month, without pay.

There are no labor laws in Taiwan. Neither are there any labor unions; the Taiwan Government discourages the unionization of labor. Nor are there any provisions for bonuses or overtime payment of labor, but employees [employers?] usually pay for overtime work

Medical attention of workmen hurt on the job is usually provided for by the employer, and consolation money is usually paid when a workmen is killed or totally disabled as a result of an accident incurred in the line of duty. Another local custom is that a worker is given 30 days' notice prior to being discharged, or in the absence of notice, 30 days' pay.

Wages in Other Trades

There follows a table showing daily wages in various other trades. This information was taken from a Government Statistical Publication and relates to the end of 1934. Later statistics are not available. However, there has been little change in the wage scale since that time.

Kind | Japanese | Formosan Chinese
Carpenter | Yen 2.80 | Yen 1.20
Brick layer | 2.80 | 1.70
Tile layer | 3.20 | 1.80
Painter | 2.50 | 1.60
Plasterer | 2.50 | 2.00
Typesetter | 2.50 | 1.80
Lithographer | 2.70 | 1.30
Book Binder | 2.30 | 1.20

No data are obtainable regarding railroad workers. The railroads are a Government monopoly, and virtually all of the trainmen are Japanese. Japanese employees of the Taiwan Government Railways are paid roughly 60 per cent more than like workmen in Japan proper. In addition they are given free housing. Formosan Chinese employees of the railways are paid according to the local scale for Formosan Chinese laborers.
