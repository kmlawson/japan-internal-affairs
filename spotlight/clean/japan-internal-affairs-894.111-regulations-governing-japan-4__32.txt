On the morning of the 16th I made inquiry of the Secretary in Charge of Foreign Affairs of the Government General, and within the next two days had received the reply that for the duration of the "China Incident" no Chinese would be permitted to reenter Taiwan if they left the island for a trip to other than some place within the Japanese Empire. The Secretary further stated that if I, as Consul, would give official assurances that these men would in no way discuss the Far Eastern trouble during their stay abroad, the Japanese officials would be willing to reconsider the case. Of course I could give no such guarantee. On the 18th I sent the information in the following form to firms in Taihoku having American interests and also to the foreign consuls here:

"As a matter of concern to you and to the Chinese employed by your firm, who may intend to leave Taiwan during the winter season, the following information will be of interest:

Mr. Choji Koshimura, Secretary in Charge of Foreign Affairs of the Government General of Taiwan states that for the duration of the present "China Incident" citizens of China now residing in Taiwan who leave Taiwan for any place abroad, except for travel within territory of the Japanese Empire, will not be permitted to reenter Taiwan upon their return from such a visit."

Mr. Coughlin has since stated that his men have definitely abandoned the idea of going to China this season.
